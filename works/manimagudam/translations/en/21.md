# Scene 21

[The Minister's house. Minister Gunaseelar speaks wearily to Vanji.]

Gunaseelar: Nothing but confusion — nothing but confusion! Where has Kalarani gone? What happened to her? Into whose hands has she fallen? Until now I know nothing! Am I to worry about that, or about the revolutionary forces that have risen on all sides against the government? That Puthumaippithan is turning the very hearts of the people into a volcano! If that volcano erupts, my authority — status — ministerial office — everything will melt and disappear in its lava!

Vanji: My lord — why wander in search of salt while standing on the seashore? Gurunathar is there as the remedy for all your worries. Is finding where Kalarani is beyond him? If he wished, could he not curse Puthumaippithan and Ponnazhagan's group and burn them to ashes?

Gunaseelar: Quite so. But when I said something like that to Gurunathar, do you know what he asked me? He asked whether Puthumaippithan's spirit had entered the Minister's body! Puthumaippithan is the one who asks questions like that in his newspaper! If we ask them too, Gurunathar only gets angry!

Vanji: Then is Gurunathar powerless?

Gunaseelar: Don't speak loudly — someone may hear! There is that proverb, `a leaf-cup for the ghee — ghee for the leaf-cup`; like that, I am his support and he is mine!

Vanji: A revolution cannot be suppressed by sham supports like these, my lord!

Gunaseelar: Vanji! You need not offer any more ideas — go! Go!

[She leaves. A guard enters.]

Guard: My lord! The newspaper editor and Gurunathar have arrived in palanquins. They say they must meet you urgently.

Gunaseelar: Bring them in! Bring them in!

[Gurunathar and Ariharanathar enter and sit.]

Gunaseelar: Why this sudden visit?

Ariharanathar: The Assembly of Nobles met urgently, unofficially, and examined the disturbances in the country. There was apparently a heated debate over the report that the king had not consented to the temple. In the end they resolved that the king himself must come before the Assembly of Nobles and consent to the temple by signing; only after he signs will the Assembly take further action.

Gunaseelar: What caused this decision?

Gurunathar: That *Manimagudam* newspaper.

Gunaseelar: Ayyo! If opposition arises against us even in the Assembly of Nobles, we will not be able to manage it at all!

Ariharanathar: Ponnazhagan and Alli's blockade in Kodikkal Nagar, and the report in *Manimagudam* that the government committed great atrocities there, have created tremendous agitation among the people. If the king and the Assembly of Nobles are not on our side at a time like this, our offices themselves are in danger!

Gurunathar: Yes. The king must somehow come to the Assembly of Nobles. He must sign the temple resolution — the Minister is responsible for making that happen!

[At that moment two guards drag in a man who looks like a madman, beating him as they bring him. The man has hundreds of copies of *Manimagudam* in his hands. The Minister, Gurunathar and Ariharanathar stare at him in surprise.]

Gunaseelar: Who is he?

Guard: My lord! He was selling *Manimagudam*. We arrested him according to your order.

Gunaseelar: Sabash!... Gurunathar! I have secretly ordered the arrest of everyone selling *Manimagudam*, wherever they are found. He is the tenth man caught like that!

Ariharanathar: Look at him — he seems like a madman. [Approaching him.] Hey... what connection do you have with this newspaper?

Man: I get money if I sell it! That's the whole connection!

Gunaseelar: Where is its editor Puthumaippithan?

Man: Don't know, sir. I was sleeping on the street. Suddenly a man came and said, `Sell these papers and keep the money yourself.` So I came to sell them — and these men caught me, Swami! Let me go! My lord! May I go?

Gunaseelar: Chi! Where do you think you're going? [He slaps him. To the guard.] Hey! Arrest him and lock him up. Make him set fire to these newspapers!

Gurunathar: Yes — let us watch to our heart's delight. Let it happen right here!

[The guards take the newspapers from him, throw them down and force him to set them alight with his own hand. When he hesitates, they beat him. Then he himself sets the papers on fire with a small torch. As he lights them, he shouts:]

Man: May the fire I too have lit blaze — blaze!

[Gurunathar watches the newspapers burn with a frenzied laugh. At that moment the palace bodyguard Ulagappan arrives. His whole body appears to be trembling. Seeing his agitation...]

Gunaseelar: Ulagappa — what happened?

Ulagappan: Danger, my lord! Danger! The king has left the palace! He has renounced the throne!

Gunaseelar: What?

Ulagappan: Here is the letter he left behind!

[Gunaseelar reads the letter.]

> Dear Minister,
>
> The crises that have arisen in the country frighten me. At any moment this land may fall into the hands of the revolutionaries. Rather than be arrested and humiliated by them then, I decided it would be better to flee now, and I have left accordingly. I have decided to renounce not merely the royal office but this very world, and to depart peacefully for the upper world. Please have Gurunathar conduct all the rites required for my soul to attain peace. That is the one help I ask of you. I take leave.
>
> Manimaran  
> Former King of Manimagudam

Ariharanathar: All our plans have collapsed. If this news becomes known outside, our rule will disappear the very next moment — and all of us will have to adorn prison cells!

Ulagappan: There is no room in prison even for that. A prison meant to hold a thousand people now has four thousand packed in like sheep and cattle!

Manimagudam Seller: [Interrupting] If all of you go inside, all of them can come outside, can't they?

[The guard angrily slaps him and warns him not to speak.]

Gurunathar: Minister! If passers-by in the street, the poor and the lowly, destitutes like this newspaper seller and crowds of labourers begin talking about the government, that is a great danger. I may not say it outside, but I know it very well. In a country, once worn-down people like this fellow [pointing to the seller] gain even a little clarity, even iron dictatorships that have been erected there will crumble to dust!... This is exactly the time, Minister, when we must be cautious...

Gunaseelar: Gurunathar! Do not frighten me more and more. Before the news that the king is gone spreads like fire, devise a plan!

Gurunathar: A plan — I must devise one?

[As he says this, he walks towards the *Manimagudam* seller and stares closely at his face — so intensely that the man becomes frightened and everyone present is astonished. Then Gurunathar looks at the king's portrait hanging there. He looks back and forth between the portrait and the man, and suddenly cries out.]

Gurunathar: We have found the king, Minister — we have found the king!

Gunaseelar: What?

Ulagappan: Where? Where? Where is our king?

Gurunathar: Minister! The king wrote in his letter that he is going to the upper world, did he not? Sabash! Sabash! Then here — we have found the king! We have found a king we can make dance exactly as we please! [To the man.] Hey, wandering fellow! Will you rule the country?

Man: What does that mean, sir?

Gurunathar: The country! The country — he asks whether you will become king of the land.

Man: King? Ayyo! No, sir... being king is dangerous...

Gurunathar: What danger? Gunaseelar!... He looks almost exactly like our king! Put royal clothes on him and he will look like the king himself! Let a doll like this adorn the court of Manimagudam! We can completely suppress the news that the king has left!

Gunaseelar: Yes — an excellent idea!

Ariharanathar: An idea that could occur to no one else... from now on, he is the king...

Ulagappan: Ah! His lucky period has begun — the fellow is enormously fortunate!

Man: I won't be king, sir — let me go... If you're king you have to go to war — earn a bad name among the people — stand all the time with sword in hand — live in fear of who will invade and who will surround the fort! No, sir, I don't want a job where I have to give up my life! If you want, I'll be Gurunathar.

Gunaseelar: Gurunathar?

Man: Yes, sir... that's the job where you gain fame without suffering! You can live without lifting knife or weapon. Who rules, who dies — what does it matter? Our own life will be comfortable! Give me that job!

Gunaseelar: You fool!... Listen to what you're told! You will be king!... I will see that no danger comes to you. If you refuse, you will receive the death sentence! Now tell me: will you refuse and suffer a death sentence, or agree and give death sentences to many others?

Man: I agree — but I won't give everyone the death sentence. There are only three people in this country whom I will sentence to death.

Ariharanathar: Who are they, man?

Man: They've already died! You wouldn't know them even if I told you.

Ulagappan: Death sentences for people already dead? Right — right!

Man: Shh! `A brave man dies only once — a coward dies many times`; I read that once in this *Manimagudam* newspaper!

[He grins as he says it.]

Gunaseelar: Hey! From now on you must not speak about the *Manimagudam* newspaper.

Man: I must speak about the country of Manimagudam! Right, sir! Ah — I forgot to ask one thing. When are you going to stage the play?

Gunaseelar: A play?

Man: I have to put on the king's costume, don't I? Isn't that a play?

Ariharanathar: Fine — a little madness even makes him exactly the right man for us! Hey! You must remain permanent king here — like us!

Man: Is that so? Right, right! Then who is permanently wearing the Minister costume now? Who is wearing the Gurunathar costume?... And what costume is he wearing?

[He points to Ariharanathar.]

Gunaseelar: Ulagappa! Don't let him keep talking! It is your responsibility to turn him into the king! You yourself must once again be the bodyguard — for this new king! Train him in exactly how the old king walked and how he sat. Quickly — get him ready to appear before the Assembly of Nobles tomorrow itself!

Ariharanathar: Yes — he has to sign before the Assembly. What will we do about that?

Gunaseelar: Yes — what shall we do? [Thinking.] Fine — we will say the king is suffering from a disorder of the brain, and that at the Assembly's request we brought him despite his physical condition! Somehow we can escape temporarily, can't we? [To the man.] Hey, wanderer! Tomorrow in the Assembly of Nobles you must act exactly like a king whose brain is disordered.

Man: Oh! Two kinds of acting? Fine — the world itself is a stage!

[He looks at Ulagappan.]

Gunaseelar: Ulagappa — take him away!

Ulagappan: Come, O king of kings, come!

Man: Ah — you go first, O black rose of roses!

[As they leave, the man winks at Ulagappan, and the two smile at each other. Does Ulagappan not know that Manimaran himself is the *Manimagudam* seller, Puthumaippithan, and now the counterfeit king?]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/21.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The final stage direction's explicit identity disclosure is preserved exactly at the point where the Tamil assembly reveals it.
- The `leaf-cup / ghee` proverb is kept literal rather than replaced with an unrelated English proverb.
- The seller's comic preference for the safer `Gurunathar` profession and the Minister's death-sentence choice are retained without softening their political satire.
- `மணிமகுடம்` remains *Manimagudam* as both newspaper title and polity name according to immediate context.
