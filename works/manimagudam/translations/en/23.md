# Scene 23

[Assembly of Nobles. Ten nobles are seated. Members of the public are gathered in a separate place. Gurunathar, Ariharanathar and the Minister are present.]

Minister: [Rising] Honoured representatives of Manimagudam! Members of the public! Today His Majesty King Manimara is visiting our Assembly. His Majesty has agreed to come despite being unwell, precisely to prove false all the reports that there is some grievance between the Assembly of Nobles and the king regarding construction of the temple. By God's command, there is no division at all in this Assembly of Nobles. There is no difference of opinion of any kind between the king and the seat of power. There is only one regrettable piece of news: our glorious sovereign has developed a slight disorder of the brain. Our Sanatana sovereign, Gurunathar, has promised to cure even that illness of the imperial sovereign's brain. Let us all pray to God that he recovers quickly — that he must recover! It is now nearly time for the king to arrive. I end my introduction here.

[The king and Ulagappan enter.]

Ulagappan: Lord of the Manimagudam — His Glorious Majesty — King Manimara — welcome! Welcome!

[As the king enters, everyone rises and bows. The king looks grandly at all of them and sits on the royal seat. Silence fills the Assembly. The king rises, grins at the Assembly and calls, `Minister!`]

Minister: Your Majesty! The resolution concerning Kodikkal Nagar...

King: Kodikkal! Kodikkal! What leg does a kodi have, man? `Kodi-kal`, they say — the leg of a creeper! Oh!... A kodi does have legs! It does! Women are called flower-creepers. Women have legs. Therefore a creeper has legs. Hm, fine — Minister! What did you say? What must be done with Kodikkal...?

Minister: It must be vacated...

King: Ayyo! If the *kaal* — the leg — is emptied, how can one walk? Impossible, impossible — one cannot walk... one would have to walk like this!

[He walks like a lame man. The nobles and the public look on with pity.]

Noble: Minister! Since the king is unwell, it would be better to take him back to the palace!

King: Palace! `Aran` means fort — `manai` means house — fort-house! Leave the fort! Leave the fort! Which fort am I to leave?

Minister: Your Majesty! We can go to the palace.

King: Impossible — I shall go only to the forest! It will take fourteen years for me to return. Until then let my crown rule this country. Here, Minister! Place the throne upon this crown and rule in my place; otherwise let fire rule!

[He gives the crown into the Minister's hand and starts as though to leave. Then he turns back.]

King: Aha! Minister! Did I give you the crown? This must not be placed upon the throne... what a sin... the throne will become dirty. Here — put this upon the throne instead...

[He takes back the crown, removes his footwear and places it in the Minister's hands. The Minister stands holding the sandals.]

King: [Looking at the crown] Are there people who do not become intoxicated by the sight of you — by royal luxury, by power, are there people who do not become intoxicated by you? [Finishing the song.] Hey, crown! Golden roof inlaid with the nine gems and pearls! Upon my head you are a rock!

[He places the crown on his head and looks at the footwear in the Minister's hands.]

King: [To the Minister] What a wonder! Minister — you are standing with *paadhukai* on your hand! Oh! Hearing `paadhukai`, did you put it on your hand? What a great genius you are! This is *paadha-kai* — a `foot-hand`; therefore it belongs on the foot. Paadhukai — paadhukai!

[He sings.]

Gurunathar: [Rising] Minister!... The king's condition causes me anguish. I cannot bear to see it. Arrange to take him away at once!

King: [Hearing this] Hey... Ulagam!

Ulagappan: Your Majesty!

King: Who is that? Who is that creature?

Ulagappan: He is our Gurunathar, my lord.

King: Guru — Nathar — oh! Is he the husband, the *nathar*, of a woman named Guru? Why would they name a woman `Guru`, man?

Ulagappan: No, my lord — Gurunathar means a great *mahan*!

King: Ah! [In surprise.] An elephant is huge!

Ulagappan: No — a great devotee!

King: What sort of fellow is this? I ask whether he is an elephant and he says a *maan* — a deer! Deer! Hey, deer! Valli-deer! A spotted deer! [He sings mockingly.] Ah — I have handed the kingdom to the Minister and come to the forest, have I not? Then this deer is neither Valli's deer nor a spotted deer — this is the magical deer, Maricha! It will cry `Rama! Rama!` [To Ulagappan.] Hey Lakshmana! Do not be fooled into thinking it cries in devotion! Take the bow! String the arrow!

[He draws his sword and extends it towards Ulagappan. Ulagappan touches the sword.]

King: Hey! When I say `string the arrow`, are you touching the arrow? It means you must *string* it, fool!

Ariharanathar: [Pleading] Your Majesty! Do not mock the Guru! He is a *periyavaal* — a great one!

King: What I have is what — a small sword? This too is a *periya vaal* — a big sword! And a war-sword! A war-sword that will leap whoever tries to stop it! [Whirling the sword and dancing.] `Without knife, without blood, a war is coming!`

Gurunathar: Minister! Does it not pain you to let His Majesty be made a spectacle like this? Take him away at once! Hey, Ulagappa! What are you doing?

King: Ulagappa, are you dancing, Ulagappa? Let the demonstrators call this a lament, appa! [Looking at the Assembly of Nobles.] I told you, `Find a way, appa` — this Jegathappan thought and his mind grew weary! [Grandly.] Since things stand like this, Ulagappa — make a new account! Lease all property commonly to everyone!

[At this moment the Minister and Gurunathar signal that the king must be taken away.]

Ulagappan: Come, my lord — let us go at once and make all property common and lease it out!

[A soldier bows and approaches to escort the king. The moment he sees him, the king trembles.]

Ulagappan: Do not be afraid, my lord! He is your own soldier!

King: What? Fear? Me, afraid? [Gesturing towards Gurunathar.] Should this lion fear that little fox?

> No fear, no fear — there is no such thing as fear!  
> Even if the sky crashes down upon my head, there is no such thing as fear!

[He sings in a loud voice. Ulagappan and the soldier drag the king inside. Everyone assembled makes a sympathetic `ich` sound.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/23.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- This scene depends heavily on deliberate sound-puns. Tamil forms such as `Kodikkal / kodi-kal`, `kaal / kaali`, `Gurunathar`, `mahan / maan`, `periyavaal / periya vaal`, and `paadhukai` are kept visibly in English where literal substitution would destroy the joke.
- The king's fourteen-year forest reference and Maricha / Rama / Lakshmana sequence are translated only to the extent printed in the verified Tamil scene; no outside epic detail is added.
- The source-visible song lines are translated, but no additional song text is imported.
- The call to make property common remains part of the counterfeit king's staged `madness` and is not rewritten as explanatory political prose.
