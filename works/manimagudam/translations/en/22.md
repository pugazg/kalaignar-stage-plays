# Scene 22

[On a street a man loudly sells *Manimagudam*. Many people buy it and read it, standing around in groups.]

Seller: *Manimagudam* — editor Puthumaippithan! Ponnazhagan and Alli's moral protest in Kodikkal Nagar! Attempt to kill Ponnazhagan! It is said that King Manimaran himself is deeply distressed on hearing of the atrocities committed by the palace cavalry in Kodikkal Nagar. Reports are circulating that conflict is raging among Gurunathar, the Minister and the king. Many more truths are soon to explode!

A huge public meeting in the capital! A long speech by Puthumaippithan! The cry raised by lakhs upon lakhs of people against Gurunathar and the *People's Servant* newspaper reached the sky! Like the roar of sea waves they cried, `We need people's government!` There is no longer life! No prosperity! No work in this country for swindlers who live by deceiving people in the name of God and religion! The awakened human being is now a lion advancing proudly — the fattened chaff are now the pack of little foxes — he has exposed them through reason!

From now on devotion — religion — God — all are ruin! Ruin! Ruin!

[Government soldiers then threaten those reading the paper, snatch the copies from their hands and throw them away. They arrest the man selling *Manimagudam* as well.]

[On the same street a seller of *People's Servant* calls out. No one buys it. At last the seller himself takes up a copy and reads aloud.]

Seller: `*People's Servant*, editor Ariharanathar — the Assembly of Nobles meets tomorrow. To personally declare his consent regarding construction of the temple, Manimagudam's ruler, Maharaja Manimara, will visit the Assembly of Nobles. Even though the king is unwell, after appearing before the Assembly tomorrow His Majesty will take a long period of rest and restore his health!`

[Those who hear the news are astonished.]

One Man: Hey, the king himself is coming! Let's go to the Assembly tomorrow, man!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/22.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The scene keeps the contrast between the suppressed *Manimagudam* report and the ignored *People's Servant* announcement.
- `அறப்போராட்டம்` is rendered `moral protest` here to preserve the source's nonviolent ethical-political framing rather than importing a later technical label.
- The source's repeated `பாழ்! பாழ்! பாழ்!` remains `Ruin! Ruin! Ruin!` rather than being softened.
