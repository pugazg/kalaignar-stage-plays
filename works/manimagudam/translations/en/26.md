# Scene 26

[The king in a private place. The king stands submissively; the Minister is seated.]

Minister: Remember everything I told you. The seventh day of Margazhi — the foundation-stone ceremony. Do not forget!

King: Yes, my lord! Just as you told me, I will hand over authority to you in front of the people, sir!

Minister: Good! Be careful!

[After speaking, the Minister leaves. As soon as he is gone, the king sits with dignity, deep in thought. Ulagappan brings him fruit juice.]

King: [Taking it] Hm. I drink fruit juice — and there I see the people of Kodikkal Nagar suffering with their children, without even a place to boil gruel and drink it!

Ulagappan: It is to remove those sufferings that you are enduring all this hardship! Eat and drink a little to overcome your exhaustion, my lord!

King: [After drinking] Ulagappa — the fruit juice is terribly salty!

Ulagappan: Salty? Ayyo! Grapes may turn sour — but they do not taste of salt!

King: It is salt, Ulagappa! This is not fruit juice! These are the tears shed by the trembling poor! You have never tasted tears; you are unmarried, aren't you? You would not have tasted them. Even the tears that run to the lips when lovers, cheek against cheek, weep in the bedchamber are not sweet but salty — then will the tears of poor mothers lying on bare earth, clutching children whose bones pierce when touched, taste sweet? No — no! There, the people of Kodikkal Nagar are crying aloud! That sound pierces my fortress walls! There — I hear it. You listen too! Listen, listen, Ulagappa, listen!

[He throws away the fruit juice.]

Ulagappan: Your Majesty, be at peace!

King: Where is peace for me? Where is peace for me? From one side — two sides? From how many sides am I attacked? When we speak of attack, news flies into a fort that crowned kings have laid siege with land armies and navies to seize the crown. That kind of attack? I would have welcomed such an attack freely! The king of the tiger-land sent a message laying down the manner of battle: no spear, no sword, shoulder against shoulder! Did I tremble? I sent him an invitation saying, `I have the strength to resist — come!`; I did not seek an escape route on hearing of attack! At sleeping time, when the town lay still, a thousand warriors entered the fort with drawn swords and approached the flagstaff to bring it down from the root. Did I not then oppose them with a hundred men and cut them apart? None of those were attacks, Ulagappa — they were games of valour to me.

But religion attacks me after building fences called Veda, Sastra and Purana — sharper than a spear and more glittering than a sword! It splits my skull, Ulagappa — it splits my skull! The ministerial seat wishes to place me in a grave and scatter flowers and puffed grain over me. The propaganda instrument that claims to exist for the people — *People's Servant*, with its intoxicating sacred name — turns the people into sheep and pours burning embers into the wound in my heart! Religion — newspaper — arrogant authority: the people attacked by all three curse me as the cause of everything! They curse, `Let this empire turn to ash!`

And the People's Forum, which echoes their voice, without thinking of removing the root causes, without considering that I am a man forced to dance as the Assembly of Nobles orders, snarls and leaps upon me — I am the target even of the attack of that furious tiger-pack, Ulagappa — I am the target! If I use my authority to remove the people's suffering and condemn the religious Gurunathar, these very people will oppose me then too! They will cry, `Sacrilege! Can you insult God's representative?` What am I to do, Ulagappa — what am I to do?

I went to transform the People's Forum into a fitting revolutionary forum. Failure — failure for me there too. Trouble upon trouble — defeat upon defeat. Traitors live on, their lives continuing untouched by spreading sorrow! There is no place for my pure, good principles! Will there remain no place at all? In this world must the principled man live on the rubbish heap while the crooked-path traveller lives in the tower? Can this not be changed? Why can it not?

How many jewels who served human society lie buried in this earth! In the name of those noble ones — in the name of those embodiments of sacrifice — in the name of those lamps of valour — Ulagappa, I tell you; I tell my own heart; citizens of Manimagudapuram, I tell you too: even if I must stake my life, I shall make lofty ideals triumph — victory — victory — victory alone!

Fallen is the religious guru's tyranny! Destroyed is the durbar of the arrogant! From this day the blackout propaganda instrument is abolished! Victory! Victory! Victory! Ulagappa...

Ulagappan: Your Majesty.

King: The Minister has just told me that I must conduct the foundation-stone ceremony for the temple in Kodikkal Nagar. I shall go to the ceremony and expose the acts of these deceivers before the people gathered there! I shall hand Manimagudapuram over to the people! The right opportunity — an opportunity to meet the people! The Minister thinks I will hand authority over to him! No! No!! I shall hand the country over to the people of the country. I shall drive these foxes into the middle of the street!! Victory! Victory! A victorious opportunity! If I lose this, no other opportunity will come! What is going to take place is not a foundation-stone ceremony! It will be a flag-raising ceremony for a new republic! A flag-raising ceremony!
