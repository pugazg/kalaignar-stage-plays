# Scene 18

[People's Forum. In one room Ponnazhagan and Kalarani are present.]

Kalarani: Do not suspect that I will escape and run away from here. Do not worry that Father may learn I am here and rescue me — I want to remain here.

Ponnazhagan: What you say may be true. But many more days must pass before I can believe it. We do not easily trust even those who want to become members of the Forum. To trust a Minister's daughter...

Kalarani: I have asked so many times and never received a proper answer. At least tell me now! Why was I abducted?

Ponnazhagan: I already told you — so that a marriage between you and the king would not take place!

Kalarani: Fine! I will not marry the king. What happens to me after that?

Ponnazhagan: Must that too be decided in this hurry?

Kalarani: Reaching a decision would satisfy my heart.

Ponnazhagan: Women themselves should decide matters of marriage.

Kalarani: And men should not oppose their decision, should they?

Ponnazhagan: Yes!

Kalarani: Let me ask one thing... I say I will stay here and not return to the palace. What is the reason for that?

Ponnazhagan: You said you were attached to the people's movement. That is the reason — what new reason is there?

Kalarani: Seen from afar, the green beauty of a grove calls a young woman closer. Once she enters the grove, one golden flower there attracts her! She plucks that flower with her soft hands, wears it in her hair and rejoices. What does that tell you?

Ponnazhagan: It tells me women like flowers...

Kalarani: Oh! You have made such a great illustration so ordinary. I mean that the grove attracts the woman — and that one flower attracts her too.

Ponnazhagan: Yes.

Kalarani: Why would a woman who came to the grove pluck only that flower?

Ponnazhagan: What else would she pluck? Leaves and twigs?

Kalarani: That is not what I mean.

Ponnazhagan: Say what you mean without circling around it.

Kalarani: The People's Forum is the grove that attracted me! And the flower there that I wish to wear and delight in... the flower...

Ponnazhagan: [Looking at her suspiciously and trying to change the subject] Kalarani! Only if you think of the People's Forum as a garden need you pine over a flower. Think of the People's Forum as a camp! Imagine it as a military encampment.

Kalarani: There is a sword there too; isn't that enough to attract women of courage?

Ponnazhagan: What do this sword and flower you speak of mean?

Kalarani: Is there not an inseparable bond between a sword and a flower?

Ponnazhagan: Warriors place a flower garland on a victorious sword! Is that what you mean?

Kalarani: Yes. You are the sword! I am...

Ponnazhagan: Kalarani! [Looks at her in surprise.]

Kalarani: I am the flower.

Ponnazhagan: A wrong thought — a very wrong thought. Change your mind immediately!

Kalarani: I cannot change it! From the day I saw you in my house; from the day I heard your beautiful words full of passion; from the day I delighted in that heroic voice raised for the welfare of the people — from that day I became your slave! I forgot that I was the Minister's daughter. In dreams — awake — in the things I saw when my eyes were open — and, when I closed them, on the inner side of my eyelids — I saw only your sacred form! I longed to know whether we would meet again. I met you. I stood upon the peak of happiness. But today your words push me down into a sea of sorrow. Please do not think wrongly of me. Do not hate me merely because I am the Minister's daughter. It is in the salt sea that the shining pearl is found! Do not forget that!

Ponnazhagan: Speak of something else.

Kalarani: Whatever I speak of, whatever I think of, I will circle round and return here. Say one word and it will be enough: you are the sword — I am the flower!

Ponnazhagan: The flower that captured my heart is already here in this Forum! A flower of revolution possessing every quality you described!

Kalarani: The alli flower?

Ponnazhagan: Yes — Alli!

[At that moment Alli enters carrying a copy of *Manimagudam*.]

Alli: Have you seen the new newspaper? *Manimagudam*! Ah! This is the voice of the people! Is this Puthumaippithan's writing — or a new flood surging forward? An article — or a wave of the sea? A mass of words — or a storm wind? People are praising it like this, standing in groups everywhere! Gurunathar is finished now! The false newspaper cannot raise its head! The Minister's repression is dust — dust!

Ponnazhagan: You yourself keep praising it. Give me the paper!

Alli: [Touches the paper reverently to her eyes.] Ah! This paper of ideals has shaken the whole country!

[She gives the paper to Ponnazhagan, then looks at Kalarani.]

Alli: Well, Kalarani! How is the atmosphere of the Forum? You still haven't eaten, have you? Come, let's go.

[She takes Kalarani's hand and calls her.]

Kalarani: No — I will eat later. You go.

Alli: Uh-uh. You come too!

[She leads her away.]

Ponnazhagan: [To himself] They say a demon comes out when you dig a well... We separated her from the king and now she says she will not separate from us! Strange women!

[He turns the pages of *Manimagudam* and reads.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/18.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- Kalarani's grove/flower and sword/flower metaphors are preserved through the confession rather than reduced to plain romantic prose.
- Ponnazhagan's revelation that the `flower` already in his heart is Alli is retained as the emotional turn of the scene.
- Alli's praise of *Manimagudam* preserves the flood, sea-wave, wind and `voice of the people` imagery.
- `கிணறு வெட்ட பூதம் புறப்பட்டது` is retained as a literal proverb-image — `a demon comes out when you dig a well` — rather than replaced by an unrelated English proverb.
