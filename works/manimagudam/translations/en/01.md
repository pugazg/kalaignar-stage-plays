# Scene 1

[Night. On a main street of Manimagudapuram, a man stands leaning against a street-lamp post. A short man runs up carrying bundles of newspapers and a rose. He comes up to the man by the lamp.]

Dwarf: Ponnazhagan is coming alone... Shall we begin our little drama?

Man by Lamp: Oh... we can begin!... Ulagappa! — Be careful! Don't forget anything I told you!

Dwarf: I'll do everything properly, just as we rehearsed it...

[Having said this, the short man goes and hides elsewhere with his bundle of newspapers. The man by the lamp checks his clothes, twists his long moustache neatly into place, strokes with his hand the large black mole growing high on his cheek, and stands looking at the sky. The short man sees the man he identified as Ponnazhagan approaching, comes back to the man by the lamp, and...]

Dwarf: Want the *People's Servant* newspaper? *People's Servant*! Only two panam! *People's Servant*... only two panam...! *People's Servant*...!

[The man by the lamp stands as though he has not noticed. Ponnazhagan too comes that way. The short man stands beside the man by the lamp and, imitating him, acts as though he too is looking up at the sky and thinking.]

Dwarf: O Moon! You are a lump of sugar candy! Why are these star-ants swarming around you? Ah — now I am going to count these white ants of the sky — one! two! three! four! five! six!...

[The man by the lamp laughs, pats the dwarf on the back, and...]

Man by Lamp: What, my boy, are you counting the stars? You've counted only up to six so far?

Dwarf: If I say six, I've counted six lakhs.

Man by Lamp: The moon is sugar candy and the stars are ants around it! Sabash! Fine imagination...

Dwarf: And it is my own imagination too..

Man by Lamp: Quite right! But these ants are only looking at this candy from a distance. That isn't the nature of ants, is it? They ought to go right onto the candy and swarm over it!

Dwarf: I don't know about all that. The great philosopher and editor of *People's Servant*, Srijath Ariharanathan, has written it. Here is the paper! [Holds it out.]

[The man by the lamp takes the newspaper. Ponnazhagan, a strongly built, tough-looking man, stands to one side stroking his short beard and enjoying their conversation.]

Man by Lamp: [To the dwarf] My boy! The editor of *People's Servant* said the moon is sugar candy and the stars are ants, didn't he? Now I will say something. Listen! You have seen a spider's web, haven't you?

Dwarf: Is it some rare fig blossom? Or a crescent seen only once in a while? Of course I've seen one.

Man by Lamp: Keep that spider's web in mind and now look at the sky! Doesn't that beautiful, cool full moon appear like a spider, and those shining stars like insects caught in the spider's web and dying there?

Dwarf: Yes! — Yes!

Man by Lamp: Our country is just like that! That is, the seat of power...

Dwarf: Is like the moon, and the people like the stars...

Man by Lamp: ...is what the *People's Servant* would write! I say the seat of power is like the spider, and the people are like insects trapped in its web!

Dwarf: That's right — perhaps you are a member of that People's Forum?

Man by Lamp: No — this is my own opinion. Do you know about the People's Forum?

Dwarf: Of course! Ponnazhagan is a great leader! A fine speaker! A man of sacrifice! And there is a woman named Alli — she speaks very well too! The association they run campaigns to change the seat of power so that the people may rule the country! Even I think that if we get rid of that king, the country will prosper.

Man by Lamp: Don't be hasty! What can the king do, poor fellow? He is a puppet who must dance as the minister and the royal guru make him dance! All authority belongs to the Assembly of Nobles! The only right given to the king is to sign wherever they point and live comfortably in the palace! So there is no use blaming the king! First we must discover where the destructive forces ruining the country really are! And here in your hand is a reactionary force that supports those destructive forces!

[The dwarf looks at the rose in his hand.]

Dwarf: The rose?

Man by Lamp: No! This *People's Servant* newspaper you sell for a living! It is a reactionary force! This paper blindly supports the seat of power, leaves the people stranded at the crossroads, and yet calls itself *People's Servant*! When the rulers impose taxes the people cannot bear, it does not stand with the people! Until now this *People's Servant* has never condemned the quarrels the government creates among the people in the name of God and religion! It calls the king a divine incarnation and keeps singing praises of every atrocity committed in his name! Should this paper be destroyed first — or should it not?

Dwarf: What you say is absolutely true! After hearing you, I no longer have the heart even to sell this paper. Shame! To live by selling this is like living by selling one's honour!

[He throws the bundles of newspapers far away. Ponnazhagan, who has been watching the events and conversation from a distance, approaches them.]

Man by Lamp: Who are you?

Ponnazhagan: Someone who took pleasure in hearing your conversation, so full of new ideas and love of country. Who are you?

Man by Lamp: I am a wanderer!

Pon.: Your name?

Man by Lamp: Puthumaippithan.

Pon.: A fitting name — Puthumaippithan! Manimagudapuram is your native land, isn't it...?

Puthumaippithan: Yes... And you?

Pon.: Have you heard of someone called Ponnazhagan?

Dwarf: Good heavens — is that you? I've seen you at public meetings, but I failed to recognize you...?

Puthu.: A rare opportunity! Do you know how exhilarating it is to meet and speak with noble men like you?

Pon.: I am equally delighted — with the thought that I am meeting an enemy of the regime!

Puthu.: The whole country is full of enemies of the government — or, in other words, friends of the people. That is all! Ponnazhagan, I have a request! Can it be fulfilled?... I wish to stand with you and work for the advancement of our motherland...

Pon.: Your wish will certainly be fulfilled! I believe you are fully qualified to become a member of our association itself!

Dwarf: Can I join too...?

Pon.: You may stay with us. Only after a few months can you become a member. What is your name?

Dwarf: That's the great wonder! My father and mother died as soon as I was born! Couldn't the people who died at least have given me a name before leaving? So I am a man without a name!

Pon.: You seem fond of roses?

Dwarf: Yes!

Pon.: You yourself are black, but your affection is for the red rose!

Dwarf: What can I do? There is no black rose!

Puthu.: Then you be the Black Rose! From now on, your name itself is Black Rose...

Pon.: A fitting name... Mm. Shall we go? Black Rose, come... come!

[Black Rose and Puthumaippithan look at one another, smile, and walk behind Ponnazhagan.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/01.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- `மக்கள் தொண்டன்` is rendered as *People's Servant* so the source's political irony remains audible in English.
- `மக்கள் மன்றம்` is rendered consistently as `People's Forum`, while `சீமான்கள் சபை` is kept distinct as `Assembly of Nobles`.
- The moon/candy/ant image and the counter-image of the moon as spider and stars as trapped insects are preserved rather than paraphrased.
- `ஸ்ரீஜத் அரிஹரநாதன்` is conservatively represented as `Srijath Ariharanathan`; no external normalization is imported.
- The final naming of `கருப்பு ரோஜா` is rendered as `Black Rose`, preserving the black/red-rose wordplay.
