# Scene 13

[The king's palace. The bodyguard is moving about. Kalarani arrives.]

Bodyguard: Welcome. What do you want?

Kalarani: I want to see the king... I came to ask whether I can see him now...

Bodyguard: Wait. I will go and inform him.

[Kalarani sits. The king takes time to come. The bodyguard returns; the curtain is drawn aside.]

Bodyguard: He has agreed to meet you... he will come now.

[The bodyguard goes inside. Kalarani waits a long time. She rises and walks about, admires the pictures there, and looks toward the curtain at the entrance through which the king will come. She sits again. At last the curtain is drawn aside. The king enters. Kalarani bows respectfully.]

King: Kalarani, are you well?

Kalarani: By your blessing, I am well.

King: What is the reason for wanting to see me so urgently at this hour?

Kalarani: I need to speak privately about a few things.

King: [To the bodyguard] Ulagappa! Keep a little distance from us.

[The bodyguard withdraws.]

King: Kalarani! Now you may speak freely.

Kalarani: I came to reach a decision about my marriage.

King: Speak so I can understand.

Kalarani: I will ask one thing; you must not become angry.

King: Ask.

Kalarani: Do you like me?

King: I do.

Kalarani: [Startled] Truly?

King: Why doubt it?

Kalarani: You like me?

King: Yes.

Kalarani: [With emotion] Do you love me?

King: Yes.

Kalarani: I do not like you.

King: That does not matter. I like you; I love you.

Kalarani: Even after I refuse you?

King: Yes.

Kalarani: I love another man.

King: He will not love you — will not care for you — as I do.

Kalarani: You must not like me or love me!

King: Thought is not a swelling flood that can be stopped by building a dam! Why should a king not love a minister's daughter?

Kalarani: Ah! [Startled.]

King: Why should an elder brother not love a younger sister with brotherly affection?

Kalarani: [Astonished] Your Majesty!

King: Kalarani is one of the sisters of the king of Manimagudapuram!

Kalarani: Your Majesty! Forgive me!

[She falls at his feet.]

King: Get up! If the Minister is hasty, his daughter is just the same!

Kalarani: Father told me that you wished to marry me.

King: Why should I marry now? And if I marry the Minister's daughter, it would be like giving a handful of flattened rice to revolutionaries who are already chewing on an empty mouth!

Kalarani: I take leave of you, my king.

King: Wait, Kalarani! You said you love another man. Who is he?

[Kalarani lowers her head.]

King: There is no need for shame. The government too will take an interest in fulfilling your wish! You are the Minister's daughter, after all. Who is he?

Kalarani: Even speaking his name is a great offence.

King: Because of the notion that a wife must not utter her husband's name?

Kalarani: No. He is an enemy of the government! If I speak his name here...

King: It does not matter. Don't be afraid. Tell me.

Kalarani: He... [Hesitates.]

King: Tell me! What if he is an enemy? Even in a rival's garden there may be a fragrant manoranjitham flower.

Kalarani: If I pointed out that manoranjitham, you might still prevent me by saying there is a flower-snake hidden in it, might you not?

King: If there is a flower-snake, preventing you would be only proper...

Kalarani: It is not a flower with a snake. The man I mean is Ponnazhagan...

King: [Starts in shock] Ponnazhagan?... You love him? The leader of the enemies... the man who has vowed to overthrow the government of this country at any moment! The head of that flock of sheep called the People's Forum! Enemy of Gurunathar, the Minister, the throne hall — of them all! And you are the beloved of that enemy! The law will not permit it!

Kalarani: Is there a law even to prevent love?

King: Yes!... Love sows the seed of the crop called equal rights! When the mansion of status falls in love with an orphan's hut, it creates the conditions for a child called equality to be born! Equal rights, equality — the Assembly of Nobles keeps watch through law so that no such dreadful events take place. If equal rights and equality come into being, what work will remain for Gurunathar? What work will remain for the army established at the cost of thousands of sovereigns? Gurunathar will lose his prestige — the throne hall will lose its splendour!

Kalarani: Then must my life be ruined?

King: Poor sister... where did you see that Ponnazhagan? How did the two of you meet? [In astonishment.]

Kalarani: I have never met and spoken with him. I do not even know whether he loves me! One day he came to our house to discuss the state of the country with Father. He spoke with Father for a long time. I saw him that day. His idealistic heart captured me!

King: An idealistic heart — danger is sprouting against the government in the Minister's own house! [He walks about thinking.] I cannot permit this!... I will stop it immediately! [Walks about.]

Kalarani: Your Majesty...!

King: Do not stand here. Go away!

[The king goes inside. Kalarani leaves in distress. The king comes out again, agitated.]

King: Ulagappa!

Ulagappan: [Entering] Here I am.

King: Ulagam! Did you hear everything?

Ulagappan: Yes.

King: Go at once! Kalarani... she will be going by the garden path. Send our men and have them carry her away!

Ulagappan: Your command!

King: Take the drug that makes one unconscious too! Leave her in the marble pavilion of the pleasure garden and come back at once and tell me. Run!

[Ulagappan runs off. The king walks about deep in thought.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/13.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The king's first `love` is revealed as brotherly affection; the deliberate misunderstanding is preserved through the repeated questioning.
- The king's extended equality/equal-rights speech is retained as the scene's political satire rather than corrected or softened.
- `மாற்றான் தோட்டம்`, `மணோரஞ்சிதம்`, and `பூநாகம்` remain garden/flower imagery within the dialogue rather than being replaced by outside equivalents.
- The closing order to abduct Kalarani, including use of an unconsciousness-inducing drug and the marble pavilion, is retained exactly as dramatic action.
