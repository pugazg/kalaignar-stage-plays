# Scene 14

[In the garden, Vanji is stringing a garland.]

"Who is that?"

[Asking this, the bodyguard Ulagappan comes there. Startled, Vanji stands without speaking.]

Ulagappan: If I ask who is here at this hour, why not answer? Are you mute... deaf... a ghost... a demon?

Vanji: [Angrily] Can't you see someone standing here?

Ulagappan: What are you, the moon, to be visible even in the dark?

Vanji: Fine indeed... this is the story of the crow mocking the black cuckoo! Looking like a monsoon cloud yourself, you have come to mock me!... O blue-cloud dark-hued lord!

Ulagappan: Ah! My blue-lotus flower bathed in my fire!

Vanji: Stop it. Do you know who I am?

Ulagappan: Not a ghost — not a demon — not mute — not deaf — a pleasure-loving Urvashi!

Vanji: Show some respect in your speech. I am the Minister's daughter's companion! You are only the bodyguard!

Ulagappan: Do you know what `mey-kappalan` means? One who protects truth — that is, one who deals truthfully with the king — his confidential secretary — his secret servant.

Vanji: Is that all?

Ulagappan: There is more. `Mey` can also mean the body! One capable of safely guarding the body of a beauty like you.

Vanji: Shall I give `mey-kappalar` a new meaning?

Ulagappan: Tell me!

Vanji: Someone who, when danger comes, knows only how to protect his own body and not how to protect a life. Is that right?

Ulagappan: You seem to be a poet too!

Vanji: Then are you a poet?

Ulagappan: Yes. I can write three hundred poems before taking a breath!

Vanji: Then write three hundred poems now, before taking a breath...

Ulagappan: Good heavens... don't you understand? `Before taking a breath, three hundred poems.`

Vanji: Ah... what a fine idea... like a bead of kundumani on a rubbish heap! No one has ever said such a thing before.

Ulagappan: If you want to gather many more such kundumani beads from me...

Vanji: What must I do?

Ulagappan: Have a thali made.

Vanji: What — a thali?

Ulagappan: Why, don't you want even that? Fine...

Vanji: Oh, I see! Sir Bodyguard! Is it proper for a man to speak like this with a woman in a pleasure garden at night?

Ulagappan: What then? Do you say it is proper for a man to speak like this with a woman in a desert in broad daylight?

Vanji: I can see your heart as clearly as a mirror...

Ulagappan: [Puffs out his chest.] Look at your face in this mirror, then. Quickly adorn yourself — line your eyes with kohl! Put on a forehead mark! You will shine!

[Vanji stretches her face toward his chest as if looking into a mirror.]

Vanji: Good heavens! This is an unsilvered mirror!

Ulagappan: You are the one who must coat it with the rasa of love...

[Vanji laughs shyly.]

Ulagappan: Ah! What are these — pearl teeth... a string of mullai blossoms? [Touches her.]

Vanji: Look here... I don't like old-fashioned descriptions like that...

Ulagappan: Come... I will say everything in a new way.

[He pulls her.]

Vanji: Where?

Ulagappan: To that platform over there... someone may come here...

Vanji: Oh no, Kalarani will look for me...

Ulagappan: Let her look! We aren't going off to heaven. We are going to bring heaven down here... come, come!

[The two leave. After a short while Kalarani comes there.]

"Vanji! Vanji!" [She calls.]

[Vanji is nowhere to be seen. Kalarani again calls loudly, "Vanji!" At that moment two or three dark figures spring upon her, hold an anaesthetic drug to her face, lift her and carry her away. Vanji, returning there, sees Kalarani being taken. Vanji screams.]

[She cries, "Kala! Kala!" It is useless. Kalarani is carried far away.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/14.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The `மெய்` wordplay is made visible rather than silently lost: `mey` can mean both `truth` and `body`, and the dialogue exploits both senses of `மெய்க்காப்பாளன்`.
- The mirror exchange preserves the source's linked `ரசம்` joke: an unsilvered mirror and the `rasa` of love.
- Urvashi, thali, mullai and kundumani are retained as source-cultural terms rather than replaced by unsupported modern analogies.
- The final abduction is preserved as staged action, including the drug used to render Kalarani unconscious.
