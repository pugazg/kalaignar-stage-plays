# Scene 2

[Huts where poor people live. Firefly-like lamps burn inside their homes to drive away the darkness. Outside the huts are many skeletal people, some lying down, some sitting and exchanging neighbourhood talk. Ponnazhagan, Puthumaippithan and Black Rose come that way. On seeing Ponnazhagan, all the poor people rise eagerly and surround him, bowing as they say, “Our leader has come; our leader has come.”]

Pon.: Well, any new news...?

An Old Man: How could there not be? In the next street seven people were struck by diarrhoea; two are already gone... whether four of them will survive is doubtful.

Pon.: Diarrhoea? I heard nothing of it when I came yesterday. Did you send word to the government medical department?

Old Man: We sent word. They said they would look into it.

Pon.: They will look into it. They will keep looking to see how many corpses go by. Elder, couldn't you at least have sent word to me in the morning?

Old Man: We did. They said you were not there, so Alli-amma and four or five volunteers came from the association and gave every help that was needed. They gave everyone preventive medicine for the diarrhoea. They too sent an urgent message to the government medical department. There wasn't even anyone to lift the dead bodies — no means to do it. Alli-amma and the volunteers removed all those bodies and cleaned the whole street before they left.

Pon.: Puthumaippithan, have you seen the worthiness of those who rule us? As though it were not enough for working people to die of poverty, can you find a government anywhere that strides about in royal ease thinking, “Let them perish of smallpox and diarrhoea too” — anywhere except Manimagudapuram?

Puthu.: World history has seen monarchies more terrible than this. But those who turn the wheel of government in Manimagudapuram today have forgotten the spectacle of those religious fanatics' regimes being buried under the earth. That is all.

[At that moment a palace official arrives. He looks grandly at the poor people there.]

Official: Who submitted the petition to the palace medical department saying that diarrhoea has spread here?

Old Man: We did.

Official: Your petition was submitted to the minister. On Gurunathar's advice, if you immediately hold festivals for Mariamman and Kaliamman and offer prayers, the diarrhoea will leave. No other treatment is necessary...

Pon.: What?... Whose advice? Is worship of Kaliamman the medicine for diarrhoea?

Official: Yes. The diarrhoea has come because of Goddess Kali's anger.

Puthu.: What is Goddess Kali angry about, man?

Black Rose: Perhaps she asked for a new sari and blouse and they didn't give them to her?

Official: Who are you, you little fool...?

Puthu.: Official, don't get angry. Answer me. If diseases can be driven away by conducting worship for Kali and Mari, why does the government need a medical department?

Official: I know nothing about all that. Gurunathar of Manimagudapuram gives the order. I perform my duty. That is all. Ruin yourselves however you like — what is it to me?

[The official leaves.]

Puthu.: Gurunathar of Manimagudapuram — a clod of earth, a frog that treats people as beasts — and he issues an order: worship Kali to cure diarrhoea!

Old Man: Then don't abuse Gurunathar. He is an incarnation of God.

Puthu.: Yes — everyone is an incarnation of God. The king is an incarnation of God. The minister is an incarnation of God. The royal Gurunathar is an incarnation of God. Everyone is an incarnation; and the ten members sitting in the Assembly of Nobles are the Dashavataram — all ten incarnations! Fine country, fine faith this is. Elder, do not be hurt because I say it: God, according to all the qualities attributed to him, is said to be utterly pure. Such a pure being would not take incarnations of treachery like these.

Old Man: Whatever it may be, brother, from the time of our elders...

Puthu.: This is how our poor people have lived as slaves. Don't we need a change?

Old Man: Fate itself would have to be changed. Can that be done?

Puthu.: Fate is only a law, isn't it? It is only the system devised by the strong to exercise power over the weak, isn't it, elder? That is what fate is.

Pon.: Friend, let us keep these explanations for later. Elder, all of you may go. I will come tomorrow morning. For now I will go to the area where the diarrhoea has struck and see to what must be done.

Old Man: Very well.

[The old man and the others leave. After walking a little distance, Ponnazhagan turns to Puthumaippithan.]

Pon.: I do not speak to these people about the superstitious beliefs they hold concerning God, fate, scriptures and customs. I move along in their own way and unite everyone against the king.

Puthu.: That is exactly what I say is wrong. There is no use uniting the people merely as enemies of the king. The one ruling this country today is not the king — he is a puppet. It is the paper in the cheat's hand, the evil in the religious fanatic's heart, and the sand-mound called the ruling class that are making this country dance. If power is to pass into the people's hands, the people's minds must first be cultivated. There is no use recovering stolen grains of paddy and scattering them over a field that has dried up. First, the light of knowledge — only following that, the light of government.

Pon.: More or less the same idea is Alli's too. I am the one who differs a little...

Puthu.: A difference is all right... there must not be a contradiction.

Pon.: Very well, shall we go?

[The three leave.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/02.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- `பேதி` is rendered conservatively as `diarrhoea`; no specific disease is inferred beyond the source wording.
- `மாரியம்மன்`, `காளியம்மன்`, `குருநாதர்` and `தசாவதாரம்` retain their source cultural identities; `Dashavataram` is briefly glossed in the dialogue because the ten-member pun is structurally important.
- The contrast between superstition and public health, and between merely opposing the king and cultivating the people's minds, is kept explicit.
- The source chain `எத்தனின் கையிலேயுள்ள ஏடும் ... மதப் பித்தனின் ... கேடும் ... அதிகார வர்க்கமெனும் மணல் மேடும்` is translated as a three-part political image rather than flattened into generic criticism.
