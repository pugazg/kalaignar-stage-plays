# Scene 4

[The minister's house. The room of his daughter Kalarani. Kalarani is asleep. Sunlight falls through the window onto her bed. Her companion Vanji enters the room carrying the morning food. Seeing Kalarani asleep...]

Vanji: Kalarani — Kalarani! Of course! After sleeping in the moon's embrace, perhaps you have no mind to rise even when the sun's hand touches you!

Kalarani: [Stretching lazily as she gets up] What is this? Embracing the moon? The sun's hand touching me?

Vanji: You went to bed when the moon rose, and you haven't awakened even after the sun came up! I merely said it in poetic style.

Kala.: So you said it in poetic style — embracing the moon, the sun's hand touching me. What am I, Tara? Or a lotus? Good thing you stopped with two of them instead of bringing in another three! Look here, Vanji! From now on don't close your eyes and babble in this kind of poetic style!

Vanji: Why should I close my eyes and babble? It is this Kalarani who babbles like that in dreams!

Kala.: Did I babble in my dream? What did I say?

Vanji: Ayyo — I am ashamed even to tell you!

[Vanji mimics Kalarani teasingly.]

Vanji: My beloved! I had almost forgotten your delightful face! Why did you stay away from me all these days?... Ah, your hand touching me — ayyo, it makes me lose myself! When the lamp of the sky is burning, what need is there for the lamp in the house? Shall I put it out? My love! Swami! Lord of my life!... My very life! You will not forget me, will you? Not merely in this birth; in every birth we shall be born together.. as twins!

Kala.: Chi!... I would never have babbled like that... You are making it up, you mischievous girl...

Vanji: All right, let it go! But Kalarani, for some days now you have been lost in some thought. What is it?

Kala.: Nothing, girl! If I don't tell you, whom else would I tell?... I'll tell you later...

Vanji: It is the matter of the letter “Ka”, isn't it, Kalarani!

Kala.: Go away!... [She combs her dishevelled hair.]

Vanji: All right!.. Eat first.

Kala.: Indeed! It seems I need not even brush my teeth!

Vanji: Why would anyone polish pearls? They call teeth jasmine buds; if you scrub them, won't they be crushed?

Kala.: Hey, poetry-mad girl — wait, wait — I'll deal with you!

[Kalarani leaves the room to brush her teeth. Minister Gunaseelar enters and speaks to Vanji.]

Gunaseelar: Vanji, where is Kalarani?

Vanji: She has only just risen — now she is hidden behind the cloud called her morning duties. She will be here soon.

Guna.: Was she awake very late last night? Where did you go? To the palace? Did you meet the king there? Did Kala speak with the king? Were you there while they spoke? What did they talk about? Did the king open his mouth about marriage? Did Kalarani agree to it? Was the king happy to hear her consent? How long did the king and Kala speak...?

Vanji: This servant has only one answer to your thousand questions: Kalarani did not go to the palace and did not see the king.

Guna.: What? She didn't go? Vanji, how many times have I told you that it is your duty to bring the king and Kalarani together and create love between them?

Vanji: What medicine is there for stubbornness, my lord? However much I tell her, she has no desire at all to meet the king.

Guna.: Vanji! Somehow she must fall in love with the king.

Vanji: What can I do? She has not developed even the love the moon has for a king.

Guna.: My love is not for the king — it is for the throne of Manimagudapuram! Once the king and Kalarani are married, I will say, “My son-in-law rules Manimagudam!” Later I will say, “My grandson rules it!” Will not the banner of Gunaseelar's lineage then fly over the royal hall of Manimagudapuram?

Vanji: Don't worry, my lord! Somehow I will cast a net over the king.

Guna.: What — you?

Vanji: Yes — I will cast the net called Kalarani.

Guna.: Sabash, Vanji! I entrust that task to you. Do it successfully. The king and Kalarani must be married soon.

Vanji: I take responsibility for that.

Guna.: A grandson must be born.

Vanji: I am not responsible for that — a granddaughter may be born instead.

[The minister laughs and leaves the room.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/04.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The moon/sun/Tara/lotus sequence is kept as the source's deliberately over-poetic joke rather than rationalized.
- `“கா” வன்னா விஷயம்` is retained conservatively as `the matter of the letter “Ka”`; the translation does not expand the teasing hint into an asserted word absent from the source.
- The repeated marriage questions and Vanji's grandson/granddaughter punch line retain their comic pacing.
- Gunaseelar's motive is kept explicit: his desired marriage is a dynastic route to the throne, not affection for the king.
