# Scene 29

[The king's palace. Alli lies unconscious on a bed. Her wound has been bandaged.]

Ulagappan: [To the king] Good fortune — the wound is not severe. If she alone had not come in the way, that sinful fellow would have killed you!

King: [Softly] Very well, Ulagappa! Go. No one must know that Alli is here! If the Minister comes, run to me and tell me immediately.

Ulagappan: Where is he going to come? If he steps outside, he will tremble thinking someone may blast his life away, and stay inside his house!

King: Fine — in any case, remain outside...

[Ulagappan starts to leave.]

Ulagappan: Now say it plainly — why should I remain here as an obstacle to you! But one thing: once she regains consciousness, wake her!

King: Mischief-maker! Go... go...

[The king walks about, looking at Alli. Then he sits on a chair, thinking, still looking at her. His heart speaks.]

King's Heart: `Alli — golden creeper filling the eye! A revolutionary painting shining like a ruby pendant on the golden necklace called the People's Forum. I can hang this painting upon the wall of my heart and admire its beauty; I cannot press this dazzling picture to my cheek and delight in it. She loves me — but wholeheartedly she does not love me, King Manimaran, nor his forts and battlements. She loves Puthumaippithan who thunders principles — that new man struggling in the storm — that mysterious being forced to live hidden. She loves him... She will not wish to live in the palace as a queen of status. Alli will live as the queen of the people's hearts! That is why I too love that revolutionary woman!

`If she loved King Manimaran, Puthumaippithan would not love her — he would hate her. Alli will not love Manimaran — she is a revolutionary — a revolutionary — a leader of the people — yet at the same time Manimaran's beloved — the king's beloved. She loves his enemy — likes him — cherishes him — loves him. Therefore she is the king's beloved... the king's beloved... the king's beloved...`

[He stands looking at her. Waves of imagination. The king touches her.]

King: Alli... my dear Alli.

[She rises.]

Alli: Who?

King: Don't you know me, Alli...?

Alli: You?

King: Queen of my heart...

Alli: King of my heart...

King: Jewel of my eyes...

Alli: My beloved...

King: My love...

Alli: My joy...

[The waves of imagination subside. Alli is still lying unconscious. The king is still seated on the same chair as before. The imagined wave ends. He rises and goes to her bedside. Alli turns in her sleep. He sprinkles water on her face. Alli wakes, sits up and looks at the king in astonishment, then looks around.]

King: What are you looking at? Why are you bewildered? In stories, when heroines are left in an unknown place, they speak the line, `Where am I?` Do you feel like saying that? Do you know where you are? Here are gleaming yellow silk curtains! A diamond-studded window made to welcome the young breeze that plays by stirring them! Look through that window and you see a rose garden smiling in many colours beneath the milk-white moon! A jasmine garden! A flower-bed made from their blossoms! A peacock seat! A ruby chair! Emerald statues! Milk with almonds! Fruit! Juice! Food! Honey! Do not all these tell you that this is King Manimara's palace?

Alli: Your Majesty, fortunately... none of the objects here has the power to speak...

King: If they could speak?

Alli: Like the statues on Vikramaditya's throne, each one would tell a story.

King: For example...?

Alli: Not one — I can show a thousand. There — the bone of a dead elephant has become a statue, has it not? But we must not forget that all these things lying here as coloured lamps, diamond figures and golden thrones are the bones of human beings who are not dead! Majesty — the elephant at least gave you its tusk as it was. Has not the human tooth been worn down to the shell so that this upper storey may rise high?

King: I understand nothing!

Alli: Forgive me — this is a conch blown at a deaf man's ear, Maharaja! Leave that aside. Why was I brought here?

King: Unconsciousness!

Alli: What?

King: Not mine!

Alli: Then whose?

King: The listener's! The knife-stab — the unconsciousness that followed — you remember what happened, don't you?

Alli: I remember. But why did that make it necessary for me to come here?

King: You saved the king of Manimagudam himself. Should I not thank you?

Alli: I do not need your thanks! Give me leave — I am going!

King: Woman!

Alli: My name is Alli!

[He pretends to be startled.]

King: Alli — Alli — unruly Alli!

Alli: Not that Alli — that was Arjuna's wife!

King: I know. You are the revolutionary Alli — one of Ponnazhagan's group — one of the conspiracy-group that came to kill me!

Alli: I am a revolutionary — I belong to Ponnazhagan's group — but I am no conspirator!

King: Leader of the conspiracy-group!

Alli: The leader of a conspiracy-group would not have rescued you from death!

King: Yes! [Confused.] Then who did come to kill me? Was it not the result of a resolution passed by your group?

Alli: Our resolution was only that there must be no temple in Kodikkal Nagar — not a frenzy of murder!

King: Then why did you care so much that the king must not die?

Alli: That too is wrong! It was not desire for the king or concern for him! It was only fear that slander and a useless accusation would attach themselves to our Forum! That is why!... That is why, Majesty, I stopped the knife that came flying. It was not because my heart trembled that Manimagudam might lose some great noble soul!

King: Then what I imagined was wrong. I was glad, thinking: `She must be compassionate — she must have learned human feeling — she must walk in the path of women of sacrifice — this Alli of sacrifice rushed forward to stop danger coming to the father of the nation!` That happiness has disappeared. You saved me to uphold the prestige of the People's Forum and protect it from stain — in short, you saved me out of self-interest!

Alli: No... no! I do not wish to say I did it out of loyalty to the crown merely to receive the smile of the sceptre-holder as my reward! I do not need the king's smile! If the people of the land do not have to weep, that alone is enough!

King: Do you pray that the people should not weep?

Alli: No — I work for it!

King: Man is born crying. Do you know why?

Alli: So that he may live laughing!

King: If a man keeps laughing all the time, they will call him a grinning fool.

Alli: No one says that about you, does anyone?

King: Do you imagine a king never weeps? Put your ear to my heart and listen — the sound of lament will pierce your ear!

Alli: There is a great difference, Majesty, between lamenting because no new countries are available to conquer and crying because there is no way even to get yesterday's gruel!

King: Whatever it may be, my doctrine is that everyone cannot keep laughing all the time!

Alli: I am speaking of all human society! If everyone has a life — if prosperity is shared — abundance and laughter will become the common property of the world!

King: Common property — shared equally — that means demolishing this palace and giving each person one stone, doesn't it?

Alli: Why — so they can place it under a tree and slap their cheeks in worship? Your Majesty... common ownership does not mean smashing the palace and handing each person a stone! The palace will certainly remain... but it will not remain a place giving shade to one king alone! It will become a college of knowledge training all the future kings of the country! Majesty... the stone that must be broken out is not in the palace wall... it is inside the hearts of the officials who move within it! That stone must be broken... shattered... reduced to dust.

[Ulagappan signals to the king that the Minister is calling him. The king gets ready to leave.]

King: Fine. Fine..! My royal office will not accept your doctrine. I shall return!

Alli: And I?

King: You...? You must remain here! There will be an inquiry tomorrow or the day after. Until then, this is more or less a prison for you!

Alli: Is this your beautiful way of expressing thanks?

King: Palace people's beauty is a beauty all its own! Alli — be careful. The guard is heavy. Do not try to escape — you may lose your head.

[The king leaves. Alone, Alli walks about unable to understand what is happening. She lies down on the floor after dimming the lamp a little.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/29.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The first intimate exchange between the king and Alli occurs explicitly inside the king's `நினைவு அலைகள்` / imagined wave; the translation preserves the return to reality before Alli actually wakes.
- The scene's argument about palace wealth, human labour, common property and the `stone` inside officials' hearts is retained without importing modern theoretical vocabulary absent from the Tamil.
- Alli's intervention against the assassin remains motivated in her own dialogue by protecting the Forum from slander, not by loyalty to the king.
- `செவிடன் காது சங்கு` is rendered as the source image `a conch blown at a deaf man's ear` rather than substituted with a different English idiom.
