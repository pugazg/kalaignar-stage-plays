# Scene 43

[Assembly of Nobles. The king, the Minister, the nobles, the prisoner and Ulagappan are present.]

Minister: I did not know that Gurunathar was such a great fraud — such a conspirator! He gave himself the proper punishment for the wrong he committed!

Ariharanathar: Enough, Minister — stop! What great model of rectitude are you? Nobles! Do you know who this Minister is, who now calls the dead Gurunathar a criminal? This Minister is the magician who caught a nameless roadside wanderer, dressed him up like the king, and made him dance according to his own wishes!

Minister: Lies, lies! What Ariharanathar says is a monstrous lie!

Noble: [to Ariharanathar] What are you babbling about? Why do you insult the king and the Minister?

Ariharanathar: I am not speaking! Here — this letter will speak! Do you know who wrote it? Manimaran, the true king of Manimagudapuram! Read it — everything will become clear!

[A noble reads it.]

Noble: What — had the king already left?

Ariharanathar: Yes... yes! Why doubt it? Do not listen to me — do not listen to the Minister either. [To the king.] Ask him himself! Hey, are you the real king? Or a king manufactured by the Minister? Tell us without denying it! Hmm!

King: Swami, Swami! Leave me alone! I am not the real king!

Ariharanathar: Did you hear? Hear more! Is it true or not that the Minister planned to use this false king at the foundation-stone ceremony, before the public, to transfer all authority into his own name?

Minister: Do not believe him — it is all false!

Ariharanathar: Hey, you tell them! Is it true?

King: Yes — it is true! The Minister told me to act like the king!

Noble: What now, Minister — why the stare?

Minister: There is nothing to stare about. The king had run away! I made this arrangement only because confusion would arise if that news spread through the country!

Noble: What authority did you have to make such an arrangement? You deceived the Assembly of Nobles! That is a grave crime! You decked a false man in golden ornaments and staged a puppet show — an enormous crime! A conspiracy no one else would dare attempt! The Assembly of Nobles must arrest him immediately!

[Everyone.]

Nobles: Yes — he must be arrested!

Senior Noble: Hmm... Ulagappa... do it!

[The Minister is arrested.]

Senior Noble: [pointing to the king] This false king too must be thrown into the dungeon and given severe punishment!

King: Lords! One word before you arrest me! I ask permission to speak for a short while. I wish to reveal some truths here!

Noble: You are a liar — a fabricator — a false man! What truth can there be in you?

King: I am neither liar nor fabricator nor false man! I myself am the true king!

Noble: What?

Ariharanathar: Do not believe him — do not believe him! He is a `nadutheru Narayanan`, a fellow from the rubbish heap! This Minister lifted him up and placed him on a tower! That is all! He is neither king nor even a clod of earth!

King: What evidence do you have that I am a false king, Editor?

Ariharanathar: One piece of evidence — you cannot write! You are incapable even of signing your name! Because you did not know how to sign like the king, the Minister told you to act as though your mind were deranged on the first day in the Assembly of Nobles!

King: True! Members of the Assembly, listen! Unable to bear the atrocities committed in the palace by the Minister, the religious guru and this Ariharanathar, King Manimaran truly did leave! But Manimaran, who renounced the country, wandered in disguise in order to expose their evil! He became the young man selling the *Manimagudam* newspaper! Government guards arrested him and brought him before the Minister! The Minister did not know that the orphaned young man standing before him was the king himself! While the king was missing and these three were racking their brains — at that very moment I was brought before them and they dressed me in the king's role! They threatened me to behave exactly like the king — they pointed a gun at Manimaran and ordered him to act like Manimaran! The Minister tried in every possible way to make me sign like the king! I simply acted as though I did not know how to sign at all! Thinking that they had fooled some simple fellow and pulled a veil over the eyes of the Assembly of Nobles, these fully ripened madmen of autocracy only fooled themselves. How long can they live by deception? The time for darkness to disappear has arrived! There — the light of the rising sun is visible! From now on there is no corner of the world in which Ariharanathars who impose blackouts of truth, religious gurus who drive away knowledge, and Ministers like this who serve the tyrants of repression can roam!

Ariharanathar: Let the Assembly not ruin itself by believing him! He is not the king at all! If he is the king, let him sign — let us see!

King: A signature! Is one enough? Do you want a thousand? Here!

[He rapidly signs many small slips of paper and sends them flying.]

King: If signatures alone are not enough, here is more evidence. [He opens his chest.] Irrefutable proof that I am Manimaran! The mark of the wound I received when I fought Chola Manimudikkari of Maruthalankaanam! A scar given by the battlefield! A wound of war! Is that enough proof? Do you want more?

Noble: Enough — enough! Your Majesty! Forgive us! We will no longer believe Ariharanathar's words!

[The prisoner who had been Gurunathar's henchman.]

Prisoner: Yes! Gurunathar and this Ariharanathar together sent me to kill the king!

Ariharanathar: Ayyo! The whole world is nothing but lies! Tomorrow itself I will publish a denial of all this in my newspaper!

Noble: We have no intention of leaving you free until then! Hmm! Arrest him too, right now!

[Ulagappan arrests him.]

Ariharanathar: Oh God! Paramasivam! Run here and save me!

Ulagappan: Hmm... he cannot run here! They say the leg of his Rishabha mount has broken!

Ariharanathar: Muruga! Save me from this atheist!

Ulagappan: Hmm... where might he be? At Valli's house? At Deivanai's house? Who knows whether your shouting will reach his ears at this hour?

Ariharanathar: Mahavishnu — at least you come, man!

Ulagappan: Uh-huh, that will not work in an emergency! They say Garuda crashed into a mountain and broke his wing!

Noble: Drag them away. All of them! Let the Minister, Ariharanathar and this murderer lie in the same cage — hmm... take them away.

[Ulagappan ties the three together and drags them away.]

King: Esteemed lords! One part of the black darkness that covered Manimagudam has been removed! For the remaining stain to disappear, the hearts of all of you must soften! This is the Assembly capable of determining the destiny of this country! Though said to have been formed to rule the land and do good for it, until now this Assembly — submitting to the religious guru's counsel and lending itself to the Minister's conspiracies — has earned the unchanging hatred of the people of Manimagudapuram! It is no ordinary thing for that hatred to disappear! Only by granting freedom to these people can that hatred be removed! A short while ago in this very Assembly we called Ponnazhagan and Puthumaippithan, who labour for the common people, terrorists — think about it! Who are the terrorists? Are they greater terrorists than the Minister in zari clothes — the religious guru with his prayer beads — the newspaper editor who lives by deceiving the people? I opposed the plan to destroy Kodikkal Nagar and build a temple, yet refusing to listen to me, through the strength of their personal authority and the power of their tricks, they demolished that poor people's quarter into earth — stamping it with the seal of the Assembly of Nobles. Are these not the terrorists? Loving lords! Let what has happened remain what has happened — let what happens from now on be good! I ask this Assembly to take the urgent decision required for that today — now — tonight!

Noble 1: What does the king wish us to do?

King: The Assembly of Nobles must be dissolved! The king must renounce the crown!

Noble 2: What?

Noble 3: Is such a thing possible?

King: It may be impossible for us! But the people will finish this matter easily! Loving lords! No one can say where the anger the people feel toward me and toward this Assembly will end! Suddenly, one day, your king's head may be kicked about like a ball; your own heads — the heads of nobles seated in the ruling assembly — may be sliced like palmyra fruits. This is not prophecy; it is experienced truth — a warning of danger shown by histories that have not disappeared! The bodies of rogues who failed to understand the principle that “the people's verdict is Mahesan's verdict” have been thrown as flesh stripped of substance along the sides of history's street! How long will people keep dragging out their lives saying, “the path God has left us”? Once the question “Why?” takes form in the womb, that revolutionary child does not even need ten months to be born! In the old mythic imagination they say, `a rishi's foetus does not remain overnight`; like that, it may arise immediately! Do you imagine that question has not arisen in Manimagudam? A pumpkin-bellied man looks at a man with a wasted body and asks, “Keep your distance, low-caste fellow — can you not see a high-caste man coming, blind fool?” At that moment the trumpet-question “Why?” is born! A man endowed with both bulk and wealth roars at one whose eyes have become pools: “Mountains of gold and a life of fullness belong to us alone — mounds of earth and a dim life are the fruits of your karma!” At that moment, lords, that question “Why?” rises with a shriek and grips our throats! “Keep away — do not come near us” will no longer work, nobles — it will not work! “They dare oppose us? Weaponless, unarmed people? Will we not shoot and burn them down?” Such proud heroic speeches are nothing but empty blasts! The gun barrels now turned toward those beating the drum of liberation may one day be turned toward us — who knows?

King: How many people in our own army have the mantra “Why?” inscribed in their hearts? At what moment will that mantra be chanted, and in the sacrificial fire of revolution the fat in our own bodies be poured as ghee? No one can name the time or hour! A storm may be detected before it arrives — a revolution cannot be detected! Nor can its result be calculated. Do you want that terrible result? Do you want the volcano of revolution to erupt and to be caught in its molten fire until you lose all form, true friends? Think for a moment! Think what will happen if you fall into the hands of revolutionaries! Those who until now possessed nothing but tears will order: “Member of the Assembly of Nobles who gathered hair-tufts, cows and beautiful women together and burned them — here, swim with your wife in this pit of fire!” The survivors of families whose guts burned and died will take a burning stick and draw lines across your backs: “Who are you? The noble who tormented us with taxes? Here — I will put lines all over your back!” Those who have lived until now beside the sewer will say: “Ah-ha! Caught at last, luxurious lord! They say you cannot sleep without rose-water baths, fruit drinks, and a bedchamber surrounded by maidens? Here is a wonderful place for you to fall and roll and sleep!” — and throw you into sewer mud. Are you ready to welcome such outcomes? Do not think I am frightening you! I am telling you that the country has ripened!

King: Those who lay believing, “The king is God's representative! The religious guru is an incarnation of Maheswaran!” have awakened through Puthumaippithan's propaganda! Those who cursed fate and turned their eyes into rivers have understood that fate is not the cause of their pain! The knowledge movement has raised its head — even in the hearts of those who had spoken only dry common ownership, fertile ideas have begun to sprout! The spirit of self-respect and the fire of freedom have begun spreading with cyclone speed! Now it is time for us to tuck in our tails! What do you say? Will you discover a new path and walk with sense? Or will you walk the old path and bow your heads to the knife? Do you want history to say, “I renounced the crown”? Or history to say, “the crown was struck down”? Which do you want? A government of equality — or a grave for each one of us? Tell me! Friends — tell me!!

Noble 1: Everything the king says is right! It is also true that the flood of revolution is overflowing its banks!

Noble 2: Let the king himself tell us a way to stop it!

King: I already told you! One way only! The path laid down by the learned! Make the government common to all!

Noble 1: What is the alternative?

King: What alternative? A general election must be held! Those who contest and win must come to power!

Noble 2: If that is the situation — if that is the proper path — it is best to act according to the king's wish!

King: That is everyone's opinion, is it not?

Noble 1: Yes! [Raises his hand.]

King: You have raised your hands! Before the people's hands are raised against us — cautiously, you have raised your hands now! I am glad! Tomorrow morning this country will hear good news! Tonight I will sleep peacefully!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/43.md`, with verified page records for scans 157–162 used to preserve long cross-page continuities; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The king's disguised identity, signature demonstration and battle-scar proof are kept distinct from the Minister's genuine counterfeit-king scheme.
- `நடுத்தெரு நாராயணன்` is retained as `nadutheru Narayanan`, a source insult, rather than replaced with an invented social category.
- The quoted caste-abuse lines in the king's revolution speech are translated as quoted oppressive speech inside his warning; they are not narrator assertions.
- The tax/stripe `வரி` wordplay is kept visible in English through `taxes / lines` and explained by context rather than silently discarded.
- `ரிஷிப் பிண்டம் இராத்தங்காது` is translated literally as the source's mythic saying; no outside mythology is imported.
- `அறிவியக்கம்` is rendered as `knowledge movement`, while `சுயமரியாதை` remains `self-respect`, preserving the source's political vocabulary.
- `முடி துறந்தேன் / முடி சாய்ந்தது` retains the crown/head ambiguity as `renounced the crown / the crown was struck down`.
- The Assembly's decision for dissolution, renunciation of the crown and a general election is represented as the source scene's political resolution.
