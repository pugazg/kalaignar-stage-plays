# Scene 39

[The king enters at the rear of the palace. Ponnazhagan has followed him there.]

King: [To Ulagappan.] Ulagappa — I forgot to tell Alli when we are to meet tomorrow!

Ulagappan: What does that matter, my lord! Alli-amma herself will come looking for you! Instead of the two of you meeting secretly in the garden like this, we could simply bring Alli-amma into our inner palace itself!

King: Hm... do you think the time when Alli and I will live without ever being parted will fail to come? It will come!

[They walk on talking. Ponnazhagan stands watching.]

Ponnazhagan: [To himself.] The king — Manimaran! Lord of Manimagudam. Alli is his lover! Lover of the enemy! The enemy's she-goat! Embodiment of treachery! A fifth column that has come in the form of a woman! A witch inside the People's Forum! The very form of conspiracy? I cannot even believe myself! She leaves saying she is going out to gather intelligence — and here she dallies with the king! A tiger in goatskin!

Disaster! Disaster! An unavoidable disaster! The People's Forum is destroyed! Manimaran has won! Manimaran has won! Ponnazhaga! Every one of your revolutionary cries has been ground to powder! I ruined myself by trusting the `paalaivanai` who entered like a storm! I was deceived by a destroyer who betrays the country! Deceived! What shall I do now? Are all my ideals nothing but dust? Is the People's Forum's principle one that can never be fulfilled? No... no! I will put an end to this right now! Alli, I will defeat intrigue with intrigue! Cheat! I will tear you into pieces! Wait, Ponnazhaga — wait!

[He leaves.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/39.md`; verified scan 137 was also consulted for the unusual source forms; no secondary English witness was used.
- Ponnazhagan's monologue is translated as his mistaken inference after seeing Puthumaippithan change into the king; the English does not endorse or correct that inference from outside the scene.
- `ஐந்தாம்படை` remains `fifth column`, continuing the established political control.
- `வெள்ளாட்டி / வெள்ளாட்டுத் தோலிலே வேங்கை` is kept close to the source's goat imagery rather than silently replacing both expressions with an unrelated English idiom.
- The verified enlarged-source form `பாலைவனையை` is deliberately retained as `paalaivanai` because the archival Tamil preserves that unusual form and the immediate scene does not securely resolve it.
