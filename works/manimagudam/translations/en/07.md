# Scene 7

[People's Forum. Puthumaippithan, Ponnazhagan, Alli, volunteers and Black Rose are present. Alli is speaking.]

Alli: If the king signs the resolution, we will have to witness the unbearable scenes of suffering when the people of Kodikkal Nagar are left without a place to live. Those crooked men who wield power will deny even now — and will always deny — that destroying Kodikkal to build a temple is the same as strangling the voices of our comrades who live in huts. To bring those grotesque rulers to the right path, our flag of protest must fly throughout the country.

Ponnazhagan: A flag flying will achieve nothing. Even if protest cries surge like ocean waves, nothing will come of it. I say we must send a message to the king right now: “You shall not sign the resolution destroying Kodikkal Nagar! If you defy this warning and sign it, your very life will be in danger!” That is the kind of warning required!

Puthumaippithan: The path our friend proposes is the path of violence. I prefer to achieve our purpose by peaceful means.

Ponnazhagan: Going to a peacock and asking it for a feather — preaching to an elephant in rut — playing the magudi to a mountain python — we do not need that kind of peace now...

Puthumaippithan: Begging a peacock for a feather — preaching to a rutting elephant — playing the magudi to a mountain python — I never called those things peace! They are examples of foolish actions. The peace I speak of concerns political civility! It concerns the dignity leaders must observe in politics! If, according to your decision, harm is done to the king's life — if the king is killed — will our ideas then rise easily? It is then that a formidable obstacle will arise in our path. The accusation that we are “a group that kills everyone opposed to its doctrine” will prevent our pure and worthy principles from spreading through the country. I ask you: is it the king who must be killed...?

Black Rose: No — that Gurunathar!

Puthumaippithan: Not him either; not Editor Ariharanathar! Not Minister Gunaseelar! What must be killed are blind ideas! Dark thoughts! You may ask: if we do not kill the tiger, can we stop its leap? If we do not strike the snake, can we escape its poisonous fang? What use is there in keeping the forest in which tiger and snake live right in the middle of the country and then trembling, “Tiger! Tiger!”? So what must first be destroyed is the forest! The dark heart! The blind idea! If they are abolished — if the forest becomes a country, anxiety will disappear — if the tiger is abolished, both tiger and snake will disappear! My principle is that we must struggle for that lasting peace!

Alli: Yes — what he says is true! If one king is killed, another king will come tomorrow. If he too is killed, another will come the next day. If we go on killing while new kings keep appearing, we will have no time even to put down the killing sword; nor will there ever be a shortage of kings waiting to be killed!

Ponnazhagan: Then are you saying the crown itself should rule and that people's government is unnecessary?

Puthumaippithan: No... no... There is no use in abolishing one king... I say the royal line itself must disappear. To abolish a dynasty does not mean taking up an axe like Parasurama of the Puranic age and killing every king one sees! People must learn to safeguard the rights they obtain from a king. Suppose we threaten a king, or bend him to our will, or turn him to our principles, transfer the government of the country from him into the people's hands, and then within a few days people's government becomes crooked and a situation arises in which people again say monarchy was better — that must not happen! For a permanent people's government to arise, the minds of the people must be cultivated. For example, the people of Manimagudapuram are angry with the king, the government and the Assembly of Nobles — but why do they not have that anger toward Gurunathar? Faith! Superstition lying thick because their minds lack development! When the king is brought down and people's government blossoms, if these Gurunathars enter the garden of renaissance like cattle, declaring themselves representatives of Mahesan, do you think people's government will live for long? It will not, friend, it will not! That government will end like the story of marrying a beautiful woman to an *ali*!

Alli: Fine speech! A splendid explanation! The People's Forum has received new ideas today! I trust Ponnazhagan too will not reject them!

Ponnazhagan: [Laughing] Your side always wins, doesn't it, Alli! And now you have found yourself the perfect ally as well.

Puthumaippithan: All right, I have a little work... I will come later... Black Rose, come, let us go!

[Alli watches Puthumaippithan leave. The poet, who until now has sat silently in a corner, suddenly sings loudly.]

“You may trust a river bursting through its dam!
You may trust a storm wind!
You may trust a hissing snake and a red scorpion;
But do not trust the liar called Puthumaippithan
who has entered this association!”

[The members of the Forum are stunned.]

Alli: What? [Shocked.]

Ponnazhagan: Poet! [Astonished.]

Poet: Do not think that because he poured water on the fire he is the embodiment of integrity!...

Alli: What are you saying — about Puthumaippithan...?

Poet: He is not the embodiment of peace; he is the very form of danger! A government spy!

Ponnazhagan: Ah... a government spy.

Poet: Yes... I saw it with my own eyes! The suspicion I had about him has been confirmed. I heard that he frequently goes toward the palace. I followed him. Last night I myself saw him enter the palace — there is no room for doubt!

Ponnazhagan: Ah! We have been deceived. In our haste we even made him a member of the Forum. It is fortunate that you warned us at least now! Poet, many thanks! Many thanks!

Alli: Somehow I still cannot call Puthumaippithan a government spy!

Poet: Then call him the leader of a fifth column! Alli! It is wrong for you to trust Puthumaippithan! Ever since he came, you have begun neglecting even Ponnazhagan!

Alli: Poet! Forgive me. It is true that I respect Puthumaippithan. At the same time I will not forget that I have accepted Ponnazhagan as my leader. I may be the executive head of this Forum — but Ponnazhagan was my leader then! He is my leader today! He will remain my leader forever.

Ponnazhagan: Alli! We must come to a decision about Puthumaippithan quickly. Tomorrow the secret executive committee of our Forum must meet. Attend to what has to be done — I am going to Kodikkal Nagar and will return!

[Ponnazhagan leaves. Alli is distressed.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/07.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- `பலாத்கார மார்க்கம்` is translated as `path of violence`, preserving the scene's explicit strategic debate between violent threat and political civility.
- `மகுடி` is retained as `magudi` rather than silently replacing the source's culturally specific instrument.
- The forest/tiger/snake analogy is translated in the order preserved by the verified Tamil assembly, including the source's unusual final formulation.
- `அலி` is retained as *ali* rather than assigning a narrower modern social/biological category not explicitly supplied by the source.
- `ஐந்தாம் படைத் தலைவன்` is rendered `leader of a fifth column`, preserving the political accusation.
- The poet's warning verse is translated only from the source-visible lines in this scene; no additional verse is imported.
