# Scene 11

[The Minister's house — Kalarani's room. Kalarani is dancing, singing sweetly. Vanji enters.]

Vanji: Bravo... bravo...

Kalarani: How was the dance?

Vanji: Even a black cobra cannot bend and sway the way Kalarani's body does.

Kalarani: Enough, crown-jewel of poets! Bundle up your comparisons... don't get carried away!

Vanji: What else can I do, Kala? What new comparison is left to make? Everything has been said so often it has gone stale.

Kalarani: So you call me a black snake?

Vanji: I am talking about the way it bends.

Kalarani: Why not say a mullai creeper?

Vanji: They have been saying that for three thousand years... Besides, a mullai creeper stays rooted in one place and sways in the wind. It bends! A snake dances in many places — bends and moves...

Kalarani: Chi!

Vanji: And hisses too!

Kalarani: [Laughing] You mischievous creature, Vanji! If he saw my dancing...?

Vanji: He would make you the peacock of his own garden!

Kalarani: I don't know whether he appreciates dance and music...

Vanji: If he doesn't, make him an admirer! Look — two fish are leaping in a lotus pond. Would he not cast a hook for them?

Kalarani: What nonsense? What lotus pond... what two fish?

Vanji: Your face and the two eyes in that face... I said it in poetic style!

Kalarani: Oh, wonderful! Vanji, stop this poetic headache for a while!

Vanji: If I stop that, what else should I begin?

Kalarani: Vanji! He and I are so far apart that we can never meet; we are two opposite poles...

Vanji: Is meeting such a great difficulty? You could meet him even now.

Kalarani: Where?

Vanji: In the palace...

Kalarani: Are you talking about the king? [Angrily.]

Vanji: Then whom are you talking about?

Kalarani: I am talking about him...

Vanji: Him... him — what a fine 'him'! Kalarani, at least tell me who this 'him' of yours is...

Kalarani: Today I am going to tell you who he is.

Vanji: Tell me!

Kalarani: I will. But on one condition — after I tell you who he is, you must never again speak to me about the king! I tell you with certainty: I have not the slightest desire for the king.

[Minister Gunaseelar enters as she speaks.]

Gunaseelar: Why, child, no desire? Is the king so crooked-looking? Is he some grotesque man?

Kalarani: Father... [Tries to say something.]

Gunaseelar: I have been trying for so many days — trying and trying — and you keep disregarding my wish, Kalarani! If your father's authority and this ministerial office are to endure, you must marry the king!

Kalarani: I can't, Father...

Gunaseelar: I order you — obey me! Kalarani, don't be upset. In the present crisis you simply must marry the king. A dangerous situation is developing in which the king may go beyond even my control. Once the king becomes related to us, I shall need no one's favour. Now, merely to protect this ministerial post, I have to nod along with the nobles, carry Gurunathar's flag, and grin submissively at the newspaper editor!

Once the king becomes my son-in-law, I need fear no one, do I? Then the nobles and Gurunathar themselves will lie at my feet!

Kalarani: Why a thousand explanations, Father? There is no path to live by earning the people's respect; so you have made a plan to use the people to preserve your ministerial office. That is all!

Gunaseelar: [Angrily] Kalarani! What did you say?

Kalarani: Until you climb onto your funeral bier, I must be sacrificed to your obsession with office. Fine. I am ready. Is the king himself willing?

Gunaseelar: Do you think he asks me every time he sees me, 'Is Kalarani well? Is Kalarani well?' without any intention? Go and see him, child. Everything will end well.

Kalarani: I am going now. Vanji, you come with me to the palace too! [Calls her in agitation.]

Gunaseelar: Go, daughter — go with a full heart!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/11.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- Vanji's snake/creeper/peacock/fish imagery and the comic escalation of poetic comparisons are retained rather than flattened.
- The repeated ambiguous `அவர்` is rendered as `him`, preserving Kalarani's refusal to name the man she means.
- Gunaseelar's dynastic and office-preservation motive remains explicit; the political satire is not softened.
- `பாடையில் ஏறும் வரையில்` is rendered by its funeral-bier image rather than paraphrased into a generic reference to old age.
