# Scene 32

[Garden. Alli and Puthumaippithan enter. Alli breathes a great sigh of relief at having escaped.]

Alli: Appada, we escaped! If we had fallen into someone's hands...

Puthumaippithan: Should one be afraid of such things? If fisherfolk were afraid of the waves of the sea...?

Alli: Whatever one may say, you have extraordinary courage! Entering the palace at night — but leave that aside. How did you know I was there?

Puthumaippithan: [Covering himself.] Wouldn't I know? Haven't I been... hm... hm... following you all along? When confusion broke out at the foundation-stone ceremony, the Minister and the others ran away! The king ordered that you be carried to the palace. Leave that aside; Alli — what necessity made you save the king?

Alli: I don't know myself! The moment I saw the king's face, some strange feeling came over me. The next instant, like lightning, a knife flew at him; without even knowing what I was doing, I stopped it. And it was for the best — if the king had died, wouldn't the blame have fallen on the People's Forum?

Puthumaippithan: Yes. But one thing! I think Ponnazhagan and the others will not like what you did.

Alli: Even if they do not like it, don't they too care that no danger should come to the People's Forum? So they will not accuse me!

Puthumaippithan: All right, Alli! I am leaving! You leave too!

Alli: Where are you going? Yes — where are you living now? What are you doing?

Puthumaippithan: Right now, I am here — talking with you. I do not know where I shall go from here.

Alli: Your plans for the future...?

Puthumaippithan: There are any number of them. Do you know that I am the owner of *Manimagudam*, Alli?

Alli: Which Manimagudam?

Puthumaippithan: The *Manimagudam* newspaper! Not the country! That is my present effort. I have great faith that through the paper we can cultivate the minds of the people of the country. Alli, what opinion does Ponnazhagan have of me?

Alli: He has immeasurable affection for you — he often regrets having suspected you in the beginning — merely hearing your name gives him a special joy!

Puthumaippithan: Is that so? Many thanks!

Alli: You asked only what Ponnazhagan thinks of you; did you ask what I think of you?

Puthumaippithan: I have the power to understand certain things without asking!

Alli: Then you do have a heart!

Puthumaippithan: It is because I have one that I can think about the people!

Alli: In that heart, storm, smoke, war and earthquake should not keep taking turns to heap only pain upon it — shouldn't a breeze enter there in place of fire too?

Puthumaippithan: I understand! You mean that when one drop of honey falls upon a tongue parched dry without water, its taste is something altogether different — isn't that so?

Alli: The thoughts of both of us are the same! But...?

Puthumaippithan: There is only a small difference over who will reveal it first!

Alli: Say there *was* a difference!

Puthumaippithan: Alli!

Alli: Ah...!

[A feeling mixed with shyness passes between them. Puthumaippithan touches her. Alli too draws closer to him. For a short while there is lovers' playful sulking and intimacy.]

Puthumaippithan: Come, look at me! Look! Ah... I had thought that only sparks of fire could rise from these eyes! Only now have I understood, beautiful-eyed one, that they hold a magnet that can draw me too! That day at Kodikkal Nagar these lips poured out thunderous woman's words; I did not know then that so much softness was in them, lightning-girl...!

Alli: And what about you? They described you as a volcano! But to me, aren't you a waterfall of delight? They called you the summer sun; only now have I understood that you can be the cool moon as well!

Puthumaippithan: Alli.

Alli: What?

Puthumaippithan: This love of ours must not become an obstacle to our struggle!

Alli: That is why I hesitated all these days.

Puthumaippithan: For a traveller crossing a desert that blisters his feet, the shade of a date-palm offers a place to rest — our love is like that for us! From now on, when I run about and work, when I climb the platform and thunder, when I break instruments of repression — I will know that there is someone for me; she will bring joy when I am weary — when I return from the field, she will bind medicine upon my wounds! Wicked men may call me a wretch, a fool — with the single word `kannaala`, she will wipe away that sorrow and calm my fury. That rising of the heart will be born in me, and I will work with still newer enthusiasm!

Alli: Not merely to remove your weariness, kannaala! Not merely to bind medicine upon your wound, my joy! I will not be only the woman who sends you off to the field — I will also be the companion who plans, `You take that battlefront; I will take this one!`

Puthumaippithan: Alli! If the two of us were not revolutionaries, would we be on battlefronts? I would be in the *aranmanai* — the palace — and you at the *arivaal-manai*, the sickle-board?

Alli: You in the palace?

Puthumaippithan: Yes — as a court poet singing praise of the government, as a servant of the Minister, as a disciple of the Guru — surely I would have got some job like that, wouldn't I?

[The sound of guards approaching is heard. Both are startled.]

Alli: It sounds as if someone is coming?

Puthumaippithan: It must be the palace guards! All right, we must not delay any longer — leave, Alli!

Alli: And you?

Puthumaippithan: Me? I will meet you tomorrow!

Alli: Where? When?

Puthumaippithan: Right here! At this very time! Hm — go! It sounds as if the guards are coming!

[They embrace and part.]

Alli: Don't forget!

Puthumaippithan: How could I forget?

Alli: Will it torment you?

[She leaves.]

Puthumaippithan: Ah — I forgot one piece of news — how is that Kalarani?

Alli: [As she goes.] Kalarani? — I will tell you tomorrow.

[Puthumaippithan looks around. Ulagappan enters.]

Ulagappan: Shall we go, my lord?

Puthumaippithan: Let us go!

Ulagappan: Take off the disguise — otherwise you cannot enter the palace!

[Puthumaippithan removes the disguise and, now in royal dress, leaves with Ulagappan. The king turns and looks back in the direction Alli went.]

Ulagappan: Come, come — the world is not going to end today! Come, you can see her tomorrow!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/32.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The scene's romantic imagery is retained alongside the explicit warning that love must not obstruct political struggle.
- `அரண்மனை / அரிவாள் மனை` is preserved through `aranmanai / arivaal-manai` because replacing both with unrelated English words would erase the source sound-play; `arivaal-manai` is glossed as the traditional sickle-board / cutting board.
- `கண்ணாளா` is retained as `kannaala` inside the quoted intimate address rather than flattened into a generic endearment.
- The terse verified source line `வாட்டுமா?` is translated conservatively as `Will it torment you?` without expanding its object.
- The ending keeps the source's identity movement explicit: Puthumaippithan removes the disguise and the stage direction then identifies him as the king.
