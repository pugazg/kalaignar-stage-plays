# Scene 6

[The Assembly of Nobles is in session. The nobles and Minister Gunaseelar are present. Gurunathar and Ariharanathar, editor of *People's Servant*, are seated there by special invitation. A short distance from the Assembly sit members of the public. Among them are Ponnazhagan, Puthumaippithan, Alli and others.]

Gunaseelar: Honourable nobles! ... Among the resolutions that this representative Assembly of Manimagudapuram must take up today, the most important concerns the construction of a temple. It would be a matter of pride for all of us if Manimagudapuram possessed a wonder that made the whole world marvel. Let the temple we are about to build be such an everlasting wonder. There is no space in the capital to build a temple on the magnificent scale we envisage. One proposal, therefore, is to clear Kodikkal Nagar, a part of the capital, and build the temple there. This proposal comes from our Gurunathar, the friend of the poor. I request everyone to offer their views on erecting the temple in Kodikkal Nagar so that this divinely inspired resolution may be passed quickly. Members of the public will also be permitted to offer useful suggestions on the matter.

[The Minister sits.]

Nobleman 1: Who is going to object to building a temple for the Lord? By all means, let it be done.

Nobleman 2: There is no need to ask anyone's opinion. Gurunathar may build the temple wherever and however he pleases.

Nobleman 3: Worship at the temple is most excellent! — One should not live in a town without a temple.

Gun.: I believe there will be no opposition to Mahesan's work. I am very pleased.

[Ponnazhagan rises.]

Ponnazhagan: I oppose building the temple in Kodikkal Nagar.

Gun.: Why...?

Ponnazhagan: Kodikkal Nagar is a poor people's quarter. Destroying a thousand huts and building a temple there is something that cannot be permitted! Where are the poor who make their living by cultivating betel vines to go? What is to become of a thousand families? If the betel-vine plots that give life to those huts are destroyed, no one will be able to extinguish the fire of poverty that will erupt. No one with a human heart will accept a temple consecration conducted with drums and music while poor people suffer with their children. If God needs a temple, choose another place. Do not throw earth into the stomachs of hut-dwellers.

Gun.: In a country this large, is there no other place for those thousand hut-dwellers to live?

Ponnazhagan: What if I turn the same question back on you? In a country this large, is there no other place to build God's temple? What is your answer, members of the Assembly? What answer have you? Why — what anger do you have against those poor creatures? Even when the season ruins the crop, even when it dies without flourishing, you compel them to pay the Kodikkal tax. They sweat to cultivate betel, pay three-fourths of what they earn as tax, and somehow pass their lives on the remaining quarter. And now those helpless people are suddenly to scatter in every direction. To issue a sudden order saying that a temple will be built there is merciless — a murderous policy that strikes a thousand families dead at once! I beg you to abandon the plan to destroy Kodikkal and build a temple there.

Gun.: Gurunathar himself will answer this.

[Gurunathar rises.]

Gurunathar: I hear a voice saying Kodikkal must not be destroyed! — A laughable voice! A laughable voice that is afraid to say a temple is unnecessary and instead screams while using the people of Kodikkal Nagar as a shield! It is precisely because such atheistic doctrine has spread that I say a temple is urgently needed! A thousand huts will be destroyed, it says — one soul is wailing over it! Let them be destroyed! A thousand huts may disappear because one temple is built; but can we forget that, at the same time, the road to salvation is being opened to lakhs upon lakhs of people in Manimagudapuram? For many to live, some must sacrifice their lives! — The temple must and shall be built in Kodikkal Nagar!

Puthumaippithan: I reject it — I reject it!

Gun.: What? — You mean a temple must not be built in Kodikkal Nagar?

Puthumaippithan: No — I say there is no need for another temple at all!

Gurunathar: Ah! — Naked atheism is dancing with its hood spread! — This man is worse than the other! He at least rejected the place; this one rejects God himself!

Puthumaippithan: Gurunathar! Do not hunt down advocates of the knowledge movement with a few words such as “denial of God” and “atheism”! Who said that saying another temple is unnecessary is atheism? Tell us; let us hear it. How many temples are there in Manimagudapuram? Shall I give you the figures? In the capital alone — five hundred temples! Across the country — six thousand temples! The total amount spent on these six thousand temples is sixty crore rupees every year! And how much wealth lies locked in those temples as land, ornaments and diamond crowns? Three hundred crore rupees, Gurunathar! Three hundred crore rupees! Three hundred crore rupees piled up as chariots, temple towers and silver finials — of no use to children crying for milk; of no use to young men wasting away without work; of no use to lives worn down to bone, labouring like ants and tortured by nobles with hearts of iron! Money that does not exist even in the treasury of Manimagudapuram! And as though this were not enough, yet another temple? — Who asked for it? — Who needs a new temple?

Gurunathar: The Lord himself asked for it!

Puthumaippithan: Did he say six thousand temples were not enough?

Gurunathar: Yes!

Puthumaippithan: Did he ask for a temple for himself after destroying a thousand homes?

Gurunathar: Yes!

Puthumaippithan: One who asks for that cannot be the Lord. According to the qualities attributed to God, what he needs is not a temple! The hearts of good people are his temple! His home! His palace! For this Assembly of Nobles to behave as though God had searched all over Manimagudapuram, failed to find even a house for rent, and were wandering with his family — and as though Gurunathar, worried by his plight, had rushed forward to build him a house — is only laughable. I beg you to abandon this idea.

Nobleman 1: Insult! Insult! He mocks Gurunathar — throw him out at once!

Gun.: Public opinion is now closed! The resolution will be put to a vote. Since no one except the nobles has voting rights in this Assembly.... everyone must remain silent.

Puthumaippithan: Minister! Think for a moment! A thousand householders will be left without refuge! This is something even God would not want! Do not kill love, bury it, build a temple over it and then write “Love is God” there! If this resolution is passed without respecting our plea — the voice of the poor...

Gun.: Will there be a revolution?

Puthumaippithan: No! But no one knows when a smoking volcano will erupt! If a thousand huts are wiped out and God is installed there — would people of intelligence call that a temple? The flowers offered to God will bear the bloodstains shed by a thousand householders! In the incense and lamps shown before him, one will see only the sorrowing faces of mothers — not a pure divine light! You will offer him pongal made from raw rice and payasam — that pongal will be an offering made by crushing the bodies of tender infants! No divine sound will rise from the temple bell, Minister; only the sorrowing tone of a thousand huts abandoned without support! No devotional song will be heard in the Lord's sanctum; only the funeral lament of workers who have lost their lives! Wailing! Weeping! The temple will become a field of corpses filled with people sobbing — it will turn into a cremation ground — a burial ground! No! Let there be no stain or degradation in God's temple. Do not bring disgrace upon God in God's own name!

Gun.: Stop! If you speak any further, we shall lose patience and be forced to arrest you. The resolution is going to pass. If you can remain quiet, I will allow you to stay; otherwise...

Puthumaippithan: We are leaving! Let the Minister be spared the trouble! God! If you exist, question these wrongdoers! Ah — after burning a thousand homes, they say you are going to sit here and smile! Can you tolerate such injustice? Fine! Very fine! Swim in the tears of the poor, then! Society of the poor — weep! God is going to smile! The government is going to build a luxurious temple for him because, hanging in mid-air, he has nowhere to live! So, poor man who cannot resist! Deceived comrade! Weep! Weep now! Fear! Bend! Rave! Lament! Sing your funeral dirge! Tears alone belong to you — no one can seize them from you. So weep, friend; weep! You have no liberation — as long as these nobles remain, as long as these Gurunathars remain, you have no liberation!

[Puthumaippithan walks out.]

Gun.: Every member of the public must leave. Mm... at once!

[Everyone, including Ponnazhagan and Alli, is driven out.]

Gun.: [Looking at the newspaper editor] I request Srijath Ariharanathar, editor of *People's Servant*... none of the incidents that occurred here....

Ariharanathar: [Obsequiously]... I solemnly assure this Assembly of Nobles that I certainly will not publish them in the newspaper. No one need worry! Hee..hee...hee.. [bares his teeth]

Gurunathar: Mm... let the resolution pass!

- `சீமான்கள் சபை`, `மக்கள் தொண்டன்` and the other recurring political terms follow the established translation controls.
- Puthumaippithan's extended temple/poverty imagery, repetitions, sarcasm and escalating lament are retained rather than compressed into explanatory prose.
