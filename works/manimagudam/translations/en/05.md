# Scene 5

[A shop selling betel leaves, areca nut, newspapers and other goods. A group stands in front buying things. One man buys a copy of *People's Servant* and opens it. Astonished, he says...]

One Man: Good heavens! Have you seen this news?

Another: What? What?....

One Man: [Reads] “Attempt to create a disturbance in front of Gurunathar's monastery — cavalry drove away the rioters. A man named Puthumaippithan obstructed people going to the monastery for worship and spoke blasphemously, asking, ‘Is not Gurunathar a human being like us? Why should we fall at his feet and offer gifts?’ As a result, a small disturbance arose. People on their way to the monastery stopped there to listen to his speech. He incited all of them and spoke slanderously about Gurunathar. Government cavalry was therefore sent and the situation was brought under control.”

Another: Who is this new fellow? Looks like a big character! Is he more extreme than Ponnazhagan?

One: What great extremist? Is abusing Gurunathar and committing blasphemy the mark of an extremist? A godless fellow!

[At that moment Black Rose comes to the shop and stands as though buying something, listening to the conversation. He interrupts them.]

B.R.: What is that? Puthumaippithan created a disturbance?

One: Yes. Here, look! [Hands him the paper.]

B.R.: [After reading] What sinners! What I saw directly with my own eyes has come out completely changed in print!

One: Oh! You saw the disturbance yourself?

B.R.: Disturbance?

One: Then what happened?

B.R.: Puthumaippithan gave a speech.

One: But it says there was a disturbance?

B.R.: In this newspaper editor's translation, a speech means a disturbance!

One: But he blocked people going to the monastery — that is true, isn't it?

B.R.: A hundred percent false. It is true that a meeting was held a short distance from the monastery. It is true that people who were going to the monastery stopped to hear the meeting. It is true that Gurunathar waited in vain because no one came to make offerings...

One: So only the part about a disturbance and the cavalry coming is false?

B.R.: That too is true! There is only a small change in the report. The cavalry did not come because a disturbance arose. The disturbance arose only after the cavalry came!

One: Must they hold a meeting right in front of the monastery?

B.R.: Isn't medicine applied where the wound is?

One: Whatever you say, I don't trust your story. Would they print lies in a newspaper?

B.R.: Get lost, man! It is trusting people like you that he runs the newspaper at all! Because there are too many Ashadabhuthis who think everything that appears in print is true... they have spun tales saying Hanuman entered an alli-stalk — Adisesha carries the whole world — a goat-kid sings songs — a squirrel builds a bridge — a child born only yesterday grants whatever boon you ask! Get lost.... go!

[Black Rose says this and leaves. The man reading the newspaper, seeing another item, says:]

One Man: Hey! It says the Assembly of Nobles is meeting today. Come on, let's go too!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/05.md`; no secondary English witness was consulted.
- The scene's central contrast between the newspaper report and Black Rose's eyewitness correction is preserved line by line, including the reversal: the cavalry did not arrive because of a disturbance; the disturbance began after the cavalry arrived.
- `மக்கள் தொண்டன்` remains *People's Servant*, consistent with Scene 1 and its political irony.
- `ஆஷாடபூதிகள்` is conservatively retained as `Ashadabhuthis` rather than replaced by an asserted modern equivalent; the surrounding catalogue of credulous tales supplies the dramatic sense.
- The source's mythic/comic examples — Hanuman in an alli-stalk, Adisesha bearing the world, the singing goat-kid, the bridge-building squirrel and the newborn granting boons — are retained without external correction.
