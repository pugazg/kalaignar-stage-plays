# Scene 19

## Kaanal Vari
## And Love's Parting

### Puhar

[Kovalan and Madhavi are travelling in a chariot through Puhar—Poompuhar...

The chariot moves through the lamp-lit streets of Puhar.

The trading houses of foreigners... warehouses... glitter with lamps...

On the seashore, men and women... are at play...

Kovalan and Madhavi come beneath a punnai tree...

There, a beautifully worked screen has been hung, and behind it...

facing the seashore and the place where the Kaveri joins the sea... they sit upon a beautiful couch arranged there.

Once they are seated...

Vasanthamalai... takes the yazh and gives it... to Madhavi...]

Madhavi receives the yazh... and draws music from it with her red-kanthal fingers... For a while, without song, she enchants Kovalan with the sound of the yazh alone...

Everyone who had been playing on the shore stands spellbound by the sweetness of the yazh...

Even the sea-wave grows calm—the sea, like a pond, slowly moves its wave-hands.

After the sound of the yazh has brought the whole shore under its spell... Madhavi gives the yazh into Kovalan's hands...]

Madhavi : Hmm... you sing first!

[Kovalan casts upon those fingers the cool glance he has drawn from Madhavi's eyes... and presses them to his own eyes...]

Kovalan : These fingers could even prick the eyes... and cause no wound—so soft are these anicham-flower fingers!...

Are these rough fingers of mine worthy to touch the strings of a yazh touched by these fingers, Madhavi?...

Madhavi : If only this yazh had life as I do... the pleasure it feels when your finger falls upon it—it would sing that as a poem.

Kovalan : Is that so, Madhavi...

Madhavi : Yes... sing... let my ears too receive the delight my eyes have received...

[Kovalan plucks the yazh and sings]

Song :

“Though the white-parasoled king,
his sceptre raised upon his crowned head,
embrace Ganga,
do not sulk—live long, Kaveri!

Though he embrace Ganga,
fish-eyed one, your not sulking—
I have known it as the great chastity
of women. Live long, Kaveri!

Though the enduring white-parasoled king,
wielding the sceptre that does not bend,
embrace Kanni,
do not sulk—live long, Kaveri!

Though he embrace Kanni,
fish-eyed one, your not sulking—
I have known it as the great chastity
of enduring women. Live long, Kaveri!”

Madhavi : Yes, a beautiful song... sweet music... but...

Kovalan : What, Madhavi?

Madhavi : In the meaning of the song...

Kovalan : In the meaning...? Is there doubt...? I sang a song praising Kaveri. Karikala Chola, the one to whom Kaveri belongs... conquered Ganga too... He marched upon the northern land and joined Ganga to himself... Seeing that, Kaveri, the first wife, did not grow angry with him... did not quarrel... This is the mark of women of chastity... O river Kaveri of such chastity... long may you live... that is the meaning!

Madhavi : Oh-ho... very well... the man who joined Ganga while Kaveri was already there... according to your song, did not stop with that either...

[She sings the passage with music]

“Though the enduring white-parasoled king,
wielding the sceptre that does not bend,
embrace Kanni,
do not sulk—live long, Kaveri!”

Kovalan : Yes... I sang it... The one who joined Ganga while Kaveri was there did not stop there; he defeated the Pandyas... and joined Kanyakumari as well. Seeing even that, Kaveri did not hate him... did not quarrel... O queen of chastity, Kaveri... long... may you live!

Madhavi : Quite right!... The husband may join Ganga... then go with Kumari too... and Kaveri is to stand watching it all? Is that it! Did you sing this song for your own protection? Leaving Kannagi for Madhavi... and then leaving Madhavi for...

[Interrupting]

Kovalan : Madhavi!... Do not take the meaning wrongly!... I sang only to show the greatness of Karikalan... and the abundance of Kaveri!...

[Taking the yazh]

Madhavi : Here... I shall sing...

[She sings]

“With bees at your sides humming richly,
clad in a garment of jewelled flowers,
opening your dark fish-like eyes and swaying,
you walked—live long, Kaveri!

All that opening of dark fish-like eyes,
all that swaying walk—I understood:
it is because your husband's righteous sceptre
does not bend! Live long, Kaveri!...”

Kovalan : There!... The reason maiden Kaveri remains undiminished in abundance is the excellence of the Chola, her husband's sceptre! Because the husband conducts himself with virtue... Kaveri smiles in happiness and gives prosperity. Is that not the meaning of your song?... That is... if the lover remains loving, the beloved too remains loving. If my love for you grows less... your love for me too will grow less... and then that love will go to someone else...

[Interrupting, angrily]

Madhavi : Exactly!... What wonderful gratitude—what beauty in the way you explain a song!...

[She puts down the yazh and rises to leave]

Kovalan : Madhavi!

[Calling to her, he goes behind]

[Madhavi walks rapidly.... Kovalan hurries behind her, calling, “Madhavi, stop”...

Among the fishing nets stands a fisherwoman. Kovalan sees her; in the fisherwoman's form he imagines Madhavi taking the shape of a fish! Such a fancy rises in his mind.]

Kovalan : Ah! Holding in her hand a stalk like that of the honey-filled blue water-lily, driving away the birds that come to eat flesh—how cruel are this maiden's eyes!

Ah! Fragrant flower given by the grove! Ripened noble speech! A face like the full moon!

[Madhavi stops and listens. Kovalan, imagining the fisherwoman as Madhavi...]

Kovalan : Ah!... A young woman dripping beauty without jewel or ornament! A form bending and swaying like a creeper wrapped in blossoms! Her waist—one handful. Beside it, a young elephant-calf narrow enough to hold? And here, one measure of honey! No—she is the famed mullai called `kuraivai`!... In speech, a parrot! Will she ever spare me the torment of love!

[He sings]

“O shore where the spreading waves arrive,
O place where sacred sand spreads wide...
O swift-spreading fragrant flower!
O grove where growth stands close—
O hair spreading fragrance as it curls—
O sacred face like the moon!
O pair of eyes, twin fish—
it is these that brought me grief!”

[Singing, he goes nearer. When he comes close, he sees that she is not Madhavi... but a fisherwoman... Startled, she too moves away...

Seeing this, Madhavi, very angry, goes to Vasanthamalai...]

Madhavi : Did you see—Vasanthamalai!

Vasanthamalai : I saw—I saw! Madhavi! Even oodal must have some limit...

Madhavi : His desire has turned toward someone other than me. He cannot hide it. The truth has come out... Let it be; I shall deliberately hurt his heart! I too shall pretend that I have fallen in love with a man bearing a spear...

Vasanthamalai : Do not, Madhavi! Play may turn into disaster...

Madhavi : Nothing will happen to him... You go away; he is coming!

[There is a statue of a warrior. Madhavi gazes intently at it. To her eyes the statue appears like Kovalan, in the warrior's youthful garments]

Madhavi : Lord of love! You did not come seeking me with true love; you came to me like a merchant who thinks my

teeth truly are pearls and begins bargaining over their price!

There is coral upon my lips; you came like a thief thinking you could take it away! You thought the redness of my cheek was a rose—there is no one to question you, so you may pluck it and go—and thus you came to me!

However you may have thought when you came, you stole my heart... body... life... all my beauty belongs to you! O life of mine standing like a statue! When will you come to embrace me in the coconut grove?

When a swan sits upon the punnai tree... all the flowers around it may be imagined as stars... and the swan in their midst as the beautiful moon; thinking so, the flowers will burst into bloom.

Do not leave Madhavi, woman of art in beautiful Poompuhar, trembling in love...

Come within my eyes...
Come, great warrior...
Come, my king...

[Angrily]

Kovalan : Stop... stop... enchantress, stop!

Madhavi : There it is!

Kovalan : Che! My dear! Lady of victory! Ruin born to bring glory to a clan of unsteady minds!

Showing art... showing fish-like eyes... showing all your bewildering charm... woman-demon who bewitches! Trusting you, I lost my heart, lost my wealth; I acquired a nature hated by kin, parents, relatives and all the friends around me. I scorched the life of noble Kannagi... wounded her heart... and until now lay fallen, stripped of every force of joy... Today I understand... who you are ... and the old glory of the clan of women from which you come.

Madhavi : My dear!... Enough, my dear, enough! Was it for this evil to come upon me that the day dawned?

Kovalan : The day has dawned... the golden rooster has crowed! Leaving holy, good Kannagi to wither, I came to feed upon a woman-stone lying in the dust... Now that darkened mind has lifted, the day has dawned, wicked woman!

Madhavi : Ayyo! I understand nothing at all! What wrong have I done?

Kovalan : Ah-ha-ha...... schemer! I sang Kaanal Vari; and with your heart fixed upon someone else, you poured out love-language woven from many deceptive lies—I heard it with these very ears!

I was deceived because I did not know the truth: even the tethering-post in a courtesan's house can wink and bewitch a man...

Madhavi : My dear! Do not kill me with unbearable slanders! Madhavi... is indeed a courtesan! But her heart is different; apart from you, my mind has never thought of anyone... and never will... Believe me!

Kovalan : You know the eight kinds of performance-lines—`kaattivari`, `kankooduvari` and the rest—used to deceive onlookers... I know that! With a dimpled face and flower-cluster eyes, with speech like the kovvai fruit...

Never again can this Kovalan be deceived! There—your ideal lover stands as a statue... wait until he comes! Wait until he gives you wealth! Then set up your shop of desire... and before him, walk away toward another wealthy lord! Go... go... carry on your hereditary trade without any loss! Is Kovalan a man without reason!! Go, go, do not stand before me! Go!... Go!

## Translation notes

- `கானல் வரி` is retained as **`Kaanal Vari`**. The four quoted song passages are translated independently from the verified Tamil printed in this scene. No published Silappathikaram or standard English verse translation was imported.
- The opening preserves Puhar's cosmopolitan commerce—foreign trading houses and warehouses—alongside the highly Tamil cultural centre of the yazh performance.
- `செங்காந்தள் விரல்கள்` and `அனிச்ச மலர் விரல்கள்` keep the named flower imagery rather than substituting generic `delicate fingers`.
- In Kovalan's first song, `கங்கை` is translated `Ganga`; `கன்னி` is retained as `Kanni` in the verse because Kovalan's following prose explicitly interprets it as Kanyakumari. The English does not pre-empt Kalaignar's own explanation.
- The song's repeated `புலவாய் / புலவாதொழிதல்` is rendered through `sulk / not sulking`, preserving the marital-love argument that Kovalan himself then explains. The English cannot reproduce every classical phonetic and metrical feature; it prioritizes the source's dramatic logic and repetition.
- Madhavi's answering song retains the crucial `திருந்து செங்கோல் வளையாமை` as the husband's **righteous sceptre that does not bend**. Kovalan then interprets the poem in explicitly relational terms, which becomes the trigger for jealousy.
- The fisherwoman passage is deliberately extravagant and sometimes source-opaque. Terms such as `குறைவையென்னும் முல்லை` are not silently normalized into a different literary reading; the English retains `kuraivai` where secure identification is lacking.
- Madhavi's speech to the warrior statue turns body imagery into commerce: teeth as pearls with a price, lips as coral, cheek as a rose to be stolen. That transactional metaphor is retained because it prepares the later accusation about price and hereditary profession.
- Kovalan's final tirade is **character speech, not translator narration**. Its abusive language about Madhavi's birth, courtesan status, sexuality and `குலத்தொழில்` is kept forceful because the dramatic rupture depends upon it, but the translation does not endorse or neutralize the abuse.
- `கணிகை` remains `courtesan`; the more hostile `காமக்கடை` is translated as `shop of desire`, preserving Kovalan's degrading commercial metaphor without silently turning it into a neutral social description.
- `காட்டுவரி / கண்கூடுவரி` are retained as `kaattivari / kankooduvari` because the source presents them as named performance categories; no speculative technical equivalence is invented.
- The closing `கோவலன் மதி படைத்தவனோ!!` is kept as a challenge about Kovalan's possession of reason; it is not converted into the movement-specific `rationalist` vocabulary.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 19 deliberately stages a conflict between classical literary ideals and lived human equality. Kovalan's first Kaanal Vari explanation praises Kaveri for not quarrelling when her royal husband joins Ganga and Kanyakumari; Madhavi immediately asks whether a wife is simply expected to watch while a husband takes woman after woman, and whether the song has been chosen for Kovalan's own protection. Kalaignar does not let the classical chastity ideal pass without a woman's interrogation.

That questioning can resonate strongly with Self-Respect/Dravidian critiques of unequal gender codes. Yet the scene does not become a programmatic speech: jealousy, art, play, erotic imagination and misunderstanding drive the quarrel. The translation therefore preserves Madhavi's question itself and keeps the later movement relation in this note.

The ending is darker. Kovalan falls back upon inherited-status abuse—`courtesan`, lineage, hereditary trade—and converts Madhavi's art and sexuality into commercial stigma. Read beside scenes 7, 10, 11, 13 and 14, Kalaignar has already given Madhavi artistic worth, choice, moral insight and sisterly concern for Kannagi. The tirade therefore exposes the violence of birth-based stigma rather than functioning as a neutral description of Madhavi. That contrast has a clear anti-hierarchical resonance, but the English does not rewrite Kovalan into a modern critic of caste or patriarchy; it preserves his failure in the scene.
