## Findings

The translation preserves the opening playful jealousy, sneeze sequence, pregnancy revelation and the `மாதவி / மாதா / பிதா` sound-play without inventing an unrelated English joke.

The parrot-and-snake narrative is translated from the verified source exactly as given. No better-known literary variant was substituted.

The most important terminology review concerns the source's deliberate coexistence of distinct forms:

- `பிராமண மாது` / `பிராமணமாது` — rendered `Brahmin woman` where the source explicitly uses `பிராமண`;
- `பார்ப்பனமாது` — preserved as `paarppana woman` in that stage direction rather than silently normalized;
- `அந்தணர் / அந்தணன்` — retained as **`Antanar`**, not `Brahmin`;
- `மறையவர்` — rendered `man of sacred lore`;
- `தமிழ் மறை` — rendered `Tamil sacred lore`.

This satisfies the project's permanent instruction that `அந்தணர்` is not automatically `Brahmin` and that Kalaignar's lexical distinctions must remain visible in English and documentation.

Kovalan's atonement argument passed semantic review: he challenges the logic of abandoning living dependants to seek expiation for the dead parrot, uses the parallel of two actions made without full knowledge, and directs the Antanar back toward family responsibility. Madhavi then turns that same principle upon Kovalan through `ஊருக்கு உபதேசம் செய்தால் போதாது!`, insisting that public moral advice be lived privately.

The Dravidian-movement note relates this to rational ethical consequence, Tamil ethical authority and accountability, while keeping those later interpretive labels outside dialogue. It also records that the source itself—not the translation—places `பிராமண`, `பார்ப்பன`, `அந்தணர்`, `மறையவர்`, and `தமிழ் மறை` side by side.

No Tamil source change was required during translation review.
