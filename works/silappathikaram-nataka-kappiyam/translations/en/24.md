# Scene 24

## Kannagi and Kovalan with Kavundhi

### On the Road

[Kavundhi, Kannagi and Kovalan walk through the fields, among crops rich with green.

Garlands of flowers too come into view—]

[A thorn pricks her foot... Kannagi sits down to remove it.]

Kovalan : Kannagi... how much cruelty I have done to you. Ayyo... you have never been accustomed to walking like this at all!

[Kovalan removes the thorn]

I am more cruel than this thorn!... This at least wounded your foot; but did not this sinner wound your heart... with a hurt that will not heal?

I am more cruel than this thorn...

[He is about to prick himself]

I am cruel... unable to bear the suffering my Kannagi undergoes... Hey, thorn! You yourself blind these eyes of mine!

[Stopping him... crying out]

Kannagi : My dear!... What is this! Looking and looking at your beauty, I am forgetting my sorrows—do you not even like that? You have plunged me into grievous sorrow!

Kavundhi : Kovalan!

Have you forgotten the Tirukkural that says, **“When adversity comes, smile!”**? For a hero, beauty lies in seeing hardships conquered—come, set out!.... Look... even the breeze from Madurai has begun to blow.....

The Pandya capital is only a little farther now... come, Kannagi—

[They walk]

*

[On the path—a fish leaps out from a field-channel, falls upon the bund and writhes!]

Kavundhi : Ah! Some human beings too leap just like this in excitement... without sensing the danger!

Kovalan : Like me!

Kavundhi : What a beautiful fish! Is not the fish the emblem on the flag of the Pandya land? Perhaps that is why it leaps without fear!

[As she says this, she puts it back into the water... the fish swims away...

From that, the image changes to the fish-banner of the Pandya land flying.]

## Translation notes

- `கவுந்தியுடன் கண்ணகி கோவலன்` is rendered directly as **`Kannagi and Kovalan with Kavundhi`** rather than recasting the source title into a more literary formulation.
- Source `முள்ளுத்த` is unusual/incomplete as printed. The immediate stage action securely shows a thorn in Kannagi's foot, so the English uses `A thorn pricks her foot` without altering the protected Tamil record.
- Kovalan's repeated `இந்த முள்ளைவிடக் கொடுமையானவன் நான்` is retained as repeated self-condemnation rather than compressed.
- The stage direction `குத்திக் கொள்ளப் போகிறான்` is preserved as Kovalan attempting to prick himself; the translation does not intensify it into a different form of self-harm not stated by the source.
- Kannagi's `உங்கள் அழகைப் பார்த்துப் பார்த்து` keeps the repeated-looking cadence: `Looking and looking at your beauty`.
- The Tirukkural quotation `இடுக்கண் வருங்கால் நகுக!` is translated independently from the Tamil printed in this scene as **`When adversity comes, smile!`**. No published/standard English Kural translation was imported.
- `துன்பங்கள் வெற்றி காணுவதுதான் வீரனுக்கு அழகு` is rendered by its immediate dramatic force as `For a hero, beauty lies in seeing hardships conquered`; no modern motivational vocabulary is added.
- The standalone `*` and the fish-to-Pandya-banner visual transition are preserved as stage structure.
- Source-specific rough forms such as `ஆருத் துயரத்தில்` and `அதனுல்தான்` are not silently regularized in Tamil; English conveys the immediate dramatic meaning conservatively.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Kavundhi's intervention continues the play's human-agency ethic. Kovalan turns suffering into self-punishment; Kavundhi redirects him toward endurance, action and responsibility, and she does so by invoking the Tirukkural rather than fatalist resignation. The source therefore places Tamil ethical literature inside practical human conduct.

The fish episode also joins everyday landscape to Tamil political-historical symbolism: an ordinary fish struggling on a field bund becomes the Pandya fish-banner. Kalaignar makes Tamil historical identity emerge from lived geography rather than from abstract exposition.

These features can resonate with Dravidian movement traditions of Tamil ethical authority, human agency and historical recovery, but the translation does not insert `rationalism`, `self-respect` or movement vocabulary into Kavundhi's speech.
