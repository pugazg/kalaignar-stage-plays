# Scene 17

## Stainless Gold Rejoices

### Kannagi's House

[Devandhi tells Kannagi what happened at the celebration]

Devandhi : They say even seasoned commanders could not subdue that elephant; your husband subdued it! The whole town is praising him !

Kannagi : Is he not a Tamil ?... Devandhi! He was not hurt anywhere, was he?...

[As tears and smiles compete with one another]

Devandhi : Ayyo, why worry so much? If he is hurt, Madhavi is there to tend him, is she not ?...

Kannagi : Madhavi!... Madhavi!!... My younger sister is fortunate!!....

[Tears well up as she leans against Kovalan's portrait.]

## Translation notes

- `மாசறு பொன்மகிழ்வு` is rendered as **`Stainless Gold Rejoices`**, retaining the `மாசறு பொன்` image already used by Kovalan for Kannagi rather than replacing the title with generic `Kannagi's Joy`.
- Devandhi's source phrase `காள்கண்ட தளபதிகள்` is difficult as printed. The immediate dramatic sense is of experienced commanders unable to control the elephant, so the English uses `seasoned commanders` while documenting that this is a contextual rendering, not a correction of the verified Tamil.
- `தமிழரல்லவா ?...` is translated directly as **`Is he not a Tamil ?...`**. Kannagi's pride and confidence are explicitly tied by Kalaignar to Tamil identity; that line is not softened into merely `Is he not brave?`.
- The stage direction `அழுகையும், புன்னகையும் போட்டியிட` becomes `As tears and smiles compete with one another`, retaining Kalaignar's personified emotional contrast.
- Devandhi's teasing about Madhavi remains teasing; it is not rewritten as hostility.
- Kannagi's `என் தங்கை பாக்கியசாலி` is preserved as **`My younger sister is fortunate`**. `தங்கை` is important: Kannagi again names Madhavi through sisterhood rather than rivalry.
- Scan 52 contains no literary text, so no English text is invented for it.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The scene contains an unusually compact but explicit Tamil-identity assertion: Kannagi responds to news of Kovalan's feat with `Is he not a Tamil?` Kalaignar thus connects courage and public reputation to collective Tamil identity in the character's own words. The translation preserves that line directly and does not expand it into later nationalist or party vocabulary.

The other important continuity is Kannagi's response to Madhavi. Devandhi teasingly points out that Madhavi can tend Kovalan if he is hurt; Kannagi answers not with jealousy but `My younger sister is fortunate`. That repeated refusal to turn the two women into simple rivals continues Kalaignar's dignity-centred portrayal across scenes 11–14. It can resonate with a Self-Respect/Dravidian emphasis on human dignity, but the scene itself remains brief, affectionate and emotional rather than doctrinal.
