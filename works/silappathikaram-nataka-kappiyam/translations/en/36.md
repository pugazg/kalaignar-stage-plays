# Scene 36

## The Sacred City in Fire

### Streets

[The goldsmith's house is burning! The goldsmith stands in the street like a tree.]

Chief Goldsmith : Within me, Pandya justice fell that very day! And so I myself become prey to the fire!

[So saying, he enters the burning house and becomes embers!]

★

[One vast forest of fire! The palace is destroyed by the great blaze! The official who stole the anklet is caught in the ruins of the palace and perishes!]

[Kannagi—with a furious voice and a storm-stride!]

Kannagi : Let it spread... let the fire spread! Let Madurai burn as my mind burns! O fire that blazed up within my heart! Destroy Madurai! Let Madurai, which took my husband as its sacrifice, perish! Perish!! People of Koodal city, who lay with your mouths shut when Kovalan was killed! Now cry out! Seeing destruction, scream! Wail! Scream! O wind, blow! O flame, rise....! O destruction, set out! Take vengeance for vengeance! Vengeance for vengeance! Vengeance for vengeance!

[Kannagi's great laughter amid the fire]

[Madurai disappears in the flames]

[The people themselves wished for the destruction of the Pandya realm that had strayed from justice.]

## Translation notes

- `தீயினில் திருநகர்` is rendered **`The Sacred City in Fire`**. `திருநகர்` retains the elevated city-register rather than being reduced to merely `the city`.
- The goldsmith's `என்னுள் அன்றே பாண்டியனின் நீதி கவிழ்ந்தது!` is kept as **`Within me, Pandya justice fell that very day!`**. His speech identifies his own earlier action with the collapse of Pandya justice; the English does not shift responsibility to fate.
- `தணலாகிறார்!` remains the stark stage image **`becomes embers!`** rather than being softened to `dies in the fire`.
- The source-printed **`★`** separator is retained between the goldsmith's death and the palace-fire sequence.
- The official is identified as the anklet-thief only because Kalaignar's stage direction itself says so. No additional punishment sequence is invented.
- Kannagi's repeated imperatives—`பரவட்டும்`, `அழியட்டும்`, `கூவுங்கள்`, `அலறுங்கள்`, `கதறுங்கள்`, `காற்றே வீசு`, `கனலே கிளம்பு`, `அழிவே புறப்படு`—remain an accelerating chain of commands rather than being summarized as a curse.
- `கூடல் நகரத்தாரே` is rendered **`People of Koodal city`**, preserving Madurai's source-specific name in the address.
- `பழிக்குப் பழி` remains **`vengeance for vengeance`**, continuing scene 34's retaliatory wording and keeping it attributable to Kannagi's character speech.
- The final source form `விரும்பினர்கள்` is unusual as printed. The English conveys the secure immediate meaning—**the people themselves wished for the destruction of the justice-failing Pandya realm**—without changing the protected Tamil reading.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 36 makes responsibility explicitly human. The goldsmith says Pandya justice had already collapsed **within him**, and he walks into the fire; the anklet-thief official dies in the ruins of the palace; Kannagi directly condemns the people of Koodal for having remained silent when Kovalan was killed. The chain of catastrophe is therefore tied to wrongdoing, institutional failure and public silence rather than narrated as unavoidable fate.

That focus can resonate with Dravidian-movement concerns with accountable authority, justice and resistance to passive submission. But the scene also contains Kannagi's furious retaliatory destruction. The translation does not recast that vengeance as a movement programme or soften the source's violent commands into a general statement about reform.

The closing stage statement is especially severe: the source says the people themselves wished to see the justice-failing Pandya realm destroyed. That political judgment is preserved as Kalaignar's stage text, not expanded into a modern theory of popular sovereignty.
