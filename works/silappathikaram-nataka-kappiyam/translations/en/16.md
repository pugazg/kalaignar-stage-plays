# Scene 16

## The Birth of Manimekalai

### Madhavi's House

[A `nanniraattu` celebration for the child is taking place at Madhavi's house... with great rejoicing...

The child is placed in the cradle, and Madhavi and her companions sing.

In the midst of the song... Kovalan arrives... Madhavi lifts the child and gives her to him. He kisses the child.]

Madhavi : Give the child a name!

Kovalan : A name?...

[Looking at the child]

O fine blend of milk, clear honey, sweet syrup and lentils, all four mingled together! O delightful breeze rising from the Pothigai Hills, sweeping along the pollen of sandalwood blossoms! Your little laugh outdoes the kuzhal and the yazh—outdoes even the `kurali`! O tender shoot of Kovalan's lineage; you are a jewel to womankind! Every movement of your feet, every movement of your hands is poetry! All is mekalai!... Mekalai!... the higher art!—and so you are Manimekalai!... Long live Manimekalai!!....

[Having given her the name Manimekalai, everyone sings blessings.

Kovalan, Madhavi, Vasanthamalai and all the others enthusiastically perform acts of charity and giving! Celebration everywhere! Koothu! Song..]

[In the street, an elephant in musth seizes a man of sacred lore...

People run in every direction!

Utter commotion! Kovalan runs forward and, with great difficulty, subdues the elephant and triumphs. Everyone garlands Kovalan, celebrates him and sings his praise.....

Madhavi runs forward and embraces him.]

## Translation notes

- `மணிமேகலை பிறப்பு` is rendered as `The Birth of Manimekalai`.
- `நன்னிராட்டு விழா` is retained as a **`nanniraattu` celebration** rather than being forced into a more specific ritual label that the scene itself does not define. The immediate action—cradle, song, naming and blessing—is translated directly.
- Kovalan's naming speech is kept as one exuberant accumulation. Milk, clear honey, sweet syrup, lentils, Pothigai breeze, sandalwood pollen, musical sound, lineage, poetry and art all build toward the child's name.
- `பாகு` is rendered here as `sweet syrup`, preserving the food-image without over-specifying its preparation.
- `குழலி / யாழ் / குறளி` are treated as named sound/music terms. `kuzhal` and `yazh` are retained; the verified `குறளி` is retained as **`kurali`** because the scene does not securely establish a one-to-one English equivalent.
- The naming wordplay `மேகலை!...மேகலை!...மேலானகலை!` is preserved through `mekalai / the higher art`, leading directly to `Manimekalai`. The English does not invent a different pun.
- `குலக் கொடியே` is rendered as `tender shoot of Kovalan's lineage`, keeping the family-line image rather than reducing it to `daughter`.
- `தான தருமங்கள்` becomes `acts of charity and giving`; the festive public generosity remains part of the scene's action.
- `மதங்கொண்ட யானை` is rendered as `an elephant in musth`, preserving the source's dangerous state.
- The source calls the person seized by the elephant `மறையவன்`. Following the permanent terminology rule, this is rendered **`a man of sacred lore`**, not automatically `Brahmin`.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 16 is celebratory rather than polemical, so its movement-context relation remains restrained. Kalaignar makes the child's naming itself an act of language and art: bodily movement becomes `poetry`, sound becomes music, and `மேகலை / மேலான கலை` is turned into the name `Manimekalai`. Cultural identity is produced through Tamil literary play rather than explanatory narration.

The scene also moves from domestic celebration to public action—charity, communal festivity and Kovalan's rescue of a stranger. The man rescued is identified by the source as `மறையவன்`; the translation preserves that term's distinction rather than flattening it into a caste label. The strongest movement-era relevance here is therefore Tamil literary-cultural naming and continued fidelity to Kalaignar's own social vocabulary.
