# Scene 1

## Drum Proclamation in Vanchi, the Ancient City

[An announcement for the birthday celebration of Prince Senguttuvan in Vanchi, the ancient capital city of the Chera land!.... A drum-announcer is seated atop an elephant. The long cloth adorning the elephant bears the bow emblem. The finely worked cloth hanging over its face too carries the bow emblem. As the elephant comes swaying through the streets of the Vanchi capital, it stirs a new awakening. The drum-announcer proclaims the message with exultation!]

Drum-announcer : Long live Tamil! May Tamilakam triumph! So that Tamilakam, girdled by the sounding sea, may shine, the great warrior and leader of the Cheras, Nedun Cheralathan, went to the Himalaya and planted the bow-banner. Today, throughout the Chera realm, let us celebrate in splendour the birthday of his beloved son, the mighty warrior Senguttuvan. Let us beat, let us beat the drum! Long live Prince Senguttuvan! Long live the Chera land! Long live the homeland of the people of refined Tamil!

[The drum resounds]

## Translation notes

- `முரசறைவோன்` is rendered as `drum-announcer` rather than merely `drummer`, because his dramatic function is public proclamation through the ceremonial drum.
- `தமிழகம்` is retained as `Tamilakam`; it is not modernized to `Tamil Nadu`.
- `இமிழ் கடல் வேலித் தமிழகம்` is rendered as `Tamilakam, girdled by the sounding sea` to retain the elevated geographic image rather than flattening it to `surrounded by the sea`.
- `விற்கொடி` is `bow-banner`, preserving the Chera bow emblem.
- The inclusive repetition `கொட்டுவோம், கொட்டுவோம் முரசம்!` is retained as `Let us beat, let us beat the drum!`; the repetition is central to the proclamation's mobilizing cadence.
- `செந்தமிழர் தாயகம்` is rendered here as `the homeland of the people of refined Tamil`. This intentionally retains both the elevation of `செந்தமிழ்` and the emotional force of `தாயகம்`; the terminology register keeps this choice open for consistency review as later scenes provide more evidence.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this pilot.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Kalaignar frames the ancient Chera announcement in the cadence of public political oratory: `வாழ்க தமிழ்! வெல்க தமிழகம்!`, the repeated drum-call, `புதிய எழுச்சி`, and the final invocation of `தாயகம்` move the scene beyond a neutral historical announcement into a collective language of Tamil identity and mobilization.

That rhetoric strongly resonates with later Dravidian movement idioms of **Tamil linguistic-cultural self-respect, Tamilakam as a civilizational geography, historical recovery of Tamil political traditions, and mass public awakening**. The connection is especially visible in the sequence from `Tamil` → `Tamilakam` → Chera historical memory → `homeland`.

The translation therefore preserves `Tamilakam`, `homeland`, the bow-banner, and the repeated collective drum-call instead of replacing them with smoother generic English. At the same time, the dialogue does **not** insert later movement terms such as `self-respect`, `social justice`, `rationalism`, or `Dravidian` because those words are not present in this scene.
