# Scene 8

## Kannagi's Married Life

### Kovalan's House:—Bedchamber

[A beautiful bed. Jasmine flowers are spread upon it. Through the lattice—the moon! Stars around the moon! Kovalan and Kannagi in the moonlight]

Kannagi : My dear! At first I was truly frightened. That incident felt as though fire had struck my heart.

Kovalan : There was no other way to free Madhavi from that crisis.

Kannagi : Even in my fear, deep within my heart, trust lay heaped like pearls in the sea, my dear!

Kovalan : Women are creatures of suspicion. Is my Kannagi alone an exception?

Kannagi : Oh, go on, my dear; I will never suspect you. You belong to me alone!

Kovalan : Yes, Kannagi! Without you there is no sweetness in my life!

Kannagi : I trembled that Madhavi, coming between us, might snatch away my peerless treasure of happiness. I feared that “the marriage solemnized while venerable ritual officiants showed the way of the sacred lore and we circled the fire” might lose its power!...

Kovalan : Kannagi! On that day, beneath the blue canopy and the pearl-blossom pavilion, when you and I joined hands, a resolve rose in my heart. I will never let that resolve weaken.

Kannagi : It is true, is it not, my dear! You will not forget me, will you?...

Kovalan : Not merely will I not forget you—O stainless gold! O right-spiralled pearl! O flawless fragrance! O sugarcane! O honey! O rare maiden! O medicine of my very life!... I shall go on describing you so. You are a gem not born among mountains! Nectar not born among waves! Music not born from the yazh!....

Kannagi : My dear!...

Kovalan : Kannagi!...

[Just then a voice calls “Kannagi” from the corridor. Kannagi leaves Kovalan and goes outside.]

[Kannagi comes into the corridor. There Maanaaykan and his wife stand with sorrowful faces.]

Mother : Kannagi!

[She runs forward and embraces her]

Kannagi, my child! I never even dreamed that the writing on your head—your fate—would turn out like this!...

[With sobbing]

Kannagi : Mother!!!

Maanaaykan : Kannagi, do not worry! Everything will end well! Until yesterday I believed that you and your husband would live your married life never parting from one another... I never imagined a storm like this would rise in between, my child!...

[With still greater sobbing]

Kannagi : Father!

[Meanwhile Maasathuvan and his wife arrive hurriedly]

[Angrily]

Maasathuvan : Kannagi! Where is Kovalan?

Kannagi : Uncle!...

Maanaaykan : Maasathuvan! Have you seen what your son has done! My daughter has become the very form of suffering!

Maasathuvan : Maanaaykar! Do not worry! I shall teach him a lesson...... Kannagi! Do not be afraid! Live on as this Maasathuvan's daughter-in-law; I will not let your heart be troubled!...

Kannagi : Uncle! Listen to me!.... Do not think wrongly of him; he is my god!

Maasathuvan : A god to you; a son to me!... After bringing disgrace upon my very lineage, I will never agree to his marrying that courtesan Madhavi.

Kannagi : He is not going to marry her at all.

Maasathuvan : Not going to marry her? If I give him the means to take you, fruit of chastity, as his wife and live a life of his own, he clings to another side like a disease! Good! Good! I shall put a full stop to this ruinous business right now.

Kannagi : Aunt!... Uncle! Without knowing the truth......

Aunt : The truth is as plain as a gooseberry in the palm, Kannagi! Do not worry, my child! Your uncle will somehow bring Kovalan back to you!

Mother : Look! My girl's whole face is swollen from crying and crying! Look how red both her eyes have become! Kannagi; did I perform penance to bear you only to see you in this state?....

[She weeps!]

Kannagi : Mother!...

Maasathuvan : Hmm! I am going now; I will say a word or two to him and come back...

[Coming from inside, laughing]

Kovalan : Father! Where are you going, Father?

Maasathuvan : Who! You?... Kovalan! You are here?

Kovalan : Why—where else should I be?

[To Kannagi]

Maasathuvan : What is this, my child; then was everything I heard a lie?

Kovalan : What you heard was true—up to the point where I bought the garland for one thousand and eight kalanju of gold! The episode of rescuing the parrot from the old man—that is all! If you heard anything beyond that, it is the work of tale-bearing owls!...

Mother : Kannagi!

Kannagi : All of you surrounded me and would not let me speak; I was trying to tell you!...

Maanaaykan : Is that so....

Kovalan : The married life of two lovers, united in thought and bound in mutual support, is going on sweetly, Uncle! To the very end, it is my duty to see that Kannagi's eyes do not fill with tears!...

Maasathuvan : We were too hasty, Kovalan!... The whole world will praise me and say, “What merit must his father have earned to gain such a good son!” That is enough for me, my son!

[He embraces Kovalan]

Kannagi's Mother : Kannagi! Who but you, my child, could have the good fortune to become daughter-in-law to such a fine family?

[Tears of joy in Kannagi's eyes]

## Translation notes

- `கண்ணகி இல்லறம்` is rendered as `Kannagi's Married Life`. `இல்லறம்` carries the ethical/domestic sense of life as a married household; the English title keeps the scene readable without reducing it to mere `housekeeping` or making it a technical religious category.
- Kannagi's `அத்தான்` is rendered contextually as `my dear`, preserving affectionate spousal address without forcing a literal kinship label into English.
- The quoted marriage line `மாமுது பார்ப்பார் மறைவழி காட்டிடத் தீவலம் செய்து நிகழ்த்திய திருமணம்` receives an occurrence-level terminology decision. `பார்ப்பார்` is **not** translated as `Brahmin`. In this immediate line the word is defined by ritual function: the figures guide the `மறைவழி` and the fire-circling marriage rite. It is therefore rendered `venerable ritual officiants`, while `மறை` is kept as `sacred lore`. This preserves the source's ritual setting without imposing a broader caste identity that the line itself does not require.
- `தீவலம்` is translated as `circled the fire`, preserving the action rather than replacing it with a generic `wedding ceremony`.
- Kovalan's praise sequence retains its accumulation and vocatives: `stainless gold`, `right-spiralled pearl`, `flawless fragrance`, `sugarcane`, `honey`, `rare maiden`, `medicine of my very life`. The following `gem / nectar / music` triad likewise keeps the repeated `not born from...` structure.
- `உன் தல எழுத்து` is rendered as `the writing on your head—your fate`, keeping the source image while making the idiom intelligible in English.
- `கற்புக் கனி` remains `fruit of chastity`, preserving Maasathuvan's elevated and conventional praise rather than silently modernizing the phrase.
- `கோள் சொல்லும் கோட்டான்கள்` is rendered as `tale-bearing owls`, retaining the comic animal image and gossiping force.
- `காதலர் இருவர் கருத்தொருமித்து ஆதரவுபட்ட இல்லறம்` becomes `the married life of two lovers, united in thought and bound in mutual support`, preserving the source's explicit emphasis on concord and support within married life.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 8 requires a more restrained movement-context reading than scenes 3–4. Kannagi explicitly invokes the fire-circling marriage rite as a source of emotional security; Kalaignar does **not** turn the ritual itself into a polemical target here. For that reason the translation does not retrofit an anti-ritual or anti-Brahmin argument onto `பார்ப்பார்`. The term is translated by its immediate ritual function rather than by an assumed caste label.

A more defensible resonance appears later in Kovalan's description of their `இல்லறம்`: it is the life of **two lovers united in thought and mutual support**, and he accepts a continuing duty not to let Kannagi's eyes fill with tears. That companionate emphasis—love, mental accord, mutual support and responsibility—can resonate with the Dravidian/Self-Respect movement's later arguments for dignity and companionship within marriage, but the scene itself remains within its own literary and familial world.

Kannagi also speaks forcefully throughout the misunderstanding: she defends Kovalan against both families, insists that they do not know the truth, and is not written as a silent object of family decision. That agency is worth preserving, but the translation does not convert it into an explicit later feminist programme.

Thus the movement relation in this scene is one of **companionate dignity and interpretive caution**, not a claim that Kalaignar is making an anti-ritual speech in Kannagi's mouth.
