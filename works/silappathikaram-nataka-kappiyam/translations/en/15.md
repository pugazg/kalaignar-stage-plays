# Scene 15

## Kovalan, His Heart Changed

### Kannagi's House

[Kannagi, without jewels or adornment of any kind, is singing a song of sorrow.

The house no longer has the splendour it once had. Her memory moves through many parts of the house.... In that memory, old scenes of Kovalan and herself delighting in one another appear before her eyes.

She embraces the cow's face and sheds tears. The cow too sheds tears.

She comes and sits upon the bed. Tear-drops fall from her eyes, drop after drop, upon the bed...

She remembers the sight of jasmine flowers once strewn there.

The standing lamp. Oil keeps dripping from its unlit wick—as though the lamp too were shedding tears just as she is!...

She comes toward the entrance, but remains within the inner hall!....

Kovalan is coming along the street, toward the house! A rooster comes near the door and tries to enter.

Kannagi drives it away and shuts the door with a `THADAAL`.

Only then does Kovalan, who has just stepped upon the threshold, start in shock... lower his head... and turn away.

Kannagi, after shutting the door, comes near the sacred portrait of Kovalan hanging in the hall...]

Kannagi : My dear!... Will you never come?... Have you forgotten me completely, my lord! Have you turned the great breath of a volcano upon me?...

Was my heart the only thing you found on which to test the sharpness of your spear! My dear! Why am I barred from burying my face upon your pearl-garlanded chest; from resting my head upon your broad shoulder and drinking the taste of Tamil! My dear!.... Kith and parents are breaking their bonds; in truth, your companion lies awake and laments. Come just once, my dear...! Show me your radiant face, my dear...! My dear! Will you not come? Will you not show mercy to this poor woman? My dear!....

## Translation notes

- `மனமாறிந்த கோவலன்` is rendered conservatively as `Kovalan, His Heart Changed`. The verified Tamil title itself is unusual; it is not silently normalized to another Tamil form or expanded into a stronger term such as `repentant`.
- The opening is kept as one continuous visual-memory montage, following the source's long bracketed stage direction rather than breaking it into editorial scene cuts.
- `நகை நட்டு எதுமின்றி` is translated by its immediate visual sense as `without jewels or adornment of any kind`; the protected Tamil wording is not altered.
- The cow, bed, jasmine and unlit standing lamp all participate in Kannagi's grief. The lamp's dripping oil remains a tear-image rather than being reduced to neutral set description.
- Source `‘தடால்’` is retained theatrically as `THADAAL`.
- `ஈட்டியிலே கூர்பார்க்க என் இதயந்தான் கிடைத்ததோ!` becomes `Was my heart the only thing you found on which to test the sharpness of your spear!`, preserving the violent rhetorical question.
- `முத்தார மார்பு`, `தடந்தோள்`, and `தமிழ்ச் சுவை` remain cumulative bodily/literary imagery. `தமிழ்ச் சுவையைப் பருகுதற்கு` is rendered `drinking the taste of Tamil`; the explicit Tamil-language image is not replaced by generic `sweet words`.
- Repeated `அத்தான்` appeals remain repeated rather than compressed.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 15 is fundamentally a scene of private grief and memory. Its strongest movement-era resonance lies not in an overt political claim but in Kalaignar's fusion of intimate emotion with Tamil itself: Kannagi imagines resting on Kovalan's shoulder and `drinking the taste of Tamil`. Tamil is made part of love, bodily memory and emotional life.

The scene also refuses to make Kannagi's suffering abstract. The diminished household, the unlit lamp, the cow, the empty bed and her sleepless appeal make material surroundings carry human feeling. That human-centred register is preserved without inserting later rationalist or movement vocabulary into her speech.
