## 2. Kalaignar-language review

PASS.

The translation preserves the scene's tonal sequence rather than converting it into flat courtly prose:

1. martial anticipation;
2. erotic wordplay;
3. domestic teasing about childlessness;
4. sudden rationalist scepticism from Ilango;
5. sibling / in-law banter;
6. a wordless affectionate closing gesture from Venmal.

The English remains speakable on stage. The short vocatives—`Venmal!`, `Ilango?`, `Sister-in-law!`, `brother!`—retain the quick dramatic rhythm.

### `விழாக்கோலம்` / `போர்க் கோலம்`

Translation: `festive array` / `battle array`.

Review: **accepted**. Repeating `array` preserves Kalaignar's compact antithesis. Translating the two phrases with unrelated wording would weaken the rhetorical mirror.

### `விழுப்புண்`

Translation: `wounds of valour`.

Review: **accepted**. This retains the honour-bearing heroic sense better than neutral `injuries` or `wounds`.

### `என் உதட்டிலே வைத்திருக்கிறேன் மருந்து!`

Translation: `I keep the medicine upon my lips!`

Review: **accepted**. The translation does not explain the kiss implied by the line; Kalaignar's flirtatious metaphor is allowed to land dramatically on its own.

### Childlessness/fatherhood exchange

The Tamil moves from Senguttuvan's joke that the birthday celebration seems to come only to them to Venmal's retort about their having no child and when he will become a father.

The English preserves the joke without supplying medical, dynastic or emotional explanation absent from the source.

### `குறும்புக்காரா!`

Translation: `You little rogue!`

Review: **accepted** as affectionate teasing in this family context. A harsher `rascal` or literal explanatory phrase would distort the emotional register.

## 3. Rationalism / Dravidian-context review

PASS WITH SOURCE/INTERPRETATION SEPARATION.

The strongest conceptual signal is explicit in Ilango's Tamil:

`ஆருடமா? நிமித்திகனிடமா? அவன் பொய்யுரைகளே நீங்களும் நம்புகிறீர்களா அண்ணி?`

The English renders this as:

`A prophecy? From the soothsayer? Sister-in-law, do you too believe his lies?`

This retains the mocking interrogative rhythm and the direct dismissal contained in `பொய்யுரைகள்`. It therefore preserves, rather than explains away, the rationalist force already present in Kalaignar's dialogue.

The separate interpretive note relates this to the rationalist / anti-superstition current of the Dravidian movement. That relation is defensible because the source itself dramatizes scepticism toward claimed knowledge of fate.

No later ideological vocabulary is inserted into dialogue. Terms such as `rationalism`, `anti-superstition`, `self-respect` and `Dravidian` occur only in the labelled interpretive note.

The note also records a second, more limited feature: Venmal is written as an active, witty participant in intimate dialogue rather than a silent courtly consort. The translation preserves that agency through her verbal timing and does not turn it into an explicit ideological claim.
