# Closing Tableau

## In Ancient Vanchi

### Festival of Installing Kannagi's Statue

*     *     *

[⟦later library stamp obscures leading letters⟧ங்குட்டுவன், Kayavaku of Lanka and other kings stand there,

and ⟦later library stamp obscures leading letters⟧ங்கோவடிகள், Sathanar and other poets stand there.

Madhavi, in the garb of renunciation, tears flowing from her eyes, brings the child Manimekalai and places her at the feet of the statue.]

## Translation notes

- This is a separate unnumbered closing tableau after Scene 38 and the three printed `*` marks. It is not treated as an invented Scene 39.
- `வஞ்சிமூதூரில்` is rendered **`In Ancient Vanchi`**, retaining the established `Vanchi` place-name and the source's `மூதூர்` sense of the old/ancient city.
- The first two tableau lines are physically damaged by a later library/accession stamp. Their leading characters are not securely recoverable from the controlling scan.
- The English therefore preserves the exact archival obstruction markers together with the source-visible Tamil suffixes `ங்குட்டுவன்` and `ங்கோவடிகள்`. It does **not** reconstruct complete names from context, memory, another edition or the published English translation.
- `இலங்கைக் கயவாகு` is rendered **`Kayavaku of Lanka`** without external historical harmonization of the name.
- `சாத்தனார்` remains **`Sathanar`** following the work's established personal-name treatment.
- `துறவுக் கோலத்தில்` is rendered **`in the garb of renunciation`**. It describes Madhavi's appearance here and does not add an unprinted formal initiation or doctrinal explanation.
- The final action remains concrete and source-timed: Madhavi, weeping, brings the child Manimekalai and places her at the statue's feet.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this tableau.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The play closes not with another royal victory speech but with a memorial act centred on Kannagi. Kings and poets stand together while Madhavi, now in the garb of renunciation, brings the child Manimekalai to the feet of Kannagi's statue. Within Kalaignar's dramatic architecture, the tableau gathers political authority, literary memory, women's lives and Tamil cultural remembrance around Kannagi's public commemoration.

That arrangement can resonate with later Dravidian-movement emphases on Tamil historical/literary memory and the public recovery of exemplary figures, while the scene itself remains a brief theatrical tableau. No later movement terminology is inserted into the translated stage direction.
