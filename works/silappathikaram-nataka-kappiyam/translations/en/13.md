# Scene 13

## Kannagi in a Storm of Sorrow

### Kannagi's House

[Kannagi is seated with tears in her eyes. Then Maasathuvan and his wife arrive....]

Maasathuvan : He will not come, my child... he will not come!......

Kannagi : Uncle!......

[She rises, wiping her tears]

Maasathuvan : That deceiver will not come, Kannagi! Kovalan has abandoned the fish caught in the net!... And you—you are like a fish cast upon the ground!....

Kannagi : No, Uncle! Nothing has happened as you imagine...... Sit down...... sit down, Aunt!... I shall bring fruit now!..........

[She runs, brings a tray of fruit and places it before them.]

Maasathuvan : My beloved daughter-in-law! I gave birth to a mad son who cannot understand your heart, pure as the thumbai flower! Forgive me, my child!... I shall remove your grief in a single moment—I am going to Madhavi's house this very instant. I shall put in chains that great sinner who was born as my son and drag him back here!...

Kannagi : No, Uncle! He went to Madhavi's house with my consent.... I myself sent him there....... If you go there and behave wildly... humiliation will surround him. I shall become a betrayer of my husband!

Aunt : Kannagi! My gold! Why was a woman like you not born from my own womb!

Kannagi : Aunt!... If I had been born from your womb... would he have become my husband?...

Maasathuvan : I would have made a man greater than he in beauty and character your husband....

Kannagi : Uncle! I cannot bear to hear you speak ill of him!...... If anyone spoke slightingly of you, would Aunt remain silent?......

Maasathuvan : Your aunt? Your aunt? If, like Kovalan, I had gone in search of a Madhavi, by now your aunt would have begun a great war of Ramayana and Bharata proportions! You alone sit here like a voiceless little creature!......

Kannagi : None of you need worry!... He will return soon.... His love for me is not lightning that flashes in the high sky! It is a blaze of light that crosses even the farthest horizon! He is mine forever!

Maasathuvan : Very well, Kannagi!... Until he reforms... until he returns, why remain alone? Come to our house!....

Kannagi : Uncle, you arranged for us to live as a separate household, according to Tamil custom!.... Only if I fail in that life should I leave this house! Even if he leaves me standing in fire and goes away, I shall remain standing there until he returns....

Maasathuvan : Daughter of Maanaaykan!... I honour your strength of mind. You are a living testimony to the greatness of the Tamil people! I bless you that the incurable sorrow settled deep in your heart may soon pass... I take my leave, my child.......

Kannagi : Very well, Uncle!......

Aunt : Kannagi... shall we go then!... Devandhi... take good care of my Kannagi!... If she needs anything, at least you come to me, child!...

[Devandhi nods]

Kannagi, I am going, my child!...

Kannagi : Very well, Aunt! Go without worrying and return safely!......

[They leave. Kannagi, her eyes wet with tears, sends them off with a smiling face....]

[Kannagi is seated. Then Maanaaykan and Kannagi's mother arrive]

Maanaaykan : Kannagi!

Kannagi : Father! Mother! Come!

Maanaaykan : What is this, child... after losing your husband, have you begun losing your wealth too?

Kannagi : Wealth!... Whose earned wealth is it? Is it not all the riches he sought by sailing the wave-filled seas! What is wrong, Father, in spending it for his satisfaction?...

Maanaaykan : Prosperity... is not the water of the deep sea that never diminishes however much you draw from it!... Do not forget, daughter, that it will slowly, slowly melt away!

Kannagi : If wealth diminishes, it can be gathered again later—but if the love my beloved, who captured my thoughts, has for me diminishes... then...

Maanaaykan : Love!... He has love for you!... If he had love, why is he lying at the feet of a courtesan? Has not that great sinner Madhavi become his empress!...

Kannagi : Do not insult Madhavi... She is greater than I am. Not only in beauty; even in love. Separated from him, I remain alive! If she were separated from him, she would not survive even half a moment!... That is why my beloved too has stayed there... It is not that he has no love for me; Kovalan knows well that Kannagi has a stone-like heart strong enough to live apart from her husband, while Madhavi's heart is like a flower that would wither at once, Father!...

Maanaaykan : Ah!... Have I received a mountain of virtue as the tender creeper of my lineage! Kovalan! Could you throw this cool cluster of blossoms into the fire and walk away?...

[Tears flow from Kannagi's eyes]

Kannagi : Do not worry, Father!

Mother : My gold! We came to console you, and are you consoling us? Leaving you to suffer like a painted doll, that sinner.......

[Angrily]

Kannagi : Mother! Speak with restraint. If all of you are going to curse him and scold him like this, from now on none of you come here.... None of you come here!... None of you come here!...

[She sobs and sobs.]

## Translation notes

- `துன்பப் புயலில் கண்ணகி` is rendered as `Kannagi in a Storm of Sorrow`, preserving the deliberate emotional counterpoint to scene 12's `இன்ப வாழ்வில் கோவலன்`.
- Maasathuvan's fish imagery is retained in full: Kovalan has left `the fish caught in the net`, while Kannagi is compared to a fish fallen upon the ground. The harsh asymmetry is not softened.
- `பதித்துரோகி` is rendered contextually as `a betrayer of my husband`. The line is Kannagi's own fear that humiliating Kovalan after she sent him to Madhavi would make her disloyal to him; the translation does not expand it into a doctrinal label.
- The Ramayana/Bharata joke is kept as a `great war of Ramayana and Bharata proportions`, preserving Maasathuvan's comic exaggeration.
- `தமிழர் வழக்கப்படி` becomes `according to Tamil custom`. The scene explicitly locates the couple's separate household within Tamil custom; this is not modernized into a contemporary legal or social category.
- `தமிழ்க் குலத்தின் பெருமைக்கு நீயொரு சான்று` is rendered as `You are a living testimony to the greatness of the Tamil people`, retaining Maasathuvan's explicit elevation of Kannagi into a Tamil collective ideal.
- `திரை கடலோடி அவர் தேடிய திரவியம்` retains the maritime image as wealth Kovalan sought by sailing the wave-filled seas.
- Kannagi's defence of Madhavi is preserved without moral correction: `அவள் என்னைவிட உயர்ந்தவள்...அன்பிலுங்கூட` becomes `She is greater than I am... even in love.`
- The repeated final `யாரும் இங்கே வராதீர்கள்` is retained three times. Kalaignar's emotional escalation is not compressed.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 13 contains a striking mixture of inherited marital ideals and distinctly Tamil social self-description. Kannagi says that Maasathuvan arranged for the couple to maintain a separate household `according to Tamil custom`; Maasathuvan then calls her a testimony to the greatness of the Tamil people. Those lines make Tamil social identity explicit and are preserved without importing a modern sociological theory into the dialogue.

The more powerful human-dignity strand lies in Kannagi's repeated refusal to build her own virtue by humiliating either Kovalan or Madhavi. She will not tolerate Kovalan being abused in her presence, and when her father insults Madhavi she answers that Madhavi is greater than she is, even in love. This continues the pattern established in scene 11: women's dignity is not made competitive by Kalaignar's Kannagi.

At the same time, the scene also speaks in the language of chastity, marital endurance and self-sacrifice. The interpretive note therefore does not retrofit the scene into a simple modern emancipation narrative. Its Dravidian-era resonance is better located in Tamil collective self-description, dignity, and Kalaignar's refusal to reduce Madhavi to a degraded rival.
