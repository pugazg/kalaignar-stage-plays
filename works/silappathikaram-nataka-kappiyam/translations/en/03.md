# Scene 3

## Celebration and Questioning

### Chera Palace

[The royal audience hall where the birthday celebration is taking place has been arranged with great splendour. King Nedun Cheralathan and Queen Narchonai are seated upon the throne! A dance by women of a warrior clan bearing bows is in progress. As Senguttuvan and Ilango enter the hall, the dancing maidens receive them and lead them to the royal seats. The two bow to their parents and sit nearby. The dance concludes. Nedun Cheralathan rises to deliver his address of blessing.]

Nedun Cheralathan : Great ones of this assembly! Today my heart leaps again and again when I think that I have Senguttuvan as my son—one endowed with the strength to increase the prosperity and fame of the good Chera land! As the old saying goes, “He who has a younger brother fears no army”; with Ilango as his younger brother, I bless my precious son on this auspicious day! I proclaim that very soon Senguttuvan shall wear the crown, bear the sceptre, and nurture Tamil aram... and that the day is near...

Assembly : Long live Senguttuvan! Senguttuvan, long live!!

[The soothsayer rises with a mischievous smile]

Soothsayer : Long live Senguttuvan! Long live Ilango, the future king!!

Cheralathan : What? Soothsayer! What are you saying?...

Soothsayer : I do not say it, Your Majesty! Fate itself says so! Though the right to the crown belongs to the elder son, Senguttuvan... the story woven by fate is otherwise! It is the younger one, Ilango, who will rule the Chera realm...

Cheralathan : [In astonishment] What?...

Assembly : While the elder lives, the crown to the younger?

[Senguttuvan's face changes. Ilango does not fail to notice it.]

Ilango : No! No!! My elder brother was born to rule!

Cheralathan : Ilango! The soothsayer's word will not fail!

Ilango : To ears made for falsehood, the soothsayer's word may never sound false!... This is an ear for truth!... It will not be shaken by hearing his prophecy!...

Soothsayer : Fate cannot be altered, Ilango!...

Ilango : Why can it not? I shall make true the Kural's thought: “Even fate will turn its back before those who strive without wavering, never sinking!” I shall conquer the fate you speak of with reason!

Soothsayer : Could anyone change the fate by which the elder Rama had to rule the forest and the younger Bharata had to rule the land?

Ilango : Bharata did not rule; Rama did not rule; was it not the sandals that ruled the land for eighteen years! Soothsayer, do not point to old epics and try to establish the rule of fate! Here... I shall create a new epic! I shall defeat your prophecy with intelligence... I shall make my elder brother the rightful king! Accept it, Soothsayer—this is my challenge! Your claim will be dust! Dust!! It will turn to dust—just watch!... I take my leave!...

[Ilango leaves the hall. The assembly is plunged into astonishment. The king and queen look at one another in anguish!]

## Translation notes

- `விழாவும் வினாவும்` is rendered as `Celebration and Questioning`, preserving the scene's movement from ceremonial celebration into an open challenge to prophetic authority.
- `கொலுமண்டபம்` remains `royal audience hall`, following the accepted scene-2 court terminology.
- The proverb `தம்பியுடையான் படைக்கஞ்சான்` is translated directly as `He who has a younger brother fears no army`, retaining its martial compactness rather than explaining the proverb.
- `தமிழ் அறம்` is rendered as `Tamil aram`. `அறம்` carries ethical, moral and civilizational force that is wider than a single English word such as `virtue`; the terminology register keeps `aram` contextual.
- `ஊழ்வினை`, `விதி` and related lines are rendered here primarily as `fate`, while `மதி` is `reason`. The dramatic opposition is therefore preserved without importing a later philosophical vocabulary into the dialogue.
- Ilango's `பொய்க் காது / பொய்க்காது / உண்மைக் காது` passage contains Tamil sound-play between `falsehood`, `ear`, and `will not fail/lie`. English cannot reproduce the full pun. The translation retains the repeated `ear / false / truth` structure and records the loss rather than replacing it with unrelated wit.
- The quoted Kural is translated independently from the source passage as `Even fate will turn its back before those who strive without wavering, never sinking!`; no standard published English Kural version was imported.
- The source explicitly says `பதினெட்டு வருடம்`; the translation therefore retains `eighteen years` without correcting it from another Ramayana tradition.
- `இதோ...நான் படைக்கிறேன் புதிய இதிகாசம்!` remains the emphatic `Here... I shall create a new epic!`, preserving Kalaignar's dramatic assertion of human creative agency against inherited fatalist precedent.
- `தூள்! தூள்!! தூளாகப் போகிறது` remains `dust! Dust!! ... turn to dust`, retaining repetition and escalation.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 3 makes the rationalist-human-agency strand far more explicit than scene 2. Kalaignar sets the soothsayer's claim that fate cannot be altered against Ilango's appeal to a Kural, his declaration that he will defeat fate with `மதி`—reason—and his refusal to treat an old epic precedent as binding proof of destiny.

The sequence is especially significant: **prophecy → challenge → Tamil ethical text → reason → criticism of inherited epic precedent → creation of a “new epic.”** This strongly resonates with the Dravidian movement's rationalist critique of fatalism, its insistence on human agency, and its use of Tamil literary traditions as intellectual resources rather than as objects of passive reverence.

The translation therefore preserves the sharp words `fate`, `reason`, `old epics`, `new epic`, and the repeated challenge. It does not insert terms such as `rationalism`, `Dravidian`, `Self-Respect Movement`, or `anti-Brahminism` into Ilango's speech, because those terms are not present in this scene. The movement relation remains interpretive and explicitly separate from the translated dialogue.
