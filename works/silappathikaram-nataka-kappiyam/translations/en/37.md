# Scene 37

## Campaign into the Northern Land

### Court of Cheran Senguttuvan

Cheran : O tiger-warriors who live in Tamilakam bearing the sword and bearing fame! The call has come for all of you! Once again, a fine opportunity to establish our valour! They say some small-minded fellows have mocked our emblems engraved upon the Himalaya—the wretch who has never seen a battlefront mocked the tiger; the useless fellow who asks the price of valour mocked the bow; the dullard who has never heard the tales of entering the field mocked the fish. Utthiram—Visithiram—Sivetham—Bairavam; do not start in surprise! These are only the names of men—Kanakan and Vijayan are said to be the leaders of these frog-kings! They say the rulers who live on the banks of the Ganga have thrown a challenge to the banks of the Kaveri! Comrades! Let us teach them sense—set out!

Priest : Why this anger in you alone, O King, when neither the Chola nor the Pandya has it?

Cheran : Because I am a Tamil! Because a Tamil's sense of honour has been diminished! The Chola and the Pandya may be my enemies; for that reason I will not let people of another land wag their tails at us, as long as I have life! Is it not my Tamil they insulted—those arrogant men! Ah... O Valluvar, sovereign who gave us the Kural! They insulted you! O Saathanar, who voices weighty thought in the Athaani mandapam, they insulted you! O master of verse! O beloved Paranar who sang the Fifth Ten! O lord of the arena, they insulted you! O sea of pearls, O Mullaik Kolli, they insulted you! O sacred lion-seat that comes with the murasu sounding in all eight directions, they insulted you!.... Mother Narchonai! They insulted the victory of Senguttuvan, son of Imayavaramban Nedun Cheralathan! Peerless poet Ilango! Dear younger brother, they insulted you! O Pandya who gave his life for justice! Nedunchezhiyan who overcame the Aryan army! They insulted you. O lesser realms whose wealth of refined Tamil never diminishes! O lion-bulls! Scholars! Poets! Artists! Men of learning! Those who insulted all of you are still alive—as kings! O beautiful capital Vanchi, surrounded by the Porunai river and groves, they insulted you! They insulted you! They insulted you!! Men of base character insulted you! With Minister Azhumbil Vel here—with warrior Villavan Kothai as commander—with strength waxing like the crescent in every one of your massed shoulders—they say creatures who ought to have been roaming within the inner chambers raised furious cries—Warriors! Let us try our hand against those tongue-unguarded wanderers! Let every warrior in the forts of Thumbivaadi—Thoranangkarpattu—Vettamangalam set out! O bulls! Tell your beloveds, “This may be the last kiss,” and depart! Are we a society afraid to die? Are we a people who fear battle, that we should surrender to those chamara-bearers? No! No! Your heads may be severed upon the field! But let them be cut away with eyes spitting fire. Your muscles may be torn and flung apart! But let them be torn while the heat of self-respect blood has not cooled! Beat the murasu! Strike the Himalaya! The Tamil has never been defeated, never been defeated! Comrades! Set out!’

## Translation notes

- `வடபுலப் படையெடுப்பு` is rendered **`Campaign into the Northern Land`**, retaining `வடபுலம்` as a directional cultural-political space rather than silently substituting a modern nation-state geography.
- Before translation, scan 87 was re-rendered and visually checked against the verified Tamil record. Direct source pixels proved three prior archival readings wrong: `தீவர்களாம்` → **`தலைவர்களாம்`**, `அன்ன நற்சோணையே` → **`அன்னை நற்சோணையே`**, and `சுடற்ற புலவனே` → **`ஈடற்ற புலவனே`**. `pages/0087.md` and `scenes/37.md` were corrected before the English was finalized. No outside edition was used.
- The tiger / bow / fish sequence retains the three Tamil dynastic emblems as the objects of insult. Kalaignar's insult-chain—battlefront wretch, valour-pricing useless man, battle-tale-ignorant dullard—is kept cumulative rather than reduced to `they mocked us`.
- `உத்திரமும் - விசித்திரமும் - சிவேதமும் - பைரவமும்` is retained as the personal-name sequence **`Utthiram—Visithiram—Sivetham—Bairavam`** because the source itself immediately jokes that these are merely human names.
- `மண்டுக மன்னர்கள்` is translated as **`frog-kings`**, preserving Senguttuvan's ridicule; the corrected `தலைவர்களாம்` becomes `are said to be the leaders`.
- `கங்கைக்கரை ... காவிரிக் கரை` remains a **Ganga-bank / Kaveri-bank** rhetorical opposition. The English does not replace it with a generalized `north versus south` summary.
- The source form `தன் மான உணர்ச்சி குன்றுததால்` is preserved by immediate rhetorical sense as **`Because a Tamil's sense of honour has been diminished!`** The Tamil is not silently regularized into a different grammatical form.
- `தமிழகம்` remains **`Tamilakam`**. The line explicitly says `தமிழன்`, so the translation retains **`Tamil`** rather than replacing it with a broader regional label.
- The long invocation of Valluvar, Saathanar, Paranar, Narchonai, Ilango, Nedunchezhiyan, Vanchi and others is preserved as an oratorical roll-call. Source-specific or uncertain epithets such as `முல்லைக் கொல்லி` are kept conservatively (`Mullaik Kolli`) rather than assigned an unsupported modern identification.
- `நீதிக்கு உயிர் தந்த பாண்டியா!` is translated **`O Pandya who gave his life for justice!`**, preserving the immediate continuity with Nedunchezhiyan's death in scene 35.
- `ஆரியப்படை கடந்த` remains **`who overcame the Aryan army`**, following the source's own political-historical framing.
- `நாகாக்கத் தெரியாத` retains the tongue-guard insult as **`tongue-unguarded`**; the English does not import a separate standard Kural quotation where Kalaignar does not quote one here.
- `தும்பிவாடி / தோரணங்கற்பட்டு / வேட்டமங்கலம்` remain the printed campaign-place names; no modern geographic identifications are added.
- `சாமரமேந்திகள்` is retained culturally as **`chamara-bearers`**, because a generic `courtiers` would erase Kalaignar's visual taunt.
- **`சுயமரியாதை` is actually present in the dialogue.** It is therefore translated directly as **`self-respect`** in `the heat of self-respect blood`, not moved into the interpretive note or weakened to `honour`.
- `முரசு தட்டுங்கள், இமயத்தை முட்டுங்கள்` retains its percussive imperative as **`Beat the murasu! Strike the Himalaya!`**; the repeated `தமிழன் தோற்றதில்லை, தோற்றதில்லை!` remains **`The Tamil has never been defeated, never been defeated!`**.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 37 differs from many earlier scenes because one of the movement's central terms is not merely a later resonance: Kalaignar himself places **`சுயமரியாதை` — `self-respect`** inside Senguttuvan's mobilizing speech. The source also repeatedly says `Tamil`, `Tamilakam`, invokes the Kural and major Tamil literary/historical figures, and treats an insult to Chera, Chola or Pandya symbols as an insult to a larger Tamil collective inheritance.

The striking political move comes when the priest asks why Senguttuvan is angry if the Chola and Pandya are not. Senguttuvan answers that the Chola and Pandya may be his enemies, yet he will not permit outsiders to insult `my Tamil`. Internal dynastic rivalry is thus subordinated, in this speech, to a wider Tamil identity and to wounded collective dignity.

That rhetoric strongly resonates with—and in the case of `self-respect`, explicitly uses—the vocabulary later central to the Dravidian/Self-Respect tradition. At the same time, this is a martial mobilization speech full of ridicule, threats and glorification of battlefield death. The interpretive note does not convert that militarism into a general statement of movement doctrine; it records the source's explicit Tamil/self-respect language and leaves the violent campaign rhetoric as Senguttuvan's own dramatic speech.
