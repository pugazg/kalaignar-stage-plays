# Scene 12

## Kovalan in a Life of Delight

### Madhavi's House

[In the bedchamber......

Madhavi is stringing a garland... Kovalan sits nearby, watching only the movement of her fingers......

Madhavi looks at him...]

Madhavi : What is it? Why are you staring without even moving your eyes?

Kovalan : Only at the movement of your fingers!...

I am savouring the wonder of dance displaying its artistic beauty in those kanthal-like fingers.... Madhavi! You were born for the art of dance itself!......

Madhavi : No!... I was born only for you!......

Kovalan : Wrong—wrong... You are not one who was created!... No one could create you!... The great beauty that glides across a body glowing with the colour of gold...... the milk-nectar that seeps from lips showing the hue of the red petals of the `ser-kendai`... the two eyes that seem like black bees moving, imprisoned within the casket of your eyelids—the dark, rain-cloud hair, curled with eye-seizing beauty and reaching down to your feet like a peacock's plume... the wealth of speech that gains its flavour because it is formed upon a tongue floating like a jackfruit segment in a spring of honey—there is no one in this world who could create all this!...... Only after seeing you will the lord of creation now make plans for how beauty is to be fashioned for maidens!...

Madhavi : My lord!... It is easy to take flowers and string a garland! Easier still, it seems, for men to look at a woman and string together a description out of words!....

Kovalan : My dear! When I describe you, is it not the words themselves that gain beauty! Is it not Tamil itself that gains glory!

Madhavi : In the same way, by resting upon your neck, this garland too will gain glory!......

[She places the garland around his neck. Kovalan then removes it and places it around Madhavi's neck. Both are filled with joy.]

## Translation notes

- `இன்ப வாழ்வில் கோவலன்` is rendered as `Kovalan in a Life of Delight`, preserving the deliberate contrast with the following scene's `துன்பப் புயலில் கண்ணகி`.
- The long praise speech is intentionally kept cumulative and excessive. Kalaignar's rhetoric moves image upon image—golden body, lips, bees in the eyelids, rain-cloud hair, peacock plume, honey, jackfruit and finally language itself. It is not shortened into a modern compliment.
- `காந்தள் விரல்கள்` is rendered as `kanthal-like fingers`, retaining the named flower image rather than substituting a different flower.
- The verified source form `சேற் கெண்டை` is difficult to resolve securely from the scene alone. It is therefore retained conservatively inside the image as `ser-kendai` rather than silently corrected or replaced by a guessed botanical/zoological term.
- The source-derived unusual `அசிவதுபோல்` is translated by its immediate movement-image—black bees seeming to move within the eyelid-casket—without altering the protected Tamil.
- `மழைக் கூந்தல்` is kept as `rain-cloud hair`, preserving the source's dark-cloud association.
- `மொழி வளம்` is `wealth of speech`, and Kovalan's climactic `தமிழுக்கு அல்லவா பெருமை உண்டாகிறது!` becomes `Is it not Tamil itself that gains glory!`; the speech explicitly turns erotic praise into praise of Tamil.
- Madhavi's reply keeps Kalaignar's wit: Kovalan `strings` verbal praise just as she strings flowers into a garland.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 12 is primarily erotic and playful, not programmatic. Its strongest movement-era resonance comes at the end of Kovalan's extravagant praise: describing Madhavi does not merely beautify his words; he says it gives **glory to Tamil itself**. Kalaignar thus makes Tamil the medium in which beauty becomes fully articulate.

That fusion of intimate emotion, literary virtuosity and Tamil linguistic pride is characteristic of the cultural politics surrounding Kalaignar's writing. The scene does not make a political argument, so the translation does not add later language-rights or movement vocabulary. It simply preserves the source's own turn from the beauty of Madhavi to the glory of Tamil.
