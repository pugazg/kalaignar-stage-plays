# Scene 9

## The Maiden Who Brought Separation

### Kannagi–Kovalan — Bedchamber

[Kannagi stands with a garland in her hand! A lamp is burning.

Singing as she holds the garland, she places it around Kovalan's neck, bends to touch his feet with her hands and bows.

Kovalan lifts her and makes her stand, and sings in the sense:

“O rain that falls when she says, ‘Rain!’
O straight-bangled woman who rises after worshipping her lord!”

The two lose themselves and reveal their affection for one another.

The song ends...

Kovalan goes apart and sits in sorrow. Kannagi comes near him.]

Kannagi : What is it, my dear... why?

Kovalan : Oodal!...

Kannagi : Oodal?... What does that mean?...

Kovalan : Oh, come now... do you not know what oodal is?... Is it not one of the finest things in the Kural's Book of Love?... A man and a woman grow angry with one another; then they make peace and become happy again.

Kannagi : No, no; why be angry at all, only to make peace later?

Kovalan : It is for the sake of making peace that one quarrels in the first place, Kannagi—that is oodal!...

Kannagi : Then is oodal beginning now?...

Kovalan : This is no oodal, Kannagi! I am going to tell you something you will not like.

Kannagi : What is it, my dear?...

Kovalan : The time is drawing near for me to leave you...

Kannagi : Ah!

Kovalan : Yes; tomorrow morning, a journey to Rome!

Kannagi : Abroad again?. My dear; are you joking?

Kovalan : No, Kannagi; Lord Augustus's palace requires fifty thousand pearls... I must take them there... in person!...

Kannagi : Was I born only to suffer like a catamaran in the waves of the sea? No; I was born with courage enough to stop this journey.

Kovalan : No barrier; no flame in your eyes! If I cross the waters, sell the shining pearls and return, gold coins will heap up like a flood through an opened sluice, and the earth will know the wealth of Tamils. Give me leave, maiden—and with it, give me here the doorway of your lips that offers a feast!

Kannagi : As you load up pearls and cut through the roaring sea, would you think of leaving a standing lamp here to die and wither?... Would you, my dear?...

Kovalan : I would not think so, my dear; only if I forgot you would there be any need to think! Kannagi! We do not keep a thought aside merely for breathing in and breathing out! You are my very breath of life! Why do you think such things? Without you there is no me! Without the moon there is no sky!

Kannagi : What if the stars wage war?

Kovalan : I shall eat no flesh...

Kannagi : Rome, they say, is a honeycomb where beauties live!...

Kovalan : There I shall live like a cripple. I shall sleep thinking of your guava-red lips, remembering that honey from the comb is here in Tamilakam. In the imagination of dreams, I shall feast only upon your beauty.

Kannagi : You will sleep; and so in dreams you will feast upon me. But even the fortune of seeing you in a dream will not be mine, my dear!...

Kovalan : Why, Kannagi?...

Kannagi : Only if I sleep can I dream, can I not? If you are not here, where will sleep come to my eyes?

Kovalan : Do not grieve, Kannagi... give me leave; let me part from you this one time! After this, forever, not even for the space of an atom will I part from you... Come now; show me the pearl of your smile that no shell contains; I shall return singing in my heart the excellence of refined Tamil that lives in my thoughts.

Kannagi : In the pain of separation I shall toss upon the soft bed as though lying upon the thorny skin of a jackfruit, my dear!...

Kovalan : When those who have parted come together again, O refined Tamil, sweetness rises like the fruit-segment inside the jackfruit!...

Kannagi : Whatever you say, you are only trying to take me and cast me upon the fire called separation!

[She weeps]

Do not kill me without killing me, you who dwell in my eyes!...

Kovalan : Do not shed pearls from your eyes, Kannagi! Two eights—sixteen weeks; after that I shall never again go away from you!

Kannagi : Is that truly so, my dear...

Kovalan : I will not part... I will not part... never again will I part...

Kannagi : You will not part?... or is there no love?...

Kovalan : Kannagi! Why such doubt?...

Kannagi : My dear!...

[She sheds tears. He wipes them...]

Kovalan : Kannagi!...

[The sound of knocking at the street door is heard... both listen]

Kovalan : You stay here! I shall go and see.

[Kovalan passes the bedchamber door, crosses the hall and opens the street door. Vasanthamalai stands there and bows.]

Kovalan : Who is it? Have you come again?

Vasanthamalai : Forgive me! Madhavi is ready to surrender her very life!

Kovalan : However you frame it, my answer is the same. I did not buy the garland in order to marry Madhavi.

Vasanthamalai : Then? Did you buy the garland in order to take Madhavi for yourself?

Kovalan : That would have been the mistake! I should have handed her over to that elderly Greek.

Vasanthamalai : Madhavi asks: is there fault in my beauty, fault in my dancing—what fault is there in me that makes you reject me?

Kovalan : Who said such a thing? A man worthy of her beauty and her dancing has not yet been born into this world!

[Kovalan struggles in a painful dilemma.
Kannagi comes there...]

Kannagi : My dear!

Kovalan : They say I must marry Madhavi... a messenger has come; I have told her it is impossible!...

Vasanthamalai : At least come and say that it is impossible to her face.

Kannagi : If you yourself go and explain the situation, will that not bring some peace to her heart?....

Kovalan : Kannagi!...

Kannagi : Come back quickly! Here...

[She brings his upper cloth and gives it to him...

She brings his footwear and puts it on his feet.]

Kovalan : There is no need for me to go at all; your thread is everything...

Kannagi : Madhavi is a good woman! Her heart too should not be hurt, should it?... Tell her, “I myself shall find a good bridegroom and arrange her marriage,” and come back!

Kovalan : All right, Kannagi; shall I go then?

Kannagi : Until you return, I shall stand right here; come back quickly...

Kovalan : All right! I shall return...

Kannagi : I cannot bear even this brief separation. ...How am I going to send you away to Rome tomorrow!...

Kovalan : Shall I go?...

Kannagi : My dear!...

Kovalan : Kannagi!...

[They part]

[Kovalan climbs into an ornate carriage resembling a palanquin, and the carriage departs... Kannagi stands watching...]

## Translation notes

- `ஊடல்` is retained as **`oodal`** on first use because the scene itself immediately defines the classical lovers' quarrel. Replacing it at once with `quarrel` would lose the named love-poetic category that Kalaignar invokes through the Kural.
- The opening two-line song is translated from the verified Tamil wording in this scene; no published Kural or literary translation has been imported.
- `ரோமாபுரி` is rendered as `Rome`; `அகஸ்டஸ் சீமான்` becomes `Lord Augustus`, preserving the source's personal/courtly reference without adding historical claims not present in the scene.
- Kovalan's trade speech retains the political-economic escalation: pearls cross the waters, gold returns like a released flood, and `தமிழர் வளம்` becomes **`the wealth of Tamils`**. The translation does not reduce this to neutral business prose.
- The erotic `உன் இதழ்க்கடையை இங்குக் கொடு` is kept as `give me here the doorway of your lips that offers a feast`, preserving Kalaignar's bold metaphor rather than euphemizing it.
- Kannagi's `குத்து விளக்கு` image is translated as `standing lamp`; the line keeps the contrast between Kovalan's seaborne journey and the wife left burning out at home.
- `விண் மீன்கள்` / `புலால் உண்ணேன்` contains a Tamil joke on `மீன்` (star/fish). English cannot reproduce the full lexical pun, so the apparently abrupt `What if the stars wage war? / I shall eat no flesh...` is retained and the loss is recorded rather than replaced with invented humour.
- `செந்தமிழ்` remains `refined Tamil` in the lovers' metaphors, where Kalaignar repeatedly makes Tamil itself the measure of sweetness, union and memory.
- `கொல்லாமற் கொல்லாதீர்` is kept as `Do not kill me without killing me`, preserving the paradoxical emotional violence of separation.
- `எண்ணிரண்டு பதினாறு கிழமை` is preserved as `Two eights—sixteen weeks`; the source's emphatic arithmetic is not silently simplified.
- `பிரியவில்லை / பிரியமில்லை` is a sound-play between `parting` and `love/affection`. English cannot reproduce the exact phonetic echo; `You will not part?... or is there no love?...` preserves the semantic turn and records the loss.
- The source line `உன் நூல்தான் எல்லாம்...` is unusually opaque in context. It is translated conservatively as `your thread is everything...`, preserving the lexical image rather than silently emending `நூல்` into a different word. This remains a scene-specific ambiguity and is not generalized in the terminology register.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 9 entwines intimate love with recurring Tamil-language imagery. Kovalan asks whether sweetness can be separated from Tamil, compares reunion to `செந்தமிழ்`, promises to return with the excellence of refined Tamil in his thoughts, and speaks of world trade as a means by which the earth will know `தமிழர் வளம்`—the wealth of Tamils. For a modern Kalaignar audience, that repeated joining of love, language, commerce and collective Tamil worth carries a clear resonance with Dravidian-era Tamil linguistic and cultural self-respect.

The scene also presents Kannagi as an active moral agent. She resists Kovalan's proposed departure, questions him sharply, and later is the one who asks him to go to Madhavi so that another woman's heart will not be wounded. Her generosity is not passive submission: she directs the action and insists on humane treatment of Madhavi.

Those resonances are recorded here without inserting movement vocabulary into the lovers' dialogue. The scene remains first of all an emotionally charged separation scene shaped in Kalaignar's poetic Tamil idiom.
