# Published-English Secondary-Witness Comparison — Scenes 19–21

Status: **complete**

Authority for all decisions remains the verified Tamil scene corpus and controlling Tamil scan. The 2009 published English edition is comparison evidence only.

## Scene 19 — `கானல் வரியும் / காதல் பிரிவும்`

Independent translation: `../19.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/19.md`

Published-English witness alignment: mainly physical scans **83–86**, under published section **19 — Melody Breaks**. The opening pre-dawn setup begins in scan 82 as the tail of verified Tamil Scene 18, confirming that section boundaries are not identical.

### Agreement

Both English versions preserve the basic dramatic arc: Madhavi and Kovalan reach the shore, Madhavi plays, Kovalan sings Kaanal Vari, she challenges the gender logic of his song, they quarrel, Kovalan mistakes/imaginatively transforms a fisherwoman, Madhavi retaliates by addressing a warrior statue, and the misunderstanding becomes a complete rupture.

### Significant differences

The independent version preserves far more of Kalaignar's source-specific form and argument:

- the cosmopolitan Puhar opening with foreign trading houses and warehouses;
- `yazh` as the instrument, rather than generic `harp`;
- the `red-kanthal` and `anicham` finger imagery;
- the full repeated Kaanal Vari stanzas rather than a heavily rewritten compact lyric;
- `Ganga` and `Kanni`, followed by Kovalan's own prose explanation of Kanyakumari;
- the repeated `sulk / not sulking` logic central to the chastity argument;
- Madhavi's direct challenge to the unequal rule that the husband may move from Kaveri to Ganga to Kumari while Kaveri must remain passive;
- Madhavi's answering song and its key `righteous sceptre that does not bend` image;
- the fisherwoman sequence, including the source's extravagant and partly opaque imagery;
- Madhavi's commerce/body metaphor addressed to the warrior statue;
- the named performance categories `kaattivari / kankooduvari`;
- Kovalan's full final abusive escalation, including lineage, courtesan status, hereditary trade and reason.

The published witness rewrites the opening song substantially. It combines Ganga and Kumari into a smoother English lyric and reduces the source's repeated rhetorical architecture. It turns the second song into a much shorter paraphrase and simplifies the sceptre logic. In the fisherwoman passage it introduces images such as `teeth brighter than pearl` not corresponding to the verified Tamil's actual sequence.

The published witness also intensifies and modernizes Kovalan's insult vocabulary in places (`strumpet`, `sinful lap`, `seductive smile`, `succulent speech`, `poison to me`) while omitting several of Kalaignar's own specific technical and hereditary-trade expressions. The independent version is harsher where the Tamil is harsh but does not substitute unrelated English abuse.

### Gender / ideological force

The independent translation correctly preserves Madhavi's interrogation of the Kaanal Vari's unequal chastity logic. This is crucial dramatic content, not an interpretive addition. The published witness retains the broad objection but smooths the song structure that gives her objection its force.

### Decision

**`no action`**

No Tamil recheck is required. The independent translation remains materially closer to the verified Tamil in structure, terminology, repetition, literary form and rhetorical escalation.

## Scene 20 — `திருந்திய கோவலன் / திரும்பி வருதல்`

Independent translation: `../20.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/20.md`

Published-English witness alignment: scans **87–89**, section **20 — Penitent Return**.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve Kovalan's return, Kannagi's acceptance, his remorse, her defence of Madhavi and Manimekalai, the collapse of his commercial fortune, refusal to depend on family wealth, Kannagi's offer of her anklets, and the decision to go to Madurai.

### Significant differences

The independent version preserves several source features weakened or removed in the published edition:

- Kannagi's repeated comic-emotional line: `My husband... even my husband must not scold my husband!`;
- `தமிழ் மறை` as **`Tamil sacred lore`**;
- Kannagi's repeated insistence that Madhavi is `my younger sister`;
- the explicit source distinction between Madhavi and Manimekalai, including Kannagi's `Manimekalai is my child too`;
- the maritime collective assertion that **Tamils have no equal in sea-trade**;
- the parallel between `sea-trade` and Kovalan's abusive `trade of the body` formulation;
- `womb of Mother Sea` at Kovalan's suicidal low point;
- `மானம்` as contextual `honour`, not automatically `self-respect`;
- Kannagi's economic agency in offering her own anklets as capital;
- the `not even for the space of an atom` promise.

The published witness generalizes `Tamil sacred lore` into `all that is the noblest and the best in Tamil culture`, which changes a source term into a broad cultural gloss. It similarly changes the maritime line from Tamil collective excellence to a claim that Kovalan personally helped put `Tamilnad in the forefront` of trade.

The published witness also adds interpretive psychological narration around Kannagi's uncertainty, Kovalan's contrition and suicidal thought. Those expansions are not required by the verified Tamil.

At the same time, the published edition softens some of Kovalan's source-specific degrading language about Madhavi into broader rhetoric about `tinsel charms`, `wiles`, and `filth`, while the independent translation preserves the actual accusatory commercial vocabulary and marks it as Kovalan's speech rather than narrator truth.

### Ethical structure

The independent translation better preserves the scene's internal contradiction: Kovalan repents but expresses that repentance through hereditary/social stigma; Kannagi repeatedly refuses that stigma and extends kinship to Madhavi and Manimekalai. That dramatic contradiction is source-supported and should not be flattened into a simple redemption scene.

### Decision

**`no action`**

No Tamil recheck or translation re-review is required.

## Scene 21 — `கலைமகள்`

Independent translation: `../21.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/21.md`

Published-English witness alignment: scans **90–91**, section **21 — Madhavi in Loneliness**.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve Vasanthamalai's news that Kovalan and Kannagi have left for Madurai, Madhavi's grief over Manimekalai, Chitrapathi's denial of Madhavi's marital standing, Madhavi's attack on her mother and the hunchback, exposure of the household's exploitation of Kovalan's wealth, and the final isolation of mother and child.

### Significant differences

The independent translation preserves Kalaignar's Tamil-centred maternal imagery:

- `O new moon, white as butter`;
- `O fruit of Tamil that lets fall words for me!`;
- `rain of kisses`;
- the father's longing for `Appa` and `Amma`;
- `baby-lisp of refined Tamil`;
- the repeated `Enough, Mother... enough!`;
- Chitrapathi's stark admission: `we let you love him for wealth`;
- Madhavi's repeated final `no support`.

The published witness compresses the child-language passage into generic endearments and removes the explicit `Tamil fruit` and `refined Tamil baby-lisp` imagery. Its footnotes on `Amma` and `Appa` are editorial explanations not needed by the Tamil source.

A more consequential difference appears in Chitrapathi's admission. The verified Tamil says, in effect, that Madhavi did not love for wealth, **but they allowed her to love him for wealth**. The independent translation preserves this distinction. The published witness instead says `You never loved him at all. We allowed you to make love to him so that we could find the means to live in comfort and style.` That changes Madhavi's emotional truth and contradicts the scene's Tamil wording.

The published witness also adds narrator conclusions such as the child watching Madhavi in `uncomprehending grief`; the verified Tamil ends with Madhavi sobbing that they have no support.

### Decision

**`no action`**

The published witness's altered Chitrapathi line is recorded as a substantive secondary-witness divergence. The verified Tamil is clear, so no recheck is required.

## Batch conclusion

Scenes **19–21** confirm the established pattern:

- classical/literary forms are often rewritten or compressed in the published witness;
- explicit Tamil-language imagery is frequently generalized or removed;
- narrator psychology is sometimes added;
- source-specific social vocabulary and rhetorical repetition are often replaced by smoother English;
- on occasion, the published witness changes substantive meaning, as in scene 21's claim that Madhavi `never loved him at all`.
