# Published-English Secondary-Witness Comparison — Scenes 4–6

Status: **complete**

Authority for all decisions remains the verified Tamil scene corpus and controlling Tamil scan. The 2009 published English edition is a secondary witness only.

## Scene 4 — `இளங்கோ துறவு` / Ilango's Renunciation

### Alignment

- Independent translation: `translations/en/04.md`
- Verified Tamil: `scenes/04.md`, source scans 21–23
- Published witness: section 4, physical scans **42–44**, `Ilango’s Renunciation`

### Material agreement

Both English versions preserve the basic dramatic sequence: Senguttuvan offers the crown to Ilango; Ilango appears in renunciant garb; the family protests; Ilango insists on his chosen course; he leaves the palace; the soothsayer encounters him outside.

### Significant differences

The independent translation follows the Tamil antithesis `விதி / மதி` directly as **fate / reason**: `This is not fate, Mother!... This—this is reason!` The published witness replaces `மதி` with the explanatory phrase `straightforward strategy`, weakening Kalaignar's compact rationalist opposition.

The Tamil describes Ilango's act as the renunciation taken by a younger brother for an elder brother `with a mother's heart`. The independent version preserves that striking maternal-fraternal image. The published witness replaces it with an explanation about the firstborn fulfilling his proper role and adds the philosophical formulation that Ilango is `a king in his own right in that he owns nothing material`; that formulation is not present in the Tamil.

The independent translation preserves Ilango's full argument that passive submission to fate would turn the Tamil land into a field of suffering and that his father's Himalayan victory itself disproves fatalistic passivity. The published witness retains the general rejection of Fate but **omits the specific Himalayan bow-banner example**.

The Tamil names `இன்பத் தமிழகம்` and `குணவாயிற் கோட்டம்` near Tilakanagar. The independent version preserves `joyous Tamilakam` and `Kunavayir Kottam`. The published witness normalizes this to `Tamilnad` and converts the destination into `the monastery on the outskirts of this city`, losing the source place-name.

The Tamil departure tableau specifically names guards, `நாவலர், பாவலர்`, women, children, farmers and merchants. The independent translation preserves those social groups. The published witness replaces them with a generalized crowd `of all walks of life` and adds psychological narration about Ilango's `inner eyes` spotting his goal.

At the close, the Tamil gives the soothsayer a comic self-interested aside: his livelihood is gone and returning to the palace would cost him his honour — `Defeat! A great defeat!!` The independent version retains this. The published witness ends with Ilango walking onward and the astrologer dumbfounded, **omitting the source's final comic punchline**.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation is materially closer to the verified Tamil in rhetoric, rationalist terminology, place-name fidelity, social tableau and comic closure.

## Scene 5 — `சிலம்பின் தோற்றம்` / The Anklet Takes Form

### Alignment

- Independent translation: `translations/en/05.md`
- Verified Tamil: `scenes/05.md`, source scans 24–25
- Published witness: section 5, physical scans **45–46**, `Birth of Anklet’s Tale`

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve the mountain-region encounter, Ilango joining the hill people, meeting Senguttuvan and Sattanar at the Periyar, the hill people's report about Kannagi, Sattanar proposing that Ilango compose her story, and the transition to Puhar.

### Significant differences

Kalaignar explicitly addresses the visitors as `மலைவாழ் தமிழர்களே` — **Tamil people of the hills**. The independent translation preserves that identity. The published witness consistently calls them `tribal folks`, `tribesfolk`, `tribesmen`, or the king's `subjects from the hills`, removing the explicit Tamil-peoplehood wording.

The Tamil stage action includes Senguttuvan, Venmal and Sattanar watching **two elephants fight** when Ilango arrives. The independent version retains it. The published witness substitutes a generalized relaxed survey of natural scenery.

The Tamil contains Sattanar's `மலைவளம் / மன வளம்` word-pair — wealth of the hills / wealth of mind. The independent translation preserves the parallel. The published witness reduces the exchange to `The pleasure is mine and also the privilege`, losing the source wordplay.

The Tamil gives a long, exact inventory of gifts — tusks, honey, sandalwood, fruit, animal young, `kurumpaadu`, mongoose, peacock, civet cat, parrot and more. The independent translation preserves the full inventory and leaves the uncertain animal term `kurumpaadu` unresolved rather than guessing. The published witness compresses the material to generic `gift-articles` and later merely says the gifts are offered.

Senguttuvan's source slogan `தமிழ்நாட்டு வளங்கள் என்றென்றும் வாழ்க!` is preserved independently as `Long live forever the riches of the Tamil land!`. The published witness replaces it with a conventional blessing to the hill people and does not preserve the Tamil-land acclamation.

The hill man's account in the Tamil is deliberately uncertain: did the dead husband return, or did Kannagi go away with him? The independent translation retains both questions and the uncertainty. The published witness interprets the incident and says the hill people are `inclined to believe that she succeeded in rejoining her husband`, adding a conclusion the Tamil does not state.

The Tamil has Sattanar explicitly propose that the Kannagi epic become an incomparable treasury speaking the greatness of the **Chera, Chola and Pandya lands**, and explains that the story belongs to the `three crowned kings`. The independent translation preserves this three-dynasty literary frame. The published witness reduces the proposal to Kannagi's life being an epic that Ilango alone can record for posterity.

The Tamil says Sattanar's narration begins while he strokes the tiger cub and Ilango begins writing; the tiger flag flies and the scene changes with Sattanar's voice. The independent version retains this restrained stage sequence. The published witness adds editorial explanation about palm leaves being equivalent to paper and describes Sattanar's prose being transformed into poetry through Ilango, neither of which is stated in the verified Tamil scene.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation better preserves Tamil identity, source inventory, uncertainty, three-dynasty historical frame, wordplay and stage action.

## Scene 6 — `பூம்புகார்ப் பொற்றொடி` / Puhar's Golden-Bangled Maiden

### Alignment

- Independent translation: `translations/en/06.md`
- Verified Tamil: `scenes/06.md`, source scans 26–28
- Published witness: section 6, physical scans **47–50**, `Beauteous Pride of Poompuhar`

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Puhar's maritime setting, the Indra Festival, international merchants, Tamil cultural performance, Kannagi awaiting Kovalan, the Greek companion, the lovers' reunion, Tamil export imagery, their Tamil/language metaphor, and the comic exchange with the Greek elder leading toward Madhavi's dance.

### Significant differences

The Tamil strikingly describes the commemorated ancestor as `இந்திரன் என்னும் பழந்தமிழன்` — **an ancient Tamil named Indran** — and calls the observance an `எழுச்சி விழா`, a festival of awakening. The independent translation preserves both. The published witness conventionalizes this into an ancestor `called Indra` and an annual festival `in honour of the memory`, removing the historicizing `ancient Tamil` and `awakening` language.

The Tamil says `தெருவெல்லாம் தமிழ் முழக்கம்!` — every street resounds with Tamil. Both versions convey Tamil sound in the city, but the independent version remains close to the line and retains the surrounding source-specific material such as full pots, golden maidens and maiden-lamps. The published witness compresses these into general decoration and gaiety.

The verified Tamil prints only the three opening praise cues — Sun, Moon and great rain — followed by `[சிலப்பதிகாரப் பாடலும், மகளிர் ஆட்டமும்]`. The independent translation deliberately translates only those source-visible cues and does **not** import a standard published Silappathikaram song. The 2009 witness instead supplies extended multi-line stanzas for Sun, Moon, cloud and an additional **Puhar** stanza. Those lines go materially beyond the verified dramatic Tamil source and therefore cannot be imported into the independent edition.

The Tamil uses `வாழ்க்கைத் துணைவி` for Kannagi. The independent version preserves `life-companion`; the published witness simplifies it to `wife` / `helpmeet`.

The Tamil Greek elder explicitly compares Tamil women to Tamil pearls and says Greece has never produced such a beauty. The independent version keeps both clauses. The published witness preserves the beauty comparison but adds `angel` and other embellishing description in stage narration.

The lovers' language metaphor is particularly important. The Tamil has `தமிழை விட்டுச் சுவை பிரியுமா?` followed by `வேற்று மொழி குறுக்கிட்டால்?` — `Can sweetness part from Tamil?` / `What if another language comes between us?` The independent translation retains this exchange without adding a political outcome. The published witness adds `It will be swept away`, a new response not present in the Tamil before Kovalan's light/harp/moon imagery.

The Tamil jokingly has Kovalan tell the Greek elder that `தாத்தா` means **handsome man**. The independent translation preserves that joke. The published witness changes the false gloss to `the equivalent of Apollo in Greek`, an invented cultural substitution.

The Tamil joke about `முத்தமிழ்` explicitly names `இயல்`, `இசை`, and `கூத்து`, with the Greek elder trying the Tamil words. The independent translation retains `Muthamizh`, `iyal`, `isai`, and `koothu`. The published witness translates them into `prose and melody` / `theatre art of Tamilnad`, sacrificing the Tamil taxonomy and much of the language-learning humour.

The source's `பொற்றொடி` title image and several elevated Kannagi descriptions are retained in the independent translation's register. The published title `Beauteous Pride of Poompuhar` is a freer editorial recasting rather than a lexical equivalent of the Tamil title.

### Decision

**No action.** The published witness contains significant expansion, cultural substitution and normalization. The independent translation remains preferable because it adheres to the verified Tamil without importing external classical verses or editorial embellishment.
