# Published-English Secondary-Witness Comparison — Scenes 22–24

Status: **complete**  
Decision for each scene: **no action**

This comparison treats the 2009 published English edition only as a secondary witness. Verified Tamil remains controlling, followed by the independently produced and reviewed English translation.

## Scene 22 — மதுரைப் பயணம் / Journey to Madurai

Independent translation: `../22.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/22.md`  
Published witness: physical scans **92–94**, section **22 Journey to Mathurai**.

### Alignment

The dramatic sequence aligns securely: meeting Kavundhi Adigal; Kovalan's description of Poompuhar; his acknowledgement of wrongdoing; the `ஆறு ஐந்து` / thirty-kaadham joke; Kavundhi's road/life metaphors; the fish, sugarcane/honey and jackfruit warnings; the `mind-monkey` remark; Kosikamani/Kausikan's delivery of Madhavi's palm-leaf; and Kovalan's instruction that the same letter be given to his father.

### Significant differences

- The independent translation preserves Kovalan's long Poompuhar catalogue—foreign horses, pepper, northern gems and gold, `aaram`, `akil`, southern-sea pearls, eastern-sea coral, Ganges wealth and Kaveri yield. The published witness reduces this to a shorter export list and introduces commodities such as **ivory** not present in the verified Tamil catalogue.
- `வாழ்க்கைத் துணைநலம்` is retained independently as **`the cherished companion of my life`**. The published witness reduces Kannagi simply to `my wife`.
- The source's deliberately comic `ஆறு ஐந்து காதம் ... ஆறைந்து முப்பது காதம்` is retained in the independent version as `six-five kaadham ... six fives ... thirty kaadham`. The published witness rearranges it to `five six kaadhams` and explains it as `five multiplied by six`. Both reach thirty, but the independent version follows the printed Tamil order rather than regularizing the joke.
- The verified Tamil says `நீர் நாயினுல் ... வாளை மீன்கள்`; the independent translation conservatively renders the immediate sense as **vaalai fish attacked by otters**. The published witness changes the animal to **beavers**, a secondary-witness substitution unsupported by the verified Tamil.
- Kavundhi's `மனக் குரங்கு` is preserved as **`the monkey of the mind`**. The published witness keeps the broad image but expands the advice into modern motivational prose—`future presents a challenge`, `sincere effort`—beyond the verified Tamil's compact `Let what happens from now on be good!`.
- The Tamil only states that Kosikamani gives Kovalan Madhavi's palm-leaf `in the forest`. The published witness adds a scene in which Kovalan has gone specifically to fetch spring water and identifies Kausikan as `a brahmin from Poompuhar`; neither detail is supplied by the verified scene.
- Madhavi's letter in the independent translation retains the source's direct accountability toward parents, wife and livelihood. The published witness compresses and recasts it into more literary English (`flight`, `lacerated heart`) and changes the closing to `Your Beloved`.
- Kovalan's tears are `pearl-like` in the verified source and independent translation. The published witness removes the explicit pearl simile.

### Decision

**No action.** The verified Tamil is clear, and the independent translation better preserves the source catalogue, relationship language, joke order, imagery and ethical economy. No Tamil recheck is required.

## Scene 23 — மாதவி துறவு / Madhavi's Renunciation

Independent translation: `../23.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/23.md`  
Published witness: physical scans **95–96**, section **23 Madhavi's Renunciation**.

### Alignment

The scene aligns securely: Maasathuvan reads the palm-leaf; Madhavi arrives with Manimekalai and women carrying gold; she returns wealth accumulated without her knowledge; Maasathuvan recognizes her moral worth; she rejects wealth after losing love; he accepts her relationally; and he takes Manimekalai in his arms.

### Significant differences

- The verified Tamil says **`நாலைந்து பெண்கள்`**—an approximate `four or five women`. The independent translation preserves that approximation. The published witness fixes it as **five maids**, making an uncertain source quantity artificially exact.
- Maasathuvan's `மாற்றுக் குறையாத பசும் பொன்` is retained independently as **`pure gold that loses nothing under assay`**, preserving the assay/touchstone metaphor. The published witness simplifies this to `a heart of unalloyed gold`, losing the specific testing image.
- Madhavi calls herself an **`உதிர்ந்த மலர்` — fallen flower**. The published witness changes this to a `withered flower which the plant has cast off`, adding explanatory imagery not printed in the Tamil.
- Maasathuvan's response is **`Madhavi... you are my beloved daughter-in-law!`** in the independent translation. The published witness changes the relationship to **`my beloved daughter`**. That is a substantive relational alteration: the Tamil explicitly folds Madhavi into the family as daughter-in-law.
- The verified Tamil simply has Maasathuvan take the child, the child smile, and tears gather in his eyes. The published witness adds narrator interpretation that the thought of becoming a grandfather `tickles him` and that Manimekalai's smile reminds him specifically of Kovalan's smile.
- The title `Renunciation` is not treated independently as formal religious initiation; the scene itself shows relinquishment of wealth and emotional withdrawal. The published witness likewise uses the title but supplies no additional source basis for a formal ascetic act.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation preserves the approximate number, assay metaphor, `fallen flower`, and especially the source-specific **daughter-in-law** relationship. The published witness's `daughter` rendering is recorded as secondary evidence only.

## Scene 24 — கவுந்தியுடன் கண்ணகி கோவலன் / Kannagi and Kovalan with Kavundhi

Independent translation: `../24.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/24.md`  
Published witness: physical scans **97–98**, section **24 Near Mathurai**.

### Alignment

The dramatic content aligns: Kannagi is pricked by a thorn; Kovalan compares himself to it and moves toward self-punishment; Kannagi stops him; Kavundhi invokes the Tirukkural; Madurai is near; a fish leaps from a field-channel, is compared with rash human action and with Kovalan, and is returned to the water before the image transitions toward the Pandya fish-banner.

### Significant differences

- Kovalan's source repetition—**`I am more cruel than this thorn`**—is preserved in the independent translation. The published witness turns the passage into smoother continuous prose and loses some of the repeated self-condemnation.
- The source has Kovalan say to the thorn, in effect, `you yourself blind these eyes of mine`, while he is about to prick himself. The independent translation preserves that specific stage action without intensifying it. The published witness paraphrases it as preferring to be blind and makes the thorn a `timely` instrument, adding interpretive rhetoric.
- Kannagi's `உங்கள் அழகைப் பார்த்துப் பார்த்து` becomes **`Looking and looking at your beauty`**, preserving repetition. The published witness compresses this to `your comely grace`.
- The Tirukkural `இடுக்கண் வருங்கால் நகுக!` is independently translated directly from the printed Tamil as **`When adversity comes, smile!`**. The published witness expands it into `the best weapon against adversity is a courageous smile`, importing interpretive language beyond the quoted source.
- The verified Tamil says the breeze from Madurai has begun to blow. The published witness instead introduces **`the fragrance from the bower of the Sangam-Tamil`**, a Tamil-cultural embellishment not stated in this scene's verified text.
- Kovalan's response to the leaping fish is the compact **`Like me!`**. The published witness expands it into a self-referential narrator-like sentence about being the `living example` of Kavundhi's observations.
- The independent translation preserves the final visual transition from the rescued fish to the **Pandya fish-banner**. The published witness ends with the fish swimming away `in the exultation of freedom` and omits the explicit banner-transition stage image.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation better preserves Kalaignar's repetition, compact humour, direct Kural wording and the fish-to-Pandya-banner visual logic. No source recheck or translation re-review is triggered.
