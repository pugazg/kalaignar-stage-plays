## Scene 13 — துன்பப் புயலில் கண்ணகி / Kannagi in a Storm of Sorrow

Independent translation: `../13.md`

Published witness: section 13, `Kannagi in Loneliness`, physical scans **69–71**.

### Alignment

The dramatic unit aligns: Maasathuvan and his wife visit Kannagi; Kannagi refuses to let Kovalan be humiliated, rejects leaving the separate household, and is praised as an exemplar of Tamil greatness; then Maanaaykan and his wife arrive and Kannagi defends both Kovalan and Madhavi.

### Significant differences

- The independent translation preserves the source's fish imagery closely: Kovalan has abandoned `the fish caught in the net`, while Kannagi is `like a fish cast upon the ground`. The published witness recasts Kovalan himself as `a fish in Madhavi's net` and adds `goldfish` for Kannagi.
- The Tamil explicitly says the separate household was arranged `according to Tamil custom` and calls Kannagi `a living testimony to the greatness of the Tamil people`. The independent version preserves those formulations. The published witness shifts the latter to `greatness of the Tamil society` and adds explanatory `essence of its fundamentals` language.
- The published witness inserts an entire episode absent from the verified Tamil: Vasantamalai is shown leaving Kannagi's house with jewel-caskets and is narratively accused of imposing on Kannagi and extracting valuables in Kovalan's name. No such action is present in the verified Tamil scene and it is not imported into the independent translation.
- Kannagi's defence of Madhavi is source-specific and stronger than a generic statement of compassion: `She is greater than I am... even in love.` The independent version preserves it. The published witness retains the broad idea but adds explanatory psychology about protection and sensitivity.
- The Tamil ends with Kannagi's threefold emotional prohibition: `None of you come here.... None of you come here!... None of you come here!...` The independent translation retains all three. The published witness reduces the repetition and adds a new explanatory line about anyone disrespecting her husband not being friend or relative.

### Decision

**no action**

The independent translation remains closer to the verified Tamil. The published witness contains additions and interpretive expansions that must remain secondary evidence only.

## Scene 14 — மாதவியின் மாண்பு / Madhavi's Nobility

Independent translation: `../14.md`

Published witness: section 14, `Madhavi's Magnanimity`, physical scans **72–75**.

### Alignment

The dramatic unit aligns: Madhavi's pregnancy is revealed through teasing; a deserted woman appeals beside the Kaveri; Kovalan reasons with her husband; Madhavi then turns Kovalan's own moral reasoning back upon his separation from Kannagi.

### Significant differences

- The independent translation preserves Kalaignar's extended teasing sequence around fatigue, sneezing, jealousy, `Ma...dha...vi`, `maathaa`, and `pithaa`. The published witness compresses the exchange and changes the wordplay to the simplified explanation that Madhavi is becoming `madha = mother`.
- The most important narrative divergence is the animal in the deserted-wife episode. The **verified Tamil source says a young parrot** (`கிளிக்குஞ்சு`) kills the snake and is then mistakenly killed by the woman. The independent translation therefore correctly preserves a **parrot-and-snake story**. The published witness substitutes a **mongoose-and-cobra story**. This is a substantive secondary-witness alteration, not authority for changing the Tamil-derived translation.
- The Tamil uses several distinct status/caste/learned terms in the same scene: `பிராமண மாது`, `பார்ப்பனமாது`, `அந்தணர் / அந்தணன்`, `மறையவர்`, and `தமிழ் மறை`. The independent translation deliberately preserves the distinctions: `Brahmin woman`, `paarppana woman`, `Antanar`, `man of sacred lore`, and `Tamil sacred lore`. The published witness largely collapses the husband to `brahmin`, losing the source's lexical distinctions.
- Kovalan's moral reasoning in the Tamil is consequence-based and ends with `All of it is training given by the Tamil sacred lore!`. The independent translation retains that explicit Tamil ethical authority. The published witness omits the `Tamil sacred lore` line entirely and instead adds practical language about the trouble and expense of pilgrimage.
- Madhavi's rebuke `it is not enough to preach to the whole town!` and her repeated description of Kannagi as `my elder sister` are retained in the independent translation. The published witness preserves the general moral reversal but expands it into a more explicit argumentative ultimatum.

### Decision

**no action**

No Tamil recheck is required: the verified Tamil is clear on the parrot and on the terminology distinctions. The published mongoose version is recorded as a secondary-witness divergence only.

## Scene 15 — மனமாறிந்த கோவலன் / Kovalan, His Heart Changed

Independent translation: `../15.md`

Published witness: section 15, `Return Thwarted`, physical scan **76**.

### Alignment

The dramatic unit aligns: Kannagi moves through the diminished household in grief; she unknowingly shuts the door just as Kovalan reaches it; Kovalan turns away; Kannagi then laments before his portrait.

### Significant differences

- The Tamil opening is an extended visual-memory montage: lack of adornment, remembered rooms, cow, bed, jasmine, standing lamp and oil dripping like tears. The independent translation preserves the full sequence. The published witness compresses it to general austerity, grief, an unlit lamp and the door action.
- Source `THADAAL` is retained in the independent translation as a theatrical sound. The published witness replaces it with ordinary prose `slams the door shut`.
- Kannagi's lament in the Tamil is highly rhetorical and bodily: volcano-breath, spear-sharpness tested on her heart, pearl-garlanded chest, broad shoulder and `drinking the taste of Tamil`. The independent translation retains these images and repeated `my dear` appeals.
- The published witness substantially rewrites the lament. It substitutes generic `poetry of your speech` for the explicit `தமிழ்ச் சுவை` / `taste of Tamil`, adds `bleary eyes`, `day and night`, `battling against despair`, `devotion`, and `face of my God`, and compresses Kalaignar's repeated direct address.

### Decision

**no action**

The independent translation preserves the source's Tamil-language image and emotional cadence more faithfully; the published witness is markedly freer here.
