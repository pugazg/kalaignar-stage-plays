Status: **complete**

Authority order for this comparison remains: controlling Tamil scan → verified Tamil scene → independently reviewed English translation → 2009 published-English witness as secondary witness only.

## Scene 10 — `கலையரசி கண்ட உவகை` / *The Queen of the Arts Finds Joy*

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/10.md`, source scans 39–41
- independent English: `translations/en/10.md`
- published witness: section 10, `Madhavi Conquers`, physical scans 62–64

### Material agreement

Both English versions preserve the broad dramatic sequence: Kovalan arrives at Madhavi's house; she gives him an elaborate welcome; the dancers gradually withdraw until Madhavi dances alone; her grief enters the dance; she collapses; Kovalan revives her; she declares that this was meant to be her final dance; the exchange moves toward love and her fear that Kovalan judges her through inherited social identity.

### Significant differences

The verified Tamil is unusually rhythmic and cumulative in the dance sequence. It repeats `ஆடுகிறாள் / ஆடுகிறாள் / ஆடிக்கொண்டே இருக்கிறாள்`, turns garment, pillar, creeper and fallen bangle into musical instruments, uses the harsh `worm fallen into fire` image, retains the theatrical `தடால்!`, and explicitly makes art rise as a wave even inside a great flood of sorrow. The independent translation preserves those structures and images closely.

The published witness compresses this into smoother narrative prose: `Dance and music rise to supreme heights`, `creeper-plants` and `pillars of rock` respond, and Madhavi writhes on a marble floor. It does not retain the repeated `She dances` cadence, the garment/bangle sound sequence, the worm-in-fire image or `THADAAL!`. It also adds several explanatory images absent from the verified Tamil, including the `temple of Art / Deity Kovalan / devotee Madhavi` framing and later the `tired leaf` image.

The verified Tamil says `மகிழ்ச்சி பூத்துக் குலுங்கும் தமிழகம்` and asks why Madhavi alone should nurture an art of sorrow in a Tamilakam where joy blossoms and sways. The independent version preserves `Tamilakam`; the published witness generalizes this to `a land where everyone seeks inner peace and joy`, removing the explicit Tamilakam geography.

Madhavi's cascading refusal in the Tamil is materially stronger than the published witness: she rejects gold, wealth, royal fame and, if Kovalan remains beside her, even flesh, life, honey and the world of the gods. The independent translation preserves the repeated `I want no...` escalation. The published witness compresses the sequence to gold, riches, royal patronage, life and art.

The closing beat also differs. The verified Tamil has Kovalan deny her fear that he sees her merely as one descended from women who bewitch men; Madhavi cries out and then swoons upon his lap. The published witness adds `So my God has relented at last!`, has Kovalan call her `my beloved`, and turns the ending into Madhavi sleeping peacefully on his lap while Kovalan admires her childlike innocence. Those are secondary-witness additions, not source controls.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation better preserves the verified Tamil's repetition, sensory rhetoric, Tamilakam reference, renunciation-like escalation and abrupt swoon ending.

## Scene 11 — `கற்பரசியின் கலக்கம்` / *The Queen of Chastity in Distress*

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/11.md`, source scan 42
- independent English: `translations/en/11.md`
- published witness: section 11, `Bravery in Grief`, physical scans 65–66

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Kannagi waiting for Kovalan, the driver's return without him, postponement of the Rome journey, Devandhi's arrival, Kannagi concealing the truth by saying Kovalan is preparing for travel, the Sita–Rama–Surpanakha analogy, and Kannagi's refusal to insult Madhavi.

### Significant differences

The verified Tamil explicitly marks Kannagi's reply `தோல்வியில் முடிந்தது!` as `(இருபொருளில்)`—with a double meaning. The independent translation preserves that editorial cue as `[With a double meaning]` and keeps the line as `It ended in defeat!...`. The published witness replaces the exchange with Devanti asking whether Madhavi stopped sending envoys and Kannagi answering simply `Of course, she has`, which removes the source's deliberate ambiguity.

The verified Tamil leaves `ஏன் ......என்ன நடந்தது ?` without a repeated speaker label. The independent version preserves that source structure. The published witness regularizes the whole exchange into conventional dialogue and adds interpretive stage prose such as the chaise returning `on weary wheels`, the driver standing `irresolute`, Kannagi being `as in a trance`, and her `steeling herself`.

Kannagi's final correction remains substantially faithful in both versions, but the independent translation retains the doubled fortune wording—`favoured by fortune; a fortunate woman`—while the published witness reduces it to `Madhavi is a fortunate woman`.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation more accurately preserves the source's double-meaning cue, unlabeled dialogue continuation and repeated fortune formulation.

## Scene 12 — `இன்ப வாழ்வில் கோவலன்` / *Kovalan in a Life of Delight*

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/12.md`, source scan 43
- independent English: `translations/en/12.md`
- published witness: section 12, `Kovalan in Newfound Joy`, physical scans 67–68

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Madhavi stringing a garland, Kovalan watching the movement of her fingers, their playful disagreement over whether she was created for dance or for him, Kovalan's extended praise, Madhavi's joke about men stringing verbal garlands, Kovalan's claim that praising her brings glory to Tamil, and the mutual garlanding.

### Significant differences

The verified Tamil's central praise speech is deliberately excessive and image-dense. It includes `kanthal`-like fingers, a golden-glowing body, the difficult source form `சேற் கெண்டை` associated with the red-lip image, black bees appearing to move inside the casket of the eyelids, dark rain-cloud hair curling like a peacock plume down to the feet, and speech gaining flavour because the tongue floats like a jackfruit segment in a spring of honey. The independent translation preserves this accumulation, including the unresolved `ser-kendai` rather than guessing.

The published witness radically compresses that imagery into `gold of your complexion`, `rain-cloud of your tresses`, `ruby of your lips`, `pearl of your teeth`, `magic in your eyes` and `drug in your speech`. `Pearl of your teeth` is not part of the verified Tamil image sequence, while several source-specific images—kanthal, eyelid-casket/black bees, peacock-plume length, honey spring and jackfruit segment—disappear.

The verified Tamil makes Kovalan say that describing Madhavi gives beauty to words and glory to **Tamil itself**. The independent translation preserves both steps. The published witness keeps only the broader claim that `the Tamil language gets enriched`, which is materially similar but less rhetorically cumulative.

The published witness ends with narrator commentary, `This time there is no resistance to her garlanding. Her conquest is complete.` The verified Tamil simply has Madhavi garland Kovalan, Kovalan remove the garland and place it on Madhavi, and both experience joy. The independent translation correctly avoids adding retrospective narrative interpretation.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation more faithfully retains Kalaignar's extravagant image-chain, preserves unresolved source language without conjectural substitution, and keeps the ending within the source stage action.
