# Published-English Secondary-Witness Comparison — Scenes 1–3

Status: **complete**

Comparison authority: verified Tamil scenes and controlling Tamil source remain primary. The 2009 published English is a secondary witness only.

## Scene 1 — `வஞ்சி மூதூரில் முரசறைதல்`

Independent translation: `../01.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/01.md`

Published witness: physical scan **37**, section **1 `Vanchi Prepares for Festival`**.

### Agreement

Both English versions preserve the opening Vanchi proclamation, the elephant, Chera bow emblem, Senguttuvan's birthday, Nedun Cheralathan's northern/Himalayan achievement, and the public acclamation of Senguttuvan and Chera land.

### Significant differences

The independent translation remains materially closer to the verified Tamil rhetoric and stage direction. It preserves `Tamilakam`, the image `Tamilakam, girdled by the sounding sea`, the repeated `Let us beat, let us beat the drum!`, and the final `homeland of the people of refined Tamil`.

The published witness instead uses `Tamilnad`, expands the scene with an expectant crowd falling silent and later dispersing, replaces the repeated drum-call with added bells/music/revelry imagery, and closes by repeating `Eternal life to Tamil! Victory to Tamilnad!`. Those additions/recastings are not present in the verified Tamil scene.

The published witness's `worthy son`, `lion-hearted prince`, `great people of this vast land`, and similar phrases are translator/editorial amplification rather than direct equivalents of additional Tamil wording.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation is better aligned to the verified Tamil source and preserves the source's Tamilakam/geographic rhetoric and repetition more faithfully.

## Scene 2 — `செங்குட்டுவன் பிறந்தநாள் விழா`

Independent translation: `../02.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/02.md`

Published witness: physical scans **38–39**, section **2 `Senguttuvan’s Birthday`**.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve the private Senguttuvan–Venmal exchange, the festive-array / future-war contrast, Venmal's playful response about healing battle wounds, the joke about childlessness, Ilango's interruption, and the affectionate departure for court.

### Significant differences

The published witness substantially elaborates the opening stage action: a full mirror, Senguttuvan watching Venmaal's reflected face, and descriptive romantic framing are not present in the verified Tamil stage direction, which simply places Senguttuvan in his private upper chamber while Venmal adorns him.

For `என் உதட்டிலே வைத்திருக்கிறேன் மருந்து`, the Tamil specifically locates the medicine on Venmal's **lips**. The independent version keeps this as `I keep the medicine upon my lips!`; the published witness adds `eager lips and finger-tips`, introducing an element unsupported by the Tamil.

Ilango's Tamil calls the soothsayer's claims `பொய்யுரைகள்` — lies. The independent translation retains that direct accusation. The published witness recasts it as a generalized statement about outgrowing `superstitions`, which is interpretively compatible with the scene but more explicit than the source wording.

The published witness also softens/restructures some family banter: `குறும்புக்காரா!` becomes `Don’t be so very arch`, while the independent `You little rogue!` more directly preserves the affectionate teasing. The final eye-signalling stage direction is also expanded in the published witness into descriptive romantic prose.

### Decision

**No action.** The comparison strengthens the existing independent choices, especially `lips`, direct treatment of the soothsayer's `lies`, and preservation of compact family banter without editorial embellishment.

## Scene 3 — `விழாவும் வினாவும்`

Independent translation: `../03.md`

Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/03.md`

Published witness: physical scans **40–41**, section **3 `Celebration Takes a Turn`**.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve the royal birthday assembly, the soothsayer/astrologer's prediction that Ilango rather than Senguttuvan will rule, Senguttuvan's visible reaction, Ilango's rejection of fate, the Rama–Bharata example, and Ilango's vow to defeat the prediction and establish his elder brother's right to rule.

### Significant differences

The verified Tamil king explicitly says Senguttuvan will bear the sceptre and foster **`தமிழ் அறம்`**. The independent translation retains this as `Tamil aram`. The published witness reduces the passage to Senguttuvan ascending the throne and taking charge of the sceptre; the `Tamil aram` element is absent.

The Tamil proverb `தம்பியுடையான் படைக்கஞ்சான்` is explicitly present. The independent translation retains it as `He who has a younger brother fears no army`. The published witness paraphrases the relationship as Ilango being Senguttuvan's `able lieutenant and companion`, losing the proverb and its martial cadence.

The Tamil's wordplay around `பொய்க் காது / பொய்க்காது / உண்மைக் காது` is difficult to reproduce. The independent translation deliberately keeps the `ear / falsehood / truth` structure and records the loss in its notes. The published witness instead introduces `ears of superstition`, which gives a stronger rationalist gloss than the literal wording supports.

The Tamil explicitly invokes the Kural and says Ilango will conquer `விதி` with `மதி` — fate with reason/intelligence. The independent translation retains the Kural-based challenge and `reason`. The published witness conveys human endeavour but adds imagery absent from Tamil, especially `chart-caged planets` and `the effulgent sun of honest intellect`.

The Tamil repeats `தூள்! தூள்!! தூளாகப் போகிறது`; the independent translation preserves the dust repetition. The published witness compresses the effect into `smash your prediction to smithereens`, losing the source repetition while adding a different English idiom.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation is more source-accountable in its treatment of `Tamil aram`, the proverb, the Kural, fate/reason opposition, the ear wordplay, and repeated `dust` rhetoric. The published witness is useful evidence of a more interpretive and rhetorically expansive translation strategy, not a reason to revise the released translation.
