## Scene 28 — `பொற்கொல்லர் முறையீடு` / `The Goldsmith's Appeal`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/28.md`, source scan 71;
- independent English: `translations/en/28.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **105–106**, section **`28 Theft on Record`**.

### Material agreement

Both English versions preserve the broad dramatic sequence: Kopperundevi complains about the delayed anklet, Nedunchezhiyan is writing, the chief goldsmith urgently reports that the queen's anklet has disappeared, and the king orders all one hundred goldsmiths to be brought for inquiry.

### Significant differences

1. **Title and setting are editorially recast in the published witness.**
   - verified Tamil title: `பொற்கொல்லர் முறையீடு`;
   - independent English: `The Goldsmith's Appeal`;
   - published witness: `Theft on Record`.
   - Tamil setting `பாண்டியன் தனிமாடம்` is handled by the independent translation as the Pandya's private chamber; the published witness calls it a palace `Sanctum`.

2. **The `puram` literary wordplay is lost.**
   - the Tamil explicitly has `புறத்துறைப் பாடல்`, followed by Kopperundevi's teasing `புறப்பாடல்...போர் முழக்கம்!`;
   - the independent translation retains `puram-theme poem / puram-song / battle cry`;
   - the published witness replaces this with the much looser `exotic poem` / `thunder of battle`, obscuring Kalaignar's lexical play.

3. **`Tamil... is a breeze! It enters everywhere.` is expanded beyond source.**
   - the independent version stays close to `தமிழ்...ஒரு தென்றல்! அது எல்லா இடத்திலும் நுழையும்.`;
   - the published witness adds `it needs no password nor a coat of arms`, an image not present in the verified Tamil.

4. **The quoted learning verse is substantially expanded in the published witness.**
   - the verified scene prints only `உற்றுழி உதவியும் / உறுபொருள் கொடுத்தும்` before the interruption;
   - the independent translation renders only those printed lines;
   - the published witness continues with `aid to the preceptor`, `service steady and sincere`, and other material not printed in this verified dramatic scene.

5. **The goldsmith report is modernized and psychologically narrated.**
   - the source says the anklet was locked safely in an iron box and that the key remained with the goldsmith;
   - the published witness adds that the key was kept `next to my heart`, adds `I am undone`, `felony`, and closes with Nedunchezhiyan `pensive and frowning`;
   - these are not required by the verified Tamil.

6. **Institutional diction is stronger in the published witness.**
   - the source `நீதி மன்றம்` is conservatively rendered `hall of justice` in the independent translation;
   - the published witness repeatedly uses `Court`, `culprit` and related procedural English. This is a translator/editorial register choice, not a reason to modernize the independent translation.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The verified Tamil is clear. No Tamil recheck and no independent-translation re-review are required.

## Scene 29 — `ஒற்றைச் சிலம்பின் ஒலி` / `The Sound of a Single Anklet`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/29.md`, source scans 72–75;
- independent English: `translations/en/29.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **107–109**, section **`29 Message of the Single Anklet`**.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve the central arc: Kannagi urges Kovalan to use an anklet as capital, they share a domestic meal, Aiyai asks Kannagi to remain for the dance, Kovalan asks to see Kannagi walk once more wearing both anklets, and only one anklet is ultimately taken for sale.

### Significant differences

1. **Major numerical divergence: `50,000` versus `5,000` kalanju.**
   - verified Tamil: `ஐம்பதாயிரம் கழஞ்சு பொன்` — **fifty thousand kalanju of gold**;
   - independent translation: `fifty thousand kalanju of gold`;
   - published witness: **`5000 kazhanjus of gold`**.

   The Tamil reading is clear and already verified. The published value is recorded as a secondary-witness divergence; it does **not** trigger a Tamil correction.

2. **The opening economic discussion is heavily abridged.**
   - the Tamil first discusses relatives in Madurai, Korkai pearls, and multiple Pandya-region trades: Thondi/Korkai exports, conches, pearls, the unusual rat-hair silk-thread garments, conch-bangle work, gold-coloured weaving and salt production;
   - the independent translation preserves this catalogue;
   - the published witness omits it and moves almost immediately to selling the anklets.

3. **Wedding-memory rhetoric is rewritten.**
   - the source has drums, conches, murasus, white-gold parasols, the marriage-day anklets, repeated `I am the cause! I am the cause!!`, and the audible `kanir, kanir` anklet sound;
   - the published witness replaces this with new prose about a `gorgeous procession`, the `elite of the city`, the bride at the threshold of a new life, and Kovalan sinking deeper in `felony`.

4. **Domestic intimacy is compressed and reinterpreted.**
   - the independent translation preserves cooking, Kannagi serving Kovalan, the separate kitchen worker, `a separate world for just the two of us`, betel/lime preparation, `taamboolam`, and Kannagi's wish to adorn Kovalan again;
   - the published witness adds `your sole duty shall be to remain my queen and bride all the time`, which is not the source formulation, and omits some of the specific household texture.

5. **`kuravai koothu` is generalized.**
   - independent translation: `kuravai koothu`;
   - published witness: `folk-dance`.

6. **The moral voice of the single anklet is drastically compressed.**
   - the verified Tamil makes the remaining anklet strike Kovalan's heart with `thousands upon thousands of hammer-blows`, recite an extended self-condemning insult-string, preserve the source-opaque `aavi-nigar meeniyaale aalavitta karumbaakki`, accuse him of tormenting Kannagi `atom by atom`, `sing such a poem at me`, and let `the earth hear it`;
   - the published witness reduces this to a brief lesson that the anklet should `hammer a lesson into my conscience` so he will not fail Kannagi again.

7. **The published witness omits the final jasmine/mango timing exchange and entrustment to Madhari.**
   - the Tamil closes with Kannagi asking Kovalan to return before the jasmine withers, Kovalan joking that he will return before the mango rots, and then explicitly placing Kannagi under Madhari's protection;
   - the published section instead moves directly to Kovalan leaving and Aiyai taking Kannagi to the dance.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The published witness's `5000` value is a substantive historical-English divergence, but the verified Tamil clearly supports `50,000`; no Tamil recheck is required.

## Scene 30 — `சிலம்போ சிலம்பு` / `Anklet, O Anklet!`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/30.md`, source scan 76;
- independent English: `translations/en/30.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **110–111**, section **`30 Anklet for Sale`**.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Kovalan attempting to sell the single anklet, local men directing him to the palace chief goldsmith, the chief goldsmith learning that Kovalan is from Poompuhar, and Kovalan being asked to wait while the king is informed.

### Significant differences

1. **The published section omits the entire opening goldsmith-workshop sequence.**
   - verified Tamil Scene 30 begins with the chief goldsmith questioning all one hundred goldsmiths before they set out for the `hall of justice`;
   - the independent translation preserves the doubled `Not us! ..Not us!...`, `did the anklet sprout wings and fly away`, `daylight robber`, and the order to set out;
   - the published witness begins directly on a broad street with Kovalan attempting to sell the anklet.

2. **The theatrical title is flattened.**
   - source / independent: `சிலம்போ சிலம்பு` / **`Anklet, O Anklet!`**;
   - published witness: **`Anklet for Sale`**.

3. **The first buyer exchange is expanded by narrator logic.**
   - source: the buyer asks for the pair; Kovalan asks whether anyone will buy this anklet alone for its ruby pellets;
   - the published witness adds that the rubies could be removed and used to make another ornament and describes the citizen as `prosperous-looking` and admiring the object.

4. **Kalaignar's colloquial exclamations disappear.**
   - `அடேயப்பா` / `அடேடேடே` are retained in the independent translation as `Adeyappa` / `Adededede`;
   - they are absent from the published rendering.

5. **The source's social aside is expanded.**
   - source: `யாரோ வாழ்ந்த வீட்டுப் பிள்ளை—`, conservatively rendered `A son of a house that once knew prosperity—`;
   - published witness: `Seems to belong to a noble family ... must have fallen on evil days ...`, adding an explicit class/status interpretation.

6. **The goldsmith's staging is altered.**
   - in the source, the two local men are already part of Kovalan's street encounter; after the goldsmith arrives, he beckons them and tells them to remain as companions;
   - the published witness says the chief goldsmith arrives **accompanied by two persons**, then adds that those persons `get the import of his special gesture` and `nod in comprehension`—mind-reading/narrator information not supplied by the verified Tamil.

7. **The goldsmith's recognition remains more explicit in the source.**
   - verified Tamil: `தேடி அலைந்தது தானுகவே கிடைத்து விட்டது!` — what he had been searching for has come to him by itself;
   - the independent translation preserves that dramatic asymmetry;
   - the published witness smooths it to `I was looking for just such an anklet`.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation remains closer to the verified Tamil in scene structure, colloquial rhythm and control of narrator knowledge.
