## Scene 31 — `வளைந்தது செங்கோல்` / `The Sceptre Bent`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/31.md`, source scan 77;
- independent English: `translations/en/31.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **112–113**, section **`31 Sceptre Bends`**.

### Material agreement

Both English versions preserve the queen's refusal to attend the hall/court without her anklet, Nedunchezhiyan's insistence that the country is greater than either ruler or consort, the `petty anklet / I myself have become petty` exchange, the chief goldsmith's claim that the thief has been caught, and the king's fatal command.

### Significant differences

1. **The political title is slightly recast.**
   - verified Tamil: `வளைந்தது செங்கோல்`;
   - independent English: `The Sceptre Bent`, preserving `செங்கோல்` as the already-failed symbol of righteous rule;
   - published witness: `Sceptre Bends`, which turns the title into a more immediate action but does not preserve the same completed-state emphasis.

2. **The private chamber is again editorially called a `Sanctum`.**
   - the source setting is `நெடுஞ்செழியன் பள்ளியறை`;
   - the independent translation uses `Nedunchezhiyan's Bedchamber`;
   - the published witness calls it `Sanctum in the Pandyan Palace` and adds the queen being on a couch and explicitly `sullen`.

3. **The queen's complaint is expanded and partly rewritten.**
   - Kalaignar's Tamil contrasts war equipment, constant news of the Pandya realm, and neglect of the consort's unadorned foot;
   - the published witness adds that the king is preoccupied with `writing poems`, `studying military science`, `public affairs`, and that court `formalities` will delay recovery of the anklet. Those are secondary-witness elaborations rather than the wording of this scene.

4. **Narrator psychology is added after the quarrel.**
   - source: the king goes off quickly;
   - published witness: `The scene has upset him and he is in a black mood.` No equivalent psychological narration appears in the verified Tamil.

5. **The goldsmith's report is made more definite than the source.**
   - source: the accused man was `trying to have the anklet repaired and sold` and had been secured;
   - published witness says `Having mended the clasp, he was trying to dispose of the anklet`, asserting completed repair not stated in the verified Tamil.

6. **The death command is retained but presented through a translator gloss.**
   - source: `உடனே அவனைக் கொன்று வருக!...`;
   - independent English: **`Go at once, kill him, and return!`**;
   - published witness: **`Konru varugha (which is Tamil for “kill and return”.)`**.

   The published witness notably does not soften the king's order, but it inserts transliteration plus explanation rather than translating the complete source command directly; the force of `உடனே` (`at once`) is not separately carried.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation remains closer to the verified Tamil's political title, domestic timing, source-limited narration and abrupt death command.

## Scene 32 — `மதுரை மண்ணில் கோவலன் குருதி` / `Kovalan's Blood on Madurai Soil`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/32.md`, source scans 78–79;
- independent English: `translations/en/32.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **114–116**, section **`32 Virtue Mowed down`**.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve the confrontation at the mandapam/hall, the accusation that Kovalan is the anklet thief, Kovalan's demand to be taken before Nedunchezhiyan, his appeal to the king's reputation for justice, his emotional collapse under the imposed label `thief`, his repeated address to Kannagi, and his killing by the guards.

### Significant differences

1. **The title is substantially reframed.**
   - verified Tamil: `மதுரை மண்ணில் கோவலன் குருதி` — explicitly Kovalan's blood on Madurai soil;
   - independent English: `Kovalan's Blood on Madurai Soil`;
   - published witness: `Virtue Mowed down`.

   The published title is interpretive and moralizing; it removes the source's explicit political-geographical linkage between the killing and Madurai's soil.

2. **Kovalan's first shock is normalized.**
   - source visibly spaces `தி ரு ட ன்`;
   - independent translation preserves **`T h i e f`**;
   - published witness reduces it to ordinary `Thief!`, losing the audible stunned fragmentation.

3. **The accusation exchange is rhetorically modernized.**
   - Kalaignar has Kovalan challenge officials who lack the ability to discover the real thief and instead blame a passer-by;
   - the published witness adds `monumental inefficiency`, `scapegoats`, `caught with the goods`, and other modern idiomatic accusation/prosecution language absent from the verified Tamil.

4. **The central Tamil-identity speech is heavily compressed and partly replaced.**
   - source/independent translation preserve: **`This is a heart steeped in Tamil`**, conception and formation in **Mother Tamil's womb**, crawling upon the soil of the noble land, and the `darkened mind / trade of theft` rhetorical pairing;
   - the published witness substitutes a more generic formulation: `a devoted child of Tamil`, thoughts charged with `the lofty melody of Tamil`, and an added statement that living on others is foreign to family tradition.

   The Mother-Tamil womb/land imagery and dark-mind/theft-trade pairing are lost.

5. **Nedunchezhiyan's ethical guardianship is modernized.**
   - source: `Guardian of the land! Guardian of justice—rectitude—aram—love—Tamil is Nedunchezhiyan!`;
   - independent translation preserves those distinct ethical terms and retains `aram`;
   - published witness replaces the sequence with `justice and righteousness, the rule of law and the tenets of equity`, then describes Tamil as `the language of love and beauty and truth`.

   `rule of law` and `equity` are modern legal vocabulary not stated by Kalaignar here; the source's distinct `நேர்மை / அறம் / அன்பு / தமிழ்` accumulation is therefore blurred.

6. **The face-reading image is technicalized.**
   - source: Nedunchezhiyan `pronounces justice by the signs of a face`;
   - published witness: he is `an expert in physiognomy`.

   The latter is a modern technical label rather than Kalaignar's royal-discernment image.

7. **Kovalan's Kannagi lament is extensively rewritten with new imagery.**
   - source/independent translation retain the `gold of ten-and-a-half assay` address, `Appeyo`, the veena cast into street dust, morning-star and peerless-gem addresses, the tower-pavilion, `makara-yazh`, gentle breeze, and the `arrow called thief`;
   - the published witness introduces a new `emerald-isle in the centre of the lake of contentment`, perpetual sunshine/moonlight, a `wanton boy`, a future home rebuilt `brick by brick`, a terrace-garden touching the skies, and stars witnessing fulfilled love.

   These are substantial secondary-witness literary substitutions, while several of Kalaignar's source-specific Tamil images disappear.

8. **The killing is staged differently.**
   - source: the commander's sword drives into Kovalan's neck;
   - published witness: after an officer's signal, the chief guardsman `mows down Kovalan with one sweep of his sword`.

   The result is the same, but the physical staging and narrator sequence are not identical.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation is materially closer to the verified Tamil's political geography, Tamil-identity rhetoric, differentiated ethical vocabulary, imagery and final stage action. No Tamil recheck is required.

## Scene 33 — `கணவன் கள்வனா?` / `Is My Husband a Thief?`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/33.md`, source scan 80;
- independent English: `translations/en/33.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **117–118**, section **`33 “My Husband a Thief ?”`**.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve the grief performance, Madhari bringing news that Kovalan has been branded/accused as a thief and killed, Kannagi collapsing, remembering Kovalan's departure, rejecting the label `thief`, running out in frenzy, the lamp falling, and fire beginning from the hut.

### Significant differences

1. **Named performance vocabulary is generalized.**
   - source/independent translation: `Kuravai Koothu` and `abhinaya`;
   - published witness: generic `folk dance` and `tableau on grief`.

   The source's named Tamil performance form and expressive-performance vocabulary disappear.

2. **The published witness restructures the location after Kannagi collapses.**
   - in the verified Tamil, the collapse, awakening, memory, outcry and flight occur as one continuous stage sequence around the koothu;
   - the published witness inserts `* * *` and relocates the awakening to `Madhari's dwelling`, creating a scene change not stated in the verified Tamil.

3. **The remembered farewell is altered.**
   - source/independent translation: **`Shall I go and return, my dear!`**, deliberately echoing the earlier farewell;
   - published witness: `Shall I start for the city, Kannagi?`

   The published version is explanatory but loses Kalaignar's direct emotional echo.

4. **Kannagi's repetitions are compressed.**
   - source: `You must not go! You must not go!`, then `A thief? A thief? No! No! No!`;
   - published witness reduces this to `you should not go` and `You, a thief! ... ... No, No...`.

   The triple negative and doubled accusation-question that drive the shock rhythm are weakened.

5. **The source's unusual emotional image disappears.**
   - verified Tamil: `உணர்ச்சி, குழுமும் கடலாகிறது`;
   - independent translation: `Emotion becomes a gathering sea.`;
   - published witness does not preserve this source-specific line.

6. **The fire sequence is given a major interpretive addition.**
   - source: in Kannagi's rush a large standing lamp falls against the hut; no one notices; the hut catches fire; fire spreads in many places while Kannagi continues running in fury;
   - independent translation preserves this immediate accidental stage causation and adds no cosmic or retrospective agency;
   - published witness adds that a `strong wind` fans the fire so that it `races towards the city of Mathurai, as if at the bidding of Kannagi the symbol of chastity.`

   Neither the strong wind nor the claim that the fire moves at Kannagi's bidding appears in the verified Tamil scene. This is one of the clearest interpretive additions in the published witness and would materially change Kalaignar's staging if imported.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation correctly retains the named performance vocabulary, continuity of the stage action, repeated shock language, source-opaque emotional image and non-mythologized fire causation.
