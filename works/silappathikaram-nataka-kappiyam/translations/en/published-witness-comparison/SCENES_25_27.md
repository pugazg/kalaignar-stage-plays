## Scene 25 — `நீதி வழுவா நெடுஞ்செழியன்`

Independent translation: `translations/en/25.md`

Verified Tamil scene: `scenes/25.md`

Published witness alignment: chiefly section 25, scans 99–100, with the queen-anklet corridor continuation moved by the published witness into section 26 / scan 101.

### Agreement

Both English versions preserve the authorship dispute, Arulalar's handwriting, Mallavanar's claim that Arulalar merely wrote to dictation, Nedunchezhiyan's threatened destruction of the literary work, Arulalar's willingness to surrender personal authorship rather than see it destroyed, the king's recognition of Arulalar as the genuine author, and the award of five thousand units of gold.

### Significant differences

The independent translation follows Kalaignar's explicitly Tamil-centred rhetoric much more closely. The verified scene says the disputants are highly learned in Tamil; Arulalar swears upon Tamil; Nedunchezhiyan is addressed as the ruler who nurtures the Tamil Sangam; Arulalar refuses the `unworthy act of burning Tamil`; and his willingness to surrender the work becomes the evidence by which the king identifies him as `the mother of this kaviyam`.

The published witness retains some Tamil-language framing, but expands and modernizes the court logic. It introduces concepts such as `perjury`, `beyond reasonable doubt`, `property shall be confiscated`, and a quasi-formal judicial decree structure that are not stated in the verified Tamil. It also adds the idea that the reward is to support Arulalar's `research studies` and that Mallavanar is a `drag on society` whose public exposure is punishment enough. Those are translator/editorial expansions, not source wording.

The independent translation preserves `kaviyam`, `Tamil Sangam`, `burning Tamil`, the maternal authorship metaphor, and `five thousand kalanju of gold` without replacing them by modern institutional language.

### Structural difference

The verified Tamil Scene 25 continues beyond the court judgment into the corridor sequence where the queen's anklet falls, the king orders its repair, and the official begins coveting it. The published witness ends section 25 before that corridor sequence and starts it as section 26 `Queen's Ornament` on scan 101. This is another confirmed case where published section numbering does not map one-to-one onto the Tamil scene boundary.

### Decision

**No action.** No Tamil recheck and no independent-translation re-review required.

## Scene 26 — `அரசியின் சிலம்பு`

Independent translation: `translations/en/26.md`

Verified Tamil scene: `scenes/26.md`

Published witness alignment: section 26 `Queen's Ornament`, scans 101–102. Its opening corridor material actually corresponds to the closing part of verified Tamil Scene 25; the workshop material corresponds to verified Tamil Scene 26.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve the palace official bringing the queen's anklet to the chief goldsmith, the promise to repair it by the next day, the chief goldsmith being distracted by another worker's rough handling, and the official stealing the anklet during that distraction.

### Significant differences

The independent translation keeps Kalaignar's compact workshop dialogue and its source-specific craft vocabulary. The chief goldsmith's interrupted greeting remains interrupted; `malligai-kal` is retained conservatively because the Tamil scene does not securely establish a one-to-one English technical equivalent; and the official's final `By tomorrow, the anklet...` remains an incomplete conversational handoff.

The published witness expands the workshop considerably. It introduces a `safe`, a key and lock, a `very junior assistant`, a `juvenile delinquent` insult, `royal metal`, a second `more experienced artisan`, and a narrator statement that the chief goldsmith is `blissfully ignorant` of the theft. These are not present in the verified Tamil scene.

The published section also relocates the preceding corridor scene into this section and adds its own prose embellishment: the anklet falls with a `musical clatter`, the maid makes a digestion joke, and the officer's fear is recast as a polished exchange. Kalaignar's Tamil instead gives the colloquial `government anklet—touch it and it is your head that will hang!` and the official's private `Cha... cha... cha......` covetous self-talk in Scene 25.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation remains closer to the verified scene and preserves the theft without invented foreshadowing or workshop detail.

## Scene 27 — `ஆய்ச்சியர் அறிமுகம்`

Independent translation: `translations/en/27.md`

Verified Tamil scene: `scenes/27.md`

Published witness alignment: section 27 `Nobility in Simplicity`, scans 103–104.

### Agreement

Both versions preserve Aiyai meeting Kavundhi Adigal near Madurai, the move to Madhari's house, Kavundhi introducing Kovalan and Kannagi, Madhari accepting them as guests, Aiyai being assigned as Kannagi's companion, and Kavundhi entrusting Kannagi to Madhari before departing.

### Significant differences

The independent translation preserves Kalaignar's social vocabulary and the exact relational economy of the scene. `ஆய்ச்சியர்` remains `Aychiyar`; Madhari is described contextually as `a woman of the Aayar people`; and `சமணத் துறவிகளுக்குப் பாற்சோறு` is translated directly as `milk-rice for the Jain ascetics`.

The published witness domesticates or expands these details. Aiyai says Madhari has gone simply to `offer rice to the monks`, removing both the milk-rice specificity and the explicit Jain identification. It adds `Welcome to Mathurai`, describes Madhari as `pious and hospitable`, and invents a narrator portrait of her house as `spick and span`, `plain living`, and `noble thinking`. None of that is stated in the verified Tamil scene.

Kavundhi's reason for entrusting the couple is also expanded. Kalaignar says that if people in Madurai merely heard their parents' names they would rush to invite them as guests, and asks Madhari to keep them until their work is finished. The published witness turns this into a contrast between `wealthiest houses` and Madhari being `nobler than the noblest`, plus a preference for a `quiet place`. Those are secondary-witness interpretations.

The independent translation also preserves the warm colloquial `Am I going to object?` and the immediate relational force of `அவள் உன் அடைக்கலம்!` as `She is now under your protection!`. The published witness formalizes the latter into `she is your ward and I shall hold you personally responsible for her welfare`, importing a stronger quasi-legal register than the source provides.

### Decision

**No action.** No Tamil recheck and no independent-translation re-review required.
