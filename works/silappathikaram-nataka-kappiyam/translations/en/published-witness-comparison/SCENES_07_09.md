## Scene 7 — `கலைக்கரசி மாதவி` / `Madhavi, Queen of the Arts`

Independent translation: `../07.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/07.md`  
Published witness: section 7, `Queen of Artistes`, physical scans **51–54**.

### Alignment

The dramatic sequence aligns: Madhavi's performance → royal reward → Chitrapathi's marriage-price arrangement → Greek elder bids → Madhavi/Vasanthamalai resistance → Kovalan intervenes → Tamil-worth challenge → Kovalan buys the garland → Madhavi asks that he be brought back.

### Significant comparison findings

- The verified Tamil has the Chola king's technical praise verse naming `எண்`, `எழுத்து`, `ஐந்தியல்`, `நாற்பண்`, the eleven dances and Madhavi's entry into the dance-arena. The independent translation preserves the technical terms and the verse structure. The published witness replaces this with a short prose blessing that Madhavi is foremost in dancing, thereby losing much of the performance taxonomy and formal cadence.
- The verified Tamil explicitly explains `தலைக்கோல்`; the independent translation retains `thalaikkol` and translates the source explanation. The published witness substitutes `baton (as a mark of her supremacy in Fine Arts)`, an explanatory editorial gloss rather than the source's own formulation.
- The published witness compresses or removes substantial comedy around the Greek elder's age, including the source's irregular `sixty-eight / only eight` joke and Vasanthamalai's extended old-cat/parrot ridicule. The independent translation preserves the source humour and its deliberately awkward age boast.
- The verified Tamil makes Madhavi's refusal explicit: she says she has decided to remain unmarried rather than accept the imposed match. The independent translation keeps this direct declaration. The published witness preserves the refusal but frames more of the surrounding action through explanatory prose and modernized idiom.
- Kovalan's decisive line in the Tamil moves from the Greek elder's slight against `தமிழகத்தின் தகுதி` to the collective assertion that Tamils have the strength and wealth to buy a hundred such garlands. The independent translation preserves `Tamilakam's worth` and `Tamils possess...`; the published witness turns this into the narrower `Dare you imply that we are bankrupts here in Poompuhar?`, reducing the wider Tamil collective rhetoric.
- The independent translation retains `swayamvaram` and `kalanju`, whereas the published witness explains and domesticates the marriage arrangement more heavily.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation tracks the verified Tamil more closely in technical performance language, humour, women's refusal, and Tamil collective rhetoric. No Tamil recheck is required.

## Scene 8 — `கண்ணகி இல்லறம்` / `Kannagi's Married Life`

Independent translation: `../08.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/08.md`  
Published witness: section 8, `Kannagi in Wedded Bliss`, physical scans **55–58**.

### Alignment

The dramatic sequence aligns: Kannagi recalls the Madhavi incident → Kovalan reassures her → marriage-memory and praise sequence → both families arrive believing a scandal → Kannagi defends Kovalan → Kovalan explains → family misunderstanding resolves.

### Significant comparison findings

- The verified Tamil gives Kannagi a specific quoted marriage formulation: venerable `பார்ப்பார்` guide the `மறைவழி`, and the couple circles the fire. The independent translation deliberately renders this contextually as `venerable ritual officiants` and `sacred lore`, preserving the source action while avoiding an unsupported automatic caste label. The published witness reduces the passage to `religious rights performed at the time of our wedding`, losing both the lexical distinction and the fire-circling detail.
- Kovalan's praise of Kannagi in the Tamil is a long accumulation—gold, right-spiralled pearl, fragrance, sugarcane, honey, rare maiden, life-medicine, then gem/nectar/music images. The independent translation retains the accumulation and vocative rhythm. The published witness condenses the sequence substantially and substitutes more generic praise.
- The Tamil mother's idiom `உன் தல எழுத்து` is preserved in the independent translation as `the writing on your head—your fate`; the published witness converts this into generic fate language.
- Kannagi repeatedly attempts to speak while the families interrupt and misread the situation. The independent translation keeps that dramatic agency and the exact complaint that they would not let her speak. The published witness retains the broad action but adds explanatory character-stage prose and modern idiom.
- Kovalan's definition of their `இல்லறம்` stresses two lovers `united in thought and bound in mutual support`. The independent translation preserves that companionate structure. The published witness simplifies it to `two persons but with one mind`, retaining part of the sense but dropping the explicit mutual-support wording.
- Source animal imagery in the gossip line (`கோள் சொல்லும் கோட்டான்கள்`) is retained as `tale-bearing owls` in the independent translation but omitted in the published witness.

### Decision

**No action.** The independent translation remains preferable on source-specific marriage terminology, rhetorical accumulation, idiom, animal imagery and companionate-marriage wording. No Tamil recheck is required.

## Scene 9 — `பிரிவினை தந்த பேதை` / `The Maiden Who Brought Separation`

Independent translation: `../09.md`  
Verified Tamil: `../../../scenes/09.md`  
Published witness: section 9, `Parting Sparks Separation`, physical scans **59–61**.

### Alignment

The major plot movement aligns: Kovalan prepares Kannagi for another Rome journey → lovers exchange separation imagery → Vasanthamalai arrives about Madhavi → Kannagi urges Kovalan to go explain personally → Kovalan leaves for Madhavi's house.

### Significant comparison findings

- The verified Tamil begins with a garlanding/bowing sequence, a short classical-style song, and an explicit discussion of `ஊடல்` (`oodal`) through the Kural's Book of Love. The independent translation preserves all of this and explains `oodal` only through the scene itself. The published witness omits this entire opening literary-love discussion and begins directly with the Rome-trip disclosure.
- The Tamil gives Kovalan a strong trade-and-Tamil-worth speech: selling pearls abroad will bring gold like a flood through an opened sluice and make the earth know `தமிழர் வளம்`—the wealth of Tamils. The independent translation retains this political-economic cadence. The published witness generalizes it into improving `the standing of Tamilnad in international trade`.
- The verified Tamil's erotic and domestic imagery—`doorway of your lips`, the wife as a `standing lamp`, breath-of-life rhetoric, guava-red lips, the jackfruit thorn/fruit contrast, and `Do not kill me without killing me`—is preserved substantially in the independent translation. The published witness heavily abridges this poetic sequence.
- The source's language-centred metaphors are also reduced in the published witness. The independent translation preserves `refined Tamil`, the love/language interplay, and the lovers' Tamil-centred imagery rather than replacing it with generic romantic prose.
- The Tamil contains wordplay that is difficult to reproduce in English: `விண் மீன்கள் / புலால்`, `பிரியவில்லை / பிரியமில்லை`, and the emphatic `எண்ணிரண்டு பதினாறு கிழமை`. The independent translation preserves the semantic turns and records losses in notes. The published witness omits most of these source-specific rhetorical features and simply states a sixteen-week return.
- The verified Tamil's Vasanthamalai exchange asks whether there is any fault in Madhavi's beauty or dance, and Kovalan replies that a man worthy of both has not yet been born. The independent translation preserves this dramatic compliment. The published witness retains the broad idea but adds phrases such as `death sentence` and `altar of unrequited love`, which are translator/editorial intensifications rather than source wording.
- Kannagi actively sends Kovalan to Madhavi so that Madhavi's heart will not be hurt and even instructs him to promise to find her a good husband. The independent translation preserves the detailed instruction. The published witness keeps the humane motive but compresses the wording.
- The published witness closes with an added `vague sense of fear` haunting Kannagi as she watches the vehicle disappear. The verified Tamil has Kannagi watching the departing carriage but does not insert that explicit narrator-like foreboding. The independent translation correctly avoids importing later hindsight.

### Decision

**No action.** Scene 9 strongly confirms the value of the independent translation's source-near method: the published witness omits much of Kalaignar's classical-love framing, Tamil-language rhetoric, wordplay, erotic/domestic imagery and repetition, while also adding interpretive prose. No Tamil recheck or independent-translation review is required.
