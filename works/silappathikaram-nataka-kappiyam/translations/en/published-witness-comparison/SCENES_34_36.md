## Scene 34 — `வஞ்சினங் கூறுதல்` / `Uttering the Vow of Vengeance`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/34.md`, source scan 81;
- independent English: `translations/en/34.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **119–120**, section **`34 Kannagi Swears Vengeance`**.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Kannagi reaching Kovalan's body, an extended lament, memories of the final domestic farewell, her realization that his innocence must be publicly established, the blood-mark on her forehead, removal of the remaining anklet and her rush toward the Pandya palace.

### Significant differences

1. **Kannagi's repeated grief-address is heavily compressed.**
   - the verified Tamil repeatedly calls `அத்தான்` through the lament;
   - the independent translation preserves the recurring **`my dear`** beats;
   - the published witness turns the lament into much smoother continuous prose and removes much of that pounding repetition.

2. **Source-specific domestic imagery is rewritten.**
   - the source remembers akil smoke, Pothigai sandalwood, parrot-green leaf, fresh `kizhichal` lime, areca nut, taamboolam and Kovalan's red lips;
   - the published witness preserves some ingredients but adds new rhetoric such as the red of Kovalan's lips coursing through Kannagi's veins in `pulsations of sheer joy`, and a `dialogue that transcends spoken words`.

3. **Kovalan's established praise-string is replaced.**
   - source / independent translation preserve `stainless gold`, `right-spiralled pearl`, `flawless fragrance`, `sugarcane`, `honey`;
   - the published witness instead calls them an `exercise in imagery` that may have evaporated like dew when the stars set and sun rose. That reflective metaphor is not in the verified Tamil.

4. **The thief / heart-thief reversal is weakened.**
   - source / independent: **`You are no thief. O thief who stole my heart, you are no thief.`**;
   - the published witness reduces the public innocence argument and does not retain this same verbal reversal with Kalaignar's compact force.

5. **The target `மதுரை மூதார்` is silently interpreted.**
   - the verified Tamil prints `மதுரை மூதார்` twice; because its exact force is uncertain, the independent translation conservatively retains **`Madurai moothaar`**;
   - the published witness replaces this with **the city of Mathurai** and a `blot on its emblem`, thereby resolving an uncertain source form without acknowledgement.

6. **`பழிக்குப்பழி` is changed from vengeance to blood-retaliation.**
   - verified Tamil: `பழிக்குப்பழி! பழிக்குப்பழி!`;
   - independent translation: **`Vengeance for vengeance! Vengeance for vengeance!`**;
   - published witness: **`Blood for blood, blood for blood`**.

   Kannagi does use Kovalan's blood as witness and makes a blood-tilak, but the phrase itself is not `blood for blood` in the verified Tamil. The published rendering intensifies and alters the wording.

7. **The published witness adds narrator interpretation around the vow.**
   - it calls Kannagi's destruction of Mathurai her `sole mission` and frames the action in a more continuous revenge narrative;
   - the source retains a more abrupt shift from lament to vow, blood-tilak, anklet and tiger-like run toward the palace.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation remains closer to the verified Tamil in repetition, lexical uncertainty, inherited phraseology and the exact retaliatory wording.

## Scene 35 — `வழக்குரை படலம்` / `The Pleading of the Case`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/35.md`, source scans 82–85;
- independent English: `translations/en/35.md`;
- published witness: physical scans **121–125**, section **`35 Volcano in Court`**;
- the published witness then moves the closing destruction curse that is still part of verified Tamil Scene 35 into its section 36.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Kannagi entering the Pandya assembly, identifying herself through Poompuhar and remembered Chola justice, accusing Nedunchezhiyan of killing Kovalan without inquiry, challenging the king's right to judge his own case, presenting the anklet evidence, proving the ruby/pearl distinction, forcing the king to acknowledge Kovalan's innocence and precipitating the deaths of Nedunchezhiyan and Kopperundevi.

### Significant differences

1. **The published title is strongly interpretive.**
   - source / independent: **`The Pleading of the Case`**;
   - published witness: **`Volcano in Court`**.

   The source frames the scene as argument and adjudication; the published title foregrounds emotional spectacle instead.

2. **The published witness inserts an opening accusation not stated there in the verified Tamil.**
   - it has Kannagi address Nedunchezhiyan immediately as **`O Monarch who acts in haste!`** and adds courtiers shocked by the form of address while the king remains impassive;
   - the verified Tamil begins Kannagi's self-identification through Poompuhar and remembered rulers without this narrator-added court reaction.

3. **The Chola justice precedents are expanded from outside the printed scene.**
   - the source refers to a king who gave his body for fame and to Manu Needhi Chola placing his son beneath the chariot wheel to guard justice;
   - the independent translation deliberately does not import an unnamed king's identity or extra story detail;
   - the published witness adds a **dove in distress**, the **death of a calf**, a `little prince` and fuller story mechanics. Those may be culturally familiar, but they are not all printed in this verified dramatic passage.

4. **Tamil political-cultural accumulations are partly normalized.**
   - the independent translation preserves `three murasus / three Sangams / three banners / Muthamizh / three crowned kings` and the source's Tamil-wide rhetoric;
   - the published witness uses more generic Tamilnad framing and smoother English prose, reducing some of the compact rhetorical parallelism.

5. **The justice critique is modernized.**
   - source / independent preserve the antithesis **`good man / powerful man`** and distinct terms `justice / rectitude / fairness / rule / aram`;
   - the published witness renders this through formulations such as `law`, `rule of law`, `good governance`, `court of law`, `investigation or trial`, `verdict`, and related modern legal diction.

   Some legal force is source-supported, but the published edition's register is notably more modern and institutional than Kalaignar's royal-ethical vocabulary.

6. **The elders are given a more formal legal identity.**
   - the source calls upon the great/wise elders and removes the accused king from sole authority to pronounce judgment;
   - the independent translation keeps them as **great elders / judges** in the source situation without calling them a jury;
   - the published witness repeatedly uses `Court`, `House`, `Council`, `verdict`, and quasi-modern courtroom labels that make the scene resemble a later formal tribunal.

7. **The proof sequence receives additional narrator imagery.**
   - source: pearls scatter from the queen's old anklet; ruby pellets scatter from the other; Kannagi breaks her remaining anklet and rubies spill;
   - published witness adds rubies `red as blood-drops`, `consternation`, a `shower of rubies`, and other narrator embellishment absent from the verified stage text.

8. **Kannagi's post-proof denunciation is extensively rewritten.**
   - the source / independent translation preserve `Tamilakam laughs`, `the Tirukkural, the sacred-lore text of Tamil land, mocks you`, detailed martial emblems, repeated `Ruin!`, and repeated **`Weep! Weep! Weep!`**;
   - the published witness substitutes new rhetoric including the `cold wind of Truth`, the `verdict of history`, `a hundred swords`, and a `worm cast in the noonday sun`, while omitting or compressing several source-specific Tamil literary and martial formulations.

9. **The king's confession contains a major semantic alteration.**
   - verified Tamil makes a source-specific contrast between **`கொன்று வருக அவனை!`** and **`கொன்று வருக அவன்`**;
   - the independent translation deliberately preserves that awkward grammatical distinction as **`Kill and return—him!` / `Kill and return—he`** rather than inventing a different underlying command;
   - the published witness changes the confession to **`Kill and return (konru varugha)` instead of `Bring him here (kondu varugha)`**.

   The printed Tamil does **not** present a `konru / kondu` (`kill / bring`) contrast here. This is therefore a substantive secondary-witness reconstruction, not an authority for changing the verified Tamil or independent translation.

10. **The published section boundary cuts verified Tamil Scene 35 early.**
   - after Nedunchezhiyan and Kopperundevi die, the verified Tamil Scene 35 continues with Kannagi's curse for Madurapuri to perish and the first city-fire stage direction;
   - the published witness ends section 35 with Kopperundevi's fall and moves Kannagi's destruction curse to section 36.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The `konru / kondu` published confession is a major secondary-witness divergence, but the verified Tamil wording is already clear and therefore does not trigger a Tamil recheck.

## Scene 36 — `தீயினில் திருநகர்` / `The Sacred City in Fire`

### Alignment

- verified Tamil: `scenes/36.md`, source scan 86;
- independent English: `translations/en/36.md`;
- published witness: physical scan **126**, section **`36 Mathurai in Flames`**;
- published section 36 begins with destruction-curse material that belongs to the end of verified Tamil Scene 35.

### Material agreement

Both versions show Madurai consumed by fire and connect the destruction to the collapsed justice of the Pandya realm.

### Significant differences

1. **The published witness absorbs the closing curse from Scene 35.**
   - published section 36 begins with Kannagi's `Blood for blood! Death and destruction!` speech;
   - in the verified Tamil structure, the corresponding city-destruction curse belongs to the **end of Scene 35**, before Scene 36 begins.

2. **The actual opening of verified Tamil Scene 36 is omitted.**
   - source / independent translation begin with **the chief goldsmith's house burning**;
   - the goldsmith says **`Within me, Pandya justice fell that very day!`** and deliberately walks into the burning house, becoming embers;
   - the published witness does not preserve this confession and self-immolation as its own dramatic action.

3. **The source separates the fates of the goldsmith and the anklet-thief official; the published witness collapses them into a general fire-death list.**
   - source: the goldsmith consciously enters his burning house; after the `★` separator, the palace burns and the official who stole the anklet dies trapped in its ruins;
   - published witness says the chief goldsmith, the anklet-thief official and guardsmen are all `swallowed up by the flames`.

   This removes the goldsmith's explicit self-judgment and alters the causal staging of the official's death.

4. **The source-printed `★` structural separator disappears.**
   - the independent translation retains the printed `★` between the goldsmith's death and the palace-fire sequence;
   - the published witness has no equivalent structural marker.

5. **Kannagi's source-specific address to public silence is omitted.**
   - source / independent: **`People of Koodal city, who lay with your mouths shut when Kovalan was killed! Now cry out!`**;
   - the published witness does not retain this accusation against the public for silence during the killing.

6. **Kannagi's accelerating command-chain is greatly reduced.**
   - source / independent preserve commands to fire, wind and destruction, and repeat **`Vengeance for vengeance!`** three times;
   - the published witness instead gives broader narrator prose about fire, people fleeing and flames consuming them.

7. **The published witness adds Kannagi as the apparent leader of the fire.**
   - it says Kannagi `seems to lead the fire mile upon mile, until she leaves the boundary of Mathurai`;
   - the verified Tamil Scene 36 does not say she leads the fire through the city in that manner.

   This continues the published witness's Scene-33 tendency to give Kannagi quasi-supernatural or symbolic agency over the spreading flames.

8. **The source's final political stage statement is omitted.**
   - verified Tamil / independent translation: **`The people themselves wished for the destruction of the Pandya realm that had strayed from justice.`**;
   - the published witness does not retain this explicit statement of popular condemnation.

### Decision

**`no action`**.

The independent translation is substantially closer to the verified Tamil's scene boundary, individual accountability, public-silence accusation, command-chain and closing political statement.

## Batch conclusion

Scenes **34–36** are complete in the secondary-witness comparison.

- Tamil rechecks triggered: **0**
- independent translation reviews triggered: **0**
- decisions: **3 × `no action`**

Most important new findings:

- Scene 34 changes Kalaignar's `vengeance for vengeance` into the stronger **`blood for blood`** and silently resolves the uncertain `Madurai moothaar` form into the city itself;
- Scene 35 contains a major published reconstruction of the king's confession: **`konru varugha` vs `kondu varugha`**, whereas the verified Tamil actually contrasts `அவனை / அவன்` after the same `கொன்று வருக` command;
- the published witness splits the verified Scene-35 ending into section 36;
- Scene 36 omits the goldsmith's explicit self-confession/self-immolation, the Koodal public-silence accusation and the final statement that the people themselves wished the justice-failing Pandya realm destroyed.
