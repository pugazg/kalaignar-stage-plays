# Published-English Secondary-Witness Comparison — Scenes 16–18

Status: **complete**

Authority: verified Tamil remains controlling. The 2009 published English is a secondary witness only.

## Scene 16 — மணிமேகலை பிறப்பு / The Birth of Manimekalai

- Independent translation: `../16.md`
- Verified Tamil: `../../scenes/16.md`
- Published witness: physical scans **77–78**, section 16 `Birth of Manimekalai`
- Decision: **no action**

### Material agreement

Both English versions preserve the central sequence: Manimekalai's naming celebration, Kovalan naming the child through an extended praise speech, public giving/charity, the elephant crisis, Kovalan's intervention, and Madhavi's pride in his feat.

### Significant differences

The verified Tamil gives the naming speech as a highly specific cumulative chain: `பாலும் தெளிதேனும் பாகும் பருப்பும்` (milk, clear honey, sweet syrup and lentils), Pothigai breeze carrying sandalwood pollen, the child's laugh surpassing `kuzhal / yazh / kurali`, `குலக் கொடியே`, the claim that every movement of hand and foot is poetry, and the `மேகலை / மேலானகலை / மணிமேகலை` naming wordplay. The independent translation preserves this structure and retains uncertain technical terms conservatively. The published witness compresses the four-part food image to `milk and honey and sugar-candy`, generalizes the Pothigai/sandalwood image to a southern breeze bearing flower pollen, removes `kurali`, removes the lineage-shoot image and substantially reduces the `mekalai / higher art` naming wordplay.

The Tamil calls the celebration `நன்னிராட்டு விழா`; the independent version retains `nanniraattu` without imposing a more specific ritual definition. The published witness instead simply narrates a lavish cradle celebration.

The largest terminology difference occurs in the elephant episode. The verified Tamil says the elephant seizes `ஒரு மறையவனை`—a `மறையவன்`. The independent translation therefore uses **`a man of sacred lore`**, following the repository's permanent distinction among learned/religious-status terms. The published witness changes this to **`a brahmin`**, collapsing a distinction the Tamil itself preserves.

The published witness also expands the elephant action into a detailed physical sequence—trunk noose, intended dashing to the ground, hazardous manoeuvres and mahout recovery—that is not stated at that level of detail in the verified Tamil, which gives the action more compactly.

### Rhetorical / cultural finding

The independent translation better preserves the child-naming scene as an act of Tamil literary wordplay rather than generic parental praise. It also protects the source's social terminology rather than translating `மறையவன்` automatically as `Brahmin`.

**Result: no Tamil recheck and no independent-translation review required.**

## Scene 17 — மாசறு பொன்மகிழ்வு / Stainless Gold Rejoices

- Independent translation: `../17.md`
- Verified Tamil: `../../scenes/17.md`
- Published witness: physical scan **79**, section 17 `Valour Seen through Tears`
- Decision: **no action**

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve Devandhi reporting Kovalan's elephant feat, Kannagi's anxious concern for his safety, Devandhi's teasing reference to Madhavi tending him, and Kannagi's affectionate recognition of Madhavi as her sister.

### Significant differences

The decisive source line is Kannagi's response to Kovalan's feat: **`தமிழரல்லவா ?...`**. The independent translation preserves this directly as **`Is he not a Tamil ?...`**. The published witness replaces the line with the generic praise **`he is as valorous as he is compassionate`**. This removes Kalaignar's explicit linking of Kovalan's courage to Tamil identity.

The independent translation also keeps Kalaignar's stage-direction image `அழுகையும், புன்னகையும் போட்டியிட` as **`As tears and smiles compete with one another`**. The published witness conveys the mixed emotion more conventionally as `in tears and in smiles`.

Kannagi's `என் தங்கை பாக்கியசாலி` is preserved in the independent version as **`My younger sister is fortunate`**. The published witness keeps the sister relation but generalizes it to `My sister is a blessed girl`, losing the explicit younger-sister relation.

The published witness adds an ending in which Kannagi looks at Kovalan's portrait and `ministers to him in her fancy`; the verified Tamil instead ends with her tears swelling as she leans against the portrait. The added imaginative nursing action is not source-supported.

### Rhetorical / cultural finding

This short scene provides unusually clear evidence that the secondary witness can neutralize explicit Tamil-identity rhetoric even while preserving the dramatic outline. The independent translation's direct preservation of `Is he not a Tamil?` is therefore confirmed.

**Result: no Tamil recheck and no independent-translation review required.**

## Scene 18 — இந்திர விழா-இருமனைகளில் / Indra Festival — In Two Households

- Independent translation: `../18.md`
- Verified Tamil: `../../scenes/18.md`
- Published witness: physical scans **80–81**, section 18 `Two Sides of the Festival`, plus physical scan **82**, opening of section 19 `Melody Breaks`
- Decision: **no action**

### Alignment note

This scene demonstrates why comparison cannot rely mechanically on published section numbering. The verified Tamil Scene 18 continues through the pre-dawn Madhavi/Kovalan bedchamber exchange and Vasanthamalai's instructions for the seashore setup. In the 2009 witness, that closing part has already been moved under published section **19 `Melody Breaks`** on scan 82.

### Festival framing

The verified Tamil explicitly raises a towering statue of **`பழந்தமிழ் மன்னன் இந்திரன்`**—`Indran, the ancient Tamil king`. The independent translation preserves that exact historicizing formulation. The published witness instead describes `the renowned Chola ancestors, Indra and Karikalan`, removing both **`ancient Tamil`** and the specific king formulation.

The Tamil also names `பட்டி மண்டபம்`, `முத்துப் பந்தர்`, `தோரண வாயில்`, the ship procession, dance and song, and celebrations in many places and forms. The published witness introduces an **army march-past** that is not stated in the verified Tamil.

### Madhavi / Manimekalai sequence

Both versions preserve Madhavi saying that Manimekalai resembles Kannagi and that she thinks of the child as Kannagi's own. The independent translation retains `elder sister Kannagi` and the source's sisterhood framing. The published witness broadly preserves this, though in smoother prose.

### Kannagi's festival distress

Kalaignar's Tamil makes the public festival physically assault Kannagi's senses: sound `tears at` her ears, fireworks seize her eyes, women's dancing becomes a spirit-dance, and music sounds like owls/night-birds. The independent translation preserves that violent sensory progression. The published witness compresses it to `cacophony`, memory and despair, and adds environmental narration such as the house being `dilapidated and shorn of light` that is not stated in this scene's verified Tamil.

The Tamil then gives the escalating direct call `அத்தான்! / அத்தான்!! / அத்தான்!!!`; the independent translation retains all three calls and punctuation. The published witness reduces this to a single prose appeal before Kannagi goes out.

### Devandhi's restraint speech

A particularly important source phrase is **`மானங் காக்கும் தமிழச்சி`**. The independent translation preserves it contextually as **`Tamil woman who guards honour`**, deliberately not forcing `மானம்` into movement-specific `self-respect`. The published witness removes the explicit **Tamil woman / தமிழச்சி** identity and recasts the appeal as generic noble-family background and respectability.

The published witness also adds strong narrator framing—Devandhi `grips her shoulders, none too gently` and `almost drags her along`—where the Tamil simply has Devandhi take Kannagi's hand and lead her away while Kannagi turns like a lifeless object.

### Closing pre-dawn sequence

The independent translation keeps the verified Tamil's twenty-eight-day festival closure, tiger-banner lowering, rooster and hour-conch, Madhavi waking Kovalan with the yazh, the named **`Kaanal Vari`**, and the detailed punnai-tree / fresh-sand / white-legged couch / painted-screen setup.

The 2009 witness moves this material into section 19, calls the yazh a generic `harp`, converts `Kaanal Vari` to a `seaside lyric`, and reduces the detailed source setting to a settee beneath a flowering tree with silk drapes. This is a clear case where preserving the named Tamil poetic form and source-specific staging is preferable to generic English domestication.

### Rhetorical / cultural finding

Scene 18 contains three source features the secondary witness repeatedly weakens: Tamil historical framing (`ancient Tamil king`), explicit Tamil-woman identity (`தமிழச்சி`), and named Tamil literary/performance forms (`Kaanal Vari`, `yazh`). The independent translation correctly preserves all three while keeping interpretive Dravidian-movement commentary outside dialogue.

**Result: no Tamil recheck and no independent-translation review required.**
