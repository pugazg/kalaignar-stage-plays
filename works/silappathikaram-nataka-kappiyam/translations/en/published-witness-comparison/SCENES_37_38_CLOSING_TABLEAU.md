## Scene 37 — `வடபுலப் படையெடுப்பு` / `Campaign into the Northern Land`

### Alignment

Verified Tamil Scene 37 is the Cheran Senguttuvan court mobilization speech on scan 87. The corresponding 2009 material appears primarily under published section 37, `March to the North`, scans 127–128.

The published section begins with an additional Periyar-bank scene featuring Senguttuvan and Venmaal before shifting to Vanchi. That opening is not part of the verified Tamil Scene 37 and is therefore treated as published-edition restructuring/addition for this alignment.

### Material agreement

Both versions retain the broad dramatic movement:

- northern rulers have mocked Tamil dynastic emblems;
- Senguttuvan treats the insult as a Tamil collective affront;
- Ganga/Cauvery opposition frames the challenge;
- Valluvar, Sathanar, Ilango, Paranar and Tamil cultural achievement are invoked;
- the army is mobilized for the northern campaign.

### Significant differences

The independent translation preserves the source's much denser rhetorical architecture:

- `Tamilakam` rather than the published witness's repeated `Tamilnad`;
- the tiger / bow / fish insult-chain with three distinct mocking characterizations;
- the comic name-sequence `Utthiram—Visithiram—Sivetham—Bairavam` followed by the explicit joke that they are merely men's names;
- `frog-kings` for `மண்டுக மன்னர்கள்`;
- the priest's precise question: why Senguttuvan alone is angry when Chola and Pandya are not;
- Senguttuvan's answer that **Chola and Pandya may be his enemies**, yet outsiders will not be allowed to insult `my Tamil`;
- the long roll-call of Tamil poets, rulers, places, scholars, artists and historical memory;
- `Nedunchezhiyan who overcame the Aryan army`;
- the fort/place-name sequence;
- `chamara-bearers`;
- the repeated death/battle imperatives;
- the source's explicit **`சுயமரியாதை` → `self-respect`**;
- `Beat the murasu! Strike the Himalaya!` and the repeated `The Tamil has never been defeated` close.

The published witness substantially compresses this roll-call and substitutes generalized formulations such as `culture and civilization`, `code supreme`, `Victory or death`, and `honour is dearer to us than life`. Those formulations convey a similar broad martial mood but are not the source's actual lexical/rhetorical sequence.

A particularly important semantic difference is the handling of Chola and Pandya. The verified Tamil says they **may be Senguttuvan's enemies** but that internal rivalry does not permit outsiders to insult Tamil. The published witness instead says the `brother-kings` may simply not be in a position to react militantly. That changes Kalaignar's sharper internal-rivalry-versus-Tamil-unity argument.

The published witness also does not preserve the explicit `self-respect` line. Because `சுயமரியாதை` is actually printed in the verified Tamil, the independent translation's direct `self-respect` is confirmed.

### Decision

**No action.**

No Tamil recheck is required. No independent-translation re-review is required.

## Scene 38 — `கண்ணகி சிலைக்குக் கல்` / `Stone for Kannagi's Statue`

### Alignment

Verified Tamil Scene 38 is printed on scan 88 at Kuyilaluvam in the Himalaya. The corresponding published witness is section 38, `Mission Accomplished`, scans 129–130.

### Material agreement

Both versions preserve:

- Senguttuvan's victory;
- Kuyilaluvam;
- Kanaka/Vijaya fleeing in ascetic/saffron disguise;
- their capture;
- the 18 years → 18 months → 18 days → 18 naazhigai sequence;
- refusal of personal royal pardon;
- return after thirty-two months;
- taking Himalayan stone for Kannagi's statue;
- making the defeated rulers carry the stone.

### Significant differences

The independent translation preserves several source-specific images and taunts that the published witness smooths or replaces:

- `all these rocks are pages inscribed by the valour of verdant Tamil` (`பைந்தமிழ் வீரம் தீட்டிய ஏடுகள்`), whereas the published witness substitutes the more generic idea that Kuyilaluvam will be `enshrined in military history`;
- the repeated `Kanaka-Vijayar! Kanaka-Vijayar!` and Senguttuvan's sarcastic chain `showing your backs—covering your heads—attacking from hiding—turning into mahaans!`;
- `rishis` / `mahaans` as source-visible satire;
- the direct command to kneel on the **soil of Tamil land** and let **that** forgive them;
- later `Tamilakam` as distinct source vocabulary;
- the defeated rulers described as the `right bullocks` to carry the stone;
- `You threw a word against the valour of the Tamils; now lift the stone!`.

The published witness adds interpretive language not stated in the Tamil, including `renunciation in the heat of the battle`, `weight of their penance`, and `magnanimity of our land`. It also generalizes the source's Tamil-land distinction into `soil of the South` in one place and uses the edition-specific `Taminad` elsewhere.

The source's punishment is deliberately concrete and mocking: the men who insulted Tamil valour must carry the statue-stone. The independent translation retains that direct force without adding a theological/moral `penance` framework.

### Decision

**No action.**

No Tamil recheck is required. No independent-translation re-review is required.

## Unnumbered closing tableau — `கண்ணகி சிலை நாட்டு விழா`

### Alignment

The verified Tamil has a structurally separate, unnumbered tableau after Scene 38 and three printed `*` marks. It is not Scene 39.

The 2009 published witness continues on scan 130 after its own three-star separator with a prose description of Kannagi's installed statue and Madhavi bringing Manimekalai.

### Material agreement

Both witnesses support the core closing action:

- Kannagi's statue has become the centre of a public commemorative gathering;
- kings and poets are present;
- Madhavi appears in renunciant/nun-like dress;
- she brings Manimekalai;
- Manimekalai is placed at Kannagi's feet.

### Source-obstruction rule confirmed

The controlling Tamil scan has a later library/accession stamp physically obscuring the leading characters of two tableau lines. The verified archive therefore preserves:

- `⟦later library stamp obscures leading letters⟧ங்குட்டுவன்`
- `⟦later library stamp obscures leading letters⟧ங்கோவடிகள்`

The 2009 published English explicitly supplies **Senguttuvan** and **Ilango** in its prose. That secondary witness may make those reconstructions contextually plausible, but it **must not be used to fill the controlling Tamil obstruction**. The archival rule is therefore confirmed: leave the obscured Tamil unresolved.

### Published additions / expansions

The published witness adds substantial closing narration not present in the surviving verified Tamil tableau, including:

- a `vast concourse`;
- rulers of Malava and Kongu;
- an interpretive description of the statue as a `deity`;
- Manimekalai gazing in wonder;
- the retrospective claim that she does not yet know she will become the heroine of Sathanar's sequel to Ilango's *Silappathikaram*.

Those may belong to the published translator/editor's framing or to material not visible in the controlling Tamil witness, but the supplied Tamil scan does not support importing them into the canonical tableau translation.

The independent translation correctly remains minimal and source-bounded: kings, poets, Madhavi in the garb of renunciation, tears, Manimekalai, and placement at Kannagi's feet.

### Decision

**No action.**

The secondary witness specifically confirms why the obscured names must remain unresolved in the canonical Tamil/English archive rather than being silently reconstructed.
