# Scene 32

## Kovalan's Blood on Madurai Soil

### Mandapam on the Street

[The goldsmith, the official and soldiers come to the mandapam where Kovalan is waiting.]

Kovalan : Goldsmith, the matter you went for was successful, was it not?

Chief Goldsmith : The matter I went for was successful! But the matter you came for has ended in failure.

Kovalan : Then the king has no need of the anklet?

Chief Goldsmith : Why would the king need an anklet? Is it not the queen who needs it?

Kovalan : [Not understanding] Goldsmith!—

Chief Goldsmith : Give the anklet to him.

[Kovalan gives the anklet to the official.]

Official : How did you come by this anklet?

Kovalan : It is my wife's anklet!

Official : Why do you need to sell it?

Kovalan : A question the buyer has no need to ask!

Official : A skilful thief!

Kovalan : [Startled]... T h i e f... Goldsmith! Why this accusation against me? What injustice is this?

Chief Goldsmith : Was stealing the queen's anklet alone justice, then?

Kovalan : Who stole your queen's anklet? Are those who lack the ability to discover that... going to cast the blame upon a man merely passing along the road?

Official : Shut your mouth! The king trembles... he quakes... to hear that into the Pandya realm, where not even the shadow of the five great sins can enter, a sin has entered in your very form!

Kovalan : I shall see that king. Officer, I am no thief.... Because reason failed me, I left the jewel-faced woman whose hand I took drowning in tears; mountains of gold, mountains of diamonds, mountains of silver beyond counting—I destroyed them all and came to another land seeking a way to live: that is the great sinner I am! My cool-graced woman of refined Tamil... a great feast to the mind... I shall explain that I am the cruel man who has even dared to sell the anklet she wore upon her red-lotus feet. Sir! This is a heart steeped in Tamil... I was conceived and formed in the womb of Mother Tamil and crawled upon the soil of this noble land... I was not fashioned with a darkened mind to carry on the trade of theft—that is what I say. I am certain that in the royal court of the great king Nedunchezhiyan, praised by every land, I shall obtain justice. Take me to the king at once!

Official : What, is the king the watchman of your house?

Kovalan : Guardian of the land! Guardian of justice—rectitude—aram—love—Tamil is Nedunchezhiyan! I have heard him praised as one who would not allow even half an atom of injustice to take place under his rule. Let that great Pandya king look upon my face! Will he see the marks of a thief? Will he point out the shadow of a stain? Before Nedunchezhiyan, the crowned king who pronounces justice by the signs of a face,

I shall stand. I shall accept his decisions. Take me to the king!

Official : You crafty thief; he will not even raise his eyes to look upon the faces of sinners like you.

Kovalan : Why—one who looks upon your face will not look upon mine?

Chief Goldsmith : Tell me, son, why so much sharpness of tongue? Not only stealing, but arrogant argument as well? Stop answering back and tell the truth!

Kovalan : Goldsmith, am I a thief?

Chief Goldsmith : A thief indeed; you are a thief!

Official : Yes; you are a thief indeed.

Kovalan : A thief; I am a thief! Kannagi, your husband is a thief! My gold of ten-and-a-half assay, that is the verdict Pandya land gives. I am a thief, my dear! Appeyo! Kannagi, you sent me away with tears overflowing from your eyes! Did you somehow know, my precious one, that such a stain would come upon me?... O honey that tastes sweet! A fitting reward for the wretch who cast you into the dust of the street like a veena! Thief! Thief! Thief! Ayyo! Your heart has already withered in longing—how will it bear the news that this degrading word has fallen upon me? Kannagi! Why did you marry me? You thought you might know happiness! I turned your life into a volcano! As though that were not enough, I have made you vulnerable to the townspeople taunting you: “Kannagi, wife of a thief!” O morning star! Peerless gem! I should have been sleeping in your lap, seated in the tower-pavilion of a storied mansion while you plucked the makara-yazh and the gentle breeze lulled us; instead, in the great city of Madurai, struck by the arrow called `thief`, I cry out; I lament; I am a thief! You tell me—am I a thief? Am I a thief?

Official : Hey; your life ends here.

[The commander's sword drives into Kovalan's neck.]

## Translation notes

- `மதுரை மண்ணில் கோவலன் குருதி` is rendered **`Kovalan's Blood on Madurai Soil`**, preserving Kalaignar's deliberate joining of the individual killing to the political-geographical ground on which it occurs.
- The scene's accusation sequence retains the spaced `தி ரு ட ன்` as **`T h i e f`**, so Kovalan's shock is audible rather than normalized.
- `பஞ்சமா பாதங்கள்` is rendered contextually as **`the five great sins`**. The translation does not add a doctrinal list or identify a religious system not named by the scene.
- Kovalan's wealth-collapse catalogue remains mountains of gold, diamonds and silver. It is not reduced to a summary such as `I lost my fortune`.
- `சீதளச் செந்தமிழாள்` is rendered **`my cool-graced woman of refined Tamil`**. The unusual image is kept elevated and Tamil-centred rather than smoothed to a generic `beloved wife`.
- `தமிழ் படிந்த நெஞ்சமிது` becomes **`This is a heart steeped in Tamil`**; `தமிழ்த் தாயின் வயிற்றினிலே...` retains **Mother Tamil** and the image of being conceived, formed and crawling upon the land's soil.
- `இருட்டு மதி ... திருட்டுத் தொழில்` preserves Kalaignar's dark-mind/theft-trade rhetorical pairing as **`a darkened mind ... the trade of theft`**.
- The central public-rhetorical accumulation is preserved: **`Guardian of the land! Guardian of justice—rectitude—aram—love—Tamil is Nedunchezhiyan!`** `அறம்` is retained as `aram` under the project's established rule where its ethical breadth matters.
- `அநீதி, அணுவிலோர் பாதி` becomes **`even half an atom of injustice`**, preserving the microscopic hyperbole rather than flattening it to `any injustice`.
- `முகக்குறியால் நீதி சொல்லும்` is translated conservatively as **`pronounces justice by the signs of a face`**. The English does not replace Kalaignar's royal discernment image with modern evidentiary language.
- `பத்தரை மாற்றுத் தங்கமே` is rendered **`my gold of ten-and-a-half assay`**. The traditional purity/assay metaphor is preserved without speculative conversion to modern karats.
- Source exclamation `அப்பேயோ!` is retained as **`Appeyo!`** rather than silently replaced by a different printed exclamation; the later explicit `அய்யோ!` remains `Ayyo!`.
- `மகர யாழ்` is retained as **`makara-yazh`**, preserving the named Tamil instrument.
- `கள்வன் எனும் கணை` remains **`the arrow called thief`**, maintaining Kalaignar's metaphorical violence before the physical sword-blow.
- The scene's final stage direction is translated directly: the commander's sword enters Kovalan's neck. No trial, confession, resistance or additional action is invented.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 32 turns the tragedy into an explicit crisis of justice and Tamil political morality. Kovalan does not merely plead personal innocence. He invokes a heart steeped in Tamil, Mother Tamil, the soil of the land, and Nedunchezhiyan as guardian of **justice, rectitude, aram, love and Tamil**. His faith is that a ruler praised for justice will hear him and distinguish accusation from guilt.

The dramatic answer is devastating: the ruler never sees him. An official and the goldsmith name him a thief, the king's prior order has already converted accusation into death, and Kovalan is killed while still demanding access to justice. The title of scene 31—`The Sceptre Bent`—therefore reaches its material consequence here: Kovalan's blood falls on Madurai soil.

This strongly resonates with Dravidian movement themes of accountable power, justice, Tamil ethical authority, opposition to humiliation and the dignity of the accused. Yet the translation does not retrofit modern rights-language or constitutional procedure into Kovalan's speech. Kalaignar's own vocabulary—Tamil, justice, rectitude, aram, love, ruler, land and injustice—carries the critique.

Kovalan's final lament also returns repeatedly to Kannagi. The state-inflicted label `thief` becomes a social injury he fears will be transferred to her as `wife of a thief`. Kalaignar thus presents arbitrary power not only as bodily violence against an individual, but as humiliation capable of spreading through intimate and social relationships.
