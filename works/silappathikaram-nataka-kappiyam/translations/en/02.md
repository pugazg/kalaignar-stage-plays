# Scene 2

## Senguttuvan's Birthday Celebration

### Vanchi

[Senguttuvan's private upper chamber. His consort Venmal is adorning him.]

Senguttuvan : Venmal! You deck me in festive array and send me to the royal audience hall. Will not a time come when, in just this way, you must deck me in battle array and send me to the field?

Venmal : Why not; are not those who win wounds of valour the warriors! For the wounds that come upon the field, I keep the medicine upon my lips!

Senguttuvan : I see! Every year, it seems, the birthday celebration comes only to us...

Venmal : Are you saying no child has been born to us?.... Ask the soothsayer who comes to the royal audience hall today for his prophecy—when will you become a father!

[Just then Ilango enters joyfully]

Ilango : A prophecy? From the soothsayer? Sister-in-law, do you too believe his lies?

Senguttuvan : [A smile spreading] Ilango?

Ilango : It is time for the celebration, elder brother! Come, let us go!

Senguttuvan : I was ready long ago! It is your sister-in-law who keeps adding polish to the adornment!

Ilango : Sister-in-law! Give brother leave and send him along! I shall wait outside at the entrance, brother!

Senguttuvan : Wait, wait, Ilango! I am coming too! You little rogue!... Venmal!...

[With her eyes Venmal conveys, “I will come.” She watches Senguttuvan leave the private upper chamber with his younger brother, and lets out a long, happy sigh.]

## Translation notes

- The Tamil deliberately pairs `விழாக்கோலம்` and `போர்க் கோலம்`; the English retains that antithesis as `festive array` / `battle array` rather than flattening both to ordinary clothing.
- `விழுப்புண்` is rendered as `wounds of valour`, retaining the heroic prestige attached to battle wounds. Venmal's following `மருந்து` remains `medicine`, allowing Kalaignar's flirtatious implication—her lips as the cure—to work without explanatory paraphrase.
- `கொலு மண்டபம்` is rendered as `royal audience hall`, preserving its courtly function without importing a modern administrative term.
- `நிமித்திகன்` is rendered as `soothsayer`, and `ஆருடம்` as `prophecy` in this scene. These choices keep Ilango's ridicule immediately intelligible and will be reviewed for consistency in scene 3, where the argument over fate becomes central.
- `அண்ணி` is retained relationally as `Sister-in-law`; `அண்ணா` is `elder brother` / `brother` according to the dramatic beat.
- `குறும்புக்காரா!` is translated as the affectionate `You little rogue!`, preserving family teasing rather than turning it into genuine rebuke.
- The translation preserves the movement from flirtation → childlessness joke → Ilango's sceptical interruption → affectionate family exit. It does not make the intimate dialogue more formal than Kalaignar's Tamil.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The most direct movement-era resonance in this scene lies in Ilango's response to divination: `ஆருடமா? நிமித்திகனிடமா? அவன் பொய்யுரைகளே நீங்களும் நம்புகிறீர்களா அண்ணி?` He does not merely doubt a particular prediction; Kalaignar gives him a quick, mocking challenge to the authority of the soothsayer and calls the man's claims `lies`.

That dramatic choice resonates strongly with the **rationalist and anti-superstition current of the Dravidian movement**, especially its insistence that inherited or ritual authority should be questioned rather than accepted simply because it claims access to fate. The resonance becomes more important because the following scene develops the conflict between fate and human agency much more explicitly.

At the same time, this remains a warm domestic scene. Venmal is witty, flirtatious and verbally equal to Senguttuvan, while Ilango's scepticism arrives through family banter rather than a political lecture. The translation therefore keeps the humour and intimacy in the dialogue and places the rationalist connection only here, in the interpretive note; it does not insert words such as `rationalism`, `superstition`, `self-respect` or `Dravidian` into the characters' speech.
