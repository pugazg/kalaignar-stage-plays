# Scene 29

## The Sound of a Single Anklet

### Madhari's House

[Kannagi is at work cooking in the kitchen. Kovalan sits dejected. Kannagi comes to Kovalan.]

Kannagi : My dear!

Kovalan : Hmm!

Kannagi : Why are you so dejected?

Kovalan : I am searching for a way to begin trading again!

Kannagi : We have many relatives in Madurai; they sell Korkai pearls and build houses like forts. Will none of them help us?

Kovalan : When I refuse even to seek such help from my father... or from your father... am I to go and seek it from relatives?

Kannagi : What other way is there?

Kovalan : It is not as though there are no ways to trade in the Pandya region! From the ports of Thondi and Korkai, conches and pearls, and silk-thread garments made from rat hair, are exported to foreign lands. There is the craft of cutting conches into bangles; the craft of weaving gold-coloured cloth; the craft of producing salt! There are many such trades in Pandya land! But to enter any trade, one needs capital!

Kannagi : My dear! I shall tell you one way—you will not be angry, will you?

Kovalan : What is it, Kannagi?

Kannagi : Those beautiful anklets you bought for me as my wedding gift for fifty thousand kalanju of gold! One of them...

Kovalan : The anklets? Kannagi! Sell the anklets? Ayyo; has the day come when these ears of mine must hear such words! Drums, conches and murasus resounded; rows of white-gold parasols shone like a king's procession; on that sweet day when we celebrated our wedding, you wore those anklets—and now, my life, you speak of destroying that adornment... I am the cause! I am the cause!!

Kannagi : Why should an ornament waste away if it cannot be of use to you?

Kovalan : Upon your lotus-flower feet it rings `kanir, kanir`, hammering into me again and again that this is the one ornament this wretch has until now left upon you without removing... and you ask me to sell that beautiful anklet?

Ayyo... what a time of hardship has come upon us!

Kannagi : You have done no harm to anyone. You will not suffer!

Kovalan : But putting everything together, I have done great harm to you, Kannagi!

Kannagi : Do not let your heart lament over everything that has gone!

Kovalan : Kannagi!

Kannagi : Come, let us eat! It has been so many days since I cooked with my own hands and served you food! Come!

[She touches his hand and calls him.]

[He rises.]

[She serves food. Kovalan eats.]

Kannagi : How is the cooking?

Kovalan : It tastes a little better than usual! Kannagi! This is your last cooking!

Kannagi : Why?

Kovalan : Once I begin trading again, I shall not let you cook after that. A separate person for the kitchen! A separate world for just the two of us! Is that right?

[Kannagi lowers her head shyly...]

Kannagi : All right, all right! Do not build castles already! Eat!

[Kannagi spreads lime on a betel leaf; folds it; gives her husband taamboolam.]

Kannagi : From the money we get by selling the anklet, after keeping what is needed for trade, buy yourself the silk garments and ornaments you want with what remains! It has been a long time since I adorned you and looked upon your beauty!

[Teasingly]

Kovalan : Hmm!

[Shyly]

Kannagi : Hmm!

[At that moment Aiyai runs inside]

Aiyai : Brother! Brother!

Kovalan : What is it, child?

Aiyai : Do not take sister-in-law away with you to the capital!

Kovalan : Why?

Aiyai : All of us are going to perform the kuravai koothu... My friends and I want sister-in-law to watch it!

Kovalan : Kannagi is not coming with me, child! I alone am going to the capital. Conduct your kuravai koothu with joy!

Aiyai : All right, brother! Sister-in-law! Be ready; in a little while we shall come to call you!

[Kannagi nods; Aiyai leaves.]

Kovalan : Kannagi! I am setting out for the capital!

Kannagi : Here!

[She moves to remove the anklets. He stops her.]

Kovalan : No, Kannagi! Ayyo... seeing your hand touch the anklet feels as though my head is being cut off!

Kannagi : Will you not listen to what I say, my dear? Once the trade we begin starts to prosper, we can buy any number of anklets like these!

[She moves to remove them]

Kovalan : Wait, wait, Kannagi! While the anklets are still upon your feet, walk once for me! Let me delight in that sight one last time.

Kannagi : My dear?

Kovalan : Yes, Kannagi! Once the anklet is sold, can I see that sight again? Come, walk! Walk, my dear!

[Tears fill Kannagi's eyes!]

[She walks]

[Kovalan's eyes become pools]

Kannagi : My dear! You are saying all sorts of things; I am afraid!

[Weeping, they embrace]

Kovalan : Do not be afraid, Kannagi! Are you not the one who keeps telling me that good times will come to us?

[In a trembling voice]

Kannagi : Good times will certainly come, my dear... they will!

Kovalan : All right, it is getting late!

[Kannagi removes one anklet; when she is about to remove the other]

Kovalan : Kannagi, do not sell both! Give me only one!

Kannagi : Only one?

Kovalan : Yes; let one anklet remain upon your foot! Whenever that single anklet raises its sound... let thousands upon thousands of hammer-blows keep falling upon the heart of this madman who wished harm upon you, a noble woman!

“Petty wretch! Mindless husk! `aavi-nigar meeniyaale aalavitta karumbaakki`! Cruel sinner who tortured her atom by atom! Murderer!”—let that single anklet sing such a poem at me; let the earth hear it. Perhaps then some sense will come to this mean-minded man; Kannagi, let sense come to me!

Kannagi : My dear! Did this sinner show you a way so that the tears shed by your blue eyes might soak your beautiful face? Do not worry, my dear! A future full of hope shines its light toward us.

Kovalan : Shall I go and return, my dear?

Kannagi : [Wiping her own eyes and his] Come now! Without worry,

with happiness—come! Come! That gentle smile that enchants me! Just once, my dear!

Kovalan : [With a smile] Kannagi!

Kannagi : My dear! Come back soon!

Kovalan : Are you afraid I shall go away and never return, as when I went to Madhavi's house?

Kannagi : But that is just it—you came back...

Kovalan : All right, Kannagi! It is getting late.

Kannagi : My dear, this is a town you do not know very well. Go safely and come back!

Kovalan : You speak as though you are speaking to a child?

Kannagi : Was I not just about to say that too!

Kovalan : To a child! Hmm; shall I go then, Kannagi!

[Teasingly]

Kannagi : My dear!

Kovalan : Kannagi!

[The sound of the kuravai koothu having begun is heard]

Kovalan : The kuravai koothu has begun; your invitation will arrive now. I shall go.

[As Kovalan moves away, Madhari gives Kannagi a tray containing flowers, a mango and fruit.]

Kannagi : My dear! Come back before this jasmine flower withers!

Kovalan : So soon? Impossible! I shall return before this mango rots!

Kannagi : Will it take that many days?

Kovalan : Hmm... Kannagi! To sell the anklet, arrange the trade... will all those efforts not take a few days?

[Madhari comes. To her]

Amma! Kannagi is under your protection.

[He takes Kannagi's hand and places it in hers]

To put it briefly, my life is in the protection of your house. I shall go, Kannagi! I shall go, Amma!

[Aiyai and the others run to Kannagi and pull her away to the kuravai koothu.]

## Translation notes

- `ஒற்றைச் சிலம்பின் ஒலி` is rendered **`The Sound of a Single Anklet`**, preserving the source's personification of the remaining anklet as moral voice and memory.
- The Pandya trade catalogue is translated from Kalaignar's verified wording as printed. The unusual `எலி மயிரினால் செய்யப்பட்ட பட்டு நூல் ஆடைகள்` is therefore rendered conservatively as **`silk-thread garments made from rat hair`**; the Tamil is not silently corrected from outside commercial history.
- `பொருள்` in the trade discussion is rendered **`capital`** because Kannagi and Kovalan are explicitly discussing the material means required to restart commerce.
- The historical value `ஐம்பதாயிரம் கழஞ்சு பொன்` remains **`fifty thousand kalanju of gold`**; no modern monetary conversion is attempted.
- The anklet sound `கணீர் கணீர்` is retained as **`kanir, kanir`** rather than replaced by a generic `jingle`, allowing Kalaignar's audible repetition to remain present.
- The protected source sequence `கழற்றுமல் / வீட்டு வைத்திருக்கும்` is not corrected in Tamil. The English conveys the immediate sense—that this is the one ornament Kovalan has left upon Kannagi—without claiming an emended Tamil reading.
- The domestic sequence—food, teasing, betel leaf/lime and `தாம்பூலம்`—is preserved. `தாம்பூலம்` is retained as **`taamboolam`** because the immediately preceding stage direction already shows the betel preparation.
- `குரவைக் கூத்து` is retained as **`kuravai koothu`**, a named cultural performance rather than a generic `dance`.
- Kovalan's request that Kannagi walk once while both anklets are still on her feet, and his decision to sell only one, are preserved without retrospective foreshadowing.
- The verified insult-string `ஆவிநிகர் மீனியாளே ஆளவிட்ட கரும்பாக்கி` is semantically source-opaque. Rather than reconstruct it from another edition or guess at a smoother meaning, the English retains it transliterated as **`aavi-nigar meeniyaale aalavitta karumbaakki`** inside Kovalan's self-condemning list. The surrounding securely intelligible insults are translated normally.
- `அணு அணுவாய்ச்` retains the source's microscopic accumulation as **`atom by atom`**.
- `அந்த ஒற்றைச் சிலம்பு என்னைக் கவி பாடட்டும்; அதைப் புவி கேட்கட்டும்` remains **`let that single anklet sing such a poem at me; let the earth hear it`**. The anklet's moral voice is not reduced to explanatory prose.
- The scan-74→75 same-speaker continuation after `கவலைப்படாமல்,` is preserved continuously; no extra Kannagi label is inserted.
- The jasmine/mango exchange retains Kalaignar's light comic timing immediately before Kovalan entrusts Kannagi to Madhari.
- `அடைக்கலம்` in the closing exchange is rendered as **`protection`**, consistent with scene 27's entrustment context.
- The anomalous printed `8` on scan 73 and the archaeological caption on scan 75 remain provenance only and are not turned into dramatic English.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Kannagi's anklet is explicitly treated as usable economic capital. She proposes selling her own marriage ornament so that commerce can restart, argues that ornament has no value if it cannot serve their life, plans how the remaining money should be spent, and repeatedly encourages Kovalan toward a future built through renewed work. That gives her material and economic agency rather than reducing the anklet to passive symbolism.

Kovalan, meanwhile, refuses to blame fate for his condition. The single anklet becomes an imagined public accuser that is to strike his conscience with `thousands upon thousands of hammer-blows` and sing his wrongdoing to the earth. This continues the play's human-responsibility current: ethical correction comes through memory, conscience and action.

The domestic scenes also matter. Cooking, adornment, teasing, taamboolam, the kuravai koothu and the jasmine/mango joke place Tamil cultural life inside ordinary intimacy rather than only in courts, kings and heroic declarations. These elements can resonate with Dravidian movement emphases on lived Tamil culture, women's agency and dignity of productive life, but the translation does not insert later movement terminology into Kannagi or Kovalan's dialogue.
