# Scene 7

## Madhavi, Queen of the Arts

### Pearl Pavilion

[Madhavi's dance is in progress...
The Chola king, members of the royal line, women of the palace, and great merchant-lords are seated.

Madhavi's mother Chitrapathi, the hunchbacked woman, and her friend Vasanthamalai sit at the front of the stage. Kovalan, Kannagi and the elderly Greek enter the pavilion and take their appointed seats.

Madhavi dances in a way that fills the assembly, the king and the others with wonder and delight. The elderly Greek is overcome by the dance.]

[In between]

Elderly Greek : Kovalan! Is that a painting?... a statue?.... Is she truly a living woman?...

Kovalan : Her name is Madhavi—the woman who can awaken the spring of youth even in your heart, browned and ripened by age. Even I am seeing her for the very first time today!...

[The dance continues and ends amid the praise of the assembly........

Madhavi steps down from the stage, comes before the Chola king and bows...]

Chola King : Number and letter, the five iyal,
the four pann,
and the eleven dances filled with pann
—upon this earth
she has set them forth; Puhar's golden-bangled
Madhavi, coming
into the dance-arena with her art!

[So saying, the Chola king presents a garland made of leaves and flowers, a thalaikkol, and a purse of one thousand and eight kalanju of gold. Then the king departs.]

(The staff held by the leader of the dancers is called the thalaikkol.)

[Chitrapathi whispers something secretly into the hunchbacked woman's ear......

The hunchbacked woman takes the garland from Madhavi, stands upon the stage and
......]

Hunchbacked woman : Great ones! Until now you have savoured the artistic skill of Madhavi, daughter of Chitrapathi. How many have waited, longing, “Will we not have the fortune of gaining Madhavi as our life-companion?” For that purpose, a swayamvaram has now been arranged!.

<!-- source scan 30: pictorial/caption-only page; no dramatic text -->

Elderly Greek : That is the way to speak! That is the way!! What must one do in the swayamvaram?

I am ready!......

[Everyone laughs]

Hunchbacked woman : Whoever buys this garland, presented to Madhavi by the king, for one thousand and eight kalanju of gold—may become her husband!...

[People in the assembly sigh to one another, “One thousand and eight in gold! Oh, heavens!”]

Elderly Greek : Here; one thousand and eight kalanju of gold!

[He runs toward the stage. Chitrapathi and the hunchbacked woman... rejoice with each other!]

Madhavi : Vasanthamalai?
...?....!....!

[Turning toward her friend]

Vasanthamalai : [In shock]
Madhavi!...

[The elderly Greek reaches the stage and unties his purse, pouring out the gold]

Elderly Greek : Hmm! I came to buy pearls from the Tamil land. One thousand and eight kalanju of gold! Here—this is not for the garland! It is for Madhavi! Give me the garland....

[Madhavi suddenly snatches the garland from the hunchbacked woman's hand, embraces Chitrapathi and sobs, “Mother!”]

Chitrapathi : Madhavi!... What is this, like a little child! Give the garland!...

Vasanthamalai : For one thousand and eight kalanju of gold... is Madhavi to marry this hundred-and-eight-year-old man?...

Elderly Greek : Who is one hundred and eight? Look at the man—a young sprout! I have finished sixty-eight; I am only “eight” now—remember that!...

[Kovalan and Kannagi look at one another.]

Vasanthamalai : Alas! Is there not a single young man in this assembly who can give one thousand and eight in gold?... Would you raise a parrot only to hand it into the paws of this old cat?...

Listen to me! Let us postpone Madhavi's swayamvaram.

Chitrapathi : Hush! Be quiet! This Chitrapathi will not break her word! Madhavi must marry.

Vasanthamalai : Who denied that! But whom?...

Chitrapathi : The man who bought this garland!

Vasanthamalai : Then I shall see to it that he does not buy this garland! Sir, elder!... Please save our Madhavi!...

Elderly Greek : I will save her—I will keep her and protect her all her life; I have enough wealth for that!

Vasanthamalai : You have enough wealth! But you do not have enough age left, sir! Another four years perhaps, or five?

Chitrapathi : Do not speak, Vasanthamalai! Madhavi, do not be stubborn for nothing! Give him the garland! You yourself said you would marry one man and live with him; now you yourself are humiliating me.....

Madhavi : Mother... I said I would marry one man and live with him—now I have decided to pass my life as a maiden, Mother.

Chitrapathi : I cannot dance to whatever tune you choose!...

[Kannagi, to Kovalan]

Kannagi : Oh, poor Madhavi!... My dear! You go and save her!...

[On the stage Madhavi falls at the elderly man's feet]

Madhavi : Sir! Forgive me!

Elderly Greek : What kind of spectacle is this! They said whoever bought the garland for one thousand and eight kalanju of gold could have you!... And they said it in the middle of the assembly! Now to change it—this I... I understand nothing at all!

[Kovalan comes onto the stage]

Kovalan : Sir! We need neither the garland nor Madhavi; come, let us go.......

Elderly Greek : Wonderful, Kovalan—this justice of your Tamil land!

Kovalan : It is not the culture of the Tamil land to garland an old man with a young parrot!

Elderly Greek : Then why say that whoever buys the garland may have Madhavi?....

Kovalan : Madhavi's mother's condition is that the man worthy of buying the garland may have Madhavi. My judgment is that only the man worthy of Madhavi may buy the garland. Come, let us go.

Elderly Greek : Impossible! No one other than me can have Madhavi!

Kovalan : Why?...

Elderly Greek : Because there is no one else here worthy enough to give one thousand and eight kalanju of gold!

Kovalan : What did you say? You are the first man to speak slightingly of Tamilakam's worth. Here—I shall prove that Tamils possess the strength and wealth to buy a hundred garlands like this... Madhavi, give me the garland! Hmm! Here is a pearl necklace worth one thousand and eight kalanju of gold!

[He receives the garland from Madhavi and gives her the pearl necklace from his own neck.

Kannagi is shocked]

[Kovalan steps down from the stage and comes to where Kannagi is seated...]

Kovalan : Kannagi, come—let us go!...

Kannagi : My dear!...

Kovalan : Do not be afraid—come!

[The assembly too disperses. The elderly Greek slips away with the crowd. Kovalan and Kannagi leave along with the crowd.]

[Seeing that Kovalan has gone, Madhavi says to Vasanthamalai]

Madhavi : Vasanthamalai! He has taken the garland and gone away!

Vasanthamalai : Why? What do you want me to do?...

Chitrapathi : Do you not understand even this? She is telling you to bring him home... What, Madhavi—is that not so?...

[Madhavi lowers her head in shyness and looks toward Vasanthamalai]

## Translation notes

- `கலைக்கரசி மாதவி` becomes `Madhavi, Queen of the Arts`, retaining the regal elevation of her artistic status.
- The Chola king's verse keeps its printed lineation. Technical terms `இயல்`, `பண்`, and `தலைக்கோல்` are retained as `iyal`, `pann`, and `thalaikkol` where an English substitution would risk falsely defining the source's performance taxonomy. The source itself explains `தலைக்கோல்`, and that explanation is translated immediately below the verse.
- `ஆயிரத்தெட்டு கழஞ்சு பொன்` is translated as `one thousand and eight kalanju of gold`; `kalanju` is retained as a historical weight/value term rather than converted into a speculative modern amount.
- `சுயம்வரம்` is retained as `swayamvaram`. In this scene the supposed marriage-choice mechanism is immediately distorted into a purchase condition; translating the term as simply `free choice marriage` would conceal that dramatic irony.
- The verified Tamil prints the comic age boast `அறுபத்தெட்டு முடிந்து நான் எட்டு தான் ஆகிறது`. The English keeps its comic irregularity as `I have finished sixty-eight; I am only “eight” now` rather than silently repairing the Tamil to a hypothetical alternative reading.
- `கிளியை வளர்த்து இந்தக் கிழட்டுப் பூனை கையிலே கொடுப்பதா?` remains the sharp `raise a parrot ... hand it into the paws of this old cat`, preserving Vasanthamalai's ridicule.
- Kovalan's reversal of the condition is preserved structurally: not `the man who can buy the garland is worthy of Madhavi`, but `only the man worthy of Madhavi may buy the garland`.
- `தமிழகத்தின் தகுதி பற்றி இழித்துரைத்த` is rendered as `speak slightingly of Tamilakam's worth`; the following `தமிழருக்குண்டு` remains explicitly `Tamils possess...`, retaining Kalaignar's collective Tamil assertion.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 7 combines comic theatre with a serious conflict over **woman, wealth and choice**. Madhavi is first celebrated as `கலைக்கரசி`—queen of the arts—yet her artistic triumph is immediately converted by Chitrapathi into a monetary marriage transaction. The event is called a `swayamvaram`, but Madhavi's own will is nearly erased by the rule that the man who pays the price may claim her.

Madhavi's refusal—she would rather remain unmarried than accept the imposed match—and Vasanthamalai's fierce defence of her choice resonate with the Dravidian/Self-Respect movement's later emphasis on women's consent, dignity and criticism of marriage arrangements that subordinate the woman to family or property interests. The scene should not, however, be relabelled a `Self-Respect marriage` scene: Kalaignar does not use that term here, and Kovalan's intervention remains embedded in the social assumptions of the drama.

Kovalan's climactic response to the Greek elder adds another strand. When the elder implies that no Tamil present has sufficient wealth, Kovalan treats it as an insult to `Tamilakam's worth` and proves the collective capacity of `Tamils`. The translation retains that pride and rhetorical escalation without inserting later nationalist or party-political vocabulary.

The interpretive note therefore records two source-supported resonances—women's dignity/choice and Tamil collective self-respect—while keeping both outside the translated dialogue.
