## Kalaignar-language review

PASS.

The translation protects the major rhetorical/comic structures:

- `painting / statue / living woman` escalation;
- the age-and-youth ridicule of the Greek elder;
- the `parrot / old cat` image;
- `wealth is enough / age is not enough` antithesis;
- the decisive reversal: `the man worthy of buying the garland may have Madhavi` versus `only the man worthy of Madhavi may buy the garland`;
- Kovalan's sudden transformation of a private marriage dispute into a collective claim about `Tamilakam's worth` and the wealth/ability of `Tamils`.

The verified Tamil's irregular age boast is not silently repaired in English; its oddity is retained and documented.

## Dravidian-context review

**PASS WITH CAREFUL QUALIFICATION.**

The strongest movement-era resonances are:

- Madhavi's refusal to be transferred according to a price-based marriage condition;
- Vasanthamalai's insistence on her choice and dignity;
- the distinction Kovalan draws between financial ability and worthiness for Madhavi;
- the later collective Tamil self-respect rhetoric when the Greek elder disparages Tamil capacity.

These themes can reasonably be related to later Dravidian/Self-Respect concerns with consent, dignity, criticism of marriage/property subordination, and Tamil collective self-respect. The translation does **not** call the event a `Self-Respect marriage` and does not insert later ideological vocabulary into dialogue.
