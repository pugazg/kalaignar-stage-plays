# Scene 25

## Nedunchezhiyan, Unswerving in Justice

### The Pandya Court

[The fort where the fish-banner of the Pandya land flies... then the fish emblem in the royal audience hall... the voice of Pandya Nedunchezhiyan!....]

Voice : In the royal audience hall of Pandya Nedunchezhiyan there is no place for injustice! Both of you who have come to argue your case are highly learned in Tamil. I am greatly perplexed by the course this case has taken.

[Then... Nedunchezhiyan and Kopperundevi come into view]

[Holding the manuscript in his hand]

Poet Arulalar! You show your handwriting as evidence that this kaviyam is yours! That must indeed be accepted! What is Poet Mallavanar's objection to this?...

Mallavanar : Does the kaviyam become Arulalar's merely because the handwriting is his?... O King! The truth is that I dictated it and my student Arulalar wrote it down!... Therefore the kaviyam is mine!

Pandya : Arulalar?

Arulalar : O Pandya king who nurtures the Tamil Sangam!... I swear upon Tamil and say this. This little kaviyam is the fruit of my own thought! I have never, ever been Mallavanar's student!

[Thinking]

Pandya : Hmm!... A difficult case! Very well, I shall give one final decision... The kaviyam belongs to neither of you. Before your very eyes I shall throw it into the fire and burn it... Is that acceptable?

Arulalar : No; no, never! Let not the unworthy act of burning Tamil take place in the court of the Pandya who protects Tamil! I wrote the kaviyam for the land itself, not for myself; let it appear under anyone's name! Let the kaviyam remain even under Mallavanar's name... do not destroy it!

Pandya : Is it not the one who has borne the child who knows the child's worth—just so! From this alone it has become clear that Arulalar is the mother of this kaviyam. It has also become clear that Mallavanar is a false poet who wishes, without labouring, to steal another's work and gain fame! That is why he did not care that the kaviyam should be destroyed!... Arulalar! You have won! This is indeed your kaviyam!.... Here!....

[Arulalar receives it]

Minister! Give him five thousand kalanju of gold as a reward!

Arulalar : Long live Tamil! Long live Pandya Nedunchezhiyan!

[The king and queen bow to the assembly and rise to leave... As they walk along the corridor beside the royal audience hall,

one of the queen's anklets slips off and falls...

The queen stops and bends down]

Pandya : What?...

Queen : The anklet has broken!

Pandya : [Picking up the anklet] Who is there!...

[A companion and an official bow]

Pandya : Give this anklet to the palace goldsmith and have it repaired and brought back!

Give me the other anklet too!

Queen : Must I remain with no anklet upon my foot?... No; repairing this one alone will do!

[The two leave]

[The official stands with the anklet in his hand...]

Companion : Why are you looking at the anklet as though you would swallow it whole?

Official : I was just wondering whether I could swallow it! Fool!... This is a government anklet—touch it and it is your head that will hang!

[He walks away from her. Even as he walks, looking at the anklet, to himself]

Official : How many thousands in gold might this anklet be worth!

[With a deep sigh]

Cha... cha... cha...... If only this alone belonged to me...... Hmm... what a useless dream of mine?....

[Consoling himself, he walks on]

## Translation notes

- `நீதி வழுவா நெடுஞ்செழியன்` is rendered as **`Nedunchezhiyan, Unswerving in Justice`**, keeping the source's ethical epithet rather than reducing the title to a neutral name.
- `கொலு மண்டபம்` follows the established project rendering `royal audience hall`.
- `காவியம்` is rendered as **`kaviyam`** in this scene because the repeated literary word itself is central to the authorship dispute. The English context makes its sense as a literary work/epic clear without replacing every occurrence with a different literary category.
- `தமிழ்ச் சங்கம்` is retained as **`Tamil Sangam`**. The scene itself gives no reason to substitute a modern academy/institutional label.
- `தமிழின் மீதாணையிட்டுக் கூறுகிறேன்` becomes **`I swear upon Tamil and say this`**, retaining Tamil as the invoked authority rather than smoothing it to a generic oath.
- Arulalar's `தமிழைக் கொளுத்தும்` is kept as **`burning Tamil`**. The emotional identification of the literary work with Tamil itself is Kalaignar's rhetoric and is not diluted to `burning a manuscript`.
- The king's `காவியத்தின் தாய் அருளாளர்` remains **`Arulalar is the mother of this kaviyam`**. The source's parenthood metaphor is preserved.
- `ஐயாயிரம் கழஞ்சு பொன்` remains **`five thousand kalanju of gold`** under the existing historical-unit rule.
- The official's `இது அரசாங்கச் சிலம்பு-தொட்டால் தொங்கிவிடும் தல!` is rendered **`This is a government anklet—touch it and it is your head that will hang!`**, preserving Kalaignar's colloquial comic threat and the source's `அரசாங்க` wording rather than replacing it with formal court language.
- `ச்சா...ச்சா...ச்சா......` is retained as the audible **`Cha... cha... cha......`** rather than removed as nonsemantic filler.
- The archaeological photograph/caption and later accession stamp on scan 67 remain provenance layers only; no English dramatic text is invented from them.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Kalaignar makes justice in this scene inseparable from Tamil literary culture. The litigants are introduced as people learned in Tamil; Arulalar swears upon Tamil; the Pandya is described as nurturing the Tamil Sangam and protecting Tamil; and Arulalar would rather surrender personal authorship than allow the kaviyam to be destroyed. Tamil is thus treated as a public cultural good larger than individual fame.

The king's test also values labour and authentic creation over appropriation without work. That can resonate with Dravidian movement emphases on dignity, labour, Tamil cultural stewardship and justice, but the translation does not retrofit modern constitutional or party vocabulary onto a royal court scene.

The closing shift to the official's covetous gaze is important dramatically: the scene that begins with a king detecting false appropriation ends with an official privately desiring royal property. Kalaignar lets human choice and institutional conduct, rather than fate, drive the next chain of events.
