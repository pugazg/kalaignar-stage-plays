# Scene 31

## The Sceptre Bent

### Nedunchezhiyan's Bedchamber

[The Pandya, dressed for the royal court and ready to set out, turns to the queen...]

Pandya : It is getting late! Are you not setting out for the hall of justice?...

Queen : I am not coming—with a foot that has no anklet!

Pandya : Why, today in the hall of justice I am going to discover the anklet-thief too!

Queen : Ah... what concern! You have time to rise and set out, to gather the equipment of war, and all day long to hear news of the Pandya realm; but is there any concern that your companion's foot lies there robbed of its beauty?...

Nedunchezhiyan : Do not forget that the land is greater than you and me!... For a petty anklet... should a queen worry this much?

Queen : It may indeed be a petty anklet to you! I myself have become petty, have I not; that is why!

Nedunchezhiyan : All right... all right... are you coming to the hall of justice or not?

Queen : Without the anklet I shall not take even one step from here...

Nedunchezhiyan : Hmm... all right... I am going!

[So saying, he goes off quickly!

[Outside, the official (the anklet-thief) is ready. He bows and follows behind.]

### Corridor

[As the official follows, the king comes quickly toward him... the chief goldsmith bows before the king.]

Nedunchezhiyan : What is it?

Chief Goldsmith : The thief who stole the anklet has been caught, O King!

Nedunchezhiyan : Where is he?

[The official starts in shock]

Chief Goldsmith : He was trying to have the anklet repaired and sold... I secured him there and ran here to bring you the news...

Nedunchezhiyan : Is that so?

[To the official]

A thief in Pandya land? Hmm... **Go at once, kill him, and return!**...

[The official and the goldsmith set out.]

## Translation notes

- `வளைந்தது செங்கோல்` is rendered **`The Sceptre Bent`**. `செங்கோல்` carries the political-ethical force of righteous rule; the title therefore signals a failure of justice, not merely a physically bent staff.
- `நீதி மன்றம்` continues as **`hall of justice`**, avoiding a falsely modern courtroom frame.
- The queen's complaint retains Kalaignar's domestic wit and escalation: war equipment, news of the realm, and finally the neglected foot of the king's companion.
- `உன்னையும் என்னையும்விட நாடு பெரியது` remains **`the land is greater than you and me`**, preserving the ruler's public-duty claim immediately before the scene exposes his judicial failure.
- `அற்பச் சிலம்பு / அற்பமாகிவிட்டேன்` keeps the repeated **`petty anklet / I myself have become petty`** word-turn rather than smoothing it away.
- The protected stage direction beginning `[என வேகமாகப் போகிறான்!` has no closing bracket in the Tamil. The English deliberately mirrors that unmatched opening bracket instead of silently repairing the source.
- `அதிகாரி(சிலம்புத் திருடன்)` is rendered directly as **`the official (the anklet-thief)`**, because Kalaignar identifies him explicitly in the stage direction at this point.
- The king's `உடனே அவனைக் கொன்று வருக!...` is kept abrupt and imperative as **`Go at once, kill him, and return!`**. It is not softened into arrest, punishment, execution after trial, or any modern legal procedure.
- The scene is translated against the verified Tamil only; no published English edition was used.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The title makes the political meaning unmistakable: the `sengol`, symbol of righteous rule, has bent. Earlier Nedunchezhiyan had ordered an inquiry into the missing anklet; here, after a single accusation from the goldsmith, he abandons inquiry and orders a man's immediate killing without seeing or hearing him.

Kalaignar sharpens the contradiction by placing the king's declaration that **the land is greater than you and me** immediately before his failure in public justice. Public duty is therefore not measured by rhetoric, war-readiness or royal authority alone; it is tested by whether power remains just when a vulnerable person stands accused.

That can strongly resonate with Dravidian movement concerns with justice, accountability and resistance to arbitrary authority. But the translation does not import modern constitutional terminology into the royal scene. The political critique remains grounded in Kalaignar's own `sengol`, `justice`, `land` and command.
