# Scene 33

## Is My Husband a Thief?

### Kuravai Koothu

[At the end of the koothu, Aiyai sings a song of sorrow at the top of her voice, with movements of anguish, and brings it to a close holding an abhinaya pose!

Beside her, her companions too raise their faces and hands toward the sky and show sorrowful abhinaya.

Kannagi loses herself, utterly absorbed in that sorrowful passage.

At that moment Madhari comes running!]

Madhari : [Crying aloud] Kannagi! Kannagi!

Kannagi : [Startled] Ah!

Madhari : They say Kovalan was accused of being the thief who stole the anklet and was killed, Amma!

Kannagi : Ah!

[With that cry she falls. Everyone runs to her and gathers around, calling, “Kannagi! Kannagi!” Kannagi, who has fallen face-down, opens her eyes. From her eyes, water surges like a waterfall!

The empty body from which all feeling has gone slowly rises. Her eyes are fixed upon some memory in a way that cannot be put into words!]

[An old scene upon the screen of memory]

Kovalan : Shall I go and return, my dear!

[Her feelings spring back to life! Kannagi's lips move! She cries out loudly]

Kannagi : You must not go! You must not go! If you go, they will kill you!...

[She seems to think to herself about what she is babbling]

Kannagi : They killed you! Accusing you as a thief, they killed you! My dear, are you a thief? A thief? No! No! No!

[She thunders it. Emotion becomes a gathering sea. She begins to run! In the speed of the rush, a large standing lamp falls against the hut.

Who is there to notice it at such a moment?

Everyone runs after Kannagi.

“Kannagi,

Kannagi!” they cry as they run.

“My dear,

my dear!” she cries as she runs.

Fire catches upon the hut from the fallen standing lamp! The hut begins to burn!

The fire spreads in many places. Kannagi keeps running in fury.]

## Translation notes

- `குரவைக் கூத்து` remains **`Kuravai Koothu`**, following the established scene-29 convention for the named performance form.
- `அபிநயம்` is retained as **`abhinaya`**. The source is describing expressive performance language and pose, not merely generic movement.
- The verified source has `உச்சக் குரலில்`; the English preserves its force as **`at the top of her voice`**.
- Madhari reports an accusation and a killing: Kovalan was **accused** of being the anklet thief and killed. The English does not let later knowledge turn the report itself into an already adjudicated truth.
- The remembered line `நான் போய் வரவா, கண்ணே!` is translated as **`Shall I go and return, my dear!`**, deliberately echoing the farewell language established before Kovalan's departure.
- Kannagi's repeated `கள்வனா? கள்வனா? இல்ல! இல்ல! இல்ல!` remains **`a thief? A thief? No! No! No!`**. The repetition is the scene's immediate shock structure and is not compressed.
- The unusual verified line `உணர்ச்சி, குழுமும் கடலாகிறது` is rendered conservatively as **`Emotion becomes a gathering sea.`** The Tamil is not normalized into a smoother source reading.
- The fallen standing lamp and spreading hut-fire occur only after Kannagi begins to run. The translation adds no omen, fate, divine causation or retrospective narrator knowledge.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 33 is first and foremost a scene of immediate shock. Kannagi hears that Kovalan has been killed under the label `thief`, collapses, re-enters their farewell through memory, and then rejects that humiliating label with the repeated cry `No! No! No!` Her resistance begins here as a human and emotional refusal before it becomes the public argument of the following scenes.

That refusal can resonate with a Dravidian-movement concern for dignity and resistance to imposed humiliation, but the scene itself contains no developed political argument yet. The translation therefore does not insert justice, rights, movement or ideological vocabulary into Kannagi's cry.

The hut-fire is likewise kept as Kalaignar stages it: a standing lamp falls in the rush, no one notices, and fire spreads while everyone runs after Kannagi. It is not interpreted inside the dialogue as cosmic judgment or destiny.
