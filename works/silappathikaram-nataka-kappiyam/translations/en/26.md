# Scene 26

## The Queen's Anklet

### The Goldsmith's House

[Carrying the queen's broken anklet, the official comes to the chief goldsmith.]

Chief Goldsmith : Come, come! Sit here! Where to in such a...?

Official : This is the anklet of Queen Kopperundevi. The king said it must be repaired quickly and sent back....

[He gives him the anklet; the goldsmith places it in a box.]

Chief Goldsmith : Why, that is nothing... I shall bring it tomorrow itself.

[The chief goldsmith rises and goes to correct the work being done by another goldsmith; while he goes, the official steals that anklet.]

Chief Goldsmith : Why are you striking so hard? What delicate work this is; if it is a gem-set ring, would you work it as though cutting a `malligai-kal`? Do not do this! Give it to him; sir, please take a look at this and do it carefully.

Chief Goldsmith : [To the official] And then...

Official : Ah, the work I came for is finished! I am going to the palace. By tomorrow, the anklet...

Chief Goldsmith : I shall bring it and hand it over.

Official : I shall take leave.

## Translation notes

- `அரசியின் சிலம்பு` is rendered directly as **`The Queen's Anklet`**.
- The chief goldsmith's opening `எங்கே இவ்வளவு...?` remains interrupted/incomplete in English as `Where to in such a...?`; the translation does not invent the missing noun/adjective.
- The source pause `அனுப்பச் சொன்னார்....மன்னர்.` is kept as a delayed `the king` rather than normalized into a smoother sentence, preserving the official's spoken timing.
- The theft is stated only where Kalaignar states it in the bracketed stage direction: **`the official steals that anklet`**. No earlier suspicion, ominous narration or foreshadowing is added.
- `மணி மோதிரம்` is rendered `gem-set ring` by immediate workshop sense.
- The craft expression `மல்லிகை கல்` is not securely reducible to a one-to-one English technical term from this scene alone. It is therefore retained conservatively as **`malligai-kal`** rather than guessed.
- `நாளைக்கே` retains emphasis through `tomorrow itself`.
- The final exchange preserves Kalaignar's conversational ellipsis: the official begins `By tomorrow, the anklet...`, and the chief goldsmith completes the practical answer.
- The lower uncaptioned illustration and printed pagination remain source-provenance layers only.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 26 is primarily a causal workshop scene, and its movement-era resonance should therefore remain restrained. Kalaignar places skilled manual craft at the centre of the action: the chief goldsmith is attentive to delicacy, technique and the quality of another worker's handling. The official, by contrast, abuses the moment of ordinary labour to steal the anklet entrusted to him.

That contrast can be read alongside a broader dignity-of-labour and accountability lens, but the scene itself makes no ideological declaration. Most importantly, the theft is shown as a human act carried out by an official; the translation does not recast it as fate, omen or institutional abstraction.
