# Scene 22

## Journey to Madurai

### On the Road to Madurai

[The sound of Kavundhi Adigal's song is heard.

Through that sound.... Kannagi and Kovalan are walking.

Seeing Kavundhi Adigal approaching them, Kovalan folds his hands and bows to Adigal.]

Kovalan : Greetings, Mother!

[To Kannagi]

Kannagi! This is Kavundhi Adigal. An ascetic of the Jain faith!

Kannagi : Greetings....

Kavundhi : Live long! Live long! Children, you are....?

Kovalan : Fine horses imported from foreign lands... sacks of pepper heaped up for export... gems and gold born of the northern hills... aaram and akil born of Kudamalai... pearls of the southern sea, coral of the eastern sea... the riches of the Ganges... the yield of the Kaveri—all these fill and adorn Poompuhar, the Chola's seaport city. That city is our home. My name is Kovalan. She is the cherished companion of my life... Kannagi!

Kavundhi : Kovalan...? Son of the great merchant Maasathuvan?... Child, what misfortune has befallen you?

Kovalan : I betrayed Kannagi, the radiant lamp!... As though that were not enough, I now commit the cruel wrong of making my wife's feet tread through spreading thorn-bushes and redden like bitter-gourd fruit.

Kavundhi : Do not grieve!.... When those who have done wrong come to understand their own wrong, that itself becomes a great punishment!.... Never mind... you are going to Madurai, are you not? I too shall come as a companion on the road—we can talk as we walk.

Kannagi : My dear.... How much farther is Madurai?

Kovalan : It will be six-five kaadham... that is all.

Kavundhi : Dear girl! Your husband is deceiving you... If `six-five kaadham` means six fives... that is thirty kaadham... What is it, child?.... Are you unable to walk?

[To Kovalan]

Kovalan!

If you want, I shall go ahead. You... carry Kannagi and—

Kovalan : What?

Kavundhi : Do you not deserve some punishment for the wrong you did her? That is why I said it... and not only that;

[As they walk]

Kannagi will not be accustomed to walking all along roads like these! On the way there will be dug-out pits covered over by heaps of withered champak flowers... if one does not walk carefully... one may fall into those deceptive pits...

Is not the path of life just the same?

Kovalan : Certainly! Why, it is truth learned through experience.

Kavundhi : If we go through the fields, vaalai fish attacked by otters will leap up trying to escape with their lives and fall before us. Kannagi will be frightened by them.

When we walk through sugarcane fields.. if we accidentally brush against the cane, the great honeycombs there may break and mingle with the flowing water.

Kovalan : All my wealth was ruined in just that way!....

Kavundhi : If we go by the grove... jackfruits hanging low from the branches will strike the head like enemies.

Kovalan : Ayyo.... On the wrong path I took, no one... not even my conscience... struck me and stopped me like that!

Kavundhi : Kovalan! Is it not when conscience sleeps that the monkey of the mind sets out to roam the town! Forget what has happened!... Let what happens from now on be good!

[They continue their journey.]

[In the forest, Kosikamani gives Kovalan a palm-leaf letter written by Madhavi.]

[Madhavi's voice]

Most beloved!... I bow at your feet. I myself became the cause of your failing in duty to your parents, of bringing harm to your own wife, and of your having to leave for another town to earn a livelihood—I beg you to console your heart that grieves over my fault.

Thus,
Yours in love.

[Kovalan reads it... then continues staring at the palm-leaf itself. Pearl-like tears fall from his eyes upon the leaf.]

Kovalan : Tell Madhavi... that Kovalan grieves for his error! This palm-leaf that she wrote to me applies to my mother and father as well... Therefore give it to my father as though I myself had given it to him...

[He reads the palm-leaf again.]

## Translation notes

- `கவுந்தியடிகள்` is retained as **`Kavundhi Adigal`**. The source itself immediately glosses the role as `சமண மதத் துறவி`, translated `an ascetic of the Jain faith`; `Adigal` is therefore not flattened to `monk` in the name.
- Kovalan's Poompuhar speech remains a long catalogue of import, export and interregional wealth. `aaram` and `akil` are retained rather than forced into uncertain commodity equivalents from outside this verified scene.
- `வாழ்க்கைத் துணைநலம்` is rendered as **`the cherished companion of my life`**, preserving Kannagi's companionate status rather than reducing the phrase to a bare `wife`.
- The physical scan-63→64 continuation `என் பத்தினியின் / பாதம் பட்டுப்...` is translated continuously without inventing a sentence break.
- `பாகற்பழம்` is kept through the source image as `bitter-gourd fruit`; the oddity of the comparison is not replaced by a smoother English colour image.
- The `ஆறு ஐந்து / ஆறைந்து முப்பது` joke depends on Kalaignar's arithmetic wordplay. English therefore retains **`six-five kaadham`** and lets Kavundhi make the joke explicit as `six fives... thirty kaadham`. `kaadham` is retained as the historical distance unit rather than converted speculatively.
- `பொய்க் குழிகள்` becomes `deceptive pits`, allowing Kavundhi's immediate life-path metaphor to remain visible.
- The verified source form around `நீர் நாயினுல் ... வாளை மீன்கள்` is not corrected. The immediate sense is translated as **vaalai fish attacked by otters**, while the Tamil record remains untouched.
- `மனக் குரங்கு` is retained as **`the monkey of the mind`**, preserving Kalaignar's vivid moral-comic metaphor.
- Madhavi's letter is translated exactly as presented in this scene. Its closing `உங்கள் அன்பு` is rendered conservatively as `Yours in love`; no missing signature or relationship word is invented.
- Kovalan's tears remain `pearl-like`, preserving the source image.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The scene continues the play's strong human-accountability current. Kavundhi does not explain Kovalan's suffering as an unavoidable fate: she says that recognizing one's own wrongdoing can itself become punishment. Her road metaphors repeatedly turn physical obstacles into lessons about conscience, choice and correction, culminating in Kalaignar's memorable `mind-monkey` that roams when conscience sleeps.

The scene also restores Poompuhar through a catalogue of maritime and inland exchange—horses, pepper, gems, gold, forest products, pearls, coral, Ganges wealth and Kaveri yield. As in earlier scenes, Tamil historical geography is imagined as commercially connected rather than isolated. That can be read alongside the movement's recovery of Tamil economic and civilizational history, but the dialogue itself remains Kovalan's description of his city.

Finally, Madhavi's letter and Kovalan's response place responsibility on human beings rather than on inherited stigma. Madhavi acknowledges harm to parents and Kannagi; Kovalan in turn recognizes that her words apply to his own failure toward his parents. The scene's ethical movement is reciprocal accountability, not punishment imposed by birth or social category.
