# Scene 30

## Anklet, O Anklet!

### Goldsmiths' Workshop

[The chief goldsmith makes all one hundred goldsmiths stand before him and questions them.]

Chief Goldsmith : Hmm... the time to go to the hall of justice is drawing near. At least now, tell the truth... who took the anklet?...

All Hundred : Not us! ..Not us!...

Chief Goldsmith : Then... did the anklet sprout wings and fly away!... All right... all right... let the Pandya king himself discover who that daylight robber is... Hmm... set out... with me!...

[The goldsmith sets out, with all of them following him.]

### Road

[Kovalan is quoting a price for the anklet at a house.]

Householder : Whatever the price, I shall take it. If it were a pair of anklets, give it to me.

Kovalan : Sir! For the sake of the ruby pellets inside it, is there no one who would buy this one by itself...

Householder : For that, go and see the palace goldsmith... perhaps he may have some use for it.

Kovalan : Greetings, sir...

Householder : A son of a house that once knew prosperity—

[Kovalan tries to sell the anklet to two other men.]

First Man : Adeyappa; an anklet beyond price! All right, take it to the palace goldsmith... he will sell it for you!...

Second Man : Adededede... look, there he himself comes... Goldsmith!

[The goldsmith arrives.]

Chief Goldsmith : What is it?...

First Man : Goldsmith, this man has a beautiful anklet.

Chief Goldsmith : An anklet?

First Man : Yes... a priceless anklet; he wants it sold for him.

Chief Goldsmith : I shall do it, sir. Tell me, son, what town are you from?

Kovalan : Poompuhar.

Chief Goldsmith : What I went searching everywhere for has come to me all by itself! Stay right here in this mandapam with the anklet; I shall inform the king and come back to take you with me.

Kovalan : Thank you very much!

[He bows and sits down. The goldsmith beckons the other men with a gesture.]

Chief Goldsmith : You two stay here with him as companions. I shall be back.

[The goldsmith leaves.]

## Translation notes

- `சிலம்போ சிலம்பு` is rendered **`Anklet, O Anklet!`**, preserving the source's exclamatory, theatrical title rather than reducing it to a neutral object label.
- `நீதி மன்றம்` continues the scene-28 convention **`hall of justice`** rather than importing a modern courtroom institution.
- The hundred goldsmiths' doubled `நாங்களில்லை! ..நாங்களில்லை!...` remains **`Not us! ..Not us!...`**; the collective rhythm is not compressed.
- `பகற் கொள்ளைக்காரன்` becomes **`daylight robber`**, preserving the chief goldsmith's comic exasperation.
- The internal heading `சாலை` remains **`Road`** inside scene 30 rather than becoming a separate numbered scene.
- The householder's `யாரோ வாழ்ந்த வீட்டுப் பிள்ளை—` is rendered conservatively as **`A son of a house that once knew prosperity—`**. The source is an idiomatic social observation; the English does not invent a specific family history beyond its immediate sense.
- `மாணிக்கப் பரல்கள்` is rendered **`ruby pellets`**, preserving the anklet's internal gem contents without importing the later evidentiary role those contents will play.
- Kalaignar's colloquial exclamations `அடேயப்பா` and `அடேடேடே` are retained as **`Adeyappa`** and **`Adededede`** rather than erased.
- The source-specific rough `தானுகவே` is not corrected in Tamil. The immediate dramatic sense is rendered as **`has come to me all by itself`**.
- The chief goldsmith's recognition is translated only as he himself expresses it. Kovalan is not given access to the goldsmith's knowledge, and no narrator foreshadowing is added.
- `மண்டபம்` is retained as **`mandapam`** in this public-road setting rather than forced into a narrower architectural English term.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 30 is primarily a trap being assembled through ordinary human choices. One hundred workers deny taking the missing anklet; the chief goldsmith is prepared to place the question before the king; yet once he encounters Kovalan carrying another anklet, the possibility of a convenient accused person appears. Kalaignar does not need fate or omen to move the tragedy forward: recognition, suspicion and institutional action are enough.

The scene also keeps skilled goldsmiths, householders, traders and public space in view. Its social world is populated by working people and commerce rather than only rulers. That can sit within a dignity-of-labour and accountability reading, but the translation does not impose a movement slogan upon the scene.

Most importantly, Kovalan remains unaware of the machinery forming around him. The translation preserves that asymmetry rather than turning the scene into retrospective moral narration.
