# Scene 6

## Puhar's Golden-Bangled Maiden

[Scenes of Puhar, with Sattanar's voice]

Sattanar : Puhar—the beautiful, illustrious city set where the Kaveri river and the sea kiss each other! The peerless great capital of the Chola empire that rules beneath the tiger flag—there, each year, the Indra Festival is celebrated as a festival of great splendour!

Before the sea-engulfment, one of the ancestors of the Chola realm that stretched as far as Kadaram—an ancient Tamil named Indran—was remembered through this festival of awakening!

At that festival the people of the city and great merchants from foreign lands take part, dancing and singing in joy...

In all three divisions of Puhar—Pattinappakkam, Maruvurpakkam and Naalangadi—revelry and dance!... The festooned gateway presented to Karikala Chola by the king of Avanti!...

The pearl pavilion offered by the king of Vachira country! The debate hall given as tribute by the ruler of Magadha! Everywhere, a great flood of delight! In the streets, ceremonial full pots! Golden maidens! Maiden-lamps!—festival array everywhere! Every street resounds with Tamil!

### Dance, Song

“Let us praise the Sun........
“Let us praise the Moon...........
“Let us praise the great rain........

[A Silappathikaram song, and the dance of women]

[Sattanar's voice continues]

Sattanar : On that auspicious day overflowing with exultation, upon the white-sand bank of Puhar's seashore, she stands utterly alone, gazing eagerly across the surging sea; ...the cherished daughter of Maanaaykan, the great giver whose generosity is like rain; a creeper born of bounty, a peerless beauty... like the Lady of the lotus
......

a beloved woman of resplendent great virtue, whom women worship and praise—her enduring name is Kannagi, O king!

[Kannagi stands there! Her eyes sweep across the sea. A ship. The sight of its arrival! Kannagi's face brightens with excitement. The ship stops a little distance away... Those aboard climb into small boats and come toward shore. Among them are foreigners—Greeks, Romans and others.

Kovalan comes in a small boat, with an elderly Greek beside him!

Seeing the boat approach, Kannagi, trembling with eagerness, walks into the sea toward it, heedless of the waves!

Kovalan too can hardly remain seated in the boat!

Elderly Greek : Who is that woman? Who dances through the sea with such courage?

Kovalan : She is my life-companion—Kannagi!

Elderly Greek : Ah... your companion! Just like the pearls of the Tamil land, the women of the Tamil land too are beauties! Ah! Until now our Greece has never created such a beauty!

[Kannagi, dipping again and again beneath the water, reaches the boat. Kovalan, unable to contain himself, leaps from the boat and comes to Kannagi. Embraced by the waves, the two embrace each other.]

Kovalan : Kannagi!

Kannagi : How many more days did you mean to leave me suffering alone like this, my dear!

Kovalan : Was loneliness sweet only to me? My gold... here... a gift I brought for you from Greece!

[He gives her a beautiful object made of ivory]

Kannagi : Tamil-land elephant ivory, in the hands of Greek craftsmen—how exquisite it has become, my dear!

Kovalan : In Greece there are countless shapely beauties who wear peacock feathers from Tamilakam as clothing and walk with peacock grace! Those who possess pearls from the Tamil land are considered people of distinction in Greece, Rome and Egypt.

Kannagi : Is that so?

Kovalan : They do not know that in the Tamil land there is one thing more priceless than pearls, coral, peacocks and agarwood...

Kannagi : What can that be? Even I do not know!

Kovalan : My Kannagi is that incomparable treasure! A sculpture untouched by life! A painting alive! Whenever I wander in foreign lands, my loving heart keeps singing a poem: “the Tamil land where Kannagi was born.”

Kannagi : My dear, from now on you will not leave me, will you?

Kovalan : Can sweetness part from Tamil?

Kannagi : What if another language comes between us?

Kovalan : A dream of the heavens! Like light to the eye—like resonance to the veena—like the moon to the sky... I shall remain by your side forever.

Kannagi : The moon to the sky? My dear! One day it cannot be seen at all! Pitch darkness! Its name is the new moon! No, my dear—not like that!

Kovalan : Kannagi! A face upon which Kama has drawn the fish, drawn the bow, drawn the dark cloud, and completed his art—a moon-face dripping honey—could I forget it, my dear! Come! Let us don the festival array! Let us bring to an end, in this one day, the suffering we endured through all the days we were apart. Come!...

[Just then the elderly Greek slowly gets down from the boat, comes near them and says]

Elderly Greek : What, Kovalan! It seems you have forgotten me altogether?...

Kovalan : Forgive me!...

[He introduces him to Kannagi]

Kovalan : He is a very wealthy man in Greece!

[Kannagi bows]

A distinguished prince among merchants! Our goods there are sold through him—old only to the eye; he has all the mischief of a young man!

Elderly Greek : Wait! Wait! Why would you say that?... Do I look like an old man?...... Tamil woman! You tell me—am I old?

Kannagi : No, Grandfather!....

Elderly Greek : Ah... say it like that! Kovalan! What does “grandfather” mean?

Kovalan : It means “handsome man”...

Elderly Greek : Ah... joy... joy... fitting... fitting...

Kovalan : He is learning Tamil only from me, Kannagi...

Kannagi : Then, of Muthamizh, he must know two—iyal and isai.

Elderly Greek : Ah... iyal... isai... I “know” them. It is only koothu I do not know!...

Kannagi : My dear; for the Indra Festival, Madhavi is dancing today at the Pearl Pavilion... why should the three of us not go and see it?...

Elderly Greek : What is “dance”?...

[Showing gestures as though dancing]

Kovalan : Koothu!

## Translation notes

- `பூம்புகார்ப் பொற்றொடி` is rendered as `Puhar's Golden-Bangled Maiden`. `பொற்றொடி` is an elevated feminine epithet; the English keeps the ornamented quality rather than reducing the title simply to `Kannagi`.
- `காவிரியாறும் கடலும் முத்தமிட்டுக் கொள்ளும்` retains the Kaveri and sea `kissing`, continuing the personified geography established in scene 5.
- `கடற்கோள்` is rendered as `sea-engulfment` without importing an external chronology. The source itself presents a Chola ancestor, `இந்திரன் என்னும் பழந்தமிழன்`, as `an ancient Tamil named Indran`; the translation preserves that striking historicizing formulation exactly rather than replacing it with the conventional divine title `Indra` in that sentence.
- `எழுச்சி விழா` becomes `festival of awakening`, retaining the source's mobilizing register.
- Puhar divisions `பட்டினப் பாக்கம்`, `மருவூர்ப் பாக்கம்`, `நாளங்காடி` are retained as `Pattinappakkam`, `Maruvurpakkam`, and `Naalangadi` rather than replaced by speculative modern equivalents.
- The three praise-lines are translated independently from the verified Tamil scene; no standard published Silappathikaram English wording was imported.
- Sattanar's elevated Kannagi description is kept elevated. `போதிலார் திருவினாள்` is rendered as `like the Lady of the lotus`, avoiding a more specific theological identification not required by the immediate wording.
- `வாழ்க்கைத் துணைவி` is `life-companion`, retaining the companionate force rather than merely `wife` in the Greek elder's question.
- `தமிழகம்` remains `Tamilakam`; source occurrences of `தமிழ்நாடு / தமிழ் நாடு` remain `Tamil land`, preserving the source distinction.
- `தமிழை விட்டுச் சுவை பிரியுமா?` is kept as `Can sweetness part from Tamil?`; the love metaphor is not flattened into a generic promise.
- `வேற்று மொழி குறுக்கிட்டால்?` remains `What if another language comes between us?`, preserving the source's explicit language metaphor.
- `முத்தமிழ்` is retained as `Muthamizh`; `இயல்`, `இசை`, and `கூத்து` are retained as `iyal`, `isai`, and `koothu` in the joke because replacing them with three unrelated English labels would destroy the Tamil cultural taxonomy and the Greek elder's language-learning humour.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 6 combines Tamil historical imagination, maritime cosmopolitanism and language pride in a way that strongly resonates with Kalaignar's Dravidian-era cultural politics. Puhar is not isolated from the world: Greeks, Romans and long-distance merchants enter its harbour, while Tamil pearls, ivory, peacock feathers and other goods circulate abroad. Yet this cosmopolitanism is narrated through an emphatically Tamil centre—`Every street resounds with Tamil`, and Kovalan measures the world's treasures against `the Tamil land where Kannagi was born`.

A particularly striking source choice is the description of `இந்திரன்` as `ஒரு பழந்தமிழன்`—an ancient Tamil ancestor of the Chola realm—whose memory is marked by an `எழுச்சி விழா`. The translation preserves that historicizing, Tamil-centred framing without replacing it by a conventional mythological explanation. This can be read as resonant with the Dravidian movement's practice of recovering and re-reading inherited cultural material through Tamil historical consciousness.

The lovers' exchange `Can sweetness part from Tamil?` / `What if another language comes between us?` also carries an unmistakable language-political resonance for a modern Kalaignar audience, even while functioning inside the scene as playful marital intimacy. The translation leaves the metaphor inside the dialogue exactly at that level; terms such as `language rights`, `anti-imposition`, or `Dravidian` are not inserted into the characters' speech.
