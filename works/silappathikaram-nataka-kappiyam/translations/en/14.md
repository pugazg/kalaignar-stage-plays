# Scene 14

## Madhavi's Nobility

### On the Banks of the Kaveri

[In a grove heavy with fruit beside the Kaveri river...

Madhavi and Kovalan dance and sing in happiness. Madhavi's beautiful dance...]

*

[Madhavi suddenly grows weary and sits down.]

Kovalan : Why? Why are you so tired, Madhavi?...

[Coming near]

Madhavi : Nothing... these days I cannot dance for long as I used to...

Kovalan : From now on I shall never ask you to dance... Is that enough?

Madhavi : Because you are angry?...

Kovalan : No anger, my cuckoo!.... I know that you should not dance too much...

Madhavi : Why?... For what reason?

Kovalan : Mm-mm... I will not tell you; you will feel shy....

Madhavi : Why should I feel shy... You must tell me.

Kovalan : Shall I tell you?....

Madhavi : Hmm!...

Kovalan : I will tell you...

Madhavi : Tell me....

Kovalan : Mm-mm ... I cannot say it....

Madhavi : You cannot, can you... Very well; I shall go and come back!....

[She tries to rise]

Kovalan : Madhavi...

Madhavi : Hmm... let go of me!...

[She rises]

[Kovalan sneezes]

Madhavi : May you live a hundred years!

[She moves aside, sits a short distance away, covers her face and weeps]

Kovalan : Madhavi... why are you crying?

Madhavi : You sneezed...

Kovalan : Yes... I sneezed... you even blessed me... then why undo it by crying afterward?....

Madhavi : You sneezed because you were thinking of someone, did you not?... Whom are you thinking of other than me?

Kovalan : Ayyo... I was not thinking of anyone... Madhavi!... More than anyone, it is you I love; you I cherish!...

Madhavi : More than anyone!... More than whom, whom? More than Alli?... More than the Thamarai next door? Tell me... more than whom, whom do you love me?...

Kovalan : Ah! What trouble is this? Is it that women, at a time like this, grow angry at everything for no reason?...

[He goes to another side. Madhavi follows him]

Madhavi : You keep saying `a time like this`; what kind of time is this?...

Kovalan : That is... the kind of time when... dancing makes you tired...

Madhavi : Why... does it make me tired?...

Kovalan : Oh-ho... have you come right back to where we left off?...

Madhavi : You will not tell me, will you? Fine!.

[She goes]

Kovalan : Stop! Madhavi!... Madhavi!...

Madhavi : What!... Madhavi?...

Kovalan : Yes... Ma...dha...vi... indeed! From now on—you are Madhavi!... Maathaa... maathaa... you... are maathaa—a mother... I... am pithaa—the father! That is why you cannot dance for long.

Madhavi : Go away...

[In shyness]

Kovalan : See... see!.. That is why I said I would not tell you...

Madhavi : My dear!....

Kovalan : Madhavi!....

[Hearing a sorrowful voice, they start and turn]

Voice : Bhagavan! Why have you brought me to this state?... Is a life without one's husband a life at all?... Ayyo... ayyo... how did his heart allow him to abandon me in this condition?... God!... Will you not at least show grace and change my husband's stone-heart and turn him back to the good path? Ayyo... will no one give me refuge? Even if this poor woman's voice does not touch my husband's heart—Lord! Has it not touched even your heart?...

[Kovalan and Madhavi, astonished, go toward the voice. A Brahmin woman stands facing the Kaveri, her hair loose, a child in her arms, wailing aloud!....]

[Coming near]

Kovalan : Mother!... Who are you?... What has happened to you?...

Brahmin Woman : Sir.... I am the wife of an **Antanar**. My husband, unwilling to forgive an offence I committed unknowingly, has rejected me and gone away....

Kovalan : What offence could it be?..

Brahmin Woman : My husband had been raising a young parrot!... One day he was away from the town... I went to the pond to bathe, leaving the young parrot to guard the child. Then a snake came to bite the child; the parrot killed it.... and ran outside covered in that blood... Returning from the pond, I wrongly thought the parrot had killed the child, and in that mistaken belief I hurled the water-pot at it and killed it.... Unable to forgive this offence... my husband... left me and the child to suffer... and went away....

Kovalan : Tch... tch.... Is that so?

Brahmin Woman : There... there... that is he, going away in the boat..... Sir... please tell them to stop the boat!

Kovalan : Hey... boatman!... Stop the boat!.. Stop!....

[Shouting]

Bring it to the bank!...

[The boat comes to the bank]

[The `paarppana` woman runs toward the boat with the child]

Brahmin Woman : Forgive me; at least for the child's face, please return!...

Antanar : Che!... Impossible!... Let no one stop me!

Kovalan : Antanar!... If you go away leaving them to suffer alone, will the dead young parrot come back to life?

Antanar : I have set out to seek atonement for the sin of having it killed unjustly.

Kovalan : True! To seek atonement for the parrot, you abandon the child you fathered and the wife you took! If they, left without support, die unjustly, that sin too will surround you!... Where will you go for atonement for that?... Antanar, without knowing whether the snake would bite the child or not, the parrot killed it! In the same way, without knowing whether the parrot had bitten the child or not, your wife killed it! So... it has become even on both sides. The proper thing is for you to go home with your wife....

Antanar : A fine explanation... an explanation even this Antanar did not understand;....

Kovalan : All of it is training given by the **Tamil sacred lore**!...

Antanar : We are going... Long live! Long live!

[They leave... After they have gone]

Madhavi : My dear... I shall ask one thing; you must not become angry.

Kovalan : Ask, my treasure!

Madhavi : You gave moral counsel to that **man of sacred lore** who had left his wife; in just the same way, your companion... my elder sister must be withering away... How many times have I told you: go and see her, then return.

Kovalan : I am ashamed to face Kannagi, my dear!

Madhavi : Is your loving me such a shameful thing? Tell me at least that.

Kovalan : All right... all right... come... let us go!

Madhavi : Mm-mm—it is not enough to preach to the whole town! I shall speak to you only if you promise that you will go and see Kannagi and come back.

Kovalan : Very well, let it be so!...

Madhavi : Truly?...

Kovalan : Certainly... I shall go today. After this incident, I too feel that I ought to go....

Madhavi : That lady wept so much merely at being separated from this Antanar; how much must my elder sister suffer, separated from you, the very form of beauty... Ayyo, if elder sister sheds tears... it will affect even the child growing in my womb!

Kovalan : Madhavi! Do not frighten me like this and make me submit. I shall certainly go and return. Is that enough?

Madhavi : My dear!...

Kovalan : Madhavi... come!...

## Translation notes

- `மாதவியின் மாண்பு` is rendered as `Madhavi's Nobility`. The scene establishes that nobility not through abstract praise but through her insistence that Kovalan apply to Kannagi the moral reasoning he has just applied to another couple.
- The pregnancy revelation depends on Kalaignar's sound-play: `மாதவி` is broken into `மாதா` (`mother`) and paired with `பிதா` (`father`). The English therefore retains `Ma...dha...vi / maathaa / pithaa` in transliteration and supplies the meanings inside the line rather than replacing the joke with an unrelated English pun.
- Madhavi's sneeze reaction is preserved as source-world belief and teasing jealousy. The translation neither validates nor editorially mocks the belief beyond Kalaignar's own comic staging.
- The source's parrot-and-snake story is translated exactly as given. It is not silently replaced by a better-known variant involving another animal.
- **Caste/learned-status terminology is deliberately not collapsed.** The source stage direction explicitly says `ஒரு பிராமண மாது`, so that occurrence is translated `a Brahmin woman`. A later direction uses the distinct form `பார்ப்பனமாது`, retained as `the paarppana woman` in the stage direction. Her speech label `பிராமணமாது` remains `Brahmin Woman` for readability.
- By contrast, the husband is repeatedly called `அந்தணர் / அந்தணன்`, and Madhavi later calls him `மறையவர்`. Following the project's permanent rule, **`அந்தணர்` is not translated as `Brahmin`**. It is retained as `Antanar`, while `மறையவர்` is rendered functionally as `man of sacred lore`.
- `தமிழ் மறை` is rendered as **`Tamil sacred lore`**, preserving the explicit Tamil ethical/literary authority invoked by Kovalan. The translation does not identify it with a particular text unless the source itself does so.
- `விமோசனம்` is `atonement` in the immediate sin/expiation argument. Kovalan's reasoning is kept practical and consequence-based: abandoning living dependants in order to atone for the dead parrot would create another wrong.
- `ஊருக்கு உபதேசம் செய்தால் போதாது!` is kept close to its idiom as `it is not enough to preach to the whole town!`, preserving Madhavi's sharp reversal of Kovalan's public moral advice back upon his private conduct.
- Madhavi repeatedly calls Kannagi `என் அக்காள்`; the English preserves `my elder sister`, maintaining the source's refusal to frame the two women simply as hostile rivals.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 14 contains one of the clearest ethical-rational strands since scenes 3–4. The Antanar says he is abandoning his wife and child in order to seek `விமோசனம்` for the parrot's death. Kovalan does not answer with another ritual prescription. He asks what happens to the living wife and child, points out the parallel between the parrot's action and the woman's mistaken action, and argues toward a practical moral conclusion: return home and take responsibility.

Kalaignar then gives that reasoning an explicitly Tamil source of authority: `எல்லாம் தமிழ் மறை தந்த பழக்கம்!`—here preserved as **`All of it is training given by the Tamil sacred lore!`**. That combination of reasoned ethical consequence and Tamil textual/cultural authority strongly resonates with the Dravidian movement's preference for Tamil ethical resources and human responsibility over fatalistic or mechanically ritual solutions.

The scene goes further by having Madhavi expose a contradiction in Kovalan himself. It is not enough to `preach to the whole town`; the moral principle must be applied at home. She asks him to face Kannagi, whom she calls `my elder sister`, and makes Kannagi's suffering morally unavoidable. This is Kalaignar's ethical argument turned inward: public wisdom without private responsibility is insufficient.

The terminology itself also matters. Kalaignar's source uses `பிராமண`, `பார்ப்பன`, `அந்தணர்`, `மறையவர்`, and `தமிழ் மறை` in the same scene. The translation preserves those distinctions rather than flattening them into one caste label. In particular, **Antanar is not equated with Brahmin**. This note records the linguistic distinction without claiming more social-historical certainty than the source supports.
