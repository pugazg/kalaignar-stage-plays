# English Translation Review — காட்சி-8

Source Tamil: `../../scenes/08.md`  
English translation: `08.md`

## Result

**PASS.**

`translations/en/08.md` is accepted as `translation-reviewed` / `passed`.

## Coverage and Kalaignar-voice review

The translation covers the bedchamber dialogue, Kannagi's fear and trust, the quoted marriage line, Kovalan's extended poetic praise, the family misunderstanding, the rapid entrance of both sets of parents, the comic reversal when Kovalan appears from inside the house, and the final family reconciliation.

Kalaignar's tonal movement remains intact: intimacy → anxiety → lyric praise → family melodrama → anger → comic revelation → reconciliation.

The praise sequence preserves accumulation rather than shortening it, and the family dialogue retains its quick vocatives, interruptions, repeated exclamations and animal/food imagery.

## Mandatory `பார்ப்பார்` terminology review

The occurrence in the quoted line is:

`மாமுது பார்ப்பார் மறைவழி காட்டிடத் தீவலம் செய்து நிகழ்த்திய திருமணம்`

**Decision: do not translate `பார்ப்பார்` as `Brahmin`.**

In this occurrence the source itself defines the immediate role through `மறைவழி` and `தீவலம்`: they are persons officiating/guiding a ritual marriage and the circling of fire. The English therefore uses:

`venerable ritual officiants`

and renders `மறை` as `sacred lore`.

This is an occurrence-level functional rendering, not a claim that `பார்ப்பார்` always means the same thing. It remains distinct from the separate repository terms `பிராமண`, `பார்ப்பன`, `அந்தணர்`, and `மறையவன்`.

The translation does not use this line to invent an anti-ritual or anti-Brahmin polemic; Kannagi invokes the rite positively as a symbol of marital security in this scene.

## Other terminology decisions

- `இல்லறம்` → `married life` in the title/context, while its relational force is preserved in Kovalan's later line;
- `அத்தான்` → `my dear` in Kannagi's intimate address;
- `உன் தல எழுத்து` → `the writing on your head—your fate`, retaining the source image;
- `கற்புக் கனி` → `fruit of chastity`;
- `கணிகை` → `courtesan`;
- `கோள் சொல்லும் கோட்டான்கள்` → `tale-bearing owls`;
- `கருத்தொருமித்து ஆதரவுபட்ட இல்லறம்` → `married life of two lovers, united in thought and bound in mutual support`.

## Dravidian-context review

**PASS WITH RESTRAINT.**

Unlike scenes 3–4, scene 8 does not support a direct rationalist attack on ritual authority. The interpretive note therefore explicitly refuses to impose one.

A defensible later-movement resonance instead lies in Kovalan's description of marriage through love, mental accord, mutual support and continuing responsibility, together with Kannagi's active defence of her husband before both families. These can be related cautiously to companionate dignity and women's agency in later Self-Respect/Dravidian discourse, without claiming that the scene itself is a Self-Respect-marriage manifesto.
