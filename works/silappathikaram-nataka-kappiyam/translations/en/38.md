# Scene 38

## Stone for Kannagi's Statue

### In the Himalaya — Kuyilaluvam

[In the war fought between Cheran Senguttuvan and Kanaka-Vijayar, Senguttuvan is victorious.]

Cheran : Villava! What is the name of this place where victory resounds?

Villavan : Kuyilaluvam.

Cheran : Kuyilaluvam—mark it down! All these rocks are pages inscribed by the valour of verdant Tamil. Victory alone is not enough, Villava; those wastrels must not be allowed to go!

Villavan : They say the Kanaka-Vijayar have run away somewhere in saffron robes, like rishis.

Cheran : Nothing new in that; it is the one habit proper to them.

[The Kanaka-Vijayar are brought under arrest, dressed as sages.]

Cheran : Who are these?

Villavan : Kanaka-Vijayar!

Cheran : Kanaka-Vijayar! Kanaka-Vijayar! Ah, heroes among heroes—what splendid battle-skill! Showing your backs—covering your heads—attacking from hiding—turning into mahaans! You stand there in the garb of peaceful sages; answer this! In the Purana, how long did the Deva-Asura war last?

Kanaka-Vijayar : Eighteen years.

Cheran : The Rama-Ravana war?

Kanaka-Vijayar : Eighteen months.

Cheran : The Bharata war?

Kanaka-Vijayar : Eighteen days.

Cheran : Warriors! Remember this: the war Cheran Senguttuvan fought in the northern land ended in just eighteen naazhigai!

Kanaka-Vijayar : O King, we kneel before you. Forgive us.

Cheran : Forgive you? I am to forgive you? Come and kneel upon the soil of Tamil land. Let that forgive you.

Villavan : O King, should we not return to Tamilakam? It has been thirty-two months since we set out.

Cheran : We shall set out, Villava; but before that, must we not take stone from here to raise a statue for Kannagi?

Villavan : Yes.

Cheran : The right bullocks to carry that stone have been found. Kanaka—Vijaya! You threw a word against the valour of the Tamils; now lift the stone!

Warriors : Long live Cheran Senguttuvan!

## Translation notes

- `கண்ணகி சிலைக்குக் கல்` is rendered directly as **`Stone for Kannagi's Statue`**. The title's action is material and political-memory oriented: stone is to be taken for the statue.
- The location `இமயத்தில் - குயிலாலுவம்` remains **`In the Himalaya — Kuyilaluvam`**; no modern geographic identification is imposed on `Kuyilaluvam`.
- The source uses joint/plural forms `கனக விஜயர் / கனக விஜயர்கள்` in this scene. English therefore uses **`Kanaka-Vijayar`** as a source-visible collective form rather than silently harmonizing every occurrence to the scene-37 `Kanakan and Vijayan` wording.
- `பைந்தமிழ் வீரம் தீட்டிய ஏடுகள்` is rendered **`pages inscribed by the valour of verdant Tamil`**. `பைந்தமிழ்` is kept as a poetic Tamil epithet rather than flattened to simply `Tamil`.
- `காஷாய உடை` is rendered `saffron robes`; `ரிஷிகள்` remains **`rishis`**. The disguising satire remains Kalaignar's.
- `மகானாய் மாறுவது!` becomes **`turning into mahaans!`**, retaining the mock ascetic-holiness register inside Senguttuvan's taunt rather than smoothing it to generic `holy men`.
- `தேவாசுர யுத்தம்`, `ராம ராவணப் போர்`, and `பாரதப் போர்` are kept as **Deva-Asura / Rama-Ravana / Bharata** war references exactly in the sequence Kalaignar gives them.
- The progression **eighteen years → eighteen months → eighteen days → eighteen naazhigai** is preserved exactly. `நாழிகை` remains **`naazhigai`**, a historical time unit; no speculative modern-hour conversion is inserted.
- `தமிழ்நாட்டு மண்ணில்` is rendered **`upon the soil of Tamil land`**, kept distinct from the later explicit `தமிழகம்` → **`Tamilakam`**.
- `முப்பத்திரண்டு திங்கள்` is rendered **`thirty-two months`** by the immediate elapsed-journey context.
- `எருதுகள்` becomes **`bullocks`** in the carrying-stone taunt, preserving Senguttuvan's deliberate reduction of the defeated rulers to beasts of burden.
- The final physically split source vocative `கனக— / விஜயா` remains **`Kanaka—Vijaya!`**, retaining the printed dash and direct taunt.
- `தமிழரின் வீரத்தின் மீது வீசினாய் ஒரு சொல்; இப்போது தூக்கு கல்!` remains **`You threw a word against the valour of the Tamils; now lift the stone!`**. The final command is not softened or summarized.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 38 turns military victory into Tamil collective memory. Senguttuvan says the rocks of the Himalaya are pages marked by Tamil valour; he refuses to make personal royal forgiveness sufficient and tells the defeated rulers to kneel on the **soil of Tamil land** and seek forgiveness from it. Tamilakam is therefore imagined as a collective moral-historical entity larger than the individual king.

Kannagi's place in that memory is equally important. Before returning after thirty-two months, Senguttuvan insists on taking stone for her statue. The woman who confronted a king over failed justice is thus carried from the Madurai tragedy into a public monument of Tamil historical remembrance. That can strongly resonate with later Dravidian practices of memorializing Tamil literary and political figures, but the translation does not insert modern movement language into the dialogue.

The scene is also openly triumphalist and humiliates defeated enemies by making them carry the statue-stone. The interpretive note therefore does not present its Tamil pride as a simple modern egalitarian statement; Kalaignar's martial satire and punitive imagery remain visible on their own terms.
