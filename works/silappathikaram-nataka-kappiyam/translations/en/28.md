# Scene 28

## The Goldsmith's Appeal

### The Pandya's Private Chamber

[Nedunchezhiyan and Kopperundevi are there....]
Kopperundevi comes on, still asking.]

Kopperundevi : What, has the anklet still not come?... Is the goldsmith still repairing it?

[Nedunchezhiyan is writing on a palm-leaf.]

Kopperundevi : Uh-hum... without answering what I asked, what is it you are writing like that?

[oodal]

Nedunchezhiyan : I am writing a puram-theme poem on the greatness of learning.

Kopperundevi : Yes!.... A puram-song... a battle cry! There is no other talk but this! This... is the bedchamber. It is neither a school... nor a military camp... do you understand?

Nedunchezhiyan : Tamil... is a breeze! It enters everywhere. Listen to the poem...

“Giving help when hardship comes,
and giving substantial wealth”...

[Before he can continue, a companion enters...]

Companion : O King; the palace goldsmith says he must see you urgently!

Nedunchezhiyan : Tell him to come!

[He says this and goes outside... standing at the doorway....]

Nedunchezhiyan : What is it?

Chief Goldsmith : Greetings... O King!

[He trembles]

Nedunchezhiyan : Why are you trembling? What is the reason?

Chief Goldsmith : Forgive me... O King;... forgive me!

Nedunchezhiyan : Tell me what has happened... Goldsmith!

Chief Goldsmith : I had kept the queen's anklet safely... locked inside a secure iron box, and I myself kept the key. When I opened the box this morning, the anklet was not there, O King; it was not there!

Nedunchezhiyan : Not there? Hmm... Did you come here merely to tell me that it is missing? Where can an anklet locked inside a box go? Goldsmith! Whom do you suspect?

Chief Goldsmith : Whom can I name? Of the hundred smiths who work under me, whom am I to single out and name, O King!

Nedunchezhiyan : Very well, bring all hundred smiths tomorrow and stand them before our hall of justice! I myself shall inquire and discover the thief.

Chief Goldsmith : Your command, O King! Forgive me; I shall take leave.

Nedunchezhiyan : Hmm!

[The goldsmith leaves]

## Translation notes

- `பொற்கொல்லர் முறையீடு` is rendered **`The Goldsmith's Appeal`**. The scene is a direct report to the king rather than a modern procedural filing, so the English avoids bureaucratic/legal over-specification.
- The source's unmatched closing bracket after `கோப்பெருந்தேவி கேட்டுக்கொண்டே வருகிறாள்.]` is deliberately reflected by the unmatched closing bracket after `Kopperundevi comes on, still asking.]`; the English does not silently repair a protected source anomaly.
- `[ஊடல்]` is retained as **`[oodal]`**, following the established project convention for the culturally specific mode of lovers' quarrel.
- `புறத்துறைப் பாடல்` is rendered `puram-theme poem`, while Kopperundevi's playful `புறப்பாடல்...போர் முழக்கம்!` becomes **`A puram-song... a battle cry!`**. Retaining `puram` preserves the literary/political wordplay instead of replacing it with an unrelated modern genre label.
- `தமிழ்...ஒரு தென்றல்! அது எல்லா இடத்திலும் நுழையும்.` remains **`Tamil... is a breeze! It enters everywhere.`** The personification and intimate humour are not flattened.
- The quoted lines `உற்றுழி உதவியும் / உறுபொருள் கொடுத்தும்` are translated independently from the Tamil printed in this scene as **`Giving help when hardship comes, / and giving substantial wealth`**. No published translation of the larger classical poem was imported.
- `நீதி மன்றம்` is rendered contextually as **`hall of justice`**, avoiding a falsely modern courtroom vocabulary while preserving the king's judicial setting.
- The king first asks for suspicion, then orders all hundred smiths brought for inquiry. No guilt is assigned in the translation before the source does so.
- The lower illustration is uncaptioned and contributes no invented dramatic English.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Kalaignar makes Tamil intrude—deliberately and humorously—even into the royal couple's private chamber: **`Tamil... is a breeze! It enters everywhere.`** Tamil here is not merely a court language or literary possession; it is imagined as an atmosphere that cannot be kept outside domestic life. That resonates strongly with the movement's insistence that Tamil belong to everyday lived culture, not only elite textual spaces.

The quoted learning poem and the king's response to the missing anklet also foreground education, inquiry and public justice. Nedunchezhiyan does not immediately condemn a named worker; he asks whom the goldsmith suspects and then orders an inquiry involving the workers. This can be related to justice and reasoned inquiry, but the note does not project modern constitutional procedure onto Kalaignar's royal setting.

The scene's humour between Nedunchezhiyan and Kopperundevi remains equally important. Movement interpretation does not override Kalaignar's domestic wit or `oodal` timing.
