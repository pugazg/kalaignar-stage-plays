# Scene 20

## Kovalan Reformed
## His Return

### Kannagi's House

[Kannagi stands before Kovalan's portrait—

She sends her gaze through the window and in many directions...... finally comes to the door and stands... she opens it. As soon as the door opens, a rooster comes inside.

Kannagi stands looking at the rooster.... Behind it, Kovalan too... comes and stands before her...

Seeing Kovalan, Kannagi freezes where she stands. Kovalan too, overcome, stands with his head bowed.

Kannagi, who has become like a tree... kneels... and lets her head fall upon Kovalan's feet. Her tears bathe his feet.

Kovalan lifts her and makes her stand...]

Kovalan : Kannagi... forgive me!....

Kannagi : My dear!....

[Tears fall, drop after drop]

Kovalan : Leaving you, the very sacred form of chastity, I found pleasure in the lap of a courtesan... this stone-hearted villain; this learned brute...

Kannagi : ...Do not scold him!... My husband... even my husband must not scold my husband!....

[She covers his mouth]

Kovalan : Jewel among womankind!... While you stand here, guarding the Tamil sacred lore... fish-eyed noble lady of delight... I fell under the sway of Madhavi, a woman whose love has a price... I came to think the whole world a slave to the waft of her fragrant flower-hair... that deceit-filled wretch... cut through the bonds of my family. With the whole land... every street... speaking low of me... she made me a vessel with a body but no life!

Kannagi : My dear! She is my younger sister! Do not say anything against her, my dear! Is little Manimekalai well?.... My dear!....

Kovalan : Little Manimekalai... the heaps of gold and jewels; you, a mountain of gold... all the jewels you wore... poured out and poured out... Was not that child born of the pleasure I gained!... A sign of sin!...

A mark of the feast of love whose taste was bought with a price! Kannagi, that child is the stain your husband acquired in a courtesan's house!

Kannagi : Ayyo.... Do not speak like that.... Though Manimekalai was born to Madhavi.... she is my child!... Manimekalai is my child too!

Kovalan : My goddess! You are worthy of my esteem! Worthy of honour! Forgive me... forgive this low man who could not understand your heart, Kannagi...

Kannagi : My dear!....

Kovalan : The fame that Tamils have no equal in sea-trade swelled within me... yet today... with a woman engaged in the trade of the body... I lost my reason; lost wealth beyond measure; and now I lament... a poor man!...

[To Kannagi]

My treasure!.... The name `great merchant of Poompuhar` is gone... A boat tosses in the storm... there is no way to reach the shore... Let us go into the womb of Mother Sea where the waves strike. Come... come...

Kannagi : My dear, why such a decision? In my mother's house there is gold stacked as high as an elephant... Who is there to forbid us if we take it?....

Kovalan : Life of my life!.... The town will say I am a man who lives on his father-in-law's handouts; my honour will be gone!.... No, no!

Kannagi : Your father measures pearls as though measuring grains of paddy—would even he refuse to support you, my dear?...

Kovalan : Father gave me wealth believing I was fit to live independently! I have no heart to lose that too and bring him a new burden! If there is another way that preserves honour, tell me! Otherwise death itself is better!

Kannagi : As long as I am here, do not wither in sorrow!.... I have two anklets upon my feet; I shall remove them and give them to you. Sell them, and we can begin trade anew, my dear!

Kovalan : The anklets! Sell the anklets? Ayyo... Kannagi... has the day come when my ears must hear such words... Those anklets that beautify your feet... the last ornament this lustful man had still left upon you without removing... you ask me to sell it, Kannagi!

Kannagi : Do not worry.... Listen to what I say.... What use is an ornament upon me, merely for beauty, if it is of no use to you? Go and sell it! Good times will surely return to us again... Listen to me.... my dear!

Kovalan : I shall listen!.... But would it not be a humiliation to sell the anklet here in Poompuhar itself?

Kannagi : Then?...

Kovalan : Let us go to the Pandya land, where no one can recognize us. There, in the capital city of Madurai, the anklet will fetch a good price!

Kannagi : Do as you wish... I too will come with you to Madurai.

Kovalan : Come. From now until death, not even for the space of an atom will I part from you, Kannagi!

Kannagi : My dear!

Kovalan : We must leave today itself without anyone knowing.... Let us prepare for the journey!

## Translation notes

- The paired title is preserved as `Kovalan Reformed / His Return`, keeping the source's two-part dramatic framing rather than replacing it with a single smoother title.
- The opening rooster/threshold image directly recalls scene 15. The English keeps the silence, bowing, kneeling and tears before dialogue begins.
- Kannagi's comic-emotional `என் கணவரை...என் கணவரோகூடத் திட்டக் கூடாது!` is kept as **`My husband... even my husband must not scold my husband!`**. The repetition matters more than polished English syntax.
- Kovalan's praise of Kannagi retains `தமிழ் மறை` as **`Tamil sacred lore`**, consistent with scene 14. It is not silently identified with a specific religious or literary text.
- Kovalan's terms for Madhavi in this scene are deliberately harsh and socially loaded. `கணிகை` remains `courtesan`; `விலை மாது` is rendered `a woman whose love has a price`; `உடல் வாணிபம்` becomes `trade of the body`. These are **Kovalan's accusatory words**, not neutral narrator labels.
- The source's commercial parallel is preserved: Kovalan contrasts Tamil excellence in **sea-trade** with his own ruin through what he calls **trade of the body**. The translation keeps the rhetorical parallel without endorsing his contempt for Madhavi.
- Kannagi repeatedly rejects that contempt. She calls Madhavi `my younger sister`, asks first about Manimekalai, and declares `Manimekalai is my child too`. Those lines are not softened or subordinated to Kovalan's repentance.
- `மானம்` is translated as `honour` in Kovalan's refusal to depend on either family. It is not automatically rendered `self-respect`, because the immediate source register is reputation, independence and familial dependence.
- `கடல் மாதா வயிறு` is retained as the **womb of Mother Sea**, preserving Kalaignar's personification at Kovalan's suicidal low point.
- The source form `கழற்றுமல்` is protected in Tamil. The English translates the immediate dramatic sense of Kovalan saying that the anklets are the last ornament he had not stripped away; it does not alter the archived Tamil.
- `அணுப்பொழுதும்` is rendered `not even for the space of an atom`, preserving the extreme smallness of the source image rather than reducing it to `not even for a moment`.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 20 is ethically unstable by design. Kovalan returns repentant, but his repentance initially expresses itself through abusive language about Madhavi's social position and sexuality. Kannagi repeatedly interrupts that logic: Madhavi is `my younger sister`; Manimekalai is `my child too`. The scene therefore separates Kovalan's shame from Kalaignar's larger dramatic treatment of the women. Read against the preceding scenes, Madhavi's birth-status cannot simply be taken as the play's moral verdict upon her.

That distinction strongly resonates with the Dravidian/Self-Respect challenge to inherited hierarchy and humiliation. The anti-hierarchical force is carried not by inserting modern doctrine but by the dramatic contradiction itself: Kovalan speaks in hereditary stigma; Kannagi refuses it and extends kinship across the supposed divide.

A second strand concerns economic dignity and independence. Kovalan refuses to live from either father's wealth because he fears the loss of `மானம்`; Kannagi answers with material partnership, offering her own anklets as capital to restart trade. The source does not use the term `சுயமரியாதை`, so the English retains `honour`, but the insistence on standing independently and Kannagi's economic agency can legitimately be related to later Self-Respect concerns in this separate note.

Finally, the scene again invokes Tamil collective worth through sea-commerce: Kovalan remembers the fame that `Tamils have no equal in sea-trade`. His fall is framed against that collective standard. The translation preserves the rhetoric while keeping his misogynistic commercial slurs marked as his own speech, not as explanatory truth.
