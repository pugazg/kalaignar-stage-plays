# Scene 10

## The Queen of the Arts Finds Joy

### Madhavi's House

[The house is being decorated in haste for Kovalan's arrival....]

### Street

[The beautiful carriage in which Kovalan arrives.... The carriage stops in the courtyard before Madhavi's house.

Kovalan steps down from the carriage.

Dancing women come forward to receive him.

Along the garden path between the house and the front entrance, an exuberant welcome is given.

Madhavi runs forward, falls at his feet and bows. Then, rising, she leads him in without taking her eyes from his face.

On both sides, dancers display their skill....

Near the threshold, Madhavi sees water poured for a plant break its course and run across the path. So that Kovalan's feet may not touch the little stream of muddy water, she removes the long silk shawl from her head and spreads it over the mud.

Kovalan hesitates to step upon it; then, looking at Madhavi's face and yielding to her pleading eyes, he walks across the silk.

Madhavi takes the garland from Vasanthamalai's hand to place it upon Kovalan, but he receives it himself and puts it around his own neck.

Kovalan is led into the dance hall and seated in a place of honour.

Under Madhavi's lead, dances that seize the mind are performed.

The women who had been dancing as a great crowd somehow, one by one, withdraw.]

Kovalan sits entranced by the dances.

The dancers diminish one by one, until at last Madhavi alone is dancing.

She dances.

She dances.

She goes on dancing. She dances with the anguish of separation, the hurt of disappointed love, the despair born of Kovalan's refusal to accept her, and an excess of sorrow all mingled together.

Kovalan is stunned.

The tip of her upper garment strikes the stone pillars and even they beat out the sound of drums. Her finger brushes the green creeper spread across the lattice; from it seems to tremble the sound of yazh and the resonance of the veena! In the anguish of separation, a bangle slips from her hand and falls with a “thop” to the floor. Even that sounds like music.

All her emotions burst forth as she keeps dancing. Sorrow reaches its height. Even then she dances.

Kovalan's heart pounds.

There is absolute silence in that gathering of dance.

Within that silence Madhavi droops like a creeper.

Like a worm that has fallen into fire—writhing, then growing still, then moving faintly at the last moment before life leaves it—so she bends and twists.

The dance does not stop... She has never danced like this before.

“THADAAL!”

Madhavi falls to the floor without speech or breath. Kovalan cries out “Madhavi!” and rushes to lift her. He takes water from nearby and sprinkles it upon her face.

In haste he squeezes a fruit and gives her its juice.

He rests her head upon his lap.]

Kovalan : Madhavi! Madhavi!!

[After a little while Madhavi opens her eyes and tears stream down in torrents]

Kovalan : Madhavi... Madhavi... do not cry! Why must you dance like this?

Madhavi : My lord! Is this not my final dance!...

Kovalan : Why do you speak so?...

Madhavi : I made this plan so that, after showing all my artistic skill to the one before whom I wished to show it, I might close my eyes for the last time, my lord!...

Kovalan : With this hasty decision of yours, the very art of dance praised by the land will perish!...

Madhavi : Can art arise except in a heart filled with joy?

Kovalan : Who said so? Even in a great flood of sorrow, a wave of art rises, Madhavi!...

Madhavi : In Tamilakam, where joy blossoms and sways, why should Madhavi alone nurture an art of sorrow and plunge the people into grief?

Kovalan : What suffering has come upon you that is so great?...

Madhavi : Then... has the stain of sorrow vanished from my life? Has the cuckoo of the grove begun to sing?... Has the bird of time sung a hymn of blessing for the dawn of my life? Tell me, my lord!

Kovalan : Madhavi!...

Madhavi : I want no gold... I want no wealth... I want no fame bestowed by the warrior-kings of the earth. If you, who dwell in my eyes, remain beside me always... I want no flesh, no life, no honey, not even the world where the gods dwell.

Kovalan : Madhavi!...

Madhavi : I spread myself upon you like a creeper; I follow the imprint of my beloved's feet! Say but one word—that I have brought light into your life, my dear!

Kovalan : Madhavi!...

Madhavi : You are troubled in your mind, thinking, “You are one who comes in the line of women who bewitch men”.... Is that not the truth?..

Kovalan : No, my dear!...

Madhavi : Ah!...

Kovalan : Madhavi!...

[Madhavi swoons and falls upon his lap]

## Translation notes

- `கலையரசி கண்ட உவகை` is rendered as `The Queen of the Arts Finds Joy`, retaining Madhavi's established artistic epithet while allowing the title's emotional turn to remain dramatic.
- The repeated source rhythm `ஆடுகிறாள். / ஆடுகிறாள். / ஆடிக்கொண்டே இருக்கிறாள்.` is preserved exactly in force as **`She dances. / She dances. / She goes on dancing.`** It is not compressed into descriptive prose.
- `விரகதாபம்` is rendered as `anguish of separation`; the classical-emotional register is retained without requiring a Sanskritic technical term in the main English text.
- The source makes Madhavi's body, clothing, bangle, pillars and creeper all become instruments of music. The translation keeps this cumulative sensory rhetoric rather than reducing it to `she dances intensely`.
- `தணலில் விழுந்த புழு` remains the harsh image of `a worm that has fallen into fire`; the image is not softened.
- The source onomatopoeia `“தடால்!”` is retained as `“THADAAL!”`, preserving its theatrical sound rather than converting it to a generic stage direction.
- `நாடு போற்றும் நாட்டியக் கலையே` becomes `the very art of dance praised by the land`, preserving the public/cultural stakes of Kovalan's warning.
- `சோகப் பெருவெள்ளத்திலும் கலை பெண்ணும் அலை எழுகிறது` is rendered as `Even in a great flood of sorrow, a wave of art rises, Madhavi!`; the flood/wave imagery is preserved rather than flattened into an abstract statement about creativity.
- `மகிழ்ச்சி பூத்துக் குலுங்கும் தமிழகம்` becomes `Tamilakam, where joy blossoms and sways`, retaining Kalaignar's personified political-cultural geography.
- Madhavi's cascading `வேண்டேன்` sequence is translated with repeated **`I want no...`** to preserve renunciation-like emotional escalation.
- `மயக்குகின்ற மாதர் வழி வந்தவள் நீ` is rendered as `You are one who comes in the line of women who bewitch men`. This preserves the socially stigmatizing idea of inherited courtesan lineage without replacing it by the harsher modern label `prostitute`.
- No published English edition was used in drafting or reviewing this scene.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

Scene 10 places artistic worth against inherited social stigma. Madhavi has already been celebrated publicly as `கலைக்கரசி`, yet she fears that Kovalan sees her not as an artist and person in her own right but as one born in a line of `women who bewitch men`. Kalaignar thus stages a conflict between **accomplishment and inherited social identity**. That can resonate with the Dravidian movement's anti-hierarchical insistence that birth should not determine human worth.

The scene also gives art a public function. Kovalan argues that Madhavi's death would destroy an art praised by the land; Madhavi answers that she does not wish to cultivate sorrow in a Tamilakam `where joy blossoms and sways` and plunge the people into grief. Art here is not merely private ornament: it belongs to the people and to Tamilakam's cultural life.

At the same time, the translation does not turn Madhavi into a mouthpiece for a later social programme. Her anguish remains erotic, artistic and personal. The relation to anti-hereditary hierarchy and public cultural dignity is therefore recorded only in this separate interpretive note.
