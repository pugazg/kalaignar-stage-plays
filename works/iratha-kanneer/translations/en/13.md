# Scene 13

“The Younger Queen! How did she dare to do something so outrageous, something she has never done before? She vowed to remain unmarried, saying she had no need for marriage—and now she calls me, ‘My dear, come!’ She is nearly forty. A woman who has guarded her renunciation all these years calls a horse groom and says, ‘Come closer!’ Then why did such a woman refuse marriage? A maiden? A deceiver? An unsolved riddle!

The clown's sister leaps to flirt with a horse groom. I have heard the story of ‘Raja Bhartruhari.’ This is even stranger than that! All right, all right—this place is no longer suitable for us. If I stay here, this Muthan may be swept away to some dangerous place in the flood of desire that lay restrained until now and has suddenly begun to surge!

I had a place to earn my food—to keep life moving. Now the danger that will destroy even that has arrived in the form of affection!

Poongavanam—ha, ha, ha! A storm has entered the flower-garden. I cannot stand against it, Appa, I cannot! Muthan, put a full stop to the palace job!”

Such turmoil and agitation rise within Muthan's mind as he runs away from Poongavanam. He has not yet crossed the palace gate when Sukadev's voice rings out.

“Hey, Muthan!”

“What is it, Sangu?”

“From now on you must not enter the palace.”

“Many thanks.”

“Our order is that you no longer have a job.”

“For the first time, this palace is giving a poor man exactly what he asked for!”

“What did you ask for?”

“I was going to ask you to dismiss me from the job—without my even asking, you are giving me what I wanted.”

“All right, get out. The moment the job is gone you say the fruit is sour.”

“Poor thing—how did you know that the fruit slipped into the milk and that this Muthan himself rejected it and walked away? I am leaving. Greetings.”
