# Scene 3

[A street in Pazhuthur

Thirusangu, the palace guard, and Prince Sukadev are walking along. Vedhalam comes toward them.

Vedhalam: Namaskaram, Sukadev!

Sukadev: Hmm, Vedhalam—everything well?... Any palm-leaf letter for me?...

Vedhalam: Nothing for you. Hmm—Thirusangu! There is one for you. Here, take it. I’ll be going.

Vedhalam gives the letter to Thirusangu and leaves. Thirusangu reads it, stuffs it into the pocket of his tunic, and continues walking with Sukadev.

Sukadev: Well, Thirusangu! Who sent the letter?

Thirusangu: Ah! He is an idle fellow. Every morning his one job is to plague me to death.

Sukadev: Ah! You made that very easy to understand—just like a poet!

Thirusangu: What can I say, thambi! I have a fellow of a marumagan. He says he wants a girl.

Sukadev: Why—are all children born male? If you say marumagan, doesn’t that mean your daughter’s husband?

Thirusangu: No, thambi, no! He wants a girl to marry.

Sukadev: What is the difficulty in that?

Thirusangu: When a tree ripens, does it have to call out, “Bats, come here!”?

Sukadev: Meaning?

Thirusangu: My daughter has come of age. Fellows are casting their nets. That is why this good-for-nothing has written the letter. What beauty my daughter has—what character! Can that fellow claim a right over her merely because of the aththan kinship, thambi?

Sukadev: Oh-ho! Is your daughter really that beautiful?

Thirusangu: Haven’t you ever seen her?

Sukadev: No... What is your daughter’s name?

Thirusangu: Muthayi! If I say Muthayi, she is truly a pearl!

Sukadev: Hmm! Every crow thinks its chick is golden!

Thirusangu: No, thambi. This is a cuckoo chick brooded by a crow!

Sukadev: Then she must be dark!

Thirusangu: I am not exaggerating, thambi. Muthayi is a lemon.

Sukadev: Don’t turn sour!

Thirusangu: What is this, thambi—you keep teasing an old man and tormenting him!

Sukadev: Thirusangu! Come, let’s go to your house!

Thirusangu: Don’t say such things! The prince coming to the house of a palace guard? What a spectacle! What a spectacle!

Sukadev: Don’t you know that I make no distinction of high and low? You are a servant to my father, but to me you are an older friend! Is that settled?...

Thirusangu: I am a fortunate man!

Sukadev: Come, let’s go.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/03.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition, web text or secondary English witness was used.
- The `மருமகன்` wordplay is kept partly in transliteration as `marumagan` because Sukadev’s joke depends on the kinship term’s competing sense.
- `அத்தான்` is retained as `aththan` where the kinship claim is itself part of the dialogue.
- The “lemon / sour” exchange is kept literally enough to preserve the source joke.
