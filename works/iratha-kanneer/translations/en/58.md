# Scene 58

[Palaiyakkarar's palace

In the hall, Baladevar, his wife and Maykainatha Swamigal sit in sorrow. From an upper window of the palace, Poongavanam watches and listens to them.

Swamigal: I have begun to doubt that Sukadev will return.

Baladevar's Wife: Ayyo—will my son not come back? What will I do?...

Swamigal: Fate can close even our eyes. Fate has such terrible power that even if Sukadev stood before us, it could draw a curtain across our sight.

From the upper window—

Poongavanam: Hmm, it has begun—an explanation of fate. Anna—you believe the Swami. The poor Swami believes fate... and fate lives by trusting the Swami. If fate keeps turning in a circle like this, Sukadev too must be wandering in circles somewhere.

Swamigal: Hasty women...

Poongavanam: Yes, Swami, that is completely true! I am hasty! I am indeed hasty!

Baladevar: [looking up] Poongavanam—enough. Stop!

At that moment Sukadev runs in crying, “Appa! Appa!” Seeing him, the queen runs to him, embraces him and says, “You have come back, my treasure!”

Sukadev: There is no time to fondle me—Appa! The matter has gone too far!

Baladevar: What is it, Sukadev?

Sukadev: Only if you come can a life be saved!

Baladevar: What are you saying?

Sukadev: A poor man is on his way to the gallows—save him! They called him a spy of an enemy country, charged him with murder, and sentenced him. Leave immediately, Appa—to the ruler of Vengaipuram!

Baladevar: Explain the matter fully!

Sukadev: Muthan has been sentenced to death—

Before Sukadev finishes, a great cry comes from the upper floor:

“Ayyo! Muthan!”

With a crashing sound, Poongavanam falls. Everyone runs upstairs. She lies without speech or breath.

Baladevar orders his men, “Go immediately and bring physician Boopathy.” They run.

* * *

The men reach Boopathy's house and call him.

“It has been twenty years since I entered that palace, Appa!” Boopathy says as he leaves with them.

* * *

Poongavanam lies on a bed. Boopathy examines her. When he finishes, he says, “Do not be afraid... it is mental shock,” and holds some medicines to her nose. Poongavanam opens her eyes.

Boopathy: Lord! Why did she faint so suddenly? Was there some sorrowful news?

Baladevar: Nothing like that. You remember the horse groom who used to work in our palace—that Muthan? They say he has been sentenced to death. The moment she heard it...

Poongavanam again cries, “Muthan!”

Boopathy's mind begins working very quickly. He looks at Baladevar.

Boopathy: There are stairs at the back of this palace, are there not?...

Baladevar: There are!...

Boopathy carefully looks around the room. The source-named “kaatrup purukkal” figures—the cow longing for its calf—the tiger in deerskin: those three figures hold his attention for a long time.

Then he begins to speak.

Boopathy: Honoured lord! Will you take me toward those rear stairs?—

Baladevar: Certainly! Come, Boopathy!—

Everyone goes toward the stairs. Boopathy counts them aloud: “One, two, three...”

Baladevar: What is this, Boopathy—are you counting the steps?

Boopathy: Ha, ha, ha—I am counting the steps. Along with them, I am counting an old event. Well, Maykainatha Swamigal! Poor Swami—he knows nothing except Thiruchitrambalam!

Baladevar: Speak so that I can understand.

Boopathy: I will. Younger Queen—you fainted the moment you heard that Muthan had been sentenced to death, did you not?

Baladevar: Yes—what of it?

Boopathy: Why should danger to Muthan affect this lady?

Baladevar: He was once a servant here.

Boopathy: There is no history in your family of grieving for servants! Lord Baladevar! Do you know why I counted these steps? To see whether the thirty-six steps I counted twenty-three years ago are still correct!

Baladevar: Explain fully.

Boopathy: I will. Twenty-three years ago—you, your wife and little Sukadev—the three of you left on a pilgrimage to Kashi and to see the Himalayas and other places, did you not?

Baladevar: Yes.

Boopathy: It took you two years to return.

Baladevar: Yes.

Boopathy: Younger Queen Poongavanam and, for her protection, a woman called Kaveri remained in the palace, did they not?

Baladevar: Yes!

Boopathy: Ah, I remember it very well. Everything appears before my eyes as though it were happening now. Listen—I will tell you exactly what I saw.

One night this Maykainatha Swamigal came running to me and called me urgently. There was a secret! He said it had to be hidden.

I came with him. On the way he blindfolded me. I refused. He gave me a bag of gold. I agreed.

I did not know where he was taking me. My eyes remained covered. I could tell that I was being led up some steps. I counted carefully. Thirty-six steps! I remember clearly.

In a dark room, in dim light, my eyes were uncovered. I looked around to discover where I was. These same three figures were there then too—the “kaatrup purukkal,” the cow seeking its calf, the tiger in deerskin—yes, these very ones!

A woman lay on the bed. Her face was completely covered with black cloth. She was writhing in labour pains. It seemed she might die at any moment.

Then I treated her. A beautiful child was born.

The Swami immediately handed the child to Kaveri. Kaveri ran away with it.

My eyes were covered again and I was brought back home. Later, through Kaveri, I learned that when the child grew up—that child was Muthan. After that I went abroad.

That sinful secret alone remained buried in the underworld. I asked the Swami many times—he never told me. But now I understand: this very room is where that child was born!

Yes—the place where a cradle ought to have been tied for Muthan, who today stands at the gallows, is this room!

But one thing, lord! Why did this Swami take such extraordinary care over the matter? That still does not make sense to me—

[Poongavanam suddenly cries out loudly.]

Poongavanam: I will tell you! I am Muthan's mother!

Baladevar: Ah!

Poongavanam: Anna, forgive me. This man—[pointing to the Swami]—this destroyer deceived me.

While you and Anni were away in the north, I fell into his net. This young ascetic came frequently, saying he would give religious instruction. One day, two days—he spoke of the world, then explained the noble qualities of the creator of the world. He described the love-play of divine incarnations.

Maya Kannan—Manmathan and Rathi Devi—Mahesan and Parvati—Valli's bridegroom—Brahma and Tilottama—Menaka and Vishvamitra—he told all these Puranic stories.

Those intoxicating stories made my mind reel. Why describe it further—I fell; I fell; I fell from the path of chastity guarded by Tamil womanhood!

He said he would give up asceticism and begin family life. In the end—Muthan came into my womb!

Then he abandoned me, saying, “The town will clap and laugh if I marry you.”

This man is Muthan's father—and I am his mother!

Boopathy: What, Swami—why the shock? Ha, ha, ha! They say detached ascetics will not seek even dust—but you went seeking Poongavanam! Ha, ha, ha!

Baladevar: Boopathy. I will give you five thousand gold coins—do not tell this shameful news outside!—

Boopathy: I do not want five thousand gold coins! Once before, because I became a slave to gold, this false Vedantin was able to shine in public for all these years.

Lord! Take those five thousand gold coins and turn this mendicant's monastery into a school where knowledge can grow.

One more thing! If you truly want this hidden secret not to become public, go immediately and save Muthan! The ruler of Vengaipuram has decided that he is a foreign spy. Only if you go can the truth come out.

Sukadev: Yes—Appa! Leave at once!—

[Everyone departs.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil scene 58.md.
- The source-visible Swami name form மாய்கைநாத is rendered as Maykainatha Swamigal rather than silently harmonized with earlier variants.
- The unusual repeated statue label காற்றுப் புருக்கள் is transliterated as “kaatrup purukkal” rather than silently normalized to the earlier love-bird wording.
- Boopathy's confession that he once accepted gold, Poongavanam's account of religious seduction, the revelation of Muthan's parentage, and the proposal to convert the monastery into a school are all preserved.
- The scene's religious satire and social-honour critique are retained without neutralization.
