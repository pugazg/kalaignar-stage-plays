# Scene 41

[Thirusangu's house

Vedhalam knocks at the door. Thirusangu comes out. His face is drawn.

Thirusangu: What news?

Vedhalam: Good news indeed, Thirusangu! What will you do if a case is brought against you for deceiving and ruining the Palaiyakkarar's son in order to give him your daughter?

Thirusangu: A case?

Vedhalam: Yes—and here is the proof! Sukadev's own palm-leaf letter, announcing he is about to become a Swami, is the witness!

Vedhalam shows him the letter and then safely keeps it in his own hand.

Thirusangu: I understand nothing. Things do not seem to have gone as I expected. Then where has Muthayi gone?

Vedhalam: Wherever she has gone. What do you say? Shall I show this letter to Baladevar—or will you do something good for me?

Thirusangu: What is this, man—pouring oil onto a burning fire?

Vedhalam: Thirusangu—if you want the fire extinguished, you must give me one hundred gold coins. Otherwise this will be shown to Baladevar. You will be accused of plotting to establish a marriage connection with the Palaiyakkarar's family. What answer do you give?

Thirusangu: I have no answer. Do whatever you like—I myself have come to a decision.

Vedhalam: Then suffer what has come. You thought you would use your daughter to capture the palace... if my own plans work, what happiness! You cast a hook to catch a great fish... poor man, even the small fish that was on the hook has leapt away.

Thirusangu: Schemer! Why do you interfere uselessly in this? Go at once!

Vedhalam: Then who should interfere? You stand ready, dressed for battle, to ruin Baladevar's family rank, you chandala! You deceive an ignorant young man... and conduct an auspicious wedding hour! Secret marriage—Glass Palace... Men like you must not be spared. Intriguer! Deceiver! Destroyer of families! A demon who blinds men's eyes by showing them a woman—watch what I do to you!

Vedhalam leaves.

Shaken, Thirusangu goes back inside. All the castles of greed he built lie shattered into dust before him. The rope-ladder he fashioned to reach a golden city has snapped into pieces. He waited for his daughter to become queen of a mansion so that he could rejoice. Now he has no right even to climb the mansion's doorstep.

Terrible clouds seem to pour darkness around him. His heart pounds. His dimmed eyes stare sharply at the roof beam. In his hand is a strong rope. He ties it to the beam and bolts the door.

“I wanted to rise as high as the sky. All my desires have now come to fit inside this tight loop of rope.”

That is all. He leaps toward death.

No one can now place him in the dock and call him guilty. Baladevar's hostility can do nothing to him either.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil scene 41.md.
- The source insult சண்டாளன் is rendered as “chandala” rather than replaced with a modern neutral term.
- The H-GATE-corrected phrase “கண்ணை மறைக்கும் பேய்” is represented as “a demon who blinds men's eyes.”
- Thirusangu's suicide is source action and is translated without adding motive beyond the scene's explicit collapse of his ambitions.
