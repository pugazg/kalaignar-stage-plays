# Scene 36

[Glass Palace, Anandapuram

[Night]

All the preparations for the wedding are under way. Sukadev stands on the upper floor of the Glass Palace waiting for Muthayi to arrive. A horse carriage stops at the entrance. Thirusangu and Muthayi get down. Muthayi does not see Sukadev standing above. She enters happily.

By then Sukadev has come down from the palace and meets them in the hall. The moment Muthayi sees him, she is shaken to the core. The whole world seems to spin around her. She faints and falls.

Sukadev: What—what happened?

Thirusangu: Nothing. She fainted from the shock of seeing you! What turmoil joy can cause in the heart, thambi!—

Sukadev: Yes, yes—even mine is beating like that now!—

Thirusangu: All right—you go upstairs! I will come later.

Sukadev: I will go upstairs—singing with joy!—

* * *

In a room of the palace, Muthayi slowly recovers from the faint and opens her eyes. Waterfalls of tears pour from them. Thirusangu, beside her, shows her the ornaments he holds.

Thirusangu: Muthayi—look at these! A pearl necklace and ornaments set with rubies and diamonds! Shining silk! Glittering diamonds! Precious gems! Girl! All these are...

Muthayi: The price paid for your honour!—The net being cast over your daughter!

Muthayi strikes the ornaments from his hands. They scatter across the floor.

Thirusangu: Listen to what I say!

Muthayi: There is nothing to say! Everything is clear now. The shell itself brings the pearl and throws it into the mud. The red-lotus vine itself sheds its petals and gives them to a buffalo.

Thirusangu: Speaking beautifully is no great achievement. The education that lets you speak like this came from the money I struggled to earn for you—and now love makes you shout whatever comes to mind! That Muthan loves only your beauty. Remember this: the beautiful body for whose sake the Palaiyakkarar's son is performing penance—I raised that body. I raised it! Do not betray me.

Muthayi: Tch! Is this how you speak to your own daughter?

Thirusangu: Daughter! If you were truly a daughter, would you let your father's heart burn like this? If you were my daughter—wouldn't you think of helping an old father, a loving father, a man who suffered many days in poverty and now hopes at last to live a little?

For my sake, am I asking you to leap into fire? Am I asking for some great sacrifice? I am saying I will place you in a position that others cannot attain and worship you there like a goddess—and you sob, gasp and lament, foolish girl!

Muthayi: Appa—do me just one favour—kill me!

Thirusangu: I did not spend all this money raising you merely to kill you.

Muthayi: Because you raised me, do you mean to kill me while keeping me alive? Appa! Do not torture me. This is your daughter's last request—her final wish—do not turn my life into fire, Appa!

Thirusangu: I have fulfilled so many wishes of yours. Can you not fulfil this one wish of mine—my first and last?

Muthayi: I have bought you tender coconuts many times. What difference is there between what you are saying and a coconut tree saying, “Just once, let me fall on your head”?

Thirusangu: Oh—too clever by half! Keep quiet! In the morning you and the prince will be married. Get ready!—

Thirusangu leaves quickly. Muthayi collapses there.

Out in the street, Thirusangu sees Vedhalam approaching. The sight gives him fresh hope. After all, it was on Vedhalam's advice that the secret wedding had been arranged at the Glass Palace in Anandapuram. Thirusangu calls eagerly:

Thirusangu: Come! You have arrived at exactly the right time.

Vedhalam: Well, Thirusangu, everything has been arranged properly, hasn't it?

Thirusangu: All the arrangements are fine. There is only one problem.

Vedhalam: What is it?

Thirusangu: Muthayi refuses to marry Sukadev.

Vedhalam: Fine! This is like saying, “The meal is ready—only there is no rice!” Didn't you say earlier that the two were lovers?

Thirusangu: Muthayi is a stubborn girl! I cannot change her determination. You must somehow...

Vedhalam: I will try. Where is she now?

Thirusangu: Inside.

Vedhalam: Good. None of you come in. I will set her right!

Thirusangu: I am fortunate indeed. Somehow bring her onto the right path.

Vedhalam goes to where Muthayi is. Thirusangu climbs the stairs and goes to Sukadev. Vedhalam sees Muthayi lying on the floor.

Vedhalam: Foolish girl! Get up! Why are you lying like this? Won't you speak? Is this the time to lie grieving? The sea is raging—and you sit on a raft singing a lament! Look to what must be done. Dear child! Tears cannot dissolve danger!

Muthayi looks at Vedhalam and sits up.

Vedhalam: Why are you crying?

Muthayi: The town laughs! I cry!

Vedhalam: True. Tender-hearted maiden—understand that there is still a chance to escape this turmoil!

Muthayi: A chance to escape?

Vedhalam: Yes—a chance! Your freedom! The sign that your suffering is departing has come in the form of Vedhalam.

Muthayi: What are you saying?

Vedhalam: You do not know how affectionately Muthan and I lived together. The gold you sent—Sumathi pocketed it! Muthan was imprisoned in the military camp. I gave the wealth I had saved with great hardship and have now rescued him.

Muthayi: Ah! Has my Muthan been released?

Vedhalam: Ayyo, poor girl! Should someone who cannot even sleep have to suffer so much? Listen to one more happy piece of news! I learned that your cruel father had deceived you and arranged this marriage, so I ran here at once to save you! I have come like a wedding guest, but I am trying to free my friend's beloved!

Muthayi: Ayya, may you live many years...

Vedhalam: Why should life be long in this unjust world, child? Let that go. Listen, little bird! Muthan will come here tonight. Leave with him!

Muthayi: Will he come?

Vedhalam: Would he fail to come? Be careful—you must both leave with great cunning. Good, I must not delay here. I will go outside and attend to what is necessary.

Muthayi: How can I bless you enough! You are charitable—good—the very form of truth—the noblest of men.

Vedhalam: The world does not know good from evil, child. Be careful. I will come.

Vedhalam leaves. Thirusangu, who has been waiting for him, eagerly asks, “What happened?” When Vedhalam answers, “Victory,” Thirusangu can barely contain his delight. The two go to Sukadev. Sukadev welcomes Vedhalam with great enthusiasm.

While matters stand so here, let us see what has become of Muthan.
