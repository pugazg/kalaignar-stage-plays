# Scene 15

[Monastery

The townspeople are gathered. Commander Vetrivelan, physician Boopathy, Vedhalam and many others are present. Maykanatha Swamigal is delivering a discourse.

Swamigal: Beloved ones! Commander Vetrivelan has just spoken on the benefits of the army for those who did not know them. His words were filled and overflowing with flavour—and heat. Vetrivelan called for men with victorious shoulders to gather in the camp of warriors where the tiger springs to defend the kingdom. I too am going to say the same.

Bhagavan Parandhaman told Parthiban to enter the Bharata war. Today people preach that there should be no war—that war is a madness. Let them read the Bhagavad Gita. They will understand how much that flute-player said about the necessity of war. Did he merely speak? That compassionate lord himself sat as charioteer.

Lord Subramanian slaughtered bloodthirsty rakshasas on the battlefield. Women too took part in war in the age of the Puranas! Bama put on battle dress and killed Narakasuran. Therefore the sacred service called warfare has the approval of the gods themselves. Shiva's trident—the spear of Shiva's son—Vishnu's discus—what do they signify? Peace? Or battle? Therefore, beloved ones, become warriors!

Not everyone who goes to battle dies. Those whose fate is to die will die anywhere and in any manner. It is not only by taking up a sword—many have ended their lives merely by slipping on a banana peel.

And even if you do die, if you die in war, warrior's heaven waits for you above. For a warrior there is fame not only on earth—but in heaven as well. Raise the garland of victory upon your broad shoulders—and for that, raise swords in your hands! Work for the unemployed. Life for those without a livelihood. A feast for the brave. Good men who defend the country! Join today! The Lord will stand by you! Thiruchitrambalam. Strike your mighty shoulders and set forth declaring, “God is our refuge!” Thiruchitrambalam—long live the path of virtue!

People: Thiruchitrambalam.

The gathering disperses. The commander and the Swami meet privately.

Commander: I offer you my deepest thanks!

Swamigal: I did my duty.

Commander: If heads of monasteries like you set out to serve the country, both worlds will be filled with light.

Swamigal: I will do all I can.

Commander: I will meet you later! I take leave.

Swamigal: Thiruchitrambalam—long live the path of grace!

The Swami returns toward his pedestal. Only physician Boopathy remains seated there. Seeing him, the Swami starts.

Swamigal: What, Boopathy! Have you still not gone?

Boopathy: I forgot even myself, Swami! I lost my heart entirely in your discourse. Ah—what meaningful words—what intoxicating speech.

Swamigal: Go, man! Stop tormenting me!

Boopathy: I am going! Hee, hee, hee! “Do not take up sword or spear—worship the Lord of Vaikuntha! Do not carry a shield in your hand—carry the sacred ash of the Lord of Kailasa!” Where is that? And now: “Raise the garland of victory upon your broad shoulders. For that, raise swords in your hands!!” Where is this? All illusion! Magic! Is it not, Swami?

Swamigal: Boopathy! Enough. Go on your way.

Boopathy: I am praising you, Swami! Praising you. Ah, Lord! What things you find uses for! They say every great saint who appears in the world is one of your incarnations. You incarnated as Paramatma, taught the Bhagavad Gita and told Arjuna to fight! Then, they say, you yourself incarnated as Bhagavan Buddha! You showed Ashoka the good path by teaching that war is evil! When even you yourself meet death, what is this Swami in comparison! Oh, I forgot. He too is a great saint! Your incarnation, isn't he!

Swamigal: Boopathy...

Boopathy: Enough, Swami! I sing “Praise! Praise!”—why are you losing your patience?

The commander told you something too; I heard that as well. He said great saints like you should also serve the country! No, sir, no. These poor people of an enslaved country—let at least this mercy remain for them! Do not make them experience your patriotism directly! Let the path of grace live safely with you.

Swamigal: When a sadhu is enraged, even the forest cannot contain him, Boopathy!

Boopathy: It is not the sadhu that is frightened! Soodhu, vaadhu, theedhu—fraud, wrangling and evil—those are what tremble.

Swamigal: Why this quarrel between the two of us?

Boopathy: Why quarrel? Just tell me that secret.

Swamigal: Boopathy—I bow to you. Please go!

Boopathy: I am going—but Swami, remember this: a lie grows intoxicating little by little and blossoms! Truth, however, explodes all at once! Ha, ha, ha! I take leave.

Boopathy goes. The Swami lets out a deep sigh and sits on the pedestal.
