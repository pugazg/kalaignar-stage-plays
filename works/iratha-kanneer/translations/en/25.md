# Scene 25

[Thendral Palace.

Palanquin-like horse carriages stand ready to depart. Mounted warriors prepare on their horses. The men recruited into the army are loaded into the carriages. The commander's decorated carriage is also waiting at the entrance. Palaiyakkarar Baladevar, Thirusangu and Sukadev stand before the commander, ready to see him off.

Vetrivelan says to a warrior:

“Where is that Muthan? Bring him too and throw him into a carriage.”

The warriors immediately run inside. Vedhalam whispers secretly into the commander's ear:

“If we make him walk until we cross the town, there will be a chance for you to see her.”

Vetrivelan: Yes! Put that donkey in fetters and drag him along. Let him walk until we have crossed the town.

The warriors who went inside open the room where Muthan is locked up.

Muthan: Why—is this my release?

A Warrior: Release from here. But the torments come after the release! Worthless fellow! Taste this freedom. You will lie mute. You will not get clothes to wear. There will be no food for your hunger. Take this savage wolf away!

Muthan: I see—are all the people of Vengaipuram like this?

Warrior: Don't speak! Put on the fetters.

Muthan is dragged out. Meanwhile Vedhalam, who has been standing in the street beside the commander, sees a woman running quickly down the road. He immediately hurries away from Thendral Palace toward her.

In the street—

Vedhalam: Where are you running, girl?

Sumathi: Oh!... Is it you?... Have you seen Muthan?

Vedhalam: It is for Muthan that I have been running around. They are about to take him to Vengaipuram right now.

Sumathi: Ayyo... I must see him.

Vedhalam: Impossible! He cannot be released now. To release him, one hundred gold coins are needed.

Sumathi: That is exactly what I have brought. Here—please give this to him.

Vedhalam: Dear girl! You have done a good deed! On Muthan's behalf, my thanks! I will go at once and rescue him. You run away from here!

[Sumathi leaves.]

Vedhalam opens the bag and looks inside. There are one hundred gold coins. His face swells with happiness. There is also a letter in the bag. Vedhalam takes the palm-leaf letter and reads it to himself:

“My beloved!

As promised, I have sent one hundred gold coins. They say a plucked bottle-gourd is no good for curry; for that reason I have kept the kisses to give you in person. This evening—our grove!

With love,
Muthayi.”

Vedhalam hides both the letter and the hundred gold coins in his clothing and returns toward Thendral Palace.

Vetrivelan has begun to depart from Thendral Palace. He tells Vedhalam to get into his carriage.

Vetrivelan: Vedhalam—come with me until we cross the town! You may return after that.

Vedhalam: As you command, lord!

The carriages begin to move. Muthan alone is kept in fetters and made to walk through the street. The townspeople gather noisily to watch Vetrivelan's procession. Muthan walks without being able to raise his eyes toward anyone. Sukadev is filled with great delight at Muthan's condition. Vetrivelan's procession moves through the streets of Pazhuthur.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil scene 25.md.
- Vedhalam's theft of both the penalty gold and Muthayi's letter is preserved as the decisive plot action.
- Muthayi's bottle-gourd/curry line is deliberately translated conservatively because the closed Tamil wording is unusual; it is not silently repaired.
- Muthan's public humiliation in fetters remains distinct from the festive military procession around him.
