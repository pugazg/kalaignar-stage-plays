# Scene 31

[Sukadev's mansion

Thirusangu sits opposite him.

Sukadev speaks in great delight.

Sukadev: Muthayi and I entered into a Gandharva marriage. She swore upon God that she would never forget me.

Thirusangu: Yes.

Sukadev: We ran through the pleasure garden—we bathed in the river—I said, “Muthayi”; she called me “Natha.” She moved her eyes—a diamond necklace for her neck! Her waist bent—I fastened a jewelled waist-belt! She put on the ornaments and smiled—beauty! Grace! My dear! My peacock! I said, “Come to the bedchamber,” and she agreed to come!

Ah! And just then I woke up!... It was all a dream, Thirusangu—all a dream!

Thirusangu: It will not remain a dream, thambi—not while this Thirusangu lives!

Sukadev: The gold and diamonds are ready—but my father must agree to turn them into a marriage chain!—

Thirusangu: That is where we must struggle a little.

Sukadev: Struggle?

Thirusangu: Yes. Arjuna became a snake to marry Alli! Velan became an old man to marry Valli!

Sukadev: I do not know what I will have to become!

At that moment Vedhalam enters carrying a stack of palm-leaf letters.

Vedhalam: You must become the next Palaiyakkarar—what doubt is there in that?

Sukadev: Well, well! Vedhalam?... You have come at the right moment, like Narada! Sit down.

Vedhalam: What weighty consultation is going on?...

Sukadev: Is there any palm-leaf letter for me?...

Vedhalam: Yes, yes!... Here!... [He gives him one.]

Sukadev: I have great faith in your brain!... So at this important moment, give me a first-rate idea.

Vedhalam: How can I give an idea, thambi, without hearing the matter?

Sukadev: This is a divine secret—you must not tell anyone! Understand?

Vedhalam: I know that if something is a secret it must not be told to anyone. But I still have to know what the secret is!

Sukadev: Thirusangu—do not be afraid—I will tell him!

Thirusangu: I am not afraid. Tell him.

Sukadev: Vedhalam—it is about a woman—

Vedhalam: Then it must be a matter of love.

Sukadev: Ah, you found it! What a brain you have! Listen, I will tell you more—Thirusangu's daughter Muthayi loves me with her whole heart.

Vedhalam: Excellent! Is that so?—[Vedhalam's face changes suddenly. He composes himself.]

Sukadev: I too have fallen into the intoxication of love. She has sworn that she will touch no one but me, touch no one but me.

Vedhalam: Ah—what majesty love possesses! The whole world is bound by love and turns within it!

Sukadev: I must marry her now, no matter what. Appa will not agree—he will say our status will be lowered! Tell me what can be done.

Vedhalam: It is a difficult problem—the Palaiyakkarar's son—the daughter of Thirusangu—there is a great distance between the two! But love is divine. That too cannot simply be denied!—Ah! An idea comes to me!

Sukadev: What—what? Thirusangu! You listen too!

Vedhalam: Your father has no child except you—so he cannot reject you! But if you ask whether he will therefore consent to the marriage, he will not do that either! There is only one way. Take the girl out of town and complete the marriage secretly. After the marriage your father will be angry for a little while—then he will calm himself down! The matter will be settled easily.

Sukadev: Ah—that is exactly the right idea!—A victorious idea that could arise only in Vedhalam's brain... What do you say, Thirusangu?

Thirusangu: I will act exactly as you wish, thambi!—

Sukadev: Then let us attend at once to what must be done.

Vedhalam's grotesque face broadens. Even more light gathers in his frightening eyes. Under his breath he mutters:

“Fools—they come to me asking for advice. Naturally I will give advice that benefits me! Vedhalam! Victory is calling you—walk quickly toward it!”
