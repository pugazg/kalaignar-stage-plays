# Scene 20

[At the grove beside the riverbank

Muthayi and her friend Sumathi walk carrying water pots. Muthayi has a packet in her hand.

Sumathi: Poor thing. After two or three days without seeing your lover, your eyes must have bloomed from waiting. Isn't that right, Muthayi!

Muthayi: Sumathi, enough—don't start.

Sumathi: Why? Does it hurt so much even to speak of it?

Muthayi: You would understand what kind of pain it is only if you experienced it yourself.

Sumathi: Lord! Spare me experiences like this.

Muthayi: Even the Lord has had them, girl!

Sumathi: What did you say!!

Muthayi: In the stories!

Sumathi: That's what I thought—had suffering made you remember God at last? You are certainly the right beloved for your lover!

Muthayi: Sumathi! Sumathi! There he is—he is coming. Run away!

Sumathi: Let him come—I am not ashamed.

Muthayi: We are, girl—go away!

Sumathi: All right, I'm going, Muthayi! You'll come before I finish bathing, won't you?

Muthayi: Keep bathing until I come. Is your body made of jaggery that it will dissolve in water?

Sumathi: My body isn't jaggery. He will come now and describe you—“rock candy, honey, boiled syrup!” and all that. Then think about whose body was cast from jaggery!

Muthayi: Chatterbox! Sumathi... if I begin teasing you...

Sumathi: Don't pour water on barren ground, Muthayi!... There he comes—talk... talk... keep talking!... But don't imagine that I alone have been carried away by the river. Come quickly!

Laughing, Sumathi goes toward the river.

Instead of walking straight to Muthan, Muthayi hides beside a flowering plant, plucks a flower and throws it at him. He catches it, crushes it and throws it away, then sits down in sorrow. Muthayi goes to him.

Muthayi: Why are you sad?... You haven't come for two days. Are you getting ahead of me because you think I will be angry?

Muthan: It's nothing, Muthayi...

Muthayi: Then what are you worrying about?... Eat this.

She opens the packet in her hand and holds out the food.

Muthan: I don't want anything.

Muthayi: What is wrong with you? Are you worried because you lost your job? Let it go. Now we can meet often, can't we?

Muthan: We cannot meet.

Muthayi: Why? Are you afraid what happened that day will happen again? I have already resolved to face all that.

Muthan: I admit defeat. I cannot stand against your father.

Muthayi: Because he works at the palace?

Muthan: No. He would say, “The place where the cuckoo should sing is a cool flower-grove!” Can I answer, “No, on a crumbling wall”?

Muthayi: I cannot believe it. He would not have called you his cool flower-grove.

Muthan: What remedy does he need in order to describe me? I know it myself—I am no garden. I am a desert!

Muthayi: If you are a desert, I am the date-palm that grows in it.

Muthan: Muthayi! They say even a camel crosses the desert only after storing food. I do not even have the means to begin the journey of life. I do not know the road.

Muthayi: Roads do not appear by themselves.

Muthan: I have found one myself.

Muthayi: Found what?

Muthan: Yes. I have found one way—I have found one way.

Muthayi: What is this?... What are you saying?

Muthan: I have decided to forget you.

Muthayi: Are you joking?

Muthan: No. Circumstance has played with my life. I have joined the army as a soldier.

Muthayi: Ah!

Muthan: I am one of the warriors of Vengaipuram! Today or tomorrow, Vengaipuram will become the place where I live.

Muthayi: Whom did you ask before joining the army?

Muthan: Whom can I ask? I asked poor Muthan. He said Muthayi is a woman who ought to become queen of a mansion—give up the thought of her. Muthan the lover shed tears. In a harsh voice, in a choked tone, poor Muthan gave the order: forget Muthayi! I have forgotten her—I have forgotten my beauty. I have forgotten the treasure that came to dwell in my thoughts. I have forgotten.

Muthayi: You will forget. Why wouldn't you! If your heart becomes a rock, if you turn into an unfeeling log, you will forget. Do not say “I will forget”—say, “I am pushing the woman who trusted me with all her heart from the top of a mountain.”

What crime have I committed that you should forget me—other than being born the daughter of a greedy father! What is love? Is it a mirror that reflects the shape of whoever stands before it? Is it a picture to be erased and drawn again? I thought love was an indestructible painting—an unmoving Himalaya. Was that only madness?

You preach justice to me now: “Go swim and play in unstable wealth.” What difference is there between you and my father, who thinks I am a commodity for the market? Why have you turned into a statue?

Muthan: Muthayi!

Muthayi: My history will not end, my lord, with “the lover forgot her—the beloved flew elsewhere”! It will end only with “he abandoned life itself”—is that acceptable? If so, in this shivering Margazhi, or in that rushing flood over there—prepare to see my corpse in one of the two.

Muthan: Muthayi—forgive me.

Muthayi: You will not even remember the thought of forgetting me, will you?

Muthan: I have already registered my name with the commander.

Muthayi: So what? Is withdrawing such a great thing?

Muthan: I can withdraw. But...

Muthayi: What now—another “but”? Are you quoting a grammar that says beauty is forbidden to a warrior? Don't be angry because I say this! Forgive me. What you have joined is not an army at all! You are going to become one of the fortress guards of the man who has enslaved the country.

Muthan: That is true. But what I was trying to say is...

Muthayi: What?

Muthan: They say that if a man joins the army and then withdraws, he must pay a penalty of one hundred gold coins. Otherwise they will not release him.

Muthayi: One hundred gold! All right—I will give it.

Muthan: You?

Muthayi: Yes. Appa has saved it for my marriage and locked it away without anyone knowing. What better use could it have than this? I will send it tomorrow morning. That will be enough, won't it?

Muthan: Enough—through whom will you send it?

Muthayi: Through my friend Sumathi.

Muthan: Even if I am not there, tell her to give it to Vedhalam at the post house!

Muthayi: All right, leave that. Eat.

She pushes the food into his mouth. Then Sumathi's loud voice rings out:

“Muthayi! I have bathed a thousand times. Even the river has run out of water. Run here quickly!”

“I'm coming!”

In a hurry Muthayi stuffs more food into Muthan's mouth until he nearly chokes, picks up her pot and runs toward the river. As she runs she calls back:

“Tomorrow I will send Sumathi with the gold. Don't forget to withdraw!”

With his mouth full of food, Muthan cannot answer and only nods. Muthayi keeps turning back to look at him as she goes—and collides with Sumathi. Sumathi has not even entered the river yet!—

Overflowing with delight, the two of them begin bathing in the river.
