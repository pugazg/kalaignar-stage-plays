# Scene 45

[Adaikkalapuri temple

A bell keeps ringing. The young ascetic Sukadev seems absorbed in meditation! The chanting of “Muthayi” is proceeding with full intensity.

At that moment a grey-bearded Swami arrives. Who is this false Swami? Sukadev may not know—but let us know: Vedhalam has come disguised as a white-bearded Swami!

Vedhalam approaches Sukadev.

Vedhalam: Young ascetic!... First, my blessings.

Sukadev: White-bearded elder! The world is false!—What do you want?

Vedhalam: Truthful young man in this false world! What is your native place? Looking at you, it seems you have lost something that captured your heart!—

Sukadev: You are a wondrous man! How did you see what lies inside me? How did you speak it? Have you drunk the divine nectar that lets sages know all three times?

Vedhalam: Thambi—I can see with my eye of wisdom that you are a man defeated in love—is that true?

Sukadev: Yes—how do you know?

Vedhalam: The virtuous know every corner of the world. You are a man of the palace! Bewitched by a woman, you have become a resident of an ashram—is that not so?

Sukadev: Yes, Swami. They say only those freed from the enchantment of women can become residents of an ashram. I, however, have been bound by that very enchantment and become a mendicant.

Vedhalam: Nothing strange in that. Vishvamitra left the palace for the forest—but even there the trouble caused by beauties did not leave him! His penance had no limit—and Menaka bore him a child!

Sukadev: Ah! Great ascetic, rich in ripened experience and abundant devotion! Through you alone I must become blessed. Grant me a boon.

Vedhalam: I understand. You must attain that four-letter woman.

Sukadev: Mu...tha...yi... Ah! You said it exactly, true guru! Four pearls make up the name of that jewel among women.

Vedhalam: I will arrange your marriage to her. You do not need this appearance at such a young age! The proper human course is to commit every outrage that must be committed—and only in tottering old age cry “Siva! Siva!”

Sukadev: [tearing off his beard and moustache] Will I really get my Muthayi? My god who came unasked! My father blessed by divine grace, who has come between Muthayi and me like Narada between Murugan and Valli! Praise! Praise!

Vedhalam: Good, come. Let us go to where I am staying. You will get Muthayi soon.

The two leave the temple. Believing that with the Swami's help he will somehow regain his beloved, Sukadev goes with Vedhalam.

Poor fellow. What difference is there between someone who mistakes the mouth of a serpent for a sandalwood bowl—and Sukadev, who fails to recognize Vedhalam beneath the Swami?
