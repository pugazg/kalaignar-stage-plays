# Scene 5

A section of the Palaiyakkarar’s palace. Muthan stands holding a magnificent horse. Dressed for riding, Sukadev walks proudly toward it. From the upper part of the palace, the Palaiyakkarar and his wife wait to watch their son ride. Beside Baladevar stands a woman of about forty. With Baladevar’s facial features and the same tall build, she must surely be his sister. Though she may be around forty, her body has not lost its firmness. There is no glow of contentment on her face. She stands as though occupied by some private thought.

Sukadev reaches the horse, looks up toward the top of the palace and says:

Sukadev: Shall I mount the horse and leap all the way up there?

Hearing this, the forty-year-old woman looks at him and teases, “No, no! First let us see whether you can hold the horse.”

Angered, Sukadev says, “Aththai! Do you think so little of me? Just watch—like Raja Desingu...” Before he can finish, she laughs and says, “Snapping like a lizard is no use... you must master Panchakalyani.” There is no warmth in that laugh.

Sukadev strokes the horse’s neck. The horse neighs and kicks. The kick lands on Muthan, who is standing behind it; he falls and gets back up. Seeing it, Sukadev’s aunt cries, “Ayyo!” Baladevar, seeing the same thing, scolds him: “That fool deserved it. Why was the blind idiot standing behind the horse?”

Sukadev: Hey, Muthan! Good that you fell. Move away!

Saying this, he springs onto the horse. The horse lifts its legs and dances wildly, trying to throw him off. Sukadev screams, “Muthan! Muthan! Run here!” Before Muthan can reach him, the horse rolls Sukadev to the ground and runs away. Baladevar and the others who have gathered rush over. By then Sukadev has stood up. The anger that should have gone toward the horse now turns upon Muthan.

Sukadev: Lazy Muthan! Haven’t you eaten your rice?... What if the horse had trampled me?

Sukadev’s Mother: Suka! You aren’t hurt anywhere, are you, my dear?

Sukadev: No, Amma! This horse is no good.

His aunt interrupts with a smile.

Aunt: Yes. That is why it stopped with just this much.

Sukadev: Aththai! Keep quiet! This sleepy-faced donkey didn’t put the bridle on properly—

Muthan: I did put it on properly!

Sukadev: Don’t answer me back, you dog that licks discarded leaf-plates! Watch yourself or you’ll get kicked!

Aunt: Why, Sukadev? You fell and now you rage at him? Muthan! Go. Palace horses all kick like that.

Muthan leaves.

Sukadev: Aththai, you are making me angry.

Baladevar: Poongavanam! Why are you defending that Muthan so much?

Poongavanam: Anna! First have your son practise on a toy horse, then let him ride a carousel horse, and only after that tell him to mount a real horse.

Sukadev: Fine! First you get married and leave the palace.

Baladevar: Hey, Sukadev! Enough.

Poongavanam: Forgive me, Appa! It was my mistake to speak with you at all.

Sukadev: Aththai! What loss is it to me if you do not speak? You have a daughter at home... and as for your own marriage...

Baladevar: Sukadev! Won’t you simply go!...

Sukadev: I’m going—I’m going!

With sorrow overflowing on her face, Poongavanam quickly leaves the place.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/05.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition, web text or secondary English witness was used.
- `Raja Desingu` and `Panchakalyani` are retained as source names.
- `எச்சில் இலை நாயே` is translated as the deliberately degrading “dog that licks discarded leaf-plates,” preserving the class-inflected insult rather than sanitizing it.
- The aunt’s “Palace horses all kick like that” is kept direct so its dramatic double edge is not explained away.
- The source line `பல்லிக்கடித்துப் பயனில்லை` is rendered conservatively as “Snapping like a lizard is no use”; the Tamil reading remains unchanged and authoritative.
