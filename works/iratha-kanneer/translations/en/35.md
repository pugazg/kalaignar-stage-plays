# Scene 35

[Commander's mansion in Vengaipuram

The feather pen in the commander's hand is scribbling something. At times the flutter that falls within his chest seems to reach even the pen. Perhaps that is why Vetrivelan keeps scribbling, “Muthayi, Muthayi.” A warrior enters and stands bowing.

Vetrivelan: What?

Warrior: Vedhalam is asking permission to see you.

Vetrivelan: Vedhalam? Tell him to come—tell him to come!

The warrior goes out. The commander's eagerness surges. Vedhalam enters.

Vedhalam: Affectionate lord! Your servant bows.

Vetrivelan: Come, Vedhalam! I imagine you have come with victory.

Vedhalam: Only the beginning of victory, lord! Our pigeon is caught by a new hunter.

Vetrivelan: What—what?

Vedhalam: I am speaking of Muthayi. The pigeon that sat upon the branch called Muthan did not fall into the snare you set—and now it is suffering as the target of a hunter called Sukadev!

Vetrivelan: Is that so?—Then I have no hope left.

Vedhalam: Take heart—even if a storm blows, the pigeon will not leave that branch. So before the hunter takes aim, if we cut the branch, both pigeon and branch will fall into the snare you set. Then we can throw the branch away and make that golden pigeon your pigeon.

Vetrivelan: Say it so I can understand—say it clearly.

Vedhalam: I will. Sukadev and Muthayi are going to be married at the Glass Palace in Anandapuram. You must tell Muthan this and cunningly send him to Anandapuram. If he goes, Muthayi will leave with him!—After that, we can take care of the rest. Send Muthan immediately!

Vetrivelan: Good! You stay hidden for a while! I will summon Muthan. Hey, who is there? [A warrior comes.] Bring Muthan here immediately! [The warrior leaves.]

The place where Muthan is confined.

His punishment in the ground fetters has been eased somewhat; he is now confined with only ordinary fetters. A warrior comes and takes him away. Muthan enters the commander's room. Vedhalam is not there.

Vetrivelan: Come, Muthan! Come! Good young man! I have made you suffer greatly. The very first time I saw you, your alert eyes and the restless energy of your body caught my attention. But after listening to Palaiyakkarar Baladevar, I tormented you too much. I regret it deeply.

Muthan: Ayya! You have changed so suddenly! How did you discover that I am without anyone to turn to? Even a god could not have come and told you!

Vetrivelan: A human being possesses something called conscience. When a human turns into a beast, that conscience becomes a pigeon and lies subdued. When beastliness is defeated, the conscience that had become a pigeon rises like a spotted peacock and dances! In that dance the beauty of truth begins to shine.

Muthan: Ah! A splendid thought. Even in this place such a thought can be born—like a red lotus sprouting in mud!

Vetrivelan: Muthan! Do you know what opened my eyes? Sukadev is going to marry your beloved Muthayi by force. When I heard this news, affection for you arose in me. Your beloved is wavering there, waiting for your help. You must go at once. You must save her.

Muthan: Is that true?

Vetrivelan: Yes. The wedding is at the Glass Palace in Anandapuram! Run before his life leaves him! Muthan! I will tell you one thing. You and Muthayi may live as a family here in this very town under my protection!—But why speak of that now? Go at once! Save the one who is your very life! Why should the flower of your true love fall and wither because of me?

Muthan: I am deeply grateful to you, Ayya!

Vetrivelan: Today! Run this very moment! Regain the love you lost!

Muthan bows and leaves the room. He is thoroughly confused. Then a distinctive sound is heard from the commander's room. Muthan hides himself and listens. Inside, the commander and Vedhalam are talking.

Vedhalam: Excellent, lord! You acted extremely well.

Vetrivelan: The fellow was completely deceived! Vedhalam! What is the next arrangement?

Vedhalam: The next? I will tell you. Muthan will rescue her somehow. The two of them will escape together. At that point some of our horsemen must attack them—separate Muthayi from Muthan—and bring her to you. Muthan will suffer without knowing where his beloved has gone. Let him perish somewhere—what is that to us?

Vetrivelan: Good. Then I will send the horsemen under your own leadership to seize Muthayi and bring her here.

Vedhalam: Excellent! I too will leave immediately for Anandapuram! After all, I must deliver Muthayi to you alive, must I not?

Vetrivelan: Good, Vedhalam!

Having heard everything, Muthan leaves the place with a firm decision.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil scene 35.md.
- The opening pen/heart image is translated conservatively from the source without treating its unusual wording as a Tamil error.
- The pigeon/branch/hunter/snare metaphor is preserved because Vedhalam uses it to explain the entire abduction plan.
- The source pronoun in Vetrivelan's “run before his life leaves him” line is retained rather than silently changing gender or referent.
- Muthan's suspicion, Vetrivelan's false repentance and Muthan's overhearing of the real plan are all kept explicit.
