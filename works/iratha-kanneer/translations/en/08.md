# Scene 8

Sukadev and Thirusangu walk through the grove beside the riverbank, talking.

Thirusangu: Thambi—ever since you came, that's enough! Ayyo! From that moment Muthayi has talked of nothing but you!

Sukadev: Is that so? What does she say? What does she say!

Thirusangu: She says there is courage in your walk. Majesty in your glance. Sweetness in your speech. And in your hand... she said there is something or other...

Sukadev: Fingers! Fingers!

Thirusangu: No, thambi, not fingers...

Sukadev: Nails! Nails!!

Thirusangu: Not that either, thambi—go on with you! How would I know all this wretched love-talk!

Sukadev: Then Muthayi is in love with me!

Thirusangu: I have read in stories of love at first sight. Only now am I seeing it in real life.

Sukadev: Ah! If there were a father like you, and if that father had a daughter like Muthayi!... the world would soon become a garden of love—Muthayi! You and I...

Sukadev stands lost in himself. Elsewhere in the same grove—

“Veena and melody!

Eyes and light!

Warrior and sword!”

Pouring such words from her coral lips, Muthayi lies with her head in Muthan's lap.

Muthan: You have subdued me! But I fear that my hope will be destroyed.

Muthayi: Why?

Muthan: At least you are the palace guard's child! I have lost my mother—I do not know who my father is—I have no kith or kin—I am alone.

Muthayi: The stars gather in crowds. The moon stands alone. But is it not the moon that the water-lily waits for?

Muthan: My lily! I am only the man who grooms the palace horses! They treat me there like a dog.

Muthayi: Do not say that. Even the dogs there have silk mattresses—almonds—servants to guard them—every comfort!

Muthan: Your father sees me every day, all my patience lying in the horse stable. Would he give you to me? A dream! A dream!

Muthayi: I will make even the dream come true. My dear! Leave your worries! Come—look at me! Won't you look? Is my face not medicine enough to cure your sorrow?

Muthan looks lovingly at Muthayi. She too purses her lips for a kiss. At once Muthayi gets up.

Muthayi: Hush! Don't fly!

Muthan: Can a skylark keep from flying at the touch of spring?

Muthayi: One wing is not enough for flight!

Muthan: That is why I am asking you to come with me.

Muthan tries to embrace her. Muthayi slips free and runs away. She runs; he chases her. Sukadev and Thirusangu, approaching the grove while talking, see them.

Sukadev: Of course! Of course! Evening at the edge of the eyes—the lovers at the edge of the grove!

Thirusangu does not know what to do. Just when he has caught and prepared what he thinks is a suitable bridegroom for his daughter, a thunderbolt large enough to fell an ox has dropped upon him. Even so, he quickly manages the situation.

Thirusangu: Prince! Do not misunderstand. This fellow has begun to assault my daughter! Hey! You witless brute! What have you done? If we had not come at this very moment, you would have ruined my family's honour! Why are you standing there like a broken wall? What do you think a woman is? If a young girl comes outside, is this how you behave, dog? I will gouge your eyes out!

Muthayi: [trying to stop him] Appa!

Thirusangu: Do not be afraid, child! He cannot do anything to you. He is just a lazy fellow who threatens women!

Muthayi: No, Appa!

Thirusangu: I understand everything! Why do you come to places like this alone? God! You saved my daughter's honour! A crore salutations to you.

Muthayi: Listen to what I am saying...

Thirusangu: There is nothing to say. Go home... do not stand facing this sinner! Go, child! Are you going or not? Hmm! Go! Go!

Unable to say anything, Muthayi leaves. She goes away laughing inwardly at how earnestly her father is trying to prove to Prince Sukadev that she is a ripe guava waiting for him.

Thirusangu: Prince! Did you see his outrage?

Sukadev: (springing at Muthan and striking his back) Hey, Muthan! Is your back muttering? Half-starved wretch! Mendicant brat! Splendid donkey!

Muthan: Have you emptied the dictionary?

Sukadev: Don't speak, cowardly dog!

Muthan: I have no intention of speaking any more.

Sukadev: Why are you standing so straight? I'll break your breastbone.

Muthan: Try it! At least then you may learn that I too have a heart.

Sukadev: Hey... speak respectfully.

Muthan: Respect? Even lumps of earth have to fear the rain.

Thirusangu: That's enough! You cannot hide your villainy beneath insolent cleverness!

Muthan: How could I? For that I would have to be born the Palaiyakkarar's son!

Sukadev: Oh! You stray who does not even know your father's name!

Muthan: Do you know your father's name?...

Enraged, Sukadev slaps Muthan across the cheek. That is enough—Muthan's hands begin to play across Sukadev's body. One fast blow sends Sukadev's turban flying until it catches on a branch. Unable to bear the beating, Sukadev takes the large spear-staff from Thirusangu's hand. Thirusangu concludes, “That's it—Muthan is finished.”

But Sukadev uses the spear-staff to retrieve his turban from the branch, puts it back on his head, and quickly leaves the place.
