# Scene 37

[Anandapuram

Along the riverbank of Anandapuram, an old man comes walking with a staff. His white beard moves in the moonlight. His ears seem as sharp as his eyes, for on hearing the sound of horses he hides behind a tree and looks toward the sound.

Four or five horses are tied there. Beside them a few warriors sit concealed, waiting for someone. The old man too watches without blinking to see what is about to happen.

A man walks slowly toward the horses. It is Vedhalam. The warriors stand when they see him. Vedhalam speaks softly:

“Muthayi will certainly get out. She does not want this marriage. There is no other way for Muthan and her to pass through this town. We must be extremely careful. We will seize only Muthayi and leave—every one of you stay alert.”

The old man hiding beneath the tree hears every word.

He slips quietly away. Even in the dim light he can walk quickly. At last his feet stop beside a garden behind the Glass Palace. His aged face lifts. He approaches the palace and climbs upward by gripping the great creepers hanging beside a window. He succeeds and drops into the interior.

Moving silently like a cat, he reaches the room where Muthayi is staying.

At that moment, do you know what Thirusangu and Sukadev are doing on the upper part of the palace? They are standing and looking at the moon.

Sukadev: Ah! Tonight, in this beautiful moonlight, I stand here with you. Tomorrow Muthayi and I will enjoy this honeyed moon together.

Thirusangu: You and I are both fortunate, thambi! I have gained you as a son-in-law—you have gained my daughter as a wife.

Sukadev: What doubt is there? Even this moon says the same thing.

Thirusangu: All right, thambi—stay here. I will go and see whether Muthayi has fallen asleep.

Sukadev: Yes. If she has slept, let her sleep. Tomorrow I will not let her sleep—I will keep talking to her.

Thirusangu goes downstairs.

* * *

The old man enters Muthayi's room. She starts on seeing him.

“Muthayi! Do not be afraid—it is I, Muthan,” the old man says.

Muthayi embraces him, cries “Aththan!” in delight and sheds tears.

Muthan: There is no time to talk. We must leave at once.

Muthayi: Vedhalam said you would come.

Muthan: Vedhalam! He is a poisonous snake. Muthayi, somehow they had to make you come here for Sukadev.

Muthayi: Sukadev? Why?

Muthan: Do you know why? Leaving now will not be simple—danger is waiting all around us.

At that moment Thirusangu's footsteps are heard. Muthan hides behind a curtain. Thirusangu sees Muthayi awake.

Thirusangu: Muthayi, haven't you slept yet?

Muthayi: I cannot sleep, Appa! I am deep in thought.

Thirusangu: What are you thinking about, child?

Muthayi: Vedhalam came and put sense into me. All this time I was foolish enough to trust that Muthan.

Thirusangu: Daughter! Are you the one speaking? Ah—Vedhalam was right. Muthayi! Only now do I feel I have received the fruit of having you as my child.

Muthayi: Appa! Will you do me one favour?

Thirusangu: A thousand—tell me.

Muthayi: In the morning Sukadev and I are to be married. Now I want to meet him and talk about a few things. My heart is longing to see him!

Thirusangu: My dear daughter! Is that all? I will send him this very moment—

Thirusangu hurries upstairs.

Thirusangu: Thambi! My golden girl still has not slept.

Sukadev: Why?

Thirusangu: She is longing to see you!

Sukadev: Is that so?

Thirusangu: She wants to see you right now.

Sukadev: Ah, my goddess of love! I am coming. If fire and cotton come near each other, can they remain still? Mama! You can sleep soundly—I am coming—

Sukadev immediately goes to the mirror, adorns himself and sets out.

Meanwhile Muthan finishes telling Muthayi what she must do and leaves the palace. Muthayi waits for Sukadev. Immersed in an ocean of delight, Sukadev enters. Seeing him, she rises, bows respectfully, and stands shyly in a corner.

Sukadev: My dear! What is this, my beauty? Come—won't you speak? Are you so shy?

Muthayi: It is not fear. It is the natural modesty that comes to women.

Sukadev: Come—sit near me!

Muthayi: No!—all that after the wedding.

Sukadev: The wedding is going to happen anyway. Muthayi, I never imagined you cared so much for me.

Muthayi: Leave that. I want to talk to you about something.

Sukadev: Something? Lover—beloved—bedchamber—a pavilion of pleasure; that is not a place for discussing matters, my dear!

Muthayi: You must not tease me like this.

Sukadev: What do you want to talk about? Speak!

Muthayi: What is your opinion about equality between men and women?

Sukadev: Half man! Half woman! My principle is that men and women should have been created as Ardhanarishvaras. If I were God, that is how I would make them.

Muthayi: Ah—you have stated a difficult great question very briefly! The woman fortunate enough to attain you will indeed be lucky! I want to ask one more thing—

Sukadev: Ask!

Muthayi: I cannot ask it and have you answer immediately. We must talk for a long time.

Sukadev: We will talk—no problem. What pleasure do I have greater than speaking with you?

Muthayi: Such a beautiful moon. Why don't the two of us go toward the riverbank and talk through some delightful stories?

Sukadev: Ah—you said exactly what I was thinking. Come! Let us go, my dear!

Muthayi: Hush—don't touch. All that tomorrow! Come, let us go.

Sukadev: I am ready.

Muthayi: You said men and women are equal. Shall you go dressed as a woman—and I as a man?

Sukadev: Ayyo—in women's clothes?

Muthayi: See! Is being a woman something degrading?

Sukadev: No, no—but if anyone saw me it would be embarrassing.

Muthayi: We are going secretly, without anyone knowing. If you and I are one life in two bodies, what is wrong if you wear my clothes and I wear yours?

Sukadev: Nothing at all! Golden one! I will do exactly as you say.

Sukadev begins removing his clothes. Muthayi goes behind a curtain, removes hers and throws them to him. They exchange clothes. Sukadev now appears as a woman, and Muthayi as a man.

Without anyone knowing, the two leave by the rear of the palace toward the riverbank.

The horsemen waiting by the river move about watching for anyone coming that way. They see a man and a woman approaching. Believing that the Muthayi for whom they have been watching has arrived, they become alert.

One Warrior: Muthan and Muthayi are both coming.

Another: Good—move!

The horses thunder forward. The riders block the path of Sukadev and Muthayi. Sukadev is startled. Would the horsemen leave him alone? Poor man—he is wearing Muthayi's clothes. Taking Sukadev for the woman, they seize him. Muthayi runs away. The warriors do not pursue her.

Sukadev's mouth is gagged. He is lifted onto a horse and carried away. Those innocent fools believe they are taking Muthayi as a feast for their master's bedchamber.

Still dressed as a man, Muthayi reaches the riverbank looking for her lover. Muthan comes out from a bush.

Muthan: Muthayi!

Muthayi: Aththan—hasn't our play worked beautifully? What comes next?

Muthan: The whole story will be told only after a kiss. Look there—see the moon and cloud embracing!

They look up at the sky. Cloud and moon kiss.
