## Source-sensitive checks

### Scene 1

Muthan’s attack on unearned religious income remains explicit. The post-house setting, palm-leaf dispatch system, varagan denomination, gold offerings and Vedhalam’s warning about public speech are all represented.

The displayed source wording `அஞ்சல் மீன்` is not silently normalized in Tamil; English transliterates it as `Anjal Meen`. Later `அஞ்சல் மனை` is translated by its source-secure dramatic sense as `post house`.

### Scene 2

Boopathy’s medicine-versus-magic argument and mockery of the Swami remain explicit. The Swami’s miracle claims, religious rhetoric and gold-offering satire are preserved rather than summarized.

`திருச்சிற்றம்பலம்` is retained as `Thiruchitrambalam`.

### Scene 3

The `மருமகன்` joke is preserved through partial transliteration because the dialogue turns on the kinship word itself. `aththan` is also retained at the marriage-claim locus. The lemon/sour joke remains visible.

### Scene 4

Muthayi’s sharp response to Sukadev, her criticism of Palaiyakkarar status, the Urvashi/Rambha joke and her closing love-song imagery remain intact. The song is translated as verse, not converted into plot summary.

### Scene 5

The horse comedy, Sukadev’s abuse of Muthan, Poongavanam’s defence of him, and the social sting of “Palace horses all kick like that” remain direct.

The source-secure but idiomatically difficult `பல்லிக்கடித்துப் பயனில்லை` is translated conservatively and documented in Scene 5 notes; it does not reopen the Tamil layer.
