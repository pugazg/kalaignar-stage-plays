# Scene 2

[Pazhuthur Monastery

Maykanatha Swamigal is seated on a gold-polished pedestal that gleams brilliantly. Near him, Baladevar sits on a seat spread with green silk. A diamond ornament shines at his ears. With curled hair hanging below the nape, tufts of hair descending beside his ears like hooks, and a small moustache, Baladevar always has the look of a man absorbed in thought. A rose-coloured tunic covers his body, with a red pitambara draped over it. That seems to be his usual—and favourite—dress. Beside him are his wife and his son Sukadev. Behind them, standing with a degree of humility, is the palace guard Thirusangu. Directly before the Swami’s pedestal the townspeople are gathered. The fragrance of benzoin and incense seems to proclaim that devotion is overflowing there like a flood. Everyone waits to hear what nectar-like words are about to fall from the Swami’s sacred mouth.

At that moment an elderly bearded man enters. Everyone’s attention turns to him. The old man sees Baladevar and bows.

Baladevar: Come, Boopathy! Come! It has been many years since I saw you! Even your beard has gone white!

The old man nods as if to say “Yes” and gives an indifferent smile. Meanwhile the Swami, looking at him, says to Baladevar:

Swamigal: A tender leaf must become a dry one! What is surprising about a beard turning white?

Boopathy: You have said one thing, and said it well! The truth is as plain as a gooseberry in the palm!

Baladevar: Boopathy! How were your travels abroad? I imagine your medical practice must have advanced many times beyond what it was before. Your journey was useful for that, was it not?

Boopathy: It certainly was! Twenty years of ceaseless wandering have not gone to waste. I learned the truth that many things which magic and mantra cannot cure can be cured by medicine—

Before the old man can finish, the Swami interrupts.

Swamigal: Thiruchitrambalam! There is nothing God cannot do!... Beloved ones... I think we may begin our sacred moral discourse on the path of grace...

Baladevar: We are waiting, Swami...

Swamigal: Yesterday I spoke about great saints... did I not? Great saints do not appear often. Nor can they! But when such saints do appear, there is nothing they cannot accomplish! They can dry up the sea—float in the sky—speak with God—make sugarcane taste bitter—make even a crow pour forth the music of the Gandharvas. Such is the divine power they possess.

Let warriors and men of wisdom have fame only on earth! Great saints have fame even in heaven. That is why I say: do not touch sword or spear! Worship the Lord of Vaikuntha! Do not take a shield in your hand! Take up the sacred path of the Lord of Kailasa!

Who are you—who am I? Mother, father, elder brother, younger brother—all are emptiness—in the end, only a handful of earth!

This universe is illusion—for those who have taken a body, the heart must journey toward the Lord!

Before the Swami’s long sermon is over, Vedhalam from the post house enters. The moment the Swami sees him, he brings the sermon to an end.

Vedhalam: Prabhu! Namaskaram! Swami! Namaste! He bows in turn to Baladevar and the Swami.

Baladevar: Are there any palm-leaf letters for me, Vedhalam?

Vedhalam: Yes. I already delivered them at the palace. A great many offerings have come for the Swami! I brought them here.

Swamigal: Offerings! Long live the devotees! How much, Vedhalam?

Vedhalam: Close to a hundred gold coins!

Swamigal: Excellent. There is the donation box!

Meanwhile Baladevar and the townspeople, Baladevar’s son Sukadev, and the palace guard Thirusangu leave the monastery. Vedhalam puts the hundred gold coins into the box indicated by the Swami. The elderly physician Boopathy, who has been watching this with indifference, gives a mocking laugh.

Boopathy: Swamigal does not touch gold, is that it?

Swamigal: Poison!

Boopathy: And is it not in that poison that this tree grows so luxuriantly!

Laughing, he points a finger at the Swami. Vedhalam leaves.

Swamigal: Boopathy! Do not torment me... go on your way.

Boopathy: Swami! Hee, hee, hee! You told the townspeople about the power of great saints! If that alone were true, you would simply make me drop dead—wouldn’t you?

Swamigal: You were gone for twenty years. Why have you come back now?

Boopathy: Why have I come? I’ll tell you. Hee, hee, hee!... Swami! Haven’t you too been hiding that secret from me for twenty years? Wait! Just wait! Do you think I’ll fail to discover it? Hee, hee, hee!

With the weary laugh characteristic of the old man, Boopathy leaves the gathering.

Swamigal: [to himself] Thiruchitrambalam—God alone is my refuge!
