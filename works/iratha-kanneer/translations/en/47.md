# Scene 47

[Seashore

On the white sand of the beach, Muthayi plays with the child. Gathering shells, delighting in the foam poured out by the hands of the waves, she becomes almost like a child herself.

Dark clouds gather in the sky. In another part of the beach, Swami Vedhalam and Sukadev are walking. The sky groans. The wind blows strangely. Lightning begins to flash.

Muthayi lifts the child, puts it into the carriage and starts toward home. But home—is it nearby? Rain has already come. The wind has grown fierce. There are no houses where Muthayi can take shelter with the child. She hurriedly pushes the carriage and runs.

Because of the rain, Vedhalam and Sukadev too walk quickly. The wind grows stronger still. The raindrops strike the body like the tips of whips. Thunder roars as though mountains are rolling.

Muthayi loses all sense of direction. The child screams. She lifts it and runs with it pressed to her chest.

Meanwhile, at the wealthy merchant's house, anxiety grows because the child has not yet returned. A horse-drawn palanquin carriage is prepared to search for it!

As Muthayi struggles against rain and wind, a tree breaks and falls upon her. She and the child fall together. Having performed this good deed, the great wind too comes to rest.

At that moment the carriage driven by the wealthy merchant reaches the spot and stops. The merchant jumps down in haste. He lifts the child and realizes it is alive.

“God! You have saved this poor man,” he exclaims.

Then that wealthy man gets back into the carriage and leaves with his child.

Muthayi lies there unable to speak, struck by the tree branch, watching the merchant's compassion-filled act.

What else can the poor do?
