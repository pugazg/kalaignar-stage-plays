# Scene 4

[Thirusangu’s house.

It has the standing one would expect of the home of a palace servant. A sweet song floats out from inside the house and mingles with the breeze. How delightful is the meaning that pours through that song! The voice showering such pleasure certainly cannot be a man’s voice. It is the cool, soothing voice of a woman. Yes—it is the musical delight given by Muthayi. The nectar-like tone rises as though a veena were vibrating, as though a flute were sounding, rippling through the young breeze and filling the modest house with complete peace.

Muthayi, who has been singing, suddenly stops. Crying “Appa!” she runs toward the doorway. But before she reaches it, her face turns shyly away.

Thirusangu: Don’t be shy, child! There is no stranger here. This is Prince Sukadev.

Muthayi: (With her head bowed) Greetings.

Sukadev: The way your hands close and open looks like a lotus flower! Thirusangu, what you said was true!

At those words Muthayi’s expression changes. Her face reddens with anger.

Sukadev: Poor thing; her cheeks have gone red with shyness.

Without saying anything, Muthayi goes into the kitchen. Sukadev looks helplessly at Thirusangu.

Thirusangu: Thambi, should you tease her like this the moment you see her?

Sukadev: What did I say—what did I say? Did I say something without even realizing it?... Thirusangu! I want to ask you for one thing. You won’t refuse me, will you?

Thirusangu: You are Prince Sukadev, son of Palaiyakkarar Baladevar. I am merely your servant. You have the right to take without even asking!

Sukadev: Ah! My eyes are spinning. My ears refuse to hear, my tongue says it will not move, my heart has stopped running!

Thirusangu: Thambi!

Sukadev: I have fallen like a fly into the pot of toddy called love.

Thirusangu can scarcely contain his delight. He rejoices, believing his purpose has succeeded.

Thirusangu: Thambi! Go now and come back tomorrow! The matter is a success!

Sukadev: How could I stay away? Thirusangu’s house is a lotus pond! Sukadev is the bee that will fly to it!... I’ll be going, Thirusangu!

Thirusangu: All right, thambi!

Sukadev leaves. Thirusangu can no longer contain his anger at Muthayi. He thinks she has insulted Prince Sukadev. So, in fury—

Thirusangu: Muthayi! Come here!

Muthayi: What is it, Appa? Has that man gone?

Thirusangu: Man?

Muthayi: Forgive me. I misspoke.

Thirusangu: The prince comes to our house and this is how you disgrace him?

Muthayi: The petty ruler of Vengai Nadu—poor man—has at least left this Palaiyakkarar family that title. He lost his armies—lost his right to rule. But only now do I see that the Palaiyakkarar family has lost even the conduct that ought to go with its palayam. What a wretched young man!

Thirusangu: Who is wretched? Is he lacking in beauty? Is he lacking in status? Foolish girl! You cannot grasp his intelligence.

Muthayi: Perhaps it is smaller than the point of a needle—that must be why I cannot grasp it. Isn’t that so, Appa?

Thirusangu: Enough! Stop it! If he wished, Urvashi and Rambha themselves would come running!

Muthayi: What is surprising in that? They ran even to sages in their ashrams—if people from a palace call them, are they going to say no? They will come, Appa, they will!

Thirusangu: Shut your mouth! Chatterbox!

Thirusangu speaks angrily and goes out. Muthayi, delighted that she has given her father a fitting reply, begins to dance. From that dance a song is born:

“Before the lover I sought—even Kama
would pass with his face veiled!
Like rain to a withered crop,
like the jasmine flower I wear!
He will bring delight!—that
beloved one will come!
A matchless honeyed face! He is the moon in the sky!
He will come! He will make love grow!”
