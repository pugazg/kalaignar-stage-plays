# Scene 55

[Vedhalam's quarters

Inside, a figure lies bundled beneath a blanket. Vedhalam and Muthayi enter. Having run there after hearing that the Aththan dearer than her own life is at the edge of death, Muthayi's terrified eyes fall upon the wrapped figure.

Vedhalam: There is your lover.

[The figure groans, “Muthayi.”]

Crying “Aththan!” Muthayi rushes to him and lifts the blanket with trembling hands.

Vetrivelan rises laughing loudly.

Vedhalam leaves the room.

How can Muthayi's condition be described in words? She is like a deer trapped in the middle while hunters surround it on all four sides.

Vetrivelan: Muthayi! Why do you stare?... Why are you afraid?... I am near you!...

Muthayi: Commander Vetrivelan! You, who ought to protect the country, have become such a shameless man... No—think of me as your sister... let me go.

Vetrivelan: Only fools would believe that flower and bee are brother and sister. My dear!...

Muthayi: Oh—let go of me! Shameless man! Poison-worm! I am not a tree that will be uprooted by your storm of lust.

Vetrivelan: Curse me! Shout well! Every letter tastes sweet. Curse me! A parrot locked in a cage does not cry out in anger. It cries from fear. With time, even that will settle down.

Muthayi: Do not come near me.

Vetrivelan: The commander of this country is calling you. Do you know what happens to those who refuse my command?

Muthayi: You will kill me. Is that all? I am not afraid of death. Is death more terrible than you? No. No. Never.

Vetrivelan: Muthayi. No flower has ever escaped from these iron arms of mine! Remember that!

Muthayi: Leave me, criminal!—

Vetrivelan: The whole world turns upon the axle called injustice.

He grabs Muthayi's hand and pulls her. She spins and falls to the floor screaming.

Vetrivelan: Muthayi! You cannot escape now—submit of your own accord!—

He touches her. Muthayi gets up, runs and crashes against the door.
