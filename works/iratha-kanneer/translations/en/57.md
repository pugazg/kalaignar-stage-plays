# Scene 57

[Vedhalam's quarters

Vetrivelan and Muthayi!—Muthayi suffers before a volcano ready to erupt.

Vetrivelan: Why do you stare? Wherever you go, I will not leave you. If you fly as a skylark, I will become the hawk circling you. If you leap as a spotted deer, I will roar as a tiger. Even if in the end you become a corpse, I will become the sandalwood log that touches your body and burns with it.

Delight of mine! Submit. Flower that blossoms in spring! Come to me. Coloured lotus! Bouquet of enchantment! I will gather you into my powerful arms—spring upon me, green parrot!

Muthayi: Sinner! Leave me!

Vetrivelan: Even cowards do not let go of fruit already in the hand or a bird caught in a snare. Muthayi—I have spoken to you this long only because if a flower is crushed and then smelled, there will be no fragrance. I warn you: do not test my patience!

Muthayi: Oh!—

[Muthayi slaps Vetrivelan.]

Vetrivelan: [sparks flying] Hmm! I will make you mute!—I will make you unrecognizable.

Muthayi is caught in his grip, unable to free herself. She screams, “Ayyo! Ayyo!”

At that moment Muthan jumps in through the roof. The commander is startled.

Crying “Aththan!” Muthayi runs and embraces him. Vetrivelan glares at Muthan.

Vetrivelan: Muthan! Do not die uselessly! Think carefully about who you are.

Muthan: I am the warrior trapped and ruined in the court of your arbitrary rule—in the pleasure-hall of dictatorship. But today that warrior has not come. I have come to give you a reward for the blood I shed—for the tortures I endured.

Mad hunter of women! Liquor and the pleasures of women may lie hidden beneath the shadow of your crown! But you drunken men of power cannot understand that a revolutionary force can surge up and crush that crown into dust!

Boiling with rage, Vetrivelan cries, “What did you say?” and strikes Muthan again and again. Unable to withstand the powerful attack, Muthan reels.

Vedhalam enters from outside. He guards Muthayi so that she cannot run away.

The commander's blows make Muthan crumple to the floor. Seeing her lover's condition, Muthayi screams. Muthan's cheeks are swollen. Blood runs from his mouth. Unable to resist the commander, he falls.

Vetrivelan stands proudly over him, triumphant at having brought Muthan down.

But Muthan writhes on the floor. Fire rises in his eyes. He breathes like a storm.

That is enough—Muthan rises.

Before the commander can stop him, Muthan rains blow after blow upon him in a counterattack. Vedhalam trembles with fear. When Vedhalam tries to kill Muthan, Muthayi stops him.

Muthan sits upon the commander's chest and strikes him fiercely. After that Vetrivelan does not rise again. His terrible eyes close.

Muthan's fury turns toward Vedhalam.

Vedhalam runs. He runs away.

Exhausted, Muthan stands. Muthayi embraces him.

Outside, Sukadev sees Swami Vedhalam running while he himself is bringing guards. He immediately shouts to them, “Do not let this man escape!”

A warrior's spear flies. Carrying it in his back, Vedhalam keeps running, then falls down a mountain slope and closes his eyes. His upper garment is torn wildly. Through the tear, a sacred thread gleams.

* * *

Sukadev and the soldiers enter the ashram.

Muthan and Muthayi are stunned. The soldiers surround Muthan.

The warrior leader sees Vetrivelan's condition and cries, “Ah! Our commander!” as he runs to him.

Vetrivelan's eyes slowly open. His lips move. If only even that movement could have been a good one!

“Muthan deserted the army. He is a spy of the enemy country. I came here to catch him... and this happened... Mutha...”

Vetrivelan's last flickering breath stops.

Warrior Leader: Who is this Muthan? This is the man, isn't it? Take him immediately to Vengaipuram! Hmm—drag him away.

The soldiers put Muthan in fetters and drag him away. Muthayi cries “Aththan!” once and collapses unconscious.

Sukadev tries to revive her. When she opens her eyes, Muthayi babbles like a madwoman.

Muthayi: Are you a murderer? A criminal?...

Sukadev: Do not be afraid! I am not a criminal... that Muthan is the criminal.

Muthayi: Ayyo... my god of love! In your hands...

Sukadev: Nothing... only a water cup... do not be afraid, my dear! Get up!

Muthayi opens her eyes wide. Her mind clears. She sees Sukadev.

Muthayi: Sinner—you have come again?

Sukadev: My Muthayi, are you angry? Is this some lovers' quarrel?

Muthayi: What are you saying now?

Sukadev: What can I say—about our marriage...

Muthayi: You unjust man! I stand at the edge of torment and you alone have come to speak of heroic passion to me—is this the lesson your palace taught you? Get out! Hmm—are you going or not?

Sukadev: No one is going anywhere. Muthayi—do not make a fuss. Come, let us complete the marriage right now.

Muthayi: Have you no eyes...? No human feeling...? They say a woman's curse is terrible—have you never learned that?

Sukadev: Nothing will enter my ears! Now you must marry me—my dear Muthayi!

Muthayi rises in fury. Her exhaustion and weakness seem to have fled somewhere. She begins to speak.

Muthayi: Hmm—I am ready. It is this body you want, isn't it? It is the polish of my flesh that has bewitched you. Here—make me your slave!

Someone has said, perhaps, that only the body was defiled while the heart remained undefiled. I do not say that. I am ready to place my chastity beneath your feet.

Donkey charging in lust! Tear my heart apart with your cruel claws! Rhinoceros whose reason is lost in sexual intoxication! Devour me! Python caressing me with the hunger of the mattress! Put my withered body into your belly!

Come—do not delay! Plunder my chastity! Chop my resolve into pieces! Representative of all those who have made women stand as dolls of prostitution! Why do you stand there? Why hesitate? Here I am ready!

My corpse speaks your desire! My dead body calls you to dalliance! Come, come, deceiver! Come! Here I am—Muthayi! Muthan's beloved! Your bondwoman!

Hearing this cry that throws out sparks, Sukadev loses his composure.

Sukadev: Muthayi! Forgive me! Forgive me!

Muthayi stands frozen. Before her Sukadev trembles like a straw.

Sukadev: Dear sister! I will act immediately. I will prove that your lover Muthan is not a criminal—not a spy of the enemy country.

Saying this, Sukadev runs out.
