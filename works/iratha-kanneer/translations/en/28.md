# Scene 28

[Boundary of Pazhuthur

A ruined Ayyanar temple lies there. Vetrivelan's army has stopped. None of the townspeople who followed the procession remain. Muthan, still in fetters, has been put into a palanquin-carriage. From their carriage Vedhalam and Vetrivelan make plans about Muthayi.

Vetrivelan: All right, Vedhalam—we have reached the boundary of the town! I regret that you are turning back.

Vedhalam: Lord, thank you for your affection.

Vetrivelan: Do not forget the matter of Muthayi! I cannot forget her...

Vedhalam: Would I forget?

Vetrivelan: She is a veena! She spoke only through tears. Yet every word vibrated in perfect pitch.

Vedhalam: That veena must come to you—that is all, isn't it? I will make her the permanent veena of your sleeping chamber.

Vetrivelan: No, no. Do not go so far as marriage! Even honey becomes cloying if poured into the cup called marriage. I prefer women to remain flowers and men to remain bees. That is my ideal song too!

Vedhalam: To your will, I am the rhythm!

Vetrivelan: Vedhalam! I am pleased. I take leave. As soon as I reach there, I will write a palm-leaf message. Arrange for me to meet that golden-bodied woman quickly!

Vedhalam: Powerful lord! I will act exactly as ordered.

Vetrivelan: I take leave.

Vedhalam bows. The carriages set off rapidly toward Vengaipuram. There is no one there who cares about the tears flowing from Muthan's eyes.
