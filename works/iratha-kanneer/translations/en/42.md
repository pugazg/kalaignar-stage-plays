# Scene 42

[Palaiyakkarar's palace

The scene begins with the Palaiyakkarar pacing back and forth in fury. Vedhalam stands nearby. It seems Vedhalam has told him everything about Thirusangu's attempt to ruin Sukadev and give him his daughter; otherwise the Palaiyakkarar would hardly be boiling with such anger.

Baladevar: Thirusangu... let him come here—watch what I do to him. Acting like a good man while doing fox-work—the hypocrite!

Vedhalam: Good men cannot be trusted in these times.

Baladevar: All right—how are we to find Sukadev?

Vedhalam: That is no great matter... I will find him.

Baladevar: I do not care how much wealth is spent. It is enough if my son is brought back to me. If Sukadev does not return—the queen will die!

Vedhalam: No one must learn that Sukadev is missing. I myself will find him cunningly and hand him over to you.

Baladevar: Many thanks. I will give you five hundred gold coins for expenses. If Sukadev is found, I will give you another five hundred gold coins as a reward.

Vedhalam is unable to speak. Fortunately he manages not to faint. A thousand gold coins are going to come to him!—

Vedhalam: Birthplace of greatness! Do I labour for the sake of a reward? If I fail to perform the duty I owe the Palaiyakkarar's family, would not that sin come as a snake and bite me?

Baladevar: Grateful man! May God help us. Go!

* * *

Vedhalam leaves the palace—listen closely to the sound inside the temple of his heart:

“They say sorrow comes with company and happiness comes alone. That is false. Happiness is marching upon Vedhalam in a line like ants! In this world one man can live in prosperity only through the suffering of many others. Shouldn't I too live in pleasure?

One man eats only by striking another. The fly is food for the spider. The spider is food for the lizard. The lizard is food for the cat. The cat is food for the Kuravan. My road is the same!

I too am a human being—I too want happiness—I too want every prosperity. My rise is moving fast. Perhaps it may even make me Palaiyakkarar of Pazhuthur. As things went, the wandering Buddha reached the Bodhi tree because of the times. One who lay baring his teeth became Chanakya who turned the world upside down!

Vedhalam's efforts are not ocean waves rising at full moon to touch the moon. They are water-drops from a black cloud trying to reach the earth. All that is needed is one cool wind! Then rain will pour! Fortune will heap itself up!”

Talking to himself, he goes away.
