# Scene 44

Muthayi raises the merchant's child with great affection. Playing with it—dancing and singing with it—gives her joy. Muthan attends to the household work.

There is a small carriage like a chariot. Muthayi seats the child in it and, singing a cheerful song, pushes it to the seashore. She plays with the child there and returns home at the beginning of night.

The wealthy merchant and his wife go to the temple for worship and return home. In the hall at the temple entrance sits a young ascetic with black beard and moustache, apparently in meditation. His eyes sharply observe everyone who comes and goes. Whenever a woman passes nearby, he chants, “Muthayi! Muthayi!”

By now everyone will have understood who this Swami is. This is the condition of Sukadev, who has lost his beloved!

Like everyone else, the merchant and his wife bow to the young ascetic and leave.

The young ascetic also catches the eye of Vedhalam, who has come there. Vedhalam immediately slips away. Who knows what poisonous idea has flowered in that strange brain of his!

After returning from the temple, Muthayi hands the child back to the mistress of the house and goes to her hut.

Muthan is waiting there. Every day is a new day for the two of them, is it not? They embrace.

Muthan: Ah—you are a strange kind of fire.

Muthayi: What—fire?

Muthan: Yes. When I am away from you, it burns. When I come near you, it cools.

Muthayi: Very good, Valluvar!...

Muthan: Whatever I see, it brings you to mind...

Muthayi: Is that so? That sounds dangerous—

Muthan: Why?

Muthayi: Suppose you see an old woman.

Muthan: Even then I will imagine you. A heart as white as the old woman's hair. Your eyebrow curved like the hunch of her back. Your golden body trembling like her trembling body. And like the walking-stick in her hand...

Muthayi: Why, Aththan!—you!

Muthan: Why, my dear!

Muthayi: Hush—is that proper?

Muthan: When a man and a woman are alone—proper or improper—the whole world is an inner chamber.

The two sing a song of delight. Night blesses them.
