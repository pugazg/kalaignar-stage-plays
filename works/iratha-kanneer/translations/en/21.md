# Scene 21

[Thendral Palace

Vetrivelan and Vedhalam are talking.

Vedhalam: Your suspicion was perfectly right! After all, you are a commander of a kingdom, are you not? You said that when he signed, he mistakenly wrote “Muthayi.” That was no mistake—there really is such a goddess.

Vetrivelan: You see? I knew it then itself! Do you know anything more about this Muthayi? My Vedhalam—there must be some connection between the two of them, mustn't there?

Vedhalam: There may be. She is the daughter of Thirusangu, chief of the palace guards.

Vetrivelan: Is she beautiful enough, with a slender waist, for there to be such a connection?

Vedhalam: She is beautiful—very beautiful.

Vetrivelan: When we stayed at Sandhana Cheri, a close friend introduced me to a beauty just like that.

Vedhalam: Is that so?

Vetrivelan: Though he was my friend, I rewarded him with twenty gold coins for arranging that fine opportunity.

Vedhalam: Oh-ho!

Vetrivelan: If possible, let us try to meet Muthayi once—what do you say, Vedhalam?

Vedhalam: That is exactly what I have begun to think about.

Vetrivelan: Excellent! Vedhalam—you have no equal in understanding a hint and acting upon it!

At that moment Muthan comes in. Seeing him, Vetrivelan stops the conversation.

Vetrivelan: What is it, man? What do you want?...

Muthan: You must forgive me.

Vetrivelan: Tell me the matter.

Muthan: The circumstances are not right... I cannot remain in the army.

Vetrivelan: Are you playing with me?... Who do you think I am? There is no permission here for you to join the army, leave it, and behave exactly as you please. Look at the man! Built like a mountain—afraid like a mouse. Coward.

Muthan: Please do not use that word. I may be poor—but I am no coward!

Vetrivelan: Do not stretch your tongue! The sword will stretch! Stand respectfully! Speak respectfully!

Muthan: There is no compulsion to join the army, is there?

Vetrivelan: There is no compulsion to join! But once you have joined, leaving is not a matter of your choice.

Muthan: But there is a condition that I can pay a penalty of one hundred gold coins.

Vetrivelan: This fellow is full of arrogance—your tail must be clipped close. What are you going to do with a body like this?... Give it in sacrifice for the country.

Muthan: I can sacrifice it—if there were a country that was ours! Pazhuthur is enslaved soil. Let an army gather to liberate it. Anyone who refuses to join that army may rightly be counted among the dead.

Vetrivelan: What did you say, traitor to the crown!

Muthan: Do not throw accusations like that at me. You said I was no warrior; I merely gave you an explanation.

Vetrivelan: He is the first man ever to speak against me! I will not leave him without finding an end for him.

Vedhalam: Please come aside for a moment.

Vetrivelan takes him to a private spot.

Vedhalam: Do not let the fellow go! If you do, he will pay the penalty and escape. Then we will not be able to get hold of Muthayi.

Vetrivelan: What can we do?

Vedhalam: Put the donkey under guard!—But one thing. I will behave toward this puppy as though I am his well-wisher. I mean, I will act the part.

The two return to where Muthan is. Even as they approach—

Vedhalam: Please forgive Muthan and let him go. He is poor.

Vetrivelan: Vedhalam! I am telling you again and again. You must not interfere in this.

Vedhalam: Why should I not interfere? If Muthan gives you the hundred gold coins he owes, what can you do to him? Muthan! The hundred gold is ready, isn't it?

Muthan: It is ready. I will give it in the morning.

Vedhalam: In the morning?... That is all right too! Commander! Muthan may go now, may he not?

Vetrivelan: No. Until the penalty gold arrives, he must remain under guard. Hey, who is there?... Put him under guard...

Vedhalam: Ayyo!... Honoured lord! Let him go. I myself will bring you the gold tomorrow morning.

Vetrivelan: Impossible! Vetrivelan gives the order. Do not stand in my way!

Muthan: Is this what justice in your government's military camp sounds like? Very fine!

Vetrivelan: Do not die uselessly! Put him in fetters.

Fetters are fastened to Muthan's hands. Vedhalam covers his face and turns away. As he leaves, Muthan watches him sorrowfully.

“Muthan! Karma from a former birth does not spare even a crowned king!—Do not be afraid, I am here.”

Saying this, Vedhalam goes. Muthan is locked in a room of Thendral Palace, with a guard posted outside.
