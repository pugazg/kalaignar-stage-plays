# Scene 32

[Vengaipuram

The soldiers' camp... Cups of liquor—and women standing with those cups in their hands! Warriors lie in the women's laps with cups of liquor at their lips. If this is the condition of the soldiers, what must be the condition of the commander?

A silk mattress—and Vetrivelan lying upon it. He is so drunk he cannot lift his head. Frenzy has reached the point where he cannot even feel how his tongue is moving. Women surround him; his eyes roll over them. Their coral lips compete with his reddened eyes.

Warriors who enter the battlefield carrying spear and sword and lift the heads of enemies in their hands now hold drinking cups—and lie caressing flower-soft feet.

It is Commander Vetrivelan's birthday! They are celebrating it with such magnificence. Dancing, singing, revelry, shouting, love-talk and erotic play add splendour to the army chief's birthday celebration.

“A thousand cups of liquor are nothing beside a single drop drunk from your lips,” the warriors praise.

“What pleasure there is for me in leaning upon your shoulders, broad as an elephant's forehead,” the beauties sigh.

Grandeur! Festival music! Wild revelry! Exhilaration!

Listening to all of it, one man lies nearby under severe punishment.

“Muthayi! Muthayi!” his mouth keeps lamenting.

Near the commander's quarters—opposite the soldiers' celebration—Muthan struggles, locked in ground fetters. Sweat pours from his body like rain. His hands and legs are bound by chains so that he cannot straighten himself from his bent position. His neck is in unbearable pain. The strong hand that could ease that pain is not here; it is in Thirusangu's house in Pazhuthur.

The agony is beyond words—so severe that he feels it would be better even if his waist were cut away. It is as though a spear were driven into every bone, needles pushed into his nerves, and all his flesh carved with a chisel.

Only the cry “Muthayi” gives him any comfort.
