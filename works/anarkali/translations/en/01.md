# Anarkali

## Scene 1

### Garden

[Anarkali and Salim sing a love song. Raja Man Singh watches from a distance.]

Salim : Anarkali! I am searching for a way never to be parted from you even for the smallest instant. I cannot find one!

Anar : And you are a warrior! Don't you know? Only after lasting peace has settled over the world, only after white doves circle the sky instead of vultures, can warriors' festivals of love at home go on without interruption. Isn't that so, my dear?

Salim : The night takes its leave without even telling us; without taking us with it, it kills us! What can I do, my love!....Shall I set out?

Anar : Yes. Duty is calling you!

Salim : Your magnetic eye holds me back. I know that too. But........

Anar : Trusting that we shall meet tomorrow, I console my lamenting heart and send you on your way with mere words of courtesy.

Salim : Under the shade of Akbar's parasol, this poor soldier must not be the cause of even the slightest wrong, my *thizhaiye*!

Anar : [Embracing him] My beloved!

Salim : I'll be going, my dear........!

[They part.]

[Raja Man Singh, having made some decision within himself, leaves the place.]

## Translation notes

- `அணுப்பொழுதும்` is rendered as `even for the smallest instant`; the verified Tamil form is not normalized.
- `நம்மைக் கொள்ளாமலே கொல்லுகிறது!` carries an internal Tamil wordplay between `கொள்ள` and `கொல்ல`. The English `without taking us with it, it kills us` preserves the contrast without pretending to reproduce the sound-play fully.
- The source-printed `ஆறுதல்கூறி-வாய்` contains an unusual hyphenated form. The English conveys the sentence's action without rewriting the Tamil source or inventing a repaired Tamil reading.
- The verified source form `என் திழையே!` is unusual and remains unresolved as a lexical form. It is therefore retained conservatively as `my thizhaiye!` rather than silently replaced by an expected endearment.
- `அக்பரது குடைநிழலில்` is rendered as `under the shade of Akbar's parasol`, retaining the royal-protection image.
- No wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted or used in drafting or review.
