# Scene 3

## Anarkali's House

[Anar is weeping—the sweet sound of Salim's voice calling “Anar” is heard. Startled, she rises like a trampled serpent.]

Anar : Come, come! O lord who has left this young heart yearning, come!

Salim : Anar! Are you angry because I did not come at the appointed time?

Anar : Do people of the royal court hall have any sense of time? Any remembrance........?

Salim : A man of the court hall! Ah, ah! What fury! Anar! Even in your anger you raise my status!........Do you still think I am a poor soldier?

Anar : I know now! O valiant one whose speech moves with a breeze-like grace! My beloved is no warrior, but the son of the Padshah who rules the earth!

Salim : Ah!

Anar : I understand! The cloud of smoke you raised has vanished! Truth has emerged like the rising sun!

Salim : Anarkali! No...... no.......

Anar : [Angrily, through tears] Yes, exactly so! You are not Salim after all! You are only an ordinary soldier! You are not a zari-flower! You are a chrysanthemum! You are the silver creeper of the palace! A fragrant mullai blossom! The one who crept over my heart is not a shining golden rose! He is a sweet rose on which the dew falls! Not a sweet king! The king of my heart! Isn't that so, Salim? Was that not how I was supposed to believe you and be deceived?

Salim : Anar!.......Listen to what I have to say!

Anar : I listened many times! Many times I listened to the false speech: “I too am a soldier of the army, bearing shield and spear and going to the battlefront!” At the time I did not know the measure of the difference in status! Had I known, this lotus in the pond would never have bloomed for the sun in the sky, O son of the lord who guards the crescent banner! O future guardian of Delhi's dominion! Why do you stare?

A honey-feast enjoyed behind the curtain! The nectar of love to be thrown away after one has had one's fill! Do you think a barrier has now appeared before that need could be satisfied?....O son of a Padshah who lives by arraying armies! Enough of coming with a soldier's gait and deceiving me! Go away!

Salim : Anar!... I never intended to deceive you! I said I was a warrior only because I feared that you would be frightened away if you knew I belonged to the royal family.

Anar : Not because I would be frightened; because I would forget you! Because I would not consent! Salim Padshah has dared an adventure that ordinary people themselves would not dare! And the reason, they say, is that he has such love for Anarkali! A tiger, they say, loved a playful spotted deer and came disguised as a goat, to show affection and make love grow! Isn't that your story too, Padshah?

Salim : Anarkali! Enough, stop! Salim is neither a tiger nor one who came in disguise. But whatever I say and however I argue with you, you are not going to believe me. A fortress wall prevents you from hearing the stainless song of love sounding softly within my heart! The curtain called “Akbar's son” prevents you from seeing that the queen of love who has become a picture within my heart is Anarkali! So neither my words nor my sighs will reach your ear! I shall show you by action, here and now, that Salim wishes only to lie as a corpse beside Anarkali's rose-like feet. Here, Anar! Here!

[Salim draws his sword and attempts to kill himself.]

Anar : [Running to him] Salim! Salim! Forgive me!

Salim : Anar! [They embrace.]

[A guard enters.]

Guard : Padshah Akbar orders that both of you be arrested!

[Raja Man Singh is standing at a distance.]

Salim : Ah!.......Raja Man Singh!

Raja : [From a distance]

Yes! That is what Akbar's rule commands. [Salim and Anar are led away.]

[Voice behind the curtain]

Salim and Anarkali were accused. Padshah Akbar sentenced both of them to death—the royal line must not be allowed to come to an end, Raja Man Singh thought; he argued with Akbar and secured the release of Salim alone! An order was issued that Anarkali be entombed alive! Hearing the news, Salim ran to rescue his beloved! What did the man who ran to save his precious one find? Alas! By then........Anarkali had disappeared within the tomb!

## Translation notes

- `மிதியுண்ட நாகம்போல்` is translated directly as `like a trampled serpent`, retaining the violent image in the stage direction.
- The elevated source phrase `தென்றல் நடை பேசும் தீரரே!` is rendered conservatively as `O valiant one whose speech moves with a breeze-like grace`; the English does not rewrite the underlying Tamil phrase.
- `ஜரிகைப்பூ` is retained as `zari-flower`, while `சாமந்திப்பூ` is rendered as `chrysanthemum`, preserving the source's conspicuous contrast rather than substituting a smoother floral pair.
- `அந்தஸ்து பேதத்தின் அளவு` is rendered as `the measure of the difference in status`, retaining the scene's rank/class vocabulary.
- `பிறைக்கொடி` is `crescent banner`; `டெல்லி ஆதிபத்தியம்` is `Delhi's dominion`. These political-geographic expressions are kept explicit.
- The accusation beginning `திரை மறைவில் அனுபவித்த தேன் விருந்து!` is retained as a sexually charged accusation and is not euphemized.
- `(திரையில் குரல்)` is translated as `Voice behind the curtain`; its death-sentence and live-entombment narration is dramatic source text, not editorial summary.
- The source-printed final `*` is retained.
- No wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted or used in drafting or review.
