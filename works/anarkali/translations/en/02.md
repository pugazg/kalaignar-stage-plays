# Scene 2

## Anarkali's House

[Anar is going happily toward her house, oblivious to the world. As she enters, she is startled to find Raja Man Singh standing there.]

Anar : Who is it?

Raja : Raja Man Singh!

Anar : Raja Man Singh!

Raja : Yes.......minister to Emperor Akbar, royal administrator, commander of the army....

Anar : [Calmly—but mischievously] Akbar's brother-in-law too! I know, my lord! By your visit this poor woman's hut has attained boundless honour.

Raja : Royal dancer! You say, “Within me, your hut has gained honour”; I say that this whole realm must gain honour through you!

Anar : I am one who dances! What qualification do I possess to bring honour to a country?

Raja : In the victorious sovereign's court hall.......

Anar : You mean the country gains fame through the dance I perform—don't you?

Raja : No....you must dance only in the sovereign's court hall.

Anar : What?

Raja : You must not dance even in Prince Salim's heart!

Anar : What?....Salim!....He has a heart! My dance in it?........

Raja : And a garden to make that scene visible.

Anar : What are you saying?

Raja : Why hide it, Anarkali?....I came running here only after seeing the two of you sing and rejoice like a pair of doves!

Anar : Very well. Has some emergency ordinance been born under Padshah Akbar's authority, declaring love to be a grave crime!....

Raja : Not yet.......but.......one must think about the qualifications of those who engage in such love!

Anar : Qualifications for love! May not a dancing girl and a warrior who protects the country love each other?

Raja : He is not a warrior. He is a king who rules the earth.

Anar : Ah!

Raja : Akbar's own son! Lord of this world!

Anar : Ah!

Raja : Salim! Salim! Salim !

Anar : Salim! Salim! Salim! No, no. He is not Salim; he is not the Emperor's son! He is only a soldier! Only a soldier! The man who captured my heart would not lie! He would not lie!

Raja : Stop acting! I know very well that a wandering woman would not cast an adventurous net over an ordinary soldier. The Emperor, the assembly hall, the *sabrakooda* couch, the imperial procession—you decided that all this could be won with one cast of your eye, and so you have intoxicated the son of crowned King Akbar with the drug of desire!

Anar : Enough, stop! Do not sting my heart, now turned into a tongue of flame, like a thousand scorpions! Anarkali gave her love to a soldier! Not to the son of one who bears the sceptre!

Raja : Are you still hiding it, Anarkali?

Anar : I was not the one who hid it! Your crown-lord's son knew that I would not respect those who mount thrones, and that is why Prince Salim told me such an enormous lie!

Raja : Let what has happened remain what has happened; let what happens hereafter be good. Here, Anarkali! I shall give you a lakh in gold. Take it and go away somewhere. Let the prince forget you!

Anar : If I had loved for gold, I would go! What is the lakh you offer? Kuberas are waiting to seek me out and pour crores and crores before me! They too—and this gold you offer too—are equal to earth in my eyes!....Equal to this earth!

[She scoops up earth and shows it.]

Raja : [Calmly] Think a little, calmly! Then answer me! You loved him thinking he was a soldier! According to your own principle, now that you know he is one who rules a land, should you not forget him? Woman! If I were in your place and such a thing happened, I would certainly forget Salim!

Anar : You would forget him........The reason is........you are not Anarkali!

Raja : Very well. What is your final answer?

Anar : You have no authority to stand in the way of my opinion!

Raja : What?

Anar : Anarkali's “freedom of love” will not bow before Delhi imperialism!

Raja : That too I shall see!

## Translation notes

- The verified source phrase `என்னுள் உன் குடிசை பெருமையுற்றது` is grammatically unusual. It is translated deliberately and literally as `Within me, your hut has gained honour` rather than silently repaired to a smoother Tamil reading.
- `கொலு மண்டபம்` is rendered as `court hall`, preserving its royal-audience function.
- The source-controlled `சப்ரகூட மஞ்சம்` is not silently identified with a more familiar historical term. The translation therefore retains the uncertain compound as the *sabrakooda* couch.
- `சிம்மாசனமேறிகளை நான் மதிக்கமாட்டேன்` is rendered as `I would not respect those who mount thrones`, preserving the verified source's unusual plural form and its anti-rank force.
- `காதல் சுதந்திரம்` is translated directly as `freedom of love`; `டில்லி ஏகாதிபத்தியம்` is translated directly as `Delhi imperialism`. The political contrast is source text, not an editorial gloss.
- `குபேரர்கள்` is retained as `Kuberas`, preserving the conventional image of immense wealth.
- No wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted or used in drafting or review.
