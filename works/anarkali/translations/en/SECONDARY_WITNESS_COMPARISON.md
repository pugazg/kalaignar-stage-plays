- the peace / vultures / white-doves contrast;

- the magnetic-eye image;

## Scene 4 — Tomb

Scene 4 contains the most consequential witness divergences because it carries Salim's sustained poetic lament and political argument about rank, privilege, freedom and justice.

### Strong agreements

Both versions preserve:

- the direct address to Anarkali and the tomb;
- condemnation of those who entombed her;
- the veena / epic-heroine imagery in broad form;
- the long catalogue of royal privilege;
- the claim that rank and royal abundance are worthless without freedom in love;
- the Akbar / Rajput Jodhabai contrast;
- the challenge to the tomb's justice;
- Salim's final attempt to die.

### Material differences

| Verified Tamil control | Independent translation | 2009 witness | Comparison finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| `மாசற்ற ஜோதி மழையே!` | `my spotless shower of radiance!` | `my flawless rain of flame!` | 2009 offers a vivid alternative for `ஜோதி`; independent version retains the compound's structure more conservatively. |

## Useful published alternatives — comparison evidence only

The 2009 witness contains several renderings that are useful as stylistic comparison evidence but do not require automatic revision:

- `liberty to love` for `காதல் சுதந்திரம்`;
- `flawless rain of flame` for `மாசற்ற ஜோதி மழையே`;
- `stone-hearted men` as a compact rendering of `கல்லினும் வலிய மனமுடையோர்`;
- continued use of `sepoy` / `Badsha` for period/court register.

These alternatives are recorded here only. They are not imported into the independent translation because comparison and revision are separate editorial decisions.

## Overall witness character

The comparison supports the following characterization:

- the 2009 published-English `Anarkali` is **complete at scene/plot level** and preserves the play's central romance, rank conflict, live-entombment plot and anti-imperial rhetoric;
- it is a **freer, smoothing translation** than the independent archival translation;
- it sometimes normalizes source anomalies, generalizes culturally specific terms, compresses repetitions or inventories, and supplies explanatory/interpretive language not visible in the verified Tamil;
- a small number of differences are materially semantic rather than merely stylistic, especially `அவள் அழுகை` → `eternal beauty`, `நீதியில்லாத பூமியில்` → `world without you`, and the altered royal-status causality;
- the independent translation remains preferable as the repository's verified-Tamil-derived archival translation because it preserves documented source anomalies, rhetorical repetitions, legal/political register and source-specific imagery more explicitly.
