# Scene 4

## Tomb

Salim : Anar! Anar! Have you vanished, my spotless shower of radiance! Great treasure among women! Birthplace of supreme beauty! Have those great sinners hidden your shapely beauty inside a grave where it can no longer meet my eyes! Exemplar of love! Garland of chastity beyond even poets' worship! Did those hearts harder than stone heap stones over you because they did not wish your smiling, flourishing lovely face to be burned and destroyed? Beloved! Anarkali! Anarkali!!

Sinners! You have destroyed my Anarkali! You have extinguished the light of my heart! You have broken the immortal veena! You have shut within a grave the deathless heroine of the great epic of love! You have hidden her tears! But, you petty celebrants, no one has the courage to tear her out of Salim's heart! No one has the power!

She is, they say, a wanderer! And I am the one who must rule a land! At the movement of my forefinger, they say, all eight directions submit! I have great armies, they say! I can cross many barriers, they say! A palace like a great city! A colossal fort! Attendants, men, arrows, elephants, armies in abundance, abundance! A jewelled crown! Many will come to serve! Do I want a gentle breeze? There is a grove for that! Do I want to swim and play? There is a stream for that! Countless people to worry whether my foot might hurt if it touches a floor spread with silk! Thousands upon thousands to wonder whether blossomed jasmine or mullai would be better to spread upon the cotton bedding........

What use is all this? What use are so many pleasures? To me, who is denied the right to accept the woman who has taken up residence in my heart? To me, who has no freedom to attain the woman I desire? Whether all this exists or does not exist, what does it matter? Why a palace? Why splendour? Why royal life? Why a thousand people to obey my command? Why? Why? Why....? Anarkali! Why should this sinner, who cannot attain you, have all these things? Why should this slave-prince, who could not prevent you from being entombed alive, have a kingdom at all? On an earth without justice, my love, why should I even have life?

They say Akbar's son must not love a dancing woman. O grave that has covered my beautiful beloved, you tell me! You swallowed her because, they say, royal status would be lowered. The Muslim Padshah Akbar may marry the Rajput Jodhabai, they say. But that Jodhabai's son must not love a moon-maiden of dance! O grave that has swallowed my beloved, is that justice? Tell me! Is the verdict you have just delivered a good one? Tell me....! You will not speak! All you know is how to drink life! Here—do that work! Do the work you are accustomed to!

[Salim attempts to die........that realm does not wish his death.]

## Translation notes

- `மாசற்ற ஜோதி மழையே!` is rendered as `my spotless shower of radiance`, retaining the source's unusual compound image rather than replacing it with a generic endearment.
- `கற்பின் ஆரமே!` is translated as `garland of chastity`; `அமர வீணை` remains `immortal veena`, preserving the source's cultural and poetic imagery.
- The repeated status/privilege inventory is retained rather than compressed. Its accumulation is central to Salim's argument that royal abundance is worthless without freedom in love.
- `பஞ்சத்திலே பரப்ப` is rendered conservatively in context as `to spread upon the cotton bedding`. The verified Tamil form itself remains unchanged in the archive.
- `நீதியில்லாத பூமியில்` is translated directly as `on an earth without justice`; the phrase is not softened into private grief.
- `ராஜபுத்திர ஜோதிபாயை` is rendered as `the Rajput Jodhabai` without importing an external historical correction or expanded identification.
- `நடன சந்திரி` is translated as `a moon-maiden of dance`, retaining the source metaphor.
- The final stage direction is translated literally as a dramatic statement: Salim attempts to die, but `that realm` does not wish his death. It is not replaced with a historically inferred outcome.
- The source-printed final `*` is retained.
- No wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted or used in drafting or review.
