# Bharathayanam

## Printed opening note

(A public meeting — people have gathered. A comrade belonging to the rationalist movement, dressed as a Bhagavatar, and another comrade in the guise of his disciple stand together and perform the *Bharathayanam* kalakshepam, singing from time to time! The people who listen benefit from it. Only the latter part is imagination! The first part — the truth of Valmiki's Ramayana.)

Note: (It will be pleasing if read in the drawn-out style of kalakshepam.)

## Continuous translation

Bhagavatar : Aho, come, my boy, disciple! Once upon a time, the kings of fifty-six realms exercised royal rule in this world.

Disciple : Yes........!

Bhag. : And by “the world” you mean........?

Disc. : Round in shape........always, at all times, going round and round.

Bhag. : You fool! That is the world of today! In the old days, “the world” meant the vast land stretching from the tip of Kanyakumari to the foothills of Mount Kailasa in the Himalayan range!

Disc. : Yes....yes........!

Bhag. : In that world spread far and wide........Dasaratha Maharaja ruled the city of Ayodhyapuri!

Disc. : Yes!........

Bhag. : Who ruled it?

Disc. : Dasaratha Maharaja!....

Bhag. : In that Maharaja's palace....sixty thousand....

(Turning to the disciple)

Disc. : There were soldiers.

Bhag. : (Startled) Soldiers?....... You wretch!....... Not soldiers, you fool!

Disc. : Ah....(recovering himself) Forgive me, Swami; sixty thousand gold coins!

Bhag. : You witless fellow....wait, wait!....What you say is true too — gold coins indeed — their bodies were all gold!....golden in colour! As you said, they were soldiers too!....But the place where they fought was different!....Women who waged battle with Dasaratha upon the bridal bed! Sixty thousand wives!

Disc. : Ayyo, yes!

Bhag. : If there had been children........

Disc. : Ayodhya would have suffered a famine!

Bhag. : Ah!....You hasty little nut! If there had been children, Dasaratha would have been happy!....Dasaratha had sixty thousand festival idols, but only three principal idols! Their names were Kausalya — Sumitra — Kaikeyi!......For that reason Dasaratha performed the Putrakameshti sacrifice. In that sacrifice a horse was slaughtered. The three principal wives slept embracing the slaughtered horse!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : The sacrificial oblation was brought forth!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Those three wives ate it and then enjoyed Indra's pleasure with the rishis who had come for the sacrifice.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : And what came of that?

Disc. : Their chastity was gone!

Bhag. : No, you fool! A pregnancy came into being....

Disc. : Yes!....

Bhag. : Aho, come, my boy, disciple! Thus, according to the verses of Valmiki's Ramayana, the sacrifice took place and the three chief queens gave birth to four sons.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Those boys grew day by day and hour by hour, learning elephant-riding, horse-riding and every other art!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Now, that fellow Dasaratha....all his infatuation, all his affection, was for his eldest son Rama!....So he decided to perform Rama's royal coronation, summoned Sage Vasishta to fix a date, and—

Disc. : Yes!........

Bhag. : Thus, when Sage Vasishta arrived, Dasaratha looked at him and said: “Aho, come, beloved and eminent sage! This humble man has a petition to make. That is to say, I am thinking of completing the coronation of our Ramachandra as Crown Prince of Ayodhya.” Thereupon Sage Vasishta stroked his greying beard and said:—

“Listen, Dasaratha Maharaja! I think you have forgotten what happened long ago when you went to marry Kaikeyi, daughter of the Kekaya king. Kaikeyi would not agree to become the wife of an aged man like you. The Kekaya king refused, asking how he could give this parrot-like maiden to an old man. But you were intoxicated by Kaikeyi's beauty, form and loveliness, like a bee drunk on honey. Because of that, you promised to give Ayodhya itself to Kaikeyi as a gift. At that very moment, according to Arya Dharma Shastra custom, you made over Ayodhya to her under the name of ‘kanya sulkam’; you agreed that you would rule this country only as Kaikeyi's representative; that Ayodhya would belong to the child born to Kaikeyi; and that, if she were not blessed with a son, Ayodhya would pass into the possession of her brother, Prince Yudhajit. You entered into this agreement and married Kaikeyi! Do not forget it, Maharaja!” Thus did the foremost of rishis remind him of the old event.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Hearing this, Dasaratha laughed aloud. “What is this, Swami? Even you speak like this? There is a proverb: ‘Tell a thousand lies and conduct one wedding.’ I married Kaikeyi according to that very rule!” he said. On hearing this, Sage Vasishta explained: “You may tell a thousand lies. But under Arya dharma you have already transferred Ayodhya into Kaikeyi's possession. Does not this attempt amount to taking it away from her?”

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : “Whatever the case, my son Rama alone must rule the country! Maharishi Vasishta! There is no use worrying about promises. Find me an auspicious lagna for the coronation! This is the right time. Bharata has gone to his grandfather's house. Before he returns, I shall hand the kingdom over to Rama. After that, it is Rama's affair and Bharata's affair! Let them deal with it; what is it to us?” Dasaratha declared firmly. Seeing this, the great sage Vasishta fixed an auspicious day and hour for the coronation, blessed them, and asked that Rama too be summoned.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Everyone in the palace came to know of this!........But Kaikeyi alone was not informed.

Disc. : Yes!........

Bhag. : The reason was........

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : What do you mean, “yes”?.......

Disc. : The reason was........

Bhag. : (Angrily) Say what it is!

Disc. : If Kaikeyi came to know, she would file a case against Dasaratha in court and obtain a “stay order” stopping the coronation!

Bhag. : Hey, all that belongs to these times!.....What court was there in those days?........

Disc. : Then where did Ravana get an “aeroplane,” Swami?

Bhag. : Hey....you impatient fellow! Don't jump to the Aranya Kandam already!

Disc. : Even if I die, I won't go to the forest, Swami.

Bhag. : Aho, come, my boy, disciple!....Thus Dasaratha was attending to all the arrangements for Rama's coronation without Bharata or Kaikeyi knowing.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Somehow the news reached Kaikeyi through her companion, the hunchback!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : When Kaikeyi heard the news, she became a hissing serpent! She raged, “Can such an injustice be allowed?” She asked the hunchback for a plan to stop it!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : At once the hunchback said to Kaikeyi: “Do not worry, woman! When Dasaratha Raja married you, he gave this Ayodhya to you as ‘kanya sulka’. Make that your foundation and raise the banner of revolt.”

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Hearing this, Kaikeyi said: “No, girl! Throughout the whole country my lord Dasaratha Raja enjoys fame and a name as a man of truth, a noble man, an eminent man and so on! If I were to say that he had already given Ayodhya to me and is now scheming to take it back, the whole world would speak disgracefully of Dasaratha Maharaja! They would call him a man who forgot his word, a deceiver, a schemer, a man who gave away a kingdom in the intoxication of lust and is now staging a deceitful drama to seize it back. I will not be the cause of such a stain upon my husband,” she said, and wept.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : The hunchback immediately laughed and said, “Then let Rama rule!” Hearing this, Kaikeyi said, “I will not agree to that either. Under that wicked man's rule, what will become of me and what will become of my son?” After lamenting thus, she continued: “Listen, hunchback! Without reminding the people of the ‘kanya sulka’ this old man granted me in Kekaya, and without bringing disgrace upon him, I myself shall bear the blame and the sin. I shall take upon myself the bad name that would otherwise fall on him and make that sacrifice.” Astonished, the hunchback straightened herself and asked, “How, amma?” Kaikeyi said: “Listen, hunchback! On an earlier occasion the king granted me two boons. Under them, I may ask for anything and receive it. I shall invoke those boons and obtain the country for Bharata and the forest for Rama....If any blame comes from this, I shall bear it for my husband.” Thus Kaikeyi spoke as though taking an oath!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Having attended to all the arrangements for the royal coronation, the aged Maharaja Dasaratha, as usual, came with every preparation for amorous sport with his young wife Kaikeyi. Parak! Parak!!

Disc. : Parak! Parak!!........

Bhag. : In Kaikeyi's palace, perfumes, special drinks and all the usual things had been placed in their proper places. Dasaratha Maharaja's natural passion intensified! Having decided that after crowning Rama he would retire to the forest, the old Maharaja came with his whole heart surging with desire and called, “Kaikeyi! Kaikeyi!” — and in what state was Kaikeyi?

Disc. : In a fury------

Bhag. : Yes! Dasaratha had hardly arrived when she scooped up a basketful of fire and poured it on his head! She screamed “kuyyo, muraiyo!” Beat her breast! Lamented! Wailed!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : She did none of those things. The daughter of Kekaya asked for two boons! One: Rama must go to the forest. The other: Bharata must rule the country!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : What did Dasaratha do when she asked for the boons?

Disc. : He granted them.

Bhag. : He did not. He realized that his scheme had been exposed. He fell at her feet. His heart burst! He died! Do you know what Kaikeyi became now?

Disc. : A widow!

Bhag. : Not only that; under the ‘kanya sulka’ arrangement, she had become the ruler of Ayodhya! From then on, what she decreed was law! She summoned Rama. “Go to the forest!” she ordered. Rama, Sita and Lord Lakshmana went to the forest! Without Kaikeyi saying a word, the people of the country learned what had happened! Through the Kekaya king, the whole world came to know the story that Rama had conspired to seize the country!....Bharata, who had gone to his grandfather's house, came racing back!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : He learned everything that had happened!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Some prominent men in the country began campaigning that it was just for Bharata to rule, but that driving Rama into the forest was unjust. Seeing this, Kaikeyi became a little afraid! She wanted the people of Ayodhya to form a good opinion of Bharata. So she devised a trick! Calling Bharata, she said: “My precious long-lived Bharata! Listen carefully to something I am going to tell you! Your father tried to seize the country he had given me and hand it to your elder brother! To prevent blame from falling on your father, I bore that blame myself and made you the rightful claimant to the country. But I hear that some people in the country are distressed that Rama went to the forest and are reviling you. So, in order for you to earn a good name, you must stage a drama!”

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : What drama? What kind of drama?

Disc. : A “Three-D” drama!

Bhag. : Not a Three-D drama, you fool! A thieving drama!

Disc. : A thieving drama?

Bhag. : Yes....When Rama went to the forest, he stole a very valuable object and took it with him! Do you know what that object was?........

Disc. : Sita........

Bhag. : Tch! When did Rama steal Sita? Sita was his wife!....What he stole and took with him was a priceless gem! If you had it, you could buy six cities like Ayodhyapuri! Rama stole that gem and carried it away!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : The news that the gem had been stolen was reported to Kaikeyi! Kaikeyi began an investigation into how it had disappeared!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : In the course of that investigation.....Kaikeyi obtained information that Rama had hidden that world-famous gem inside his footwear and taken it away!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Just then Bharata returned from the Kekaya country. Kaikeyi said to him: “Brother Bharata! Your elder brother has hidden in his sandals a matchless gem that cannot be found in all the fourteen worlds! Ayodhya without that gem is like a sky without the sun, like a face without eyes! Somehow that gem must be brought back.”

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Bharata stood blinking in bewilderment, not knowing what to do.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Then Kaikeyi said: “Bharata! Do not be afraid! A plot must be defeated by a plot! From this very moment cry out, ‘Rama must not go to the forest; he alone must rule the country!’ I shall try to pacify you — do not listen! Take the ministers and chief officers with you and set out declaring that you are going to see Rama and bring him back! Do not stop lamenting! Cry, ‘My lord! My lord!’ Fall at his feet! As long as I am here, he will be afraid to return to the country! If he shows the slightest sign that he may actually come back, I shall make sure he does not! Abuse me to my face!....Stage the drama so well that all the ministers and chief officers praise your brotherly love!

“‘Elder brother, you are the son who does not forget his father's word! You rejected the earth in obedience to your father's command and took on this ascetic garb — are you not the noble one! Those who are not you are gods!’ Keep praising him like this and bewilder everyone! Do not even give Rama an opportunity to say, ‘I am coming back’; speak carefully! Do not forget one thing, Bharata! Our gem is hidden in Rama's sandals! All these arrangements are only to seize it! Beg and plead, ‘Even if you will not rule, let your sandals rule the country,’ and somehow wrench them away and bring them back! In the midst of the ministers and chief officers, Rama will not be able to take out the gem! He will blink! He will be bewildered! He will say, ‘No, little brother, don't touch the sandals — it is a sin!’ You say, ‘Are these sandals? They are your world-renowned divine feet, elder brother!’ and take them off yourself and bring them back, my son!” Thus spoke Kaikeyi, and Bharata set out in delight to perform the drama.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Following his mother's instructions, and taking chariots, elephants, cavalry and infantry with him for protection so that neither Rama nor Lakshmana might kill him, Bharata went to Rama. With cunning and theatrical skill, weeping and lamenting, he made it appear that he had undying affection for his elder brother! Rama did not believe it either — but for the sake of the ministers and chief officers standing before them, the two embraced one another!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Do you know what happened when they embraced one another like that?

Disc. : He stabbed him to death with a concealed tiger-claw!

Bhag. : You fool! That's the story of Shivaji!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : The palace people who saw the two embrace shed tears of joy!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : At once Bharata looked at Rama and said: “Elder brother! You came to the forest because you would not disobey father's word!....Give me your sandals in your place! I shall crown them and rule as their representative.” And he removed the sandals from Rama's feet.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : With no other way out, Rama had to give him the sandals in which the gem was hidden.

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Bharata did not realize that there was a plot even in Rama giving him the sandals. Kaikeyi did not realize it either!

Disc. : Yes! They did not realize it!

Bhag. : A gem in the sandals! A gem valuable enough to buy entire realms! So Bharata placed those sandals upon his head and carried them away!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : Later, Bharata argued that it was unjust for Rama to invade the country that rightfully belonged to him.

Disc. : Rama replied that since his sandals had ruled for fourteen years, the prescriptive right arising from enjoyment of rule belonged to him........

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : “Once more than twelve years have passed, prescriptive right arises; if it is fourteen years, surely that right has arisen,” all the great sages opined!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : At once Bharata left the country, had a fire-pit kindled in a place called Nandigrama, jumped into it and gave up his life!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : The gem too, which was in his lap, burned in the fire, burst and shattered!

Disc. : Yes!

Bhag. : All those who read this *Bharathayanam*, those who sat nearby and heard it being read, and those who heard it from people who had heard it........

Disc. : Will go to moksha!

Bhag. : No, no! Those who read it and those who heard it will go neither to *motcham* nor to *mosam* — neither to salvation nor to deception! They will gain rational understanding!........

Disc. : *Nama Pagutharivupathe!*

*Bharathayanam* is concluded!

## Translation notes

- The title `பரதாயணம்` is retained as *Bharathayanam*. Its form deliberately echoes the naming pattern of *Ramayanam* while centring Bharata; an explanatory English title would flatten that wordplay.
- `காலட்சேபம்` is retained as *kalakshepam*: the opening explicitly frames the performance in the traditional drawn-out storytelling/singing mode, which the play then turns to satirical rationalist purpose.
- Source speaker-label structure is reflected as `Bhagavatar`, `Disciple`, and the abbreviated `Bhag.` / `Disc.` rather than replacing the dramatic exchange with narrative prose.
- `உற்சவ விக்கிரகம்` / `மூல விக்கிரகம்` is rendered as `festival idols` / `principal idols`, preserving the temple-image metaphor through which the sixty-thousand wives are contrasted with the three chief queens.
- The `கற்பு` / `கரு` joke cannot be reproduced phonically in English. `Their chastity was gone!` / `A pregnancy came into being` preserves the semantic turn and its comic timing.
- `கன்யா சுல்கம்` / `கன்யா சுல்க` is retained as `kanya sulkam` / `kanya sulka`, rather than being reduced everywhere to a modern legal label. In context it functions as the asserted bride-price/settlement on which the satire's property argument is built.
- English loanwords in the Tamil — `கோர்ட்`, `கேஸ்`, `ஸ்டே ஆர்டர்`, `ஏரோப்ளேன்`, and `த்ரீ டி` — remain visibly modern in English because the anachronism is the joke.
- `பராக்! பராக்!!` is retained as `Parak! Parak!!` as a heraldic/performance cry rather than assigned an uncertain paraphrase.
- The Three-D / `திருடி` exchange is translated as `Three-D` / `thieving`; the exact Tamil sound-play cannot be carried over, so the note records the loss rather than inventing a different joke.
- The verified Tamil physically assembles the anomalous `நீயல்ல` / `வர்` as `நீயல்லவர்`, not the semantically expected `நீயல்லவா`. The translation therefore preserves the anomaly literally as `Those who are not you are gods!` rather than silently repairing Kalaignar's printed witness.
- `பாதுகாப்பாக ரதகஜதுரகபதாதிகளை` is rendered as `taking chariots, elephants, cavalry and infantry with him for protection`; the compound military list is unpacked for intelligible English without changing its function.
- The legal joke in `அனுபவ பாத்தியதை` / the twelve-year rule is rendered as `prescriptive right arising from enjoyment of rule`, keeping the property-law register that makes the exchange comic.
- The closing distinguishes `மோக்ஷம்` / `மோட்சம்` from `மோசம்`. English cannot reproduce the near-homophonic Tamil play cleanly, so `moksha`, `motcham`, and `mosam` are retained at the punchline with the sense `salvation` / `deception` made explicit.
- `நமப் பகுத்தறிவுபதே!` is retained as *Nama Pagutharivupathe!* because it parodies a devotional/Sanskritic closing formula while replacing its object with `பகுத்தறிவு` — rational understanding.
- No published English witness was used to draft or review this translation.

## Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note

This note is **interpretive context, not translated source text**.

The play itself explicitly frames the performers as belonging to `அறிவியக்கம்` and closes by rejecting both religious salvation and deception in favour of `பகுத்தறிவு`. Its satire repeatedly brings mythic authority, ritual, monarchy, property claims and scriptural narration into the language of public argument, legal procedure, political campaigning and comic cross-examination.

That framing strongly resonates with the rationalist and anti-mythic polemical tradition associated with the Dravidian movement. The translation preserves the abrupt anachronisms, courtroom vocabulary, political-performance language, ritual terms and final invocation of rational understanding rather than smoothing them into a neutral retelling of the Ramayana.

At the same time, this note does not treat every satirical claim in the dialogue as an independent historical assertion. The translated source remains the dramatic polemic printed in this edition; the interpretive movement-context note is kept separate from the characters' speech.
