# Scene 4

Location: Road.

(Kandhan and Ayyar argue.)

(Ayyar carries a bundle.)

Kandhan: Hey, Ayyar! Come this way! What is that bundle?

Ayyar: This? Things given at the wedding.

Kandhan: How much rice is there?

Ayyar: Why? Five padi.

Kandhan: Hey! What do you need five padi of rice for? Do you not know this is the rationing era?

Ayyar: What do I care what era it is? I can go and get it even from the ration officer's own house.

Kandhan: Get lost. You keep the rice, but hand over those five rupees too.

Ayyar: Why are you asking me for it?

Kandhan: Are you giving it or not?

Ayyar: Ayyo! I will give it. Do not come near me. Look at this—God created them to give, created us to receive, and created fellows like you to beat it out of us. O God! The times have gone bad!

Kandhan: Yes! The black hen has laid a white egg—O Money! Money that sleeps in the iron chests of iron-hearted men who, though workers labour day and night, crying “hunger, hunger,” will not say even at midnight, “Here, eat a little”! O Money, you who created this wretched state in which the poor lie without food in stick-built huts while the rich sing sweet songs in their storeyed houses! Life-blood of the world! How much longer will you live in the houses of these idlers? The workers' advance is coming! Come over to us. I will take care of you. (Pazhani enters.)

Pazhani: Ah! If only this country had a thousand true comrades like him! Kandhan, who spoke fire and brought Ayyar to heel, is Azhagappan's servant. If only his master had as much in him as this man does...? Whatever happens, the country rests its hopes on students and workers. We shall see.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/04.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- `padi` is retained as the source rice measure.
- Kandhan's apostrophe to money retains the class rhetoric, the hut/storeyed-house contrast and the coming workers' advance rather than reducing the speech to summary.
- The opening “black hen / white egg” image is kept literally because the locked Tamil gives no basis for replacing it with a more familiar English idiom.
