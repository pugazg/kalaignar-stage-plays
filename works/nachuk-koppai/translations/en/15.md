# Scene 15

Location: Pond.

(Santha draws water.)

(Ekambaram enters.)

Ekambaram: Santha! You have received the punishment you deserved. The world has taken revenge on you for your betrayal. You deceived Ekambaram—and you yourself were deceived. You forgot my love, became greedy for money and married Azhagappan. Its result ended in suffering. Even in the widow's garb left by broken love, the darting of her eyes has not stopped. Do you love me again? Or do you intend to live a life of prostitution? No! Santha did not deceive me. With her eyes she poured love over me again and again and went away. The movement of her lips seemed to ask, “Love, is there forgiveness?” That woman longs to leap into the world of reform. O world, you tell me! May I forgive Santha? Will the world consent to my forgiving Santha? Tch! Am I to forgive the woman who deceived me? A kiss for the foot that kicked me? Sugar for the mouth that spat on me? No! Santha has not forgotten me. And even if she had, what then? Is she not the spark burning in my heart? The same society that sacrificed her for money cut her life apart. The same society that bound her life broke it. The same shastra that arranged her marriage made her a widow. The same orthodoxy that refused to accept her love cut away her thali. Santha may have lost her husband and her heart may have broken, but the feeling rising within that heart has not ceased. Does she want to marry me again? Yes! There is no doubt. Santha truly wants it!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/15.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- Ekambaram's monologue deliberately moves between accusation, self-correction and renewed conviction; that unstable rhetorical movement is preserved rather than smoothed into a single emotional position.
- The harsh `விபசார வாழ்வு` question is translated directly because it is part of the character's internal conflict.
- `shastra`, `orthodoxy` and `thali` are retained/distinguished so the social-religious chain in the argument remains visible.
- The repeated “same society / same shastra / same orthodoxy” structure is preserved as a rhetorical sequence.
