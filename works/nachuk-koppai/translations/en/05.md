# Scene 5

Location: Ekambaram's house.

(He looks at Santha's picture and weeps.)

(Song. Meenakshi enters.)

Ekambaram — song. Santha. [Source-held: two adjacent source clusters following “Santha” are unreadable; no characters are inferred.]

(Meenakshi enters.)

Meenakshi: Ekambaram, my son! You have been starving yourself for two days like this. What will happen to your body? Come, let us eat.

Ekambaram: Amma! I do not want food now.

Meenakshi: You keep crying “Santha, Santha.” She is living comfortably in her husband's house, while you are the one suffering here. All right! Come eat.

Ekambaram: Ayyo! Please do not trouble me. I do not want anything now. Go away.

(Adikesava Mudaliar enters.)

Adikesava: Hey, Meenakshi! Has Ekambaram eaten?

Meenakshi: That is why I am calling him. He says he does not want food!

Adikesava: Does not want food? What else does he want—Santha?

Ekambaram: Father, do not trouble me again and again. Enough. Leave me alone.

Adikesava: I thought perhaps you would earn a name as a sadhu.

Ekambaram: Yes! Even a man who cheats can earn the name “sadhu”!

Adikesava: Do not talk too much—you over-clever dog!

Ekambaram: Speak with some sense, Father.

Adikesava: Sense, nonsense! If I hit you, your teeth and everything else will break.

Ekambaram: Father! Why would you not hit me? I am no longer ready to speak with you. When the wife of a worker exhausted from a whole day's labour has been bitten by a snake, suppose a creditor arrives saying, “Pay the debt you owe”—or when a ship has sunk and a man is swimming through the sea to reach shore, suppose a giant fish opens its mouth to welcome him. That is how you speak to me while I am already wasting away in pain. Father! Would anyone offer wondrous words to a poor wretch crying that he has not even a rag to tie around himself? What am I to do? As the smoke of war approaches our town, as the smell of war strikes the shores of India, are you not a servant of the Allies who carried on the work of destruction? Why would you not speak? You will speak still more.

Adikesava: Hey, shameless fellow! Is this how you speak to me—the man who fathered you, raised you, taught you, fed you and built up this flesh of yours? A useless wretch, and still full of bravado! I thought I should treat you with so much respect. You ruined it yourself. Now you have learned to talk and you go on without even taking a breath. Get out, you idiot son! (He goes inside.)

Meenakshi: Ekambaram, my son! Should you speak to Father like that? He too has gone away angry. Come, let us go.

Ekambaram: Amma! I do not want anything. Go away.

Meenakshi: I should have planted pirandai in the womb that bore you! From now on, I have lost even the claim that you are my son. Go!

(She goes inside.)

Ekambaram: Chandalas! Ayyo! My beloved has abandoned me. My father thinks of me as herd-cattle. My mother raves as though she were a demon. Murderers who do not understand my condition! It is no longer right to remain here. I must run away somewhere.
