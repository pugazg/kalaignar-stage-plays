# Scene 16

Location: Maniyappa's house.

Maniyappa: What is this? I told that Karuppan to come, and he still has not turned up. (Karuppan enters.)

Karuppan: Greetings!

Maniyappa: Hey, who told you to make me bow?

Karuppan: No, sir. I said I was bowing to you.

Maniyappa: Is this respect? Have you become such a big man now?

Karuppan: They sent word for me. I left the gruel I was drinking just as it was and came. What is the matter, sir?

Maniyappa: Do you know Pazhaniyappan?

Karuppan: Some god with devotion, is he? Tell me everything. I haven't gone there, sir!

Maniyappa: Not that, fool. My son Pazhani.

Karuppan: Oh, him? The one who holds public meetings and says, “Don't drink toddy, don't drink arrack; eat in a Pallan's house and wash your hands in a Paraiyan's house”? Him?

Maniyappa: That is the fellow.

Karuppan: Why does he need all this town trouble?

Maniyappa: Not just town trouble. He has brought it right into my house.

Karuppan: Tch! Tch! A man who creates trouble like this should be tied up properly.

Maniyappa: Hey! Do you know our Santha?

Karuppan: I don't know her, sir.

Maniyappa: The one we married off in Ariyalur and who came back widowed—that is her!

Karuppan: Oh! I know her well. What happened to her?

Maniyappa: Nothing. They say she must be remarried!

Karuppan: What, a widow's marriage? In this land where Sita, Panchali, Draupadi and Nalayini were born—a widow's marriage? Let that pass, sir.

Maniyappa: I spoke two harsh words to Pazhani. Because of that he left the house. You are the one who must bring him back.

Karuppan: Will he come if I call him?

Maniyappa: Hey, you... idiot! You need not call him. Frighten him by saying that if he shows his head near this association, he will lose that very head. He will reform. What are you thinking?

Karuppan: Nothing to think about. I do not even have money for toddy. I was thinking of going to my uncle's house.

Maniyappa: If you want money, ask for it. (He gives money.) Hey, do not waste the money however you please—buy toddy and arrack with it.

Karuppan: There is money, sir, money. What is needed is people.

Maniyappa: Fine, fine. The moment you get the money you start talking like this? Hey, there is one important thing I have to tell you. The matter must be finished secretly.

Karuppan: What, sir? Are you teaching a fish-fry how to swim? I will take care of everything.

Maniyappa: Get going. (Karuppan leaves.) Santha!

Karuppan: What, father?

Maniyappa: Bring me some coffee, child.

(She brings it.)

Maniyappa: Santha, my girl! Your brother is already wandering around like this. Have you too gone bad?

Santha: I have not gone bad in any way.

Maniyappa: Not gone bad? Is that why you want to remarry?

Santha: A wife whose husband dies is expected to fall down and die with him, is she? What are you saying, Father! When Mother died, did you too prepare to fall down and die? You even tried to marry a second wife.

Maniyappa: Tch, donkey! Do you know whom you are speaking to?

Santha: I am speaking to my father. I am asking for my rights. What is wrong in that, Father?

Maniyappa: Is this how one speaks to one's father? If there is no rain in the country, how will it rain? If prostitutes multiply, is it not an age of destruction?

Santha: Father! Anna's policy is to abolish prostitution. Your policy is to encourage it. Not only you—even your religion encourages prostitution, does it not?

Maniyappa: Tch, donkey! Santha! If you want to stay here obeying my word, stay. Otherwise drag some man away and run off with him. Prostitute! Prostitute! Prostitute! (He leaves.)

(Soliloquy.)

Santha: Sinner! I am a prostitute! I am a prostitute! I spoke openly without hiding what lay in my heart. That is prostitution! I gave voice to the thought beating in the hearts of countless widows. That is prostitution! What is prostitution? Is it prostitution for women who have lost their husbands to stand in tears and rags? You lost your wife—why do you still wear the mark on your forehead? You lost your wife—why do you still use all kinds of perfumes? You lost your wife—why do you still live a life of pleasure? Do feelings belong only to you? Do rights belong only to you? When you see women you bare your teeth—is that not a life of prostitution? Is what I did prostitution? Is what you did prostitution? What is prostitution? O folly that cannot understand the state of a mind—perish! You separated from me the husband I sought; is that not prostitution? O demon of difference that cut my life apart—be destroyed! Be gone! What is prostitution? What is prostitution?

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/16.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- The caste labels `Pallan` and `Paraiyan` are retained because Karuppan's line explicitly invokes caste-bound dining practices; no modernization is inserted into the dramatic text.
- `kallu` / `sarayam` are translated as toddy / arrack to preserve the alcohol register.
- The Sita / Panchali / Draupadi / Nalayini sequence is retained as spoken, including its rhetorical redundancy.
- Santha's widow-rights argument, accusation against religion, repeated `prostitute` refrain and comparison with a widower's freedoms are translated directly rather than softened.
