# Scene 18

Location: Rationalist Association.

[Pazhani, Sivaguru.]

(Pazhani sits in distress.)

(Song.)

Sivaguru: Comrade! As you told me yesterday, I turned through the *Kamba Ramayanam* and the *Periya Puranam* from beginning to end.

Pazhani: What conclusion did you reach from them, comrade?

Sivaguru: What conclusion is there, comrade? A poem came to me from it. Shall I say it?

Pazhani: Say it. Let us hear.

Sivaguru: Our poets lick and drink the nectar of art! O fool who swoons and sings praise—turn the pages and see. If obscenity is poured into a rotten plate and called payasam, will scholars drink it? Kambar and Kilar fashioned heated art in our precious Tamil land. Is art for thought—or art for restraint? O age that rejects and casts away—come quickly! How is the poem?

Pazhani: Ah! What a beautiful poem! After all, is this not the land where Bharathidasan was born?

Sivaguru: Yes, comrade! The elders say, “Even the loom in Kamban's house will sing poetry.” But today's sacred saying of Tamilagam is, “Even the pots and pans in Bharathidasan's house will compose tunes,” comrade!

Pazhani: Sivaguru, did you not go home?

Sivaguru: My friend!... Are you still distressed?

Pazhani: My distress is that I will not live to see the Dravidian land that is about to separate!

Sivaguru: Should you, of all people, speak like this?

Pazhani: I myself have left a sister wasting away at home, while orthodoxy rages, tears run from her eyes, and fools call her a widow. Am I to stand watching and do nothing? And you ask me to speak differently?

Sivaguru: If, after your father's time, your sister marries...?

Pazhani: I will kill my sister right now and then kill myself. Will you at least then hand Santha over to Ekambaram?

Sivaguru: Comrade! You are speaking in anger. Can a dog's tail be straightened?

Pazhani: If it will not straighten, cut it off and throw it away, comrade!

Sivaguru: Lie down and sleep peacefully. We can talk tomorrow morning. I am leaving.

(Sivaguru leaves.)

(Pazhani mixes poison in a cup.)

(He writes a letter.)

Vengeful father of this wretched society! You ruined Santha's life in many ways. You made her a widow. You destroyed her. Let Ekambaram accept my sister. Finish the work I leave unfinished. Santha! Ask Ekambaram's forgiveness. O Dravidian world! Gain self-respect. What use is my living if I could not shape Santha's thinking? What use is my death? I am going to die.

Yours,

Pazhaniyappan.

Pazhani: (Holding the poison.) Father! You ruined me. You shattered Santha's life too. Here—I am dying. Let your heart be cooled. Let this dead society laugh and rejoice. Santha! For your sake I am cutting short my life.

(As he speaks, he sees the portrait of Arignar Anna there. His mind is shaken.)

Anna! Commander of the Dravidian movement! Are you telling me that suicide is a coward's act? Are you asking whether it is worthy of a brave man? Are you calling me a coward unable to act? Yes. From that day I became a reformer; from today Santha is a reformer; forever Ekambaram is a reformer.

Must these three reformers submit to fools swollen with religious arrogance? What can money and office do? Can they destroy feeling? Or burn away our principles? They cannot. O Money! O Religion! We shall take you on.

(He throws down the poison cup and breaks it.)

(Karuppan enters.)

Karuppan: Hey, who is making that noise?

Pazhani: Who are you?

Karuppan: I ask who you are, and you ask me back! Hey, what business do you have in this association? You keep talking about association-this and association-that; I will break you to pieces. Do you know this lion?

Pazhani: Hey, will you not leave respectfully?

Karuppan: Hey, boy, I came here to drive you out. What do they say? “The spirit that came to settle drives out the village spirit.”

Pazhani: Get lost, you drunkard.

Karuppan: Hey! You call me a drunkard? You? Me?

Pazhani: Tch, donkey! Get out. (The two grapple. Karuppan stabs Pazhani with a knife.)

If one speaks the truth, do you snarl? If one asks for inquiry, do you rage? If one says, “Know your duty,” is the answer a knife-stab? If one says, “Think about your condition,” is the answer murder? Ah! Father!

(He dies.)

(Dawn breaks. The police arrive.)

Telugu Policeman: “Ivala emo kolai sinni undade—edu emi?”

(He picks up the knife.)

“Adē Pazhaniyappan kodukku Maniyappa Mudaliar. Maniyappa Mudali kodukku Pazhaniyappan kaadu. Evaro sesina pani.”

(He takes out his whistle and blows it.)

Malayali Policeman: “Enda! Ethukku vilichchi?”

Telugu Policeman: “Kesava Nair. Kudu.”

Malayali Policeman: “Enda sola.”

Telugu Policeman: “Enda, Kesava Nair. Inda Alvar Naidu okkavaramā veettile ledu. Emi kalatta, emi pani, emi jagadam, emi Kesava Nair. Poyi jemakayil kanra.”

Malayali Policeman: “Oorum nee pom viluchchinuva. Bayamā irukku.”

Telugu Policeman: “Appo nee ikkade undu. Naan poyittu paathunnu.”

Malayali Policeman: “Enikku bayamāyide. Nee pothālu.”

Telugu Policeman: “Manasu leyum āga vayithileyum āchu. Seekiram randi.”

(Theft.)

(The Sub-Inspector enters.)

Sub-Inspector: Hey, 208! What is this?

Telugu Policeman: Murder, sir.

Sub-Inspector: How did it happen?

Telugu Policeman: “Tellede, sir.”

Sub-Inspector: What are you here for, then?

Telugu Policeman: “Emitiledu.”

Sub-Inspector: Look at the record.

Telugu Policeman: Sir.

Sub-Inspector: (Reads the letter.)

Right. Pazhaniyappan must have committed suicide. Hey, 104, go and bring Maniyappa Mudaliar.

(104 leaves.)

Sub-Inspector: Where are you?

Malayali Policeman: Me.

Sub-Inspector: What are you saying?

Malayali Policeman: ......

Sub-Inspector: What is this fellow? Ask him and he speaks Malayalam; ask that one and he speaks Telugu. I have to beat my chest dealing with the lot of you.

Telugu Policeman: “Avunu.”

Sub-Inspector: This is complete madness.

Malayali Policeman: “Athe, athe.” Maniyappa Mudaliar too—“athe, athe!”

Sub-Inspector: A.C. 2081, you are no longer fit for the job.

Telugu Policeman: “Enduku, sir?”

Sub-Inspector: Your stomach has grown far too large.

Telugu Policeman: “Avunu.”

Sub-Inspector: So we do not need you.

Telugu Policeman: “Emi, sir—bonus?”

Sub-Inspector: Where is your belt?

Telugu Policeman: It does not fit around my stomach.

Sub-Inspector: Give me the diary.

(Sivaguru enters.)

(Sivaguru looks at the letter.)

Sivaguru: What—suicide? Pazhaniyappa, did you commit suicide? Did society drive you to suicide? Until late yesterday you were speaking with me so warmly. I left telling you, “Lie down and sleep peacefully.” Is this the result? Nectar born to kill social corruption! Full moon that came to break blind belief! Have you been destroyed? Has your life been extinguished? You turned a town gripped by orthodox frenzy into a fortress of rationalism. Servant who came to reform the ruined life of the Tamil people! Did this society, which will itself disappear after suffering only a little longer, destroy you? My heart burns when I think of what you endured. This fire! Fire! Fire! Will it not set this social fortress ablaze? Useless society! Rotten demon! Did you kill Pazhaniyappan? What wrong did he commit? Why this act of vengeance? Ancient society falling into the pit! Caste restrictions that are about to die! Insect! Wretched chariot! Cry, whine, fear! Bow—babble! You too are human among humans, not earth! Open your eyes and tell me whether Pazhaniyappan's death is just. Santha loved Ekambaram. You condemned her as an ill omen to society. Why? You called her a *kammanatti*—without thinking that the coming storm would strike you too! This is a world! This is a life! For this, a society! And one God to rule all of it! Tch! No shame! Those who live as people are called mere people! A life of pomp earns the title of Mathadhipathi. The man who torments and crushes the worker receives the title of philanthropist! The man who makes lies his livelihood is called a true devotee! For such a world, even truth is a curse. Society laughs on hearing of your death—though a fox has no strength to strike down a lion, if the lion dies in fury will not the fox's heart rejoice? Ayyo! My friend! Where will I now hear your Tamil speech, your heroic breath, your sweep of words? From whom will I hear them? Rich Tamil that roared like a lion! Lifelong comrade! Did this society strike you down? Society that stood in the way—you will certainly be defeated.

(Santha enters.)

Santha: Ah! Anna! Did you die for me? Did you perish for me? I will not remain here and live amid injustice. Let us find joy in the world of death. Here—I am coming too! (She raises the knife.)

Ekambaram: (Running in.) No! No!!

Sivaguru: No. Let this knife not take your life. Let it be used to crush society's ignorance. In Pazhani's blood I begin your life together. Long live Tamil! Long live Dravidam!

**The End**

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/18.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- The Kamba Ramayanam / Periya Puranam criticism, Bharathidasan/Kamban comparison, Dravidian/Self-Respect rhetoric and final social critique are translated only from the closed Tamil scene; no outside doctrinal or historical material is added.
- `Arignar Anna` is retained as a title/name because the source stage direction explicitly identifies the portrait that redirects Pazhani away from suicide.
- The police dialogue is deliberately written in Tamil-script approximations of Telugu/Malayalam speech. English therefore preserves those lines as conservative romanized performance text instead of “correcting” them into standard Telugu/Malayalam or guessing meanings not secured by the closed Tamil artifact. Source-secure Tamil framing lines are translated normally.
- `kammanatti` is retained as a transliterated historical insult in Sivaguru's speech rather than silently substituting a modern label.
- Source-visible `முற்றும்` is rendered as **The End**.
- Non-authorial `4063` and the printer imprint remain outside the dramatic translation.
