# Scene 9

Location: Maniyappa Mudaliar's house.

(Ayyar enters.)

Maniyappa: What is this? Not a letter or anything from Santha's house. I cannot understand a thing!

(Ayyar enters.)

Ayyar: What, Mudaliar! Looks as though you were setting out.

Maniyappa: Yes, Swami! Have you forgotten us or what?

Ayyar: What, Mudaliar! You say that so easily. How could I forget you?

Maniyappa: Yes. What brings you here now?

Ayyar: Would I come without some business?

Maniyappa: Then what is the matter?

Ayyar: Hmm. There is something.

Maniyappa: What is it? Tell me.

Ayyar: The Radha Kalyanam is coming up.

Maniyappa: For whom? Your daughter?

Ayyar: Tch! Tch! What is this...? How could you ask that, when you are such a devout man? Have you joined your son too?

Maniyappa: I know only what somebody tells me. That is how I learn everything.

Ayyar: We are celebrating as a festival the sacred story of Radha and Krishna that happened in the previous yuga. That is Radha Kalyanam.

Maniyappa: What must I do for it, Swami?

Ayyar: If you help with some contribution, you will receive Bhagavan's grace. Besides, there will be a katha-kalakshepam by Sudakodi Surya Narayana Ayyar. You must certainly come too.

Maniyappa: Good heavens, Swami! Would I fail to come? (Money is given.)

Ayyar: Yes. We need three marakkal of raw rice.

Maniyappa: Why, Swami?

Ayyar: We have to make *thuthivannam* for Bhagavan.

Maniyappa: Swami, the paddy has not been threshed. It will be threshed only next month. Once it is threshed I will send it.

Ayyar: Giving it next month will not serve the purpose. I will take rice from our house and do it now. You can give it back later.

Maniyappa: Then all right. Go and come back.

Ayyar: When you come, eat at your own house before coming.

Maniyappa: Why, Swami?

Ayyar: There, food is to be served only to Brahmins. It is not for Shudras.

Maniyappa: What shortage of food do I have, Swami! By Bhagavan's grace and your blessings, there is plenty.

Ayyar: Then all right. I am going.

Maniyappa: Go and come back.

(Ayyar leaves. A lame beggar enters. Song.)

Lame Beggar: Sir!

Maniyappa: Who are you?

Lame Beggar: I have been starving since morning, sir!

Maniyappa: I asked who you are, and you have come reading me a starvation petition! Who are you?

Lame Beggar: Sir! I am lame—a beggar. I have nothing for my stomach. You must show me some kindness.

Maniyappa: Have I built a choultry here for everyone who comes and goes? Get out the way you came.

Lame Beggar: I am terribly hungry, sir!

Maniyappa: Are you going, or shall I kick you?

Lame Beggar: If you tell me to go, I will go. Do not kick me.

Maniyappa: A donkey that begs for alms has talk too? Will you not leave respectfully?

Lame Beggar: Sir! I came because I had heard you were a great dharmakartha. I did not know that adharma danced here with its hair let loose. I did not know that poison had been given the name nectar.

Maniyappa: A donkey who knows so much about dharma! Get out.

Lame Beggar: Arrogant speech! This is not your fault. It is the fault of your uncultivated mind. Irrigate the field called thought with the water called inquiry. Plough it with rationalism. Level the mounds and hollows called high and low. Pull out entirely the weeds called blind belief. Then the crop called discernment will grow. Only then will you know what dharma is and what adharma is. If good thought itself can be our God, why a temple? If one has hands and legs with which to work, why free feasts for a man who sucks away the property of others?

Maniyappa: Hey! What do you think you are saying? You do not know even a little respect. What is a beggar fellow like you talking about? I will kick you. I will kick you to pieces. What grand disguise are you putting on?

Lame Beggar: Sir! Do I put on a disguise? Do I wear saffron and live as a libertine? Do I smear sacred ash and cultivate baseness? The waves rising in my heart become the movement of my lips. My mouth says only what my stomach asks. Is this a disguise? Fine! It seems you do not know the man who truly wears disguises! You lie enchanted by him, while spitting harsh words at poor people like me. At least from now on, recognize the truth. May the world of reason guide you.

(Song. He leaves.)

Maniyappa: That scoundrel fellow said something or other and went away. O God!

(Pazhani enters.)

Pazhani: It is all the scoundrel's work—his.

Maniyappa: What? Whose scoundrel's work?

Pazhani: What else? Was it not his doing to create both worker and rich man? Do you think it a noble act to create one man who labours and another who eats and enjoys? Is it not your God's work that the worker should wither in famine while the rich man sings sweet songs on a soft bed? Five thousand rupees for temple kumbabhishekam! Ten thousand rupees for a Brahmin feast! You pour money out for all this—but has your wealth ever been used to save the worker dying in a collapsing hut, apart from the dream that it will buy you heaven?

Maniyappa: Hey, my boy! Why should we get involved in all this town trouble? Earn a name as a good boy!

Pazhani: Father!

Maniyappa: What is it, son?

Pazhani: I do not want a name as a good boy. Nor do I want a title from a daylight impostor. Whatever my reason and ability tell me is good, I will do and finish it. I will not hesitate. This society grinds its teeth at me. The day when its teeth are exposed is drawing near. Father! You gave Santha to Azhagappan without asking me. Look at the letter she has written.

Maniyappa: What has she written? Has a letter come? Read it, read it. Let us hear.

Pazhani: (Reading.) Dear Anna! Greetings. I am well here—or so one might say. At the age when my life should have been made prosperous, Father bent its course. For a little while I was happy, thinking the husband I received was a good man. In the end I came to hate him. My husband is a drunkard—not a family man! Lying in the lap of liquor has now left him with a grave disease. A danger he cannot survive. A calamity with no release. “Whatever troubles come, endure them patiently” may be Father's golden saying. But this is not the age of Nalayini. Reply immediately. Yours, Santha.

Maniyappa: I thought you were reading it and adding your own descriptions. Santha seems no better either. Yes—what reply are you going to write?

Pazhani: Is your heart made of iron? What reply can I write? I can only write that Maniyappa Mudaliar's son lives like an unfeeling log of wood. Money... money... money. That is what ruined her.

Maniyappa: Where is this fellow going to survive? Hmm. Somehow I have saved two or three coins, and he goes around making speeches about everything. O God!

Telegraph Peon: Is Maniyappa Mudaliar here?

Maniyappa: Here I am, standing like a tree! What is the matter?

Peon: A telegram has come.

Maniyappa: What gram?

Peon: A telegram.

Maniyappa: A telegram? Give it here. This seems to be written in English.

Peon: Put your signature here. Bring a pen.

Maniyappa: Here, take it. What is this, sir? Is everyone who comes and goes going to be just like the people in this house?

Peon: Do not get angry. All right, I am going.

Maniyappa: All right, go.

Peon: Some change...

Maniyappa: For how many rupees?

Peon: To drink coffee.

Maniyappa: Is that so? See that shop over there? Go there, pay the money and drink.

Peon: I am asking you.

Maniyappa: You were asking me? Come tomorrow. All right. Everyone who comes and goes grabs at me for money. Am I a tree that grows coins? Let anyone come now—will I give even a single pie? Is money water to wash with? Everyone has begun taking Maniyappa Mudaliar for an easy mark. Let it be. My boy! I cannot understand this. Hey, Pazhani! Come here. Read this.

Pazhani: It has come from Azhagappan.

Maniyappa: What does it say—are they coming?

(Pazhani shakes his head.)

Maniyappa: What, you do not understand it? Is this what I spent money to educate you for?

Pazhani: “Azhagappan danger. Start immediate.”

Maniyappa: What is this *thaat-poot* you are saying? I cannot understand a thing. Say it in Tamil.

Pazhani: It says Azhagappan is in grave danger. They have asked us to come immediately.

Maniyappa: All right. Let us leave. Ayyo! These times are not good at all. What else is going to happen, O God!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/09.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- User-confirmed `சுடகோடி` is preserved conservatively as **Sudakodi** in the name `Sudakodi Surya Narayana Ayyar`; no external meaning or normalization is assigned.
- `Radha Kalyanam`, `katha-kalakshepam`, `marakkal`, `dharmakartha`, `kumbabhishekam`, `dharma/adharma` and Nalayini are retained where the cultural or rhetorical term itself matters.
- The source term `துத்திவண்ணம்` is not securely interpretable from the closed Tamil layer; it is carried as `thuthivannam` rather than guessed.
- The beggar's agricultural metaphor for rational inquiry and Pazhani's class/religious critique are preserved in full.
- The letter's `குடிகாரர்-குடித்தனக்காரரல்ல` wordplay is rendered as “a drunkard—not a family man.”
- Maniyappa's comic failure to understand the English telegram is preserved; source gibberish `தாட்டுப் பூட்டு` is carried as `thaat-poot` rather than normalized.
