# Scene 7

Location: Azhagappan's house.

(Friends arrive. Kandhan is present.)

(Sivaguru, Kandhan and Azhagappan. Card-playing; a drinking party.)

Azhagappan: What, brother! Today's play is no fun at all.

Sivaguru: Brother! All the kings look like queens, and all the queens like kings. What, brother! There is no interest at all.

Azhagappan: Hey, Kandha! Deal.

Sivaguru: What, brother! Ganapathi and Jambu still have not come.

Azhagappan: They will come, brother.

Jambu: Good morning, brother.

(Jambu enters.)

Azhagappan: Oh! Good morning! Hey, Kandha! Pull up a chair. Why so late, brother?

Jambu: There was a little work at home.

Sivaguru: Where is Ganapathi? I don't see him.

Jambu: He has gone to the temple, brother.

Azhagappan: What is new today? It looks like some festival that never happens on ordinary days.

Sivaguru: Oh! Isn't today the Thiruvathirai festival?

Kandhan: Yes, master! The paper says it is a first-class sight—silk saris walking about while young men's eyes perform a tandava!

Sivaguru: What is this “first-class sight,” boy?

Kandhan: That is it, master. See that holy paste running on Parama Sivan's forehead because Parvathi has come of age—ah! A first-class sight!

Sivaguru: What, Azhagappa? Your servant has risen to this level now?

Azhagappan: That? He has started reading the *Dravida Nadu* paper these days. That is why he talks like this.

(Ganapathi enters.)

Ganapathi: Good morning, Azhagappan!

Azhagappan: What, man, why so late?

Ganapathi: I went to the temple, and then my wife said she had to go to her native place. I sent her off and came.

Azhagappan: Hey, Kandha, pour some for Ayyar.

Kandhan: Swami, just have it.

Ganapathi: Kandha! Get me a cigarette.

Sivaguru: Brother! We are all in high spirits today. Sing us a song.

Azhagappan: What, brother! You ask me to sing—who is going to listen? My tone has gone bad too. When I was a little boy I acted in a school play. Kandha, what did we stage?

Kandhan: You staged a play.

Azhagappan: We staged *Nallathangal*. I acted as Lohithasan in it.

Kandhan: Master! Lohithasan in *Nallathangal*...!

Azhagappan: Hey! What do you know about how the story goes?

Jambu: Hey! Would he say it if he had not acted? Then sing, brother.

Ganapathi: If you know how to sing, sing. Otherwise shout, brother—let us have some fun.

Sivaguru: Hey, Jambu! If our Azhagappan sings, even Kittappa will have to turn into Ettappa and run away.

Azhagappan: You fellows are troubling me so much, insisting that I must sing. If I refuse, the formality between us will be spoiled. I will sing:

“I saw a train run over the bridge,
The train became the mail, O cuckoo!
The train that left the station too
Runs away at speed, O cuckoo!”

Sivaguru: There you have it—Ramalinga Swami's *Arutpa*!

Ganapathi: *Arutpa*, is it? Then sing, brother.

(Song.)

Azhagappan: What, brother! I sang in those days and you all listened and enjoyed it. Now my voice is like a donkey's.

Jambu: It is very good. You could make a record of this very song.

Ganapathi: Brother, it is time. I am leaving. Azhagappan, I am going.

Azhagappan: You're going...? Then go.

Sivaguru: Brother! I am thinking of going to the cinema today. Will you come too?

Azhagappan: My chest pain is very bad. You go by yourself.

Sivaguru: What, man? Three hundred days of the year you say you have chest pain. It is only for today. Let us go and enjoy ourselves.

Azhagappan: What picture is showing?

Sivaguru: *Thara Sasangam*.

Azhagappan: What, brother! After waiting all this time, are we going to *Thara Sasangam*? Someone is supposed to rub oil on someone—is that what we are going for?

Sivaguru: It is a mythological picture, man—a mythological picture.

Azhagappan: Then all right. We can go. Reserve one seat—first class.

Kandhan: Master! I will come too.

Azhagappan: Then reserve two seats.

Sivaguru: He is only a servant. The floor is enough for him.

Azhagappan: He is a servant—during working hours! At other times he is my dearest friend.

Sivaguru: Then I am going.

Azhagappan: Sivaguru! Reserve three seats. And send one first-class “setanpadi tax” too.

(Sivaguru leaves.)

Azhagappan: Hey, Santha! Santha! Hey, Santha! Where has she gone?

Santha: Why?

Azhagappan: Why do you keep silent, woman?

Santha: Leave that aside. Why did you call me? Tell me what you called me for.

Azhagappan: Must your face always be so sour? Must your speech always snap? Why this hatred?

Santha: Ah! Am I supposed to be delighted by the way you conduct yourself?

Azhagappan: Even if you are not delighted, you could at least refrain from hating me, could you not?

Santha: I did not create that hatred by myself.

Azhagappan: Then who did? Did some God or other enter you and make you dance?

Santha: I am not a dasi!

Azhagappan: Then what are you... a devadasi?

Santha: I am not prepared to answer all this. Tell me what you wanted.

Azhagappan: You are my slave!

Santha: To love—not to authority!

Azhagappan: You stuff yourself with rice in my house. At least for that you are my slave, aren't you?

Santha: There is another creature that wags its tail at the sight of food. Do not talk too much.

Azhagappan: Tch! Ungrateful woman!

Santha: Ah! Which kindness of yours have I forgotten? In a mansion, in a flower garden heavy with fragrance, you made me a concubine so that you could drink love. Is this the affection and tenderness you show me?

Azhagappan: Oh-ho-ho... where did you learn all this? Mansion, is it? Tower too, is it? What is all this? Fine. Looks as though your brother taught you. Come on—begin, once more.

Santha: What is the use of blaming you? I should blame the sinners who pushed me into this abyss.

Azhagappan: Oh! Go and bring my coat.

(Santha brings the coat.)

Azhagappan: Hey! Take me and leave me outside. What, woman, are you leading me outside? Are you taking me toward the backyard? Are you trying to take me there and kill me?

Santha: No. This is the street side, isn't it?

Azhagappan: Street side? Look properly. All right, do not do things like this. Lift a stone and drop it on my head while I am asleep. I am going out now. I will come back and speak to you. You have drunk liquor and are talking in drunkenness. I will be back, you bloody beggar.

(He leaves.)

Santha: I have drunk liquor and I am speaking? It was not liquor that was given to me. It was deadly poison. I rave in the stupor caused by drinking it. Rusted society! The poison you gave me is killing me while keeping me alive. I sought out my own ruin! I said that even if the sea vanished, love would not. I lost my reason. I threw earth over Ekambaram's hopes. O demon of difference that cut my life apart! Is the time of your destruction not near? Is this justice? Is this fairness? Is this righteousness?

End of scene.

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/07.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- The source's English code-switching—`brother`, `good morning`, `first class`, `wife`, `tone`, `begin/once more` and cinema/register jokes—is retained where it drives the comedy.
- `Dravida Nadu`, `Thiruvathirai`, `tandava`, `Nallathangal`, `Lohithasan`, `Arutpa` and the film title `Thara Sasangam` are retained as source-specific names/terms.
- The Tamilized source form `செடன்பாடி டேக்ஸ்` is semantically insecure in the closed Tamil scene; English therefore carries it conservatively as “setanpadi tax” rather than inventing a meaning.
- The source `பிலடிபகர்` is a Tamilized English insult in context and is rendered as “bloody beggar”; this does not alter the closed Tamil form.
- Santha's “poison” speech keeps the social indictment and the recurring love/ruin imagery explicit.
