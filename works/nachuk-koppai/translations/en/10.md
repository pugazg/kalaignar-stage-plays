# Scene 10

Location: Road.

(The lame beggar enters.)

(Sivaguru and Pazhani meet.)

Lame Beggar: What a world! There is *tiruppani* to build temples for stone and earth. But there is no *tiruppani* for a poor man like me. What a country full of folly!

(Song. He leaves.)

(A group of friends enters.)

Sivaguru: Ganapathi, where are you going?

Ganapathi: I am going to the office.

Sivaguru: Do you know what has happened?

Ganapathi: What is it?

Sivaguru: Our Azhagappan is sick, it seems. We can go and see him.

Ganapathi: I am not coming. It is time for the office.

Sivaguru: Hey, Jambu! Will you at least come?

Jambu: Get lost. The other day, when I asked for one glass, he gave me only half a glass. Go see him? I am leaving.

(They stand looking at Pazhaniyappan.)

Sivaguru: Hey, ungrateful fellows! Who are you, man? Why are you staring at me?

Pazhani: I came as a guest to Azhagappan's house. My name is Pazhaniyappan.

Sivaguru: Oh! So you are the Pazhaniyappan?

Pazhani: Yes.

Sivaguru: Yes. Has Azhagappan died? No—has he survived?

Pazhani: He will not survive.

Sivaguru: Why?

Pazhani: It is all the divine play of liquor.

Sivaguru: What! Does liquor have a divine play too?

Pazhani: Yes. Liquor! Liquor! It is that which has thrown this world into disorder!

Sivaguru: Liquor! Liquor! Ah! It is that which has thrown this world into disorder!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/10.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, web text, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- `tiruppani` is retained in the lame beggar's opening line because the scene turns on the contrast between sacred temple-work and the absence of help for a poor human being.
- `திருவிளையாடல்` is rendered as “divine play,” preserving Pazhani's irony about liquor.
- The friends' refusal to visit Azhagappan, including Jambu's half-glass complaint, remains deliberately comic and unsentimental.
