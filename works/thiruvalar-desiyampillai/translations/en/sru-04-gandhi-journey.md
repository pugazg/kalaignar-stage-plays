The two of them rise and set out to see Gandhi. Along the way there are many flower shops. Beautiful garlands have been tied and hung up. There is no one to be seen doing business in any of the shops. Desiyam Pillai asks Yama, “What a wonder! Shops with no people!”

“The people are floating about in spirit-form. Your eyes cannot see them. It seems the ministers in your realm, by taxing flowers, dissolved and melted away all their flesh and bones. That is why the spirits have now come here along with the flower shops. We received this news only just now by telegram.”

Yama tells Desiyam Pillai this as though it were the most ordinary matter. Desiyam Pillai scoops out and holds forward one hundred naya paise. Someone takes them. The coins disappear. A rose garland is placed in Desiyam Pillai's hand.

He receives it respectfully and begins walking behind Yama. The soft fragrance of jasmine, mullai and other flowers wafts “kama kama” through the air. The scent of incense fills the heart with delight. Gold-plated steps glitter brilliantly; diamonds set here and there shower light. Yama stops beside a great door inlaid with the nine gems and calls, “Nanda! Nanda!!”

A young man with a dark complexion, a sturdy body, and an attractive appearance comes and stands beside Yama.

Yama shakes his hands and embraces him.

“What, Nanda! Are you well?” Yama asks after his welfare and introduces Desiyam Pillai.

Nandan stretches out his hand to welcome Desiyam Pillai with a handshake. Desiyam Pillai pulls his hand back and stands with disgust filling his face.

“I am a man who came through a bath of fire, ayya! I am the young man who burned in fire because ordinary water could not wash away the dirt called caste! Just touch me!” Nandan cries, and embraces Desiyam Pillai. The shock makes Desiyam Pillai faint.

Then Nandan brings him water. Saying, “In danger there is no sin,” Desiyam Pillai drinks it and recovers from his faint.

After obtaining an entry permit from Nandan, Desiyam Pillai and Yama enter the prison called Heaven to see Gandhi.

Rows upon rows of lamps are burning. Sweet music spreads everywhere. Dancing women are performing here and there. A splendid pavilion overflowing with beauty enough to dazzle the eye! In its centre, a priceless throne of the nine gems! A maharaja is seated upon it. Gems flash so brightly that one might wonder whether this is the maharaja's clothing or whether he has wrapped himself in the stars of the sky. Desiyam Pillai looks closely. He realizes that the great *Poppanthar* who had been praised as a naked mendicant, embodiment of non-violence, partner of the poor, friend of the worker, and example of simple living is now sitting there in delight, clothed in gold, gems and glittering diamonds.

“Ayya! What is this spectacle?”

“This grotesque spectacle is the punishment given to me for the evils I committed in the earthly world, appa,” says Gandhi.

Desiyam Pillai moves to place the garland in his hand around Gandhi's neck.

“Stop! Stop! Do not put that garland on me!” Gandhi says, stretching out his hand to prevent him.

“Why do you stop me, ayya?” Desiyam Pillai asks in alarm.

“I told you even then: first get Congress off your neck and then come put a garland on me. Without doing that, are you trying to daze me by putting only a garland on me?” Gandhi becomes angry.

“A garland, is this? Great sinners! A garland, is this? Look properly! Have you picked up pieces of bone and come to put them on me as though they were a garland of buds? Have you taken white flowers soaked in the blood of innocents, strung them as though they were roses, and set out to hang them around my neck? Look! Yama has dressed me in royal clothes, made me live amid royal luxuries, destroyed my peace of mind, and is torturing me in this prison; you are crueler even than this Yama! Do not stand before me! Go away!”

“What sacrilege have we committed against you?” Desiyam Pillai suddenly asks.

“Must you commit still more sacrilege? Yama made me a king—you have made me a god!”

“Isn't that an honour for you?”

“It is an honour for me! But it is a means for you to fill your stomach!”

“What are you saying, Mahatma?”

“In temples, a priest acts as though he has extraordinary devotion to the idols of the gods. Even if the priest has no devotion to the god represented by those idols, he does not even have attachment to the idol itself! People imagine that the priest has enormous reverence for the idol. So they give respect to the priest too. That great sinner will cover a fired brick with a wet cloth and pretend to offer piping-hot ‘Pongal naivedyam.’ ‘Ah! What devotion this priest has!’ the devotees will stand open-mouthed. In this way the priest goes on deceiving the devotees. As long as the devotees remain deceived, it is a festival for the priest! All that remains for the god is the brick-offering! And in the town the priest wins the title of a virtuous man! There are some parties in the earthly world just like this, appa. Party leaders see their god only in a brick...... the priests live in delight...... In that category, the first-class party is Congress! The first-class priest is you! The first-class party-dupe; the god—me!”

Gandhi's eyes fill with tears. Desiyam Pillai struggles, unable to reply. Yama strokes the buffalo.

Gandhi begins speaking in a trembling, tear-choked voice.

“Was the trouble all of you took to send me here a small thing? When I was with you, I questioned the injustices you committed and tried to stop them; because you resented that, there was no one even to take care to protect me. There was the powerful army the white man had left behind. As though even that were not enough, you spent crores upon crores increasing military strength. What did all that matter? He finished the job in an instant! Here in ‘Heaven’ I am suffering indescribable agony.

“You drove me away, sinners! I hear that even the possessions dear to my desire and affection have been handed over to the robber!

“They have rebuilt the hut in which I lived as a splendid hall of darkness and hung bats inside it as festoons! They have let wealthy chameleons play all along the verandahs! They have let ‘turkeys’ dance in the courtyard of the hall and made the music of principle-less *kottaans* resound! Like horned cattle, they have grown only [unresolved glyph cluster], and one or two keep butting each other and have turned the hall into a battlefield!

“Ah, sinners! The precious walking stick on which I leaned during my Navakali journey has, I hear, been transformed into a snake like the one in the story of ‘Moses’ and is biting working people to death! They have cast such a spell upon it!

“I expected that the tiny watch that used to hang neatly from my waist would have been kept safe by you. I hear that they have handed that watch over to black-marketeers and told them to use it to tell the time!

“I used to grieve that even tiny living creatures should not die from contact with my footwear. Now, I hear, each of you wears those very sandals in turn and uses them to suppress, trample and crush thousands of people!

“You ruined all these possessions. You destroyed my home too. Let them go; let everything go. Have you at least properly protected the treasure that is my beloved child, ‘Harijan Welfare,’ which was like my nose and like my heart? Does not my dear child now look like a ‘savalai pillai’? A nose running with mucus, a [unresolved descriptive cluster] mouth, a protruding belly, bent legs and arms, breath dragging as though [unresolved descriptive cluster]—is it not in just such a state, staggering and stumbling, that they have left the jewel I brought forth destitute in the middle of the street, beside the drain, on a mound of dust! Could you not have protected at least that one treasure? I hear that, merely as an outward show of protecting it, you have appointed an ‘old ayah’ for the great man. Those old women, they say, look like ‘Poothaki,’ who came to kill Kannan. My child, they say, suffers with no one to support it!

“In this condition, have you come to put a garland on me with your dirt-stained hands?

“Go! Go! Do not stand before me!”

—Gandhi's anger crosses all bounds.

At that moment a man runs in quickly and whispers something secretly into Yama's ear.

Yama cries out, “Ah!”

“What? What?” Gandhi asks.

“Here—this Desiyam Pillai, it seems, set Swami Ranganatha on fire at Srirangam and then ran here!” Yama shouts.

“Ah! He set Ranganatha on fire? Tell me in detail! Tell me in detail!” Gandhi asks in agitation.

“Yes! Unable to bear the injustices this man committed, Ranganatha himself, it seems, caught fire and burned! They have discovered that this Desiyam Pillai was the cause of that fire accident. He has run here because it would be dangerous for him to stay there!” Saying this, Yama raises his mace toward Desiyam Pillai.

Desiyam Pillai grabs Gandhi's feet and weeps, “Ayyo! Ayya! Ayya! Save me!”

“Get up! Get up! Do not touch my feet! The bloodstain on your hand will stick to my feet!” Gandhi says, shaking off his hands and rising.

“You have incurred the Lord's anger, caused even his own body to burn, and become the supreme sinner responsible for the fire. There is no place for you even in *nagam*! There is no place for you anywhere! How can I forgive you? Yama Dharma Raja! Please take him away! I do not even like looking at the face of this man, like a devouring demon. It is a great sin to look upon the unjust ruler who roasted in fire Ranganatha himself, my chosen deity! Drag him away at once!”

—Having made this plea to Yama, Gandhi calls Nandan and quickly goes inside with him. Desiyam Pillai remains standing, looking only at Yama.

Yama says, “Mm, walk! Walk!” The buffalo gives Desiyam Pillai a butt in the back and pushes him forward.
