His wife comes and stands before him with the morning coffee.

“You look strange. Your whole face has changed! You seem as though something has frightened you?” she asks. Taking his wife's hand, he seats her on the cot, takes the coffee, and drinks it piping hot.

Revived by the hot coffee, he folds his hands before the picture of Pillaiyar hanging there and prays: “O Lord! Everything I saw must turn out to have been only a dream! Nothing must happen, my lord!” The servant girl runs in with that day's newspapers. Desiyam Pillai hurriedly separates them and looks through them.

“Ayyo!”—he cries out without realizing it.

“What? What? Has somebody died?” his wife asks eagerly.

“No! Kovai, Madurai, Virudhai—everywhere Congress has suffered a crushing defeat! A crushing defeat! Shame! Shame!!” Desiyam Pillai laments, his voice trembling.

“Good! They ought to lose properly! Your Congress deserves a thorough beating!” his wife says, overflowing with delight.

“Why, woman, are you so jealous? Why should Congress lose? Tell me, tell me!”

“Under your Congress rule, what is the price of a padi of rice? Was there ever a time when even vegetables became scarce? Under this sinners' rule, even betel leaf and areca nut, which give poor people a little cheer for half an anna, have become scarce! Sick people cannot get fruit! Even when they can, it costs three times as much! They have slapped taxes on everything!”

“Whom have we taxed, woman? If we tax traders, why should you suffer—why all this worry?”

“If you tax the traders, the wholesaler puts it on the retailer's head. And the retailer puts that tax on the heads of the people who buy from him, doesn't he? You came to govern without even knowing this; govern—”

“Can anyone govern without taxes! Is there any government in any country that rules without imposing taxes?”

“I am not saying there should be no taxes. If you cut the nine lakhs poured out every year for the Governor, and the wasteful spending in Ooty, you could reduce so much!”

“Woman, woman! When did you learn to talk like this...? Hm? Who taught you?”

“Nobody taught me! I understand it myself! As though that were not enough... once elections started happening in the city, politics itself came right up to the doorstep of our house! I used to wonder whether people like us could even see ministers. The ministers themselves came looking for our house! Would these ministers come if we invited them to a wedding function at our house? But when it is election time, they all come one after another—that is what all the women living in the huts on the next street are saying!”

“They'll talk—they'll talk! Whether they talk or not, you will! For some days now you've been acting differently!”

“Even so, go on—your sense is getting worse and worse. I hear you people go and tear down ‘Long Live Tamil!’”

“Yes! Can people paste things all over walls however they like, violating the anti-obscenity law?”

“Is ‘Long Live Tamil’ obscene?”

“That is not obscene! Writing it on walls like that is obscene! Pasting it is obscene!”

“You do not even understand what obscenity means! I say it is because you regard the very word ‘Tamil’ as obscene that you hesitate even to name this country ‘Tamil Nadu.’”

“Yes—think whatever you like. Look here: don't talk to me now and make me angry! Lakhs upon lakhs of money have been wasted, and in the end things have come to the point where we may have to sit in a corner...”

“Was it money from your own house that was spent? In every town there are mill owners, bus owners, landlords and zamindars—so many innocent souls like that. You take their money and stage an election fireworks-show!”

“When I think that the man who lost this time after spending so much money—or even anyone who watched him lose—will not dare stand in the next election, the whole future looks dark, woman!”

“Let it be! Let it be!! To dispel the darkness, the ‘Rising Sun’ will come!”

“What! Rising Sun! Oh-ho-ho! Was that DMK meeting held in this street yesterday? Is it after listening to that that you've turned upside down? I understand! I understand.”

“Only me? The whole street has turned upside down. Aren't all the votes for the Rising Sun?”

“Are you collecting votes yourself! ... Woman... if you talk like this, you had better forget me! If you want your husband to be humiliated, then vote for the Rising Sun!”

“It is right that a husband should not be humiliated! But at the same time, may my son be humiliated?”

“What? What?”

“Yes—our boy is working for the ‘Rising Sun,’ isn't he?”

“Ayyo, ayyo! Is the whole family opposing me? In the one meeting those fellows held yesterday, they must have broken up many families like this—fine, fine! I am going right now to arrange today's Congress meeting!”

“Who is coming to speak for Congress?”

“Speak? What is there to speak about! It is enough to curse those DMK boys to our hearts' content! Two speakers! Four goondas! Today I'll show them a thing or two!”

—Saying this, Desiyam Pillai rises in agitation and goes out. His wife laughs mockingly at her husband's anger.

## Translation notes

- Drafted only from the reviewed Tamil SRU; no OCR/PDF substitute, later edition, web text, general-knowledge reconstruction, or secondary English witness was used.
- The source has no speaker labels in this domestic exchange; the English preserves the paragraph-by-paragraph turn order instead of inventing labels.
- `padi` (rice measure), `anna`, Pillaiyar, Kovai, Madurai, source-form `Virudhai`, Ooty, and `Tamil Nadu` are retained where they carry period or source-specific meaning.
- The source colloquial `நம்ப` is rendered contextually as “our/us”; the translation does not normalize the Tamil archival layer.
- `தமிழ் வாழ்க!` is rendered directly as “Long Live Tamil!” and `உதய சூரியன்` as “Rising Sun,” preserving the political slogan/symbol register.
- `தி. மு. க` is rendered as `DMK`; no outside political explanation is inserted.
- `ஆலை முதலாளி / பஸ் முதலாளி / நிலப்பிரபு / ஜமீன்தார்` is translated as mill owners / bus owners / landlords / zamindars, retaining the source's social-political list.
- `இரண்டு பேச்சாளர்! நாலு குண்டர்கள்!` is kept as the abrupt “Two speakers! Four goondas!” rather than smoothed into neutral prose.
