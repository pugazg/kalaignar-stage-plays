Desiyampillai is being held under guard in the guesthouse within Yama Dharma Raja's palace.

He called it a Pleasure Pavilion; is this what is called a Pleasure Pavilion? Desiyampillai looks around in astonishment, his eyes darting in alarm. The tender heads of tiny children are piled up everywhere.

Ayyo! Is this a pleasure palace?—Desiyampillai cries out.

Yes! This is the pleasure palace! What did you think these things lying here in heaps were? These are the potatoes of our country! This is what grows around our pleasure palace!—laughing as he spoke, a figure appeared before him. That figure was “Sri Mayanam,” who holds the post of military commander in Yamaloka!

Yama Dharma's programme of events is arranged according to Mayanam's wishes. In keeping with that programme, the military commander had come there to interview Desiyampillai before Yama did.

What is this, sir! You cut off children's heads, heap them here, and call this place a Pleasure Pavilion? Desiyampillai asked, grief and anger competing in his voice.

In our country, children's heads like these are piled inside the Pleasure Pavilion itself; but in your country, it seems, children's heads can be picked up outside the Pleasure Pavilion! There is not much difference between the two!—the commander replied mockingly.

Absurd! Slander!! Who told you that? Lies! Lies!!—Desiyampillai shouted.

You deny a truth accepted by everyone who has settled here so far! Look—every one of these heads gathered in heaps was brought from your country! Many of them came from the wombs of unmarried women! Many grew in the bellies of widows! Thousands floated in rivers and ponds! Countless heads were picked up in the field of poverty! On the foreheads of some children a mark of shame has been branded. Around the necks of some, the impressions of their mothers' thumbs are deeply stamped. After studying those impressions, our court fingerprint experts have concluded that they are indeed the prints of compassionate women. We have also found that the pupils of some children are shaped just like circular Ashoka Chakras. We have entrusted research on that to one of our scientific geniuses. The result has not yet come out. What do you say now?

Hearing the military commander's angry speech, Desiyampillai stood with his head bowed.

At that moment five or six women came and stood before him, carrying bowls in their hands. Were they women? Pillai looked at them closely. Yes; they were women indeed! He came to a conclusion.

Mm! Have a blood drink!—the commander offered. Desiyampillai looked at the bowls! Ayyo! His eyes reeled—blood was brimming and overflowing from every bowl!

A blood drink?—Desiyampillai screamed.

Yes—this is Kallakudi drink! That is Thoothukudi juice! This is Salem extract! That one was squeezed at Valparai!—the commander went on, listing them one after another. Desiyampillai could no longer remain standing.

The commander laughed loudly.

Why are you so startled? All these drinks were made in your own country, were they not! Do you think they are foreign goods? No; they are goods from your own country! Have at least a few drops!—the commander insisted stubbornly.

No! This is holy water prepared to bathe Mother Earth! How can one drink it here as a beverage?—Desiyampillai answered with some anger. The commander would not relent.

Do you know what punishment is given to those who fail to accept Yamaloka's hospitality properly?

Desiyampillai stared wide-eyed!

They must be born in the earthly world as elementary-school teachers!

No—no! I will not refuse the hospitality; I accept it. Give me the bowls! Desiyampillai sat down in a slight daze.

Where is Akashvani?—the commander demanded sharply.

Here I am!—a gigantic, half-naked woman came running in. A body like one smeared with ground coal! A face like ground covered in nerunji thorns! She was Yamaloka's songbird! That Akashvani began singing in the Mukhari raga; as soon as he heard the song, Desiyampillai gained fresh energy. He gazed without blinking at Akashvani's face. She too kept her eyes fixed on him. An indescribable emotion surged up between the two of them.

O lotus of beauty! O divine maiden! O treasure impossible to find! O honey-tongued one! O sweet fruit! O clear nectar! Come closer!—he babbled in his intoxication. She too came closer. The two embraced. The military commander closed his eyes and turned the other way.

The music stopped. Desiyam Pillai floated in an intoxication of pleasure. After a long while, Akashvani, unable to bear parting, parted from him and left.

Suddenly came the blare of *tharai*, *tappattai* drums, and conches. Following it, Yama Dharma entered the guest palace.

Well, Desiyampillai! All the hospitality was properly carried out, wasn't it?—Yama asked.

Ah! It went very well. The very peak of the hospitality was Akashvani's entrance! Ah! Ah! It cannot even be described!—Desiyampillai replied enthusiastically.

Though I had arranged a long interview with you, it has become impossible to carry it out. Our interview will have to be completed in a short time!—saying this, Yama looked at the commander and ordered: Go at once and announce that it is not possible for our assembly to meet today; without knowing the explanation of “tax,” there is no use in meeting—therefore the assembly will meet only tomorrow morning.

The commander immediately performed a headstand in salute and withdrew.

## Translation notes

- This English artifact is translated only from the reviewed Tamil `SRU-02`; no OCR, PDF text, later edition, web text, or secondary English witness was used.
- `Pleasure Pavilion` renders source `உல்லாச மண்டபம்`; `pleasure palace` is retained separately where the Tamil source uses `உல்லாச மாளிகை`.
- `Sri Mayanam`, Akashvani, Mukhari, *tharai*, *tappattai*, and *nerunji* are retained by transliteration where substitution would erase source register or wordplay.
- The source-anomalous assembled phrase `வறுமை வயவில்` is rendered conservatively as “in the field of poverty”; this translation does not alter the closed Tamil reading.
- The Kallakudi / Thoothukudi / Salem / Valparai beverage sequence is kept close to the source's place-name-and-drink wordplay rather than replaced with explanatory political prose.
