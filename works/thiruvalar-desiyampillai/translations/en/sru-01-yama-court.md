# Thiruvalar

# Desiyam Pillai

Yama Dharma Raja's durbar [paper loss]. Warriors clad in garments of bone stand in formation on both [paper loss]. In skull [paper loss], festoon lamps are lit throughout the court hall, and to keep their light from dimming, human fat is poured into them from time to time and nerve-wicks are inserted. A terrifying figure raises its head, its eyes bulging, and cries in a loud voice, “Ayyo! Ayyo!!” That is the sound signal announcing the king's arrival in the assembly!

Following that sound, four buffaloes, decorated with silk cloths, come walking in. Behind them comes Yama Dharma, seated on the shoulders of two fools; he descends beside a seat made of corpses.

The assembly erupts in a single roar—the joyous welcome of “Ayyo!” The court poet, Poet Oppari, rises and offers a song of praise. Next begins the *kollisatti* dance. Vadivazhagi-Vaikkarisi, the chief royal dancer, takes part in the dance. The gleam of teeth like elephant tusks, thrust out beyond blood-reddened lips, [paper loss], and he loses his heart. Each dance[paper loss] is bound with a noose, [paper loss], rewarded, and sent away.

“The daily proceedings of the court hall are beginning,” announces Chitraputhiran.

All the chief members of the assembly speak in their respective *padai*. (Here *padai* means *bhasha*, that is, language.)

As those who have come from various parts of Yamaloka are presenting their grievances, Yama Dharma Raja begins to doze a little. Chitraputhiran, who is nearby, wakes him and wins the praise of the assembly. The king's inquiry begins.

Yama: So far I have listened to the speeches of the assembly representatives. You are supposed to be in the cremation ground; why have you come into this assembly and started taking my life?

OneRepresentative: Point of order! Mr. Speaker! The work of taking lives belongs only to the king, not to us.

Everyone looks toward the Speaker's seat. Chitraputhiran announces that the Speaker will be late to the assembly that day, and the point of order is dropped. Yama's inquiry resumes.

Yama: If anyone interrupts while I am speaking, I will give all of you life again and bring back feelings such as hunger, disease, hardship, and sorrow. I have been protecting all of you who have settled in Yamaloka, keeping you from having to suffer again in the earthly world. Shouldn't you at least obey me in return for that protection?

A Voice: Isn't being made to submit in this way dictatorship? Where is democra[paper loss] Where is democracy?

Yama rises in a fury, crunches and eats the head of one representative, and, after letting out a belch,

Who is that fellow, speaking improper words even after being warned? Are you going to behave properly? Or shall I give you life and send you back to your mother-world?

All: No, Your Majesty! We will behave properly!!

Yama: It isn't your fault. You have forgotten that you are all settlers here, while the ghosts and demons of this country voted for you in a general election, elected you, and sent you here. They are the ones to blame. Never mind, Chitraputhiran! Read this year's financial statement before the assembly and be done with it.

Chitra: I shall read it and be done with it, my king!

One Representative: (rising nervously) Your Majesty! Since copies of the financial statement have been given to us, we shall read them and understand it ourselves. Our humble request is that time not be wasted by making Chitraputhiran read it aloud.

Yama: All right! A useful suggestion! Accepted. I shall give you only a brief account of the main points in the financial statement. Last year the export of souls from our country was fairly liberal. This year, family planning has been implemented even in the Indian subcontinent, so we have not been able to export in large numbers the souls that have accumulated here. This has caused the government an indescribable loss; we must find a way to make up that loss.

One Representative: What if we simply stop bringing in more and more souls from now on?

Yama: A good question. But the member asking it is not merely a bachelor; in the earthly world he has no close relatives at all—no elder or younger sister, no elder or younger brother. Only his mother and father, and they have already come here. The other members are not like that! They keep submitting petitions, with powerful recommendations, asking us somehow to bring their own relatives here!

In this situation, what can we do to avoid the loss our realm is suffering?

A Voice: We can impose a tax?

Yama: Who said that? Impose a tax? Until now this assembly has never even heard such a word. Tax! Tax!! Tax!!! What a novel word! Who uttered it? Stand up, let us see you!

(A large figure wearing khadi clothes and a khadi cap rises and stands.)

Yama: Who are you? How did you get into this assembly?

Khadi Figure: My name is Desiyampillai! I had come as a spectator and was sitting upstairs! When you asked for a way to make the country's economy prosper, I became carried away by emotion and jumped down here; forgive me.

Yama: You have undermined the dignity of the assembly. And that is not all; without my order—without obtaining an entry permit for our country—you have trespassed into my realm! Perhaps you came here in an illicit boat! I am not going to let you off easily. You must explain the meaning of the word “tax.”

Desiyam Pillai: My lord! You must not consider me an ordinary man—if your vehicle is a buffalo, mine is a bullock vehicle; the two of us are almost alike! And that is not all; I was born in 1885! For all these years I have tried so many times to come here, see you, and leave; I could never manage it. I would even set a “kedu”—today, tomorrow—but somehow, at the very last moment, the journey would suddenly be cancelled. It is my good fortune that I have received your darshan. Sir! I heard that my younger father, Mahatma Gandhi, came here; where is he now?

Yama: Oh-ho, are you related to Gandhi as a son? Because that Gandhi spoke insultingly of us, I have locked him up in the utterly subterranean prison called “Heaven.” You cannot see him now! And now I am going to arrest you too and keep you, as a government protective detainee—with no shortage of honours—in my palace guesthouse. Our interview is tomorrow. Then we shall discuss the word “tax.” After that, the punishment due to you will be given.

Desiyam Pillai: Punishment?

Yama: Yes—our court will inquire into the matter, and punishment will be given according to the opinion of the majority.

Desiyam Pillai: Oh-ho, so there is democratic rule here too?

Yama: Almost exactly like your country.

In your country the Speaker has to arrive at the appointed time. In our realm, he may come if he likes—or if he does not, it does not matter. Apart from that, it is all the same kind of democracy! There is only one thing we have not learned from you: “tax.” That alone!

Desiyam Pillai: I shall explain that tomorrow too.

Yama: Glad to hear it! Guards! Arrest this living creature and place him under protection in our Pleasure Pavilion!

Desiyam Pillai: Pleasure Pavilion? Then is it Udhagai Mandalam?

Yama: Almost the same! In your Udhagai Mandalam a cold breeze blows! Here a fragrant corpse-breeze blows! Experience it and see! You will understand the difference between the two—hey! Take him away! I will see you tomorrow!

(Desiyam Pillai is taken away. The Speaker arrives. The assembly is adjourned until the next morning.)

## Translation notes

- The translation follows only the reviewed Tamil SRU and retains all **7** `[paper loss]` markers; no missing wording is reconstructed.
- `Thiruvalar` is retained as the source title/honorific rather than normalized to an English honorific.
- Source speaker-label variation is kept visible: `OneRepresentative:` reflects source `ஒருபிரதிநிதி:` while `One Representative:` reflects the later spaced form `ஒரு பிரதிநிதி:`; `Chitra:` reflects source `சித்ரா:`.
- Underworld/funerary names and wordplay such as *Oppari*, *kollisatti*, and Vadivazhagi-Vaikkarisi are transliterated rather than silently replaced with explanatory prose.
- The source explicitly glosses *padai* through *bhasha*; both terms are retained so the source's wordplay remains traceable.
- `kedu` and `darshan` are retained where a single smooth English substitute would erase the source's register or joke.
- `Udhagai Mandalam` is retained in the Pleasure Pavilion exchange because the source's sound/wordplay depends on the Tamil place-name form.
