The interview between Yama and Desiyampillai begins.

Yama : What is your father's name?

Desiyam Pillai : Don't you know? The famous English lord Hume is my father!

Yama : You are an Englishman's son? Then how did you get the name Desiyam Pillai?

Desi : Mahatma Gandhi took me in and brought me up. He gave me the name Desiyam Pillai!

Yama : In your country, what is a *keeripillai*?

Desi : It is an animal! An enemy of snakes!

Yama : You too are a *pillai*, aren't you; whose enemy are you?

Desi : Hee hee hee!

Yama : There is also a *thennampillai* in your realm, isn't there?

Desi : Yes; that is the wish-fulfilling tree of the earthly world!

Yama : One *pillai* is an animal; another *pillai* is a tree! All right, never mind—what is an *anilpillai*?

Desi : That? That is the creature that built a bridge for Rama.

Yama : Oh! In your country even a squirrel builds bridges! Doesn't the government build them?

Desi : Hee hee hee!

Yama : Never mind! What does *kilippillai* mean?

Desi : That? It is a bird that repeats whatever it is told!

Yama : What does it say—“All right, let's see! All right, let's see!”—isn't that it?

Desi : Hee hee hee!

Yama : You call yourself Desiyam Pillai; in what way are you higher than these four *pillais*?

Desi : I belong to *uyarthinai*; they are *ahrinai* creatures! Mere living beings! Creatures without rational sense!

Yama : What is rational sense?

Desi : A difficult question! You too are asking me the same question those Kazhagam people ask me in the earthly world—what a nuisance that even death has not freed me from it!

Yama : Dead? Who said that? Don't lie! You have come to this country only as a spectator. No one has recognized you as a citizen of this country! Only after looking into your conduct and antecedents can the matter of making you a citizen of this country be brought before the assembly! Never mind that; first explain tax to me!

Desi : In our realm, “tax” means Congress!

Yama : What does Congress mean?

Desi : It means sacrifice!

Yama : What does sacrifice mean?

Desi : Office, permit, licence—there are many meanings like that!

Yama : Is “tax” black in colour? Red in colour? What is its true colour?

Desi : Does tax have one colour? Two colours? Doesn't it have *pancha-varnam*—five colours?

Yama : But *pancham* means famine, they say?

Desi : Yes—that is the very colour of tax too!

Yama : All right—what good does tax do?

Desi : Population growth will fall; crowding in the country will thin out, and things will be nice and open!

Yama : If we introduced such a tax in Yamaloka too......?

Desi : There would be a way to reduce congestion here too?

Yama : In your realm, who are the experts at imposing taxes?

Desi : I am, of course! Haven't I myself come here now?

Yama : On what things in particular can a tax be imposed?

Desi : Taxing things that decay is the best thing to do!

Yama : What things decay in our country? (thinking) Oh! Corpses! Corpses! We can tax them!

Desi : You say you will tax the decaying things of your country—the corpses; won't that provoke any opposition?

Yama : Opposition? Does imposing a tax even produce opposition? Do you know what punishment awaits those who oppose things in Yama Dharma's realm?

Desi : What punishment?

Yama : They will be expelled immediately; and it does not end there. The punishment is this: they must be born again in the earthly world. Not only that; they must stand as candidates in elections, pay money to obtain votes, and somehow save themselves from defeat!

Desi : All right; in our country some people are elected without a contest. Are all of them people receiving their punishment?

Yama : They have already paid the offering they were required to pay—right here!

Desi : Are the candidates you mention Congress candidates? Or from some other party? Or independents?

Yama : Why, man, are you talking about independents here? Those independents have no *kudi*, no *soranai*, no gratitude, no loyalty—nothing at all!

Desi : Ayyo, ayyo.........are all independents like that? No! No! Perhaps... one or two... if they were like Kumbakonam...

Yama : Never mind. After the corpse tax, what tax can we impose? Tell me that idea too!

Desi : I can count on my fingers only the things that have not been taxed in our country. It is very difficult to recall and recite the list of taxes that have been imposed!

Yama : Then what are the things that have not been taxed?

Desi : Fig flowers! Horses' horns! Roosters' eggs! Things like these have been exempted from tax in our country.

Yama : Sabash! I shall tax those here as well, Desiyam Pillai! Thank you very much! I am delighted that today's interview has proved useful to Yamaloka's development plans. Ah, I forgot to ask you one thing. I hear that in your realm there is a great magician called the “Five-Year Plan.” Is he really such a clever fellow? There is a general opinion here that he ought to be brought over—would he be a worthy person to bring here?

Desi : Hasn't he come here yet? I heard he had arrived even before me...!

Yama : No! No! I thought I should ask you first and then bring him.

Desi : He is certainly a great magician! His name alone sounds frightening, but if you look at the man, he will be wasted thin like a *devangu*! He also suffers from elephant-hunger!

He never eats anything by the thousands or the hundreds. Everything he stuffs into his stomach is counted only in crores. Since he is the court magician in our realm, the contagious disease called corruption spreads everywhere; apart from that, there is no benefit at all. I strongly recommend that you kindly bring him here!

Yama : A man afflicted with the disease of corruption? Good heavens, how did I not know this until now! Rather than reduce congestion through taxes, we could reduce crowding by a considerable amount through the disease of corruption...! Excellent! Excellent! I shall send someone for him tomorrow itself!

Desi : Do send someone! Even if he comes, there are children lined up in his family to look after things back home. There are plenty of second, third, and so on—little Five-Year-Plan magicians!

Yama : Let today's interview come to a pleasant end—if you have any needs, you may tell me.

Desi : My needs are nothing extraordinary. I must be released from this Pleasure Pavilion. Next, I must be given an opportunity to meet Mahatma Gandhi!

Yama : Must you absolutely see Gandhi? Isn't he lying in the subterranean prison called Heaven? It has become impossible for you, a first-class prisoner of Nagaloka, to meet that third-class prisoner!

Desi : Somehow, you must show me grace—only if I see the great Gandhi will my longing be satisfied!

Yama : All right; I shall arrange it!—Come away with me.

Desi : A garland, right...we can buy one...

## Translation notes

- Drafted and reviewed only against the complete reviewed Tamil SRU; no OCR, PDF substitute, later edition, web text, or secondary English witness was used.
- The repeated *pillai* sequence is central wordplay. `keeripillai`, `thennampillai`, `anilpillai`, and `kilippillai` are therefore transliterated in the relevant setup lines rather than flattened into unrelated English nouns; where the source immediately supplies the referent, the English follows it.
- *uyarthinai* / *ahrinai* are retained because the joke depends on Tamil grammatical classification; the surrounding English supplies the source's rational/non-rational contrast without replacing the terms.
- *pancha-varnam* / *pancham* is retained to preserve the sound-based five-colours/famine joke.
- `kudi` / `soranai` are retained in the independent-candidate satire because the paired source terms do not have a safely exact one-word English equivalent here; `gratitude` and `loyalty` are translated directly where the source is clear.
- The Kumbakonam reference is retained without explanatory reconstruction because the SRU itself gives no further gloss.
- The proverbial impossible objects—fig flowers, horses' horns, and roosters' eggs—are translated literally so the tax-exemption joke remains visible.
- The Five-Year Plan is translated as a personified “great magician” throughout. *devangu* is retained as the source comparison rather than replaced with an uncertain animal identification.
- The assembled Tamil contains the source-supported but syntactically unusual continuation `ஆளைப் பார்த்தே மானால்`; the English renders only the transparent dramatic sense of that clause and does not use it to revise the Tamil archival layer.
- `crores` is retained as the source scale term.
- `Nagaloka`, `Yamaloka`, and `Heaven` preserve the source's mythological/political register.
- The source's closing `மாலைதானே...வாங்கிக் கொள்ளலாம்...` is rendered conservatively as “A garland, right...we can buy one...” and remains the final line of this SRU; the following travel prose belongs to SRU-04.
