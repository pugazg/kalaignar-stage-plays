# Scene 20

Location: Kaveri's house.

(Kannan sits with his head bowed; Kaveri Amma is speaking.)

Kaveri:— Kanna! Listen to what I say! Today's Congress has become like a black cobra taking up residence in a termite mound built by ants... It is no longer right for you to remain there... Think about it... A man inside a burning house should run outside and help put out the fire—not stay inside and play with the flames...

Kan.:— Amma! A burning house... a mound entered by a black cobra... today's Congress...! I do not deny it... But would there be a son who runs away from the mother who bore him merely because she lies on her deathbed?...

Kave.:— Take your own example a little further... Would they keep a mother's corpse inside the house just because it is their mother's body and let it rot?... They say a son's duty is to perform at the proper time the things that must be done... Kanna! Do that duty!...

Kan.:— Amma!

Kave.:— Do not forget the cruelty that turned your father into a corpse, Kanna! Do not forget the worthless men who wave the white fly-whisk for Hindi, Kanna!

Kan.:— The people who killed Appa are cruel men—fanatics of repression—I accept that. But... I do not like opposing the good idea that Hindi should be India's common language, its single official language, Amma; I do not like it!...

Kaveri:— If Hindi comes, Tamil will be ruined; the life of Tamils will be ruined.

Kan.:— How will it be ruined, Amma?... That's just somebody's wild shouting!...

Kave.:— Not wild shouting!... A warning given for the future...

Kan.:— I cannot accept it! Tell me how Tamil will be ruined—how the life of Tamils will be ruined—if Hindi comes!

Kaveri:— Kanna!... I know that if salt is mixed into milk, the milk curdles and spoils... I do not know all the research into exactly how it spoils or why it spoils... Does the truth that salt will spoil milk disappear merely because I do not know that research?

Kan.:— Even if it would be ruined that way... can't we prevent it while remaining in the Congress? We should remain inside the fortress of sacrifice built by the freedom fighters and labour to protect the people's language and remove hunger; leaving it is not right!

Kave.:— Kanna! You are no different from the fisherman who, even after being warned that a storm is coming and the sea is going to rage and that the houses must be evacuated at once, still hesitates to leave his house!...

Kan.:— Amma! Don't confuse me!... I don't understand anything!...

Kave.:— I do not have the full power to make you understand either... Kanna! To get the explanation that will open your eyes, read Anna's journal *Kanchi*... read the Kazhagam's *Nam Naadu*!...

Kan.:— Ayyo! A Congressman touching a Kazhagam newspaper with his hand? Sin! Sin!...

Kaveri:— Forget the newspaper. At least listen to their speeches!...

Kan.:— Ha! These are Congress ears; they won't listen to Kazhagam speeches...

Kave.:— Don't Congressmen listen to Kazhagam speeches in the Legislative Assembly?...

Kan.:— They have no other way; they listen!.... But can they get up and run outside because of that?

(He rises.)

Kave.:— Kanna! Where are you going?...

Kan.:— I'll be back, Amma?...

(He leaves.)

(Kaveri goes to her husband's portrait.)

Kave.:— It seems even the blood you shed has not turned your son onto the right path...

(Curtain)
