# Scene 21

Location: Thiraviyam Pillai's house.

(Maragatham is singing a Kazhagam song... Dasavatharam and Kannan enter. They listen to the song.)

Marag.:— Who is it...

Dasa.:— Maragatham... This is Kannan, Kannan the hero of duty... Kannan the *karmaveerar*, the cannon of the Congress Mahasabha! A propaganda cannon! Kanna... this is Maragagam, the boss's daughter... daughter straight against father!... DMK... The first vote you have to canvass is this one. Keep talking... Amma... look after him... I'll go... Ayya will be at the mill; I'll bring him right now...

Kan.:— Come back quickly...

Dasa.:— Oh... I'll be right back...

(He leaves.)

Marag.:— Sit down...

Kan.:— It's all right...

Marag.:— Will you eat something?...

Kan.:— No...

Marag.:— Instead of sitting idle until Appa comes, why don't you read something?...

Kan.:— It's all right...

(Maragatham brings him a book titled *Nanbargal Ketpatharku* — “For Friends to Ask” — and gives it to him.)

Kan.:— What book?... *Nanbargal Ketpatharku*, written by Annadurai... Ah!... This book?...

(He flings it away—and jumps up.)

Marag.:— Why?... Why?... Was there a scorpion or something in it? In the book?... Did it sting you? Shall I put medicine on it?...

Kan.:— This DMK mischief of yours will never go away!... There was no scorpion or anything...

Marag.:— Then why did you throw the book down... and jump up?

Kan.:— I never read DMK books...

Marag.:— All right... have you read the *Tirukkural*?... the *Tirukkural* written by Tiruvalluvar?...

Kan.:— Why, was he some DMK leader?... I've read plenty of the *Tirukkural*... why do you ask?

Marag.:— Have you read the kural, “Whatever the matter, from whomever one hears it, wisdom is to discern the truth in that matter”?

Kan.:— I have...

Marag.:— What does it mean?...

Kan.:— Whatever idea one hears, whoever may say it, wisdom is to examine and know the truth in that idea!...

Marag.:— Then isn't it an argument against Valluvar's own kural to speak stubbornly saying, “If Anna says it, I won't listen... if he writes it, I won't read it”? 

Kan.:— All right... what exactly is written in it? Tell me, let's see...

Marag.:— I'll tell you...

(She takes the book—and reads with dignity.)

DMK is the embodiment of labour! DMK is a camp built with the blood shed by many martyrs! DMK is a battle camp opposing Congress authoritarianism. DMK is a party that received the votes of thirty-four lakh people. DMK is the single largest opposition party in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly. DMK is a democratic organization maintaining contact with the people day after day. DMK has become an established forum of virtue that thinkers say can grow into the ruling party in the coming election. If DMK is destroyed, a great calamity will strike the very system of democracy. Only if DMK continues to work can the language be protected, prosperity be gained and a new life be found... This book of awakening says all this...

Kan.:— It says all about you! Isn't there anything about us?...

Marag.:— There is plenty about you... Here... read it—

(She gives the book into his hand... turns the pages and shows him.)

“Congress rule is proceeding in an authoritarian manner... it has acquired the confidence that however it behaves, there is no one to question it... You read it yourself...

(He continues reading.)

(Reading.)

Kan.:— Congress rule uses social-welfare-plan facilities that were created for the general benefit of the people for party propaganda itself—Congress rule has introduced procedures under which only Congress party people can easily obtain facilities such as loans, grants, ploughs, bullocks and fertilizer meant to help farmers...

Congress rule acts with a party outlook even in granting permission to start industries...

Congress rule has turned even the pure institution called the cooperative sector into chess pieces for the party. Congress rule, under the pretext of expanding industrial prosperity, has strengthened the capitalist system.

Congress rule, even after laws were enacted to abolish the landlord system, has granted many kinds of exemptions and made it possible for those wanted by the Congress party to continue as lords.

Congress rule, as though capitalists and lords were not enough, has introduced licence and permit systems in trade, made many people suddenly rich, and turned them into those who feed and nurture the party.

Congress rule sees but pretends not to see large-scale evasion of the income tax that major capitalists ought to pay, and thereby becomes an accomplice to those capitalists' fraud.

Congress rule knows that black-marketing and profiteering exist, yet instead of devising ways to abolish those evils it uses even them for the interests of its party.

Congress rule has poured out crores upon crores of money and still has not been able to reach an adequate level of food production.

Congress rule inflicts cruelty by imposing indirect taxes that affect poor and middle-class people far more heavily than the direct taxes imposed on the wealthy.

Congress rule, when farmers ask for rights—when workers ask for higher wages—when ordinary employees ask for their grievances to be removed—pounces on them and suppresses them, standing as a supporting hand to the capitalist—

Congress rule causes suffering to poor and middle-class people.

Kan.:— (Reading) It refuses to undertake bold efforts to remove the rise in prices.

Congress rule, by giving foreign capitalists room to establish themselves and grow here, is creating a great future danger—

Congress rule, for the sake of growing the party, admits into the party those who cannot accept socialism and thereby ruins democracy.

Within the Congress party that has accepted responsibility for governing, the lineage of sacrifice has greatly diminished; people without principles and selfish exploiters have become Congressmen, seized the party, and through it have been given room to turn the administration into their puppet...

(With anguish.)

Kan.:— True—true—one hundred percent true.....

(He reads further.)

Congress rule is trying to establish Hindi imperialism and belittle speakers of other languages.... Maragatham! All the rest is right.... It is only on this language policy that I differ...

Marag.:— What?

Kan.:— Shouldn't India have one national language?

Marag.:— Why one; there are fourteen languages, aren't there?...

Kan.:— Shouldn't there be a common language—a link language—to connect people who speak all the languages?

Marag.:— Isn't English enough as a common language that connects not merely people in India but people throughout the world? Do we need one path for the cat and another path for its kitten! Can't both go by the same path?

Kan.:— If one has to go and work in North India... won't Hindi be needed as support?

Marag.:— Hindi is not spoken throughout North India. If you go to Bengal, Bengali... if you go to Punjab, Punjabi... if you go to Orissa, Oriya... if you go to Maharashtra, Marathi; for contact with those states—and for contact with foreign countries—English is enough... Hindi is not additionally necessary...

Kan.:— Isn't English a foreign language?

Marag.:— In a country with many cultures—customs—languages... to the speaker of one language, another language is foreign... who would want one to dominate another?.... That is why we say all sixteen national languages should become official languages—and until then English should continue—

Kan.:— Whatever it may be.... a foreign country's language....

Marag.:— Strange.... Foreign food! Foreign loans! Foreign industrial assistance! Foreign experts! Foreign capital! Foreigners intervening in the devaluation of the rupee! Shame does not arise in any of these... does it arise only when we say that English, the world's common language, should continue!...

Kan.:— But Kamarasar himself has said Tamil will not be ruined by Hindi...

Marag.:— The Tamil he speaks will not be ruined! Because it has already been ruined as much as it can be ruined... I am speaking of the Tamil of *Silappathikaram*—the Tamil of *Chinthamani*—the Tamil of *Tirukkural*—the Tamil of *Manimekalai*—*Akam*—*Puram*—*Kalithogai*, *Kalingathu Parani*—this Tamil will be ruined—Tamils will become third-class people, slaves to the people of Uttar Pradesh whose mother tongue is Hindi... It was to prevent this evil that people died by self-immolation... many were shot dead... the lives of tender children were taken... student jewels floated like flowers in pools of blood. Why; what caused your beloved mother's flowers, forehead mark and sacred *mangalyam* to be lost? Hindi! Because of Hindi!! Because of that *mandhi* that leapt into Tamil Nadu! *Mandhi!!* Hindi! It is a red fire that scorches and chars Tamil life! A great fire that comes to destroy Tamil!... It is the duty of the Tamil to put it out!... To remain idle even now... is folly! Folly!!

Kan.:— Maragatham! Enough... enough... You have opened my eyes... Maragatham!

Marag.:— Truly?...

Kan.:— Yes... the slackening that entered my public life ends today... From this moment, with a heart reborn—and with new enthusiasm—I will work for the growth of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam... Maragatham! You are the sister who showed the path for my life... My mother Kaveri has two children... I am the elder—you are the younger.

Marag.:— Amma will be very happy when she hears this... Well, well... Appa and Dasavatharam are coming... Anna! I'll tell you a secret... act accordingly...

(She says something in his ear and leaves.)

Dasava.:— What, Kannan... It's become very late, forgive me...

Kan.:— It's all right...

Thiraviy.:— Kanna!... Dasavatharam told me everything... very happy... Next week, the Congress Democratic Socialist Conference... I'm the one presiding... Dasavatharam is the... inaugurator... you are the chief speaker... That is like the flag-hoisting for our election work... you must look after all the work... Is the money enough... do you need more?...

Kan.:— I'll take more later...

Thiravi.:— Fine... I'm standing in the election trusting you... the conference... must be conducted successfully...

Kan.:— It will be, you'll see... from that day there'll be a great turning point in the election!

Thiravi.:— Let it happen—let it happen, let the turning point happen—Hey, Dasavatharam... look after Kannan... let the food be ready... Maragatham! Maragatham!!

(He goes inside.)

Dasava.:— What, Kannan? Did you change Maragatham's mind?...

Kan.:— A mind has changed!

(Picking up the flowers scattered there, in surprise.)

Dasava.:— Why, man... what's this... flowers scattered everywhere...

Kan.:— Your suspicious mind will never leave you... Learn the difference between elder sister, younger sister, mother and daughter, sir! Don't talk whatever comes into your mouth! I'm leaving...

(He leaves.)

Dasava.:— Oh, father! Conference... conference...

(Curtain)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/21.md`; no OCR, MD first-pass, PDF or secondary English witness was used for drafting.
- The source's irregular `மரககம்` in Dasavatharam's introduction is represented as `Maragagam` rather than silently normalized to `Maragatham`; the character is otherwise named Maragatham where the locked scene says so.
- The irregular phrase around DMK's continuing public contact is rendered at its transparent semantic level as “maintaining contact with the people day after day”; the Tamil archival wording itself remains unchanged.
- The long quoted critique of Congress rule is translated as a sequence of accusations rather than summarized, preserving the source's repetition of `காங்கிரசாட்சி` and its escalating political rhetoric.
- The source says fourteen languages in one exchange and later sixteen national languages; both numbers are preserved exactly as source claims and are not externally reconciled.
- `சமதர்மம்` is rendered as `socialism` in this political context; `கழகம்` is retained as `Kazhagam` where the dialogue uses it generically and `Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam` where the full party name is explicit.
- The `இந்தி / மந்தி` rhyme is preserved by retaining *mandhi* (“monkey”) in the climactic line rather than inventing an English rhyme.
- Literary names such as *Silappathikaram*, *Chinthamani*, *Tirukkural*, *Manimekalai*, *Akam*, *Puram*, *Kalithogai* and *Kalingathu Parani* follow the forms supported by the Tamil scene; no outside textual commentary is added.
- `அண்ணையிலிருந்து` in the election-turning-point line is rendered conservatively as “from that day”; the locked Tamil layer is not corrected.
