# Scene 16

Location: The Pleasure Pavilion

(Everywhere in the Pleasure Pavilion, the heads of little children lie heaped in piles.)

Thir.:— He called it a Pleasure Pavilion! Is this what that name means?

(Looking at the heads.)

Ayyo! What is this—the heads of children! Is this a pleasure palace?

(The Commander enters.)

Commander:— Yes! This is the pleasure palace! What did you think these children's heads lying in heaps were? These are our wild potatoes! This is what grows around our Pleasure Pavilion.

Thir.:— Ayyo! Yes... who are you?

Commander:— My name is Srimayanam. Commander of the army of Yamaloka. The programmes are drawn up according to me. In keeping with the programme so arranged, I have come to interview you before Yamadharma Raja does.

Thir.:— Is that so? What is this, sir? You heap up children's heads and call the place a Pleasure Pavilion!

Commander:— Inside our country's Pleasure Pavilion, children's heads are piled up like this. But in your country children's heads can be picked up outside the Pleasure Pavilion. There isn't much difference between the two!

Thir.:— Absurd! Slander! Who said that? Lie! Lie! All lies!

Commander:— You deny a truth accepted by everyone who has settled here so far. Look—every one of these heads gathered into heaps was brought from your country. Many of them arose in the wombs of unmarried women. Many grew in the bellies of widows; thousands floated in rivers and ponds. Countless heads were gathered from the fields of poverty. On the foreheads of some children a mark of shame is stamped. On the necks of some, the impressions of their mothers' thumbs are deeply pressed. On examining those impressions, our court fingerprint experts have concluded that they are the prints of compassionate, merciful women. We also found that the pupils of some children's eyes were formed like round Ashoka Chakras. We have entrusted research on that to one of our scientific savants. The result has not yet come out. What do you say now?

Thir.:— What is there to say?

(He lowers his head. At that moment a messenger brings a bowl.)

Commander:— Mm! Drink the blood beverage!

Thir.:— Ayyo! What is this?

Commander:— Blood overflowing and bubbling;

Thir.:— A blood drink? No! No!

Commander:— Do you know the punishment for those who fail to accept properly the hospitality offered in Yamaloka?

Thir.:— What punishment?

Commander:— They must be born on earth as primary-school teachers!

Thir.:— Ayyo! No! No! I will accept the hospitality without refusing it. Give me the bowls!

(Thiraviyam Pillai drinks and becomes intoxicated.)

Commander:— Akashvani!

(An unattractive woman enters.)

Akaa.:— Usur!

Commander:— (To Thiraviyam Pillai) She is Yamaloka's cuckoo of music! Akashvani! Look after him!

Akaa.:— Achcha!

(Thiraviyam Pillai looks at Akashvani. Love is born between Pillai and Vani. The two sing and dance.)

Thir.:— Aha! Beautiful maiden! Enchantress! Treasure impossible to find! Honey-tongued one! Sweet fruit! Clear nectar!

(He embraces Akashvani.)

Commander:— Ayyo!

(He leaves in disgust.)

(Yama enters.)

Yama:— Well, Thiraviyam Pillai! All the hospitality went properly, did it not!

Thir.:— Aha! It went very well. The very summit of the hospitality was Akashvani's entrance! Aha! It cannot even be described!

Yama:— Though I had arranged a long interview with you, it has become impossible to carry it out. Our interview will have to finish in a short time.

Yama:— You—(looking at the Commander)—go at once and announce that it is not convenient for our Assembly to meet today; because there is no use in the Assembly meeting without understanding the explanation of tax. So announce that the Assembly will meet tomorrow morning!

(The Commander leaves.)

Yama:— Shall we begin our interview?

Thir.:— We may begin!

Yama:— How did you come to have the name Thiraviyam Pillai?

Thir.:— I have plenty of money, that's why—

Yama:— Mm! In your country, who is *keeripillai*?

Thir.:— It is an animal! An enemy of the snake!

Yama:— You too are a Pillai, aren't you? Whose enemy are you?

Thir.:— )4( الم الم اهم

Yama:— There is also something called *thennampillai* in your kingdom, isn't there?

Thir.:— Yes; that is the wish-giving tree on earth!

Yama:— What! One Pillai—an animal! One Pillai—a tree! Never mind, who is *anilpillai*?

Thir.:— That? That is the *ainthu* that built a bridge for Rama!

Yama:— Oh! In your country even a squirrel builds bridges? Doesn't the government build them?

Thir.:— U! U! U!

Yama:— Never mind! What is *kilippillai*?

Thir.:— That? It is a bird that repeats whatever it has been told.

Yama:— What does it say! “Let us see, let us see—let us see, let us see,” is that it?

Thir.:— اهم اهم اهم

Yama:— You call yourself *Desiyam Pillai*; in what way are you superior to these four Pillais?

Thir.:— I am *uyarthinai*. Those are *ahrinai* creatures. Mere lives. *Ainthus* without rationality.

Yama:— What is rationality?

Thir.:— A difficult question! Those Kazhagam people on earth asked me the same question, and now you ask it too. Even here this trouble won't leave me!

Yama:— Explain tax!

Thir.:— In our kingdom, “tax” means Congress!

Yama:— What does Congress mean?

Thir.:— Sacrifice.

Yama:— What does sacrifice mean?

Thir.:— Office! Permit! Licence! It has many meanings like that.

Yama:— Is tax black in colour or red? What is its true colour?

Thir.:— Does tax have one colour or two? Isn't it *pancha-varnam*—five-coloured?

Yama:— But doesn't *panjam* mean poverty?

Thir.:— Yes, that is the colour of tax too!

Yama:— Fine! What benefit comes from tax?

Thir.:— Population growth will fall. The crowding of people will lessen and things will be more open.

Yama:— If such a tax were brought into force in Yamaloka too!

Thir.:— A way would arise to reduce crowding here as well!

Yama:— Who are the experts at imposing taxes in your kingdom?

Thir.:— Me! I'm the one! And now I've come here!

Yama:— Specifically, on what things can tax be imposed?

Thir.:— It is especially good to impose tax on things that decay!

Yama:— What things decay in our country!

(Thinking.)

Corpses! Corpses! We can tax them!

Thir.:— You say you will tax the corpses, the decaying things of your country; won't that create any opposition?

Yama:— Opposition? Can imposing a tax create opposition too? Do you know the punishment for opposition under the rule of Yamadharman?

Thir.:— What punishment?

Yama:— They will be expelled immediately. And the punishment does not end there. They must be born again on earth. Not only that; they must stand as candidates in elections, pay money to buy votes, and preserve their honour! Never mind that. What tax can be imposed after the corpse tax? Tell me that idea.

Thir.:— In our country I can count on my fingers only the taxes that have not been imposed. It is difficult to remember and recite the list of taxes that have been imposed.

Yama:— What are the things that have not been taxed?

Thir.:— Fig flowers—horses' horns—roosters' eggs—and such things have been granted tax exemption in our country.

Yama:— Sabash! I will tax those as well! I forgot to ask one thing. I hear there is a great magician in your kingdom called the Five-Year Plan. Is he very clever? There is a general opinion going around that he should be brought here. Is he a person worthy of being brought?

Thir.:— Has he still not come here? I heard that he had arrived here before me.

Yama:— No... no! I thought I would ask you before having him brought.

The.:— He is certainly a great magician. Only the sound of his name is frightening; if you look at the man, he will be thin like a *devangu*. And he has an elephant's hunger! He does not eat in the thousands or the hundreds! Everything he stuffs into his stomach is in crores! Because he is the court magician in our kingdom, nothing useful comes of it except that the contagious disease called corruption spreads. I strongly recommend that you kindly bring him here.

Yama:— Let our interview end pleasantly here for today! If you need anything, you may say so!

Thir.:— The need is nothing extraordinary! I must be released from this Pleasure Pavilion. Next, I must be given an opportunity to meet Mahatma Gandhi!

Yama:— Must you necessarily see Gandhi? Is he not lying in the underground prison called heaven? It has become impossible for you, a third-class prisoner of hell, to meet that first-class prisoner!

Thir.:— Somehow you must show mercy! I have not seen Gandhi the Great there. Only if I see that great man will my longing end!

Yama:— Very well, I will arrange it! Depart.

Thir.:— I will need a garland, won't I?

Yama:— A garland, is it? You can buy one.

(The two leave.)

(Curtain)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/16.md`; no OCR, MD first-pass, PDF or secondary English witness was used for drafting.
- The verified anomalous tokens `)4( الم الم اهم`, `U! U! U!`, and `اهم اهم اهم` are retained exactly. They are not interpreted or normalized.
- The source's Pillai-wordplay is preserved by retaining `keeripillai`, `thennampillai`, `anilpillai`, and `kilippillai` in transliteration. The locked source form `தேசீயம்பிள்ளை` is represented as `Desiyam Pillai` rather than silently changed to the character's established name.
- The locked `ஐந்து` / `ஐந்துகள்` forms in the squirrel and grammatical exchanges are retained as *ainthu* / *ainthus* rather than repaired by context.
- `உயர்திணை` / `அக்றிணை` are retained as *uyarthinai* / *ahrinai* so the source's grammatical/rationality argument is not flattened into a different conceptual scheme.
- The `பஞ்ச வர்ணம்` / `பஞ்சம்` sound-play is preserved through *pancha-varnam* / *panjam* rather than replaced with an invented English pun.
- The source changes the speaker label once to `தே:` during the Five-Year Plan discussion. The English preserves that anomaly as `The.:—` rather than silently assigning the line to a normalized label.
- `தேவாங்கு` is retained as *devangu* because the locked Tamil scene supplies the comparison without a supported English identification.
