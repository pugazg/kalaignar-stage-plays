# Scene 1

[A student forum is in session. The hall is filled with male and female students. The student Maragatham is speaking.]

Maragatham: A Tamil multitude like a flood; a multitude armed with valour—one in heart, though seen as many in body. When will the day come when the world trembles, realizing that none can approach them through deceit? The day we find an answer to the revolutionary poet's question... This day, when a war-parani rings out: “Even if my own mother stops me, I will not spare the one who scorned Tamil!”... This day, when the drum proclaims: “Our shoulders that sport in the death-field of savage cruelty are shoulders of victory!”... The magnificent day when young Tamils—rising Tamils—have set out with the stride of a charging bull, declaring, “Tamil is called nectar; sweet Tamil is equal to our life!” The day the students' honour-brigade rose—the day the womenfolk seethed—the day the warrior people roared in valour. On this day, let us remember the martyrs who gave their lives to protect Tamil. Let us turn toward the direction where their graves lie and offer a warrior's salute.

(All the students rise and stand in silence, then sit down.)

Maragatham: Today, the day of those great martyrs... here in this forum named for Maanava Mani Sarangapani... the Maanava Mani Rajendran Memorial Arts Association has arranged a one-act play. The play is called *Vanchi-yum Kanchi-yum*! Written by Murasoli Maran, M.A.! Suppose Cheran Senguttuvan, who died many centuries ago, were suddenly to rise from his grave and see the Tamil Nadu of today... What would he think... what would happen? That is the idea of the play... And now it begins: *Vanchi-yum Kanchi-yum*!...

(Curtain)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/01.md`; no OCR, MD first-pass, PDF or secondary English witness was used for drafting.
- The source's martial rhetorical register, repeated “this day” cadence and quoted political-poetic imagery are retained rather than reduced to prose summary.
- `பரணி` is retained as `parani`, the source's explicitly martial poetic term.
- `மாணவ மணி` is retained as the source honorific `Maanava Mani` in the two memorial names rather than silently replacing it with an inferred biographical label.
- The embedded play title `வஞ்சியும் காஞ்சியும்` is transliterated as *Vanchi-yum Kanchi-yum*.
