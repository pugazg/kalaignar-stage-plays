# Scene 24.

## Conference:

(Thiraviyam Pillai, Dasavatharam, Kannan and the assembled crowd.)

Dasa.:— Great people! I now stand before you to inaugurate our Congress Democratic Socialist Conference. When it is a Congress conference, people generally do not come in large numbers... Now we have made all sorts of other arrangements to gather people... Just because people have not come in large numbers, can one say the Congress has no strength—no respect? People read all the news about the Congress in the newspapers and learn it... that is why they do not come to hear speeches. Even the crude way we speak here, the newspaperman prints properly, with clean grammar... One might ask, are there only a few literate people in the country... shouldn't the rest, the illiterate people, come? The illiterate ask the literate and learn... this is socialism. Now I will “cut” this socialism... no... no... I will inaugurate the Socialist Conference... and sit in my seat... Next... one who has long been... labouring in the Congress... a partner of the poor... a guide to socialism... our valiant Thiraviyam Pillai will preside over the conference and deliver the main address...

(Thiraviyam Pillai rises amid demonstrations and comes forward. A garland is placed on him.)

Thiraviyam:— Great people! Brothers and sisters! The main need for democratic socialism... is a plan... a Five-Year Plan. A plan... without it, people like us cannot live... Five is a special number for India itself! Five elements—five senses! Five Pandavas—Nehru's five principles! Most special of all... on *saathanudaiya* hands and feet there are five fingers...

Dasa.:— Even human intelligence is five, isn't it, sir...

Thir.:— Hey... that's four, man... Therefore, great people! The plan laid down for our country too is a Five-Year Plan... Some people say this Five-Year Plan knocked only at the rich man's door... and never knocked at the poor man's door... What door does a poor man's house have; only if there were one could it be knocked on!... Only if the rich man lives can the poor man live... I'll give an example... only when a river has plenty of water does water come into the canal... the rich man is like the river... the poor man is like the canal... Capitalists and landlords need not be frightened on hearing the word socialism. This conference is precisely to explain it and remove that fear... In this...

Thir.:— More clearly than I can... our Kannan is going to speak. He... from now on, leaving aside all that intense socialism... is going to join us and work for a Congress victory... Our leader Kamarasar himself has said that trustee-socialism is enough... Rich people... that is, people like us... should behave like trustees... You know how trustees behave... We may say we'll abolish the rich... but can we?... Begging, “Rich people! Please give up a little,” is Congress socialism; the socialism suited to us!... Otherwise would great men such as Pattakkararu—Chetti Naattaru—Pollachchiyaru—Vada Pathimangalaththaru—Vaandaiyaru—Vageesanaru—Mooppanaru—Udaiyaru—such enormous crorepatis, lakhpatis, bus owners, mill kings and landlords all remain in the Congress?

Whatever may be the case, the Congress is the only party that works for the poor...

Even now we have cancelled land tax up to one crore rupees for five or six big companies...

Someone will ask, why not cancel land tax for farmers?... Is the tax on the man? It is on the land, isn't it? Is it fair for a man to ask that the tax on the land be cancelled?...

If someone says prices have gone up... what does that mean? The people's purchasing power has increased! Rice isn't available! One big uproar! How can it be available? In the country, every year one crore more children are born...

Dasa.:— In the same way there's a count of people dying too, isn't there!

Thir.:— Why are you saying that now, man?

Dasa.:— There's a rice expense for that too... *vaaikkarisi*.

Thiravi.:— Oh... is that why you said it? Never mind. Then look... nationalize the bus industry—nationalize the banks—nationalize the textile mills... Only then, they say, is it socialism... can it be done? Even now, in the bus industry run by our government there are losses in lakhs. Why, just one dairy farm at Madhavaram! Forty lakhs loss there... what nationalization do you want... Generally... for our election... if we chant “socialism, socialism” like a mantra—what is it? Black? Red? Green? Blue?... Congress socialism, you see, is a Parijatha flower!... No one was able to pluck it... the one who struggled greatly and plucked it and brought it was Kamarasar! Look here... I'll show you that Parijatha flower...

[A man enters carrying a large flower on a silver plate.]

Thir.:— Did you see... the Parijatha flower! The flower of Congress socialism!... I do not have the talent to describe it... Kannan will now speak about this Parijatha flower whose fragrance fills the whole country...

Kan.:— (Taking the flower and speaking) Aha!... O Parijatha flower!... O Parijatha flower that came to shower mercy upon the great men of wealth in the Congress! O Parijatha flower that took the blood of the poor and painted itself red!... O Parijatha flower that pushes labourers—farmers—weavers—those who work day after day shedding tears of blood—down to the lowest level, while making the rich still richer! O Parijatha flower that lets mill kings—lords of wealth—moneylender monarchs—little Kuberas float in a city of pleasure!

Thiravi.:— (Stunned) Hey, hey... what are you saying!...

Kan.:— O Parijatha flower that made S. K. Patil speak the locked source's claim about Marx, workers and capitalists, and say, “Therefore I have no faith in socialism”! O Parijatha flower that created an adulterated socialism by saying Congress socialism will contain both a public sector and a private sector! O Parijatha flower that blew into the wind even the resolution once passed at the Bhubaneswar Conference to nationalize the rice mills! O Parijatha flower that has elected fourteen maharajas on behalf of the Congress in *paararoomanram*! Like a fool begging a tiger, “Do not swallow the *vellarattai*—let it go,” O Parijatha flower that has created a condition in which they shamelessly fall at the feet of the rich and plead, “Rich people! Give something up for the poor!”... You are a Parijatha flower only in glitter! A Parijatha flower only to the eye!... In truth... you keep alive the man who plunders the whole town and throws it into his furnace; you deceive the worker and snatch his vote—you are a paper flower! A paper flower!! To lakhs upon lakhs of farmers, workers, poor and middle-class people, the socialism the Congress shows is nothing but a paper flower. A paper flower has no fragrance; Congress socialism will not be attained! Understand, friends! Understand.

Come away from the spider's web called Congress! I have come out. I have come out of the tent of these masquerading socialists. You too come away... the true socialist camp... is the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam functioning under the leadership of Anna, who radiates the light of knowledge... Come, come!

Paper flower!...

Others:— Has no fragrance!

Kan.:— Congress socialism—

Others:— Has no sweetness!

(The crowd disperses; Thiraviyam and Dasavatharam embrace each other and cry.)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/24.md`; no OCR, MD first-pass, PDF or secondary English witness was used for drafting.
- The entire conference sequence is translated from the closed Tamil artifact, including Dasavatharam's opening press/literacy joke, Thiraviyam's Five-Year-Plan speech, the rice/funeral-rice exchange, the Parijatha prop, Kannan's reversal speech and the final call-and-response.
- The locked form `சாதனுடைய` in the five-fingers passage is semantically insecure and is therefore retained as *saathanudaiya* rather than silently expanded to a guessed phrase.
- The long sequence `பட்டக்காரரு—செட்டி நாட்டாரு—பொள்ளாச்சியாரு—வட பாதிமங்கலத்தாரு—வாண்டையாரு—வகீசனாரு—மூப்பனாரு—உடையாரு` is transliterated rather than externally identified or normalized.
- `வாய்க்கரிசி` is retained as *vaaikkarisi* so the source's death/rice joke is not replaced with an invented English equivalent.
- `பாரிஜாதப்பூ` is rendered as `Parijatha flower`; the climactic contrast with `காகிதப்பூ` is rendered as `paper flower`, preserving the title metaphor and the slogan `காகிதப்பூ மணக்காது`.
- The locked Marx/S. K. Patil sentence is syntactically insecure. The translation explicitly marks it as the source's claim instead of supplying a cleaner quotation not supported by the Tamil artifact.
- The irregular `பாராரூமன் றத்தில்` is retained as *paararoomanram* rather than silently normalized to a modern institutional spelling.
- The source-sensitive `வெள்ளரட்டை` in the tiger image is retained as *vellarattai* rather than guessed from context.
- `சமதர்மம்` is rendered as `socialism` in the Congress/DMK ideological passages, matching the immediate political context without changing the locked Tamil wording.
