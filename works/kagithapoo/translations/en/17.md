# Scene 17

Location: Heaven

(Thiraviyam Pillai goes to place a garland on Mahatma Gandhi.)

Gandhi:— Stop! Stop! Don't put the garland on me!

(He prevents him.)

Thir.:— Why, revered sir, do you stop me?

Gandhi:— I said even then: dissolve the Congress and come garland me. Without doing that, are you trying to dazzle me by putting a garland on me alone? Is this a garland? Look carefully. Have you gathered bone-dust and come to place it on me as though it were a garland of buds? Have you taken white flowers soaked in the blood of innocents, strung them as though they were roses, and set out to hang them around my neck? Look at Yama here—he keeps me in royal clothes and royal comforts, destroys my peace of mind, and tortures me in this prison. You are crueller than this Yama. Do not stand before me. Go away!

Thir.:— What offence have we committed against you to deserve this?

Gandhi:— Was the labour all of you put in to send me here such a small thing? When I was with you, I questioned all the wrongs you committed. I tried to stop them; and out of resentment over that, there was no one even to take care to protect me. There was the strong army the white man left behind. As though even that were not enough, you spent crores upon crores and increased military strength. What use was any of it? Someone finished the matter in a snap! Here in heaven I am struggling in unspeakable suffering. You hounded me away, sinners! I hear that even the possessions dear to my desire and affection have been handed over to looters! I hear that the hut in which I lived has been rebuilt as a splendid dark hall and that bats have been hung inside that hall like festoons! I hear that wealthy chameleons have been allowed to play all along the verandah! I hear that *vaan kozhigal* have been made to dance in the courtyard while the music of principleless *kottaans* resounds. I hear that only *kombu maazhgal* have been fattened up, made to butt one another, and turned the hall into a battlefield!

Thir.:— Revered sir!

Gandhi:— You sinners! I hear that the precious walking stick I leaned on during my Navakali journey has, like the one in the story of Moses, turned into a snake and now bites working people to death! I expected that the little watch hanging neatly from my waist would have been kept safe by you. I hear that you handed that watch over to black-marketeers and told them to watch the time and loot accordingly. I used to yearn that not even the tiniest living creature should die from contact with my sandals. Now I hear that each of you wears those sandals in turn and suppresses—tramples—crushes—turns thousands of people into mud!

(Thiraviyam Pillai is silent.)

Gandhi:— Let all that go. Have you at least properly protected the treasure that is my beloved child, as dear as my breath and my heart—“Harijan Welfare”? Does not my cherished child look like a wasted child? A nose running with mucus! A mouth shedding sores! A distended belly. Shrunken arms and legs; dragging breath like that of seventy-year-old elders. In just such a state, staggering and stumbling, have you not abandoned the jewel I begot helpless by the roadside, beside the drain, on a mound of dust? And in this condition you have come to place a garland on me with your filthy hands? Go! Go! Do not stand before me!

(Thiraviyam Pillai falls down.)

(Curtain)
