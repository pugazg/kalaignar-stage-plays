# Scene 14

Location: Thiraviyam Pillai's house

(At night, he stands facing a picture of Kali and performs puja... after showering flowers on it...)

Thiraviyam:— Parameswari! Parvati! Lokakali! Parasakthi!... Mahakali! Mahakali!! Ambike!... Bestow your grace... In the 1967 election I must win and become a minister—become a minister... The opposition must not get even a single seat in the Legislative Assembly... Every seat must come to our party... Maheswari!... Make me a minister, mother! Make me a minister!...

(From behind the picture.)

Voice of Kali:— Devotee!

Thiravi.:— (In astonishment) Devi!

Voice of Kali:— Your wish will not come true!

Thiraviyam:— Ah!...

Voice of Kali:— You will lose! The people will defeat you!

Thiravi.:— Kali!

Voice of Kali:— For appearing like Kali and shooting down everyone who came within sight—for turning the country into a famine-wilderness where gruel tanks had to be set up—the people are going to answer you... the people are going to answer you...

Thiravi.:— (Crying) Ayyo... Mother, after keeping you in my house and worshipping you all these days... is this the reward?...

(Maragatham comes from behind Kali without Thiraviyam Pillai seeing her.)

Marag.:— (In her natural voice) Appa! Appa! Why are you talking all by yourself?

Thiravi.:— Gone, everything's gone, dear!... All the castles I built in my mind about becoming a minister have turned into mud forts. Kali won't grant the boon, dear! She won't grant it!

Marag.:— At least now do you understand, appa, that even the gods aren't on the Congress side! If you compete with Kali herself and suck blood, won't Kali get angry?

Thiravi.:— I have opposition even inside my own house!... Maragatham! Don't you have even a little desire to become a minister's daughter?

Marag.:— What is a minister, appa... five years... and after that no one even knows the address!... Is office such a great thing?... Appa... what work did we do for the people; what service did we render for the language—that is the account the future will ask. Whether someone was a minister or a maharaja... no one will care about all that...

Thiravi.:— We Congressmen don't worry about the future now... As long as we have it, what do we want? Minister? Bus magnate? Mill ruler? Industrial king? Landlord? Some title—some office like that—we need it! That's our goal!... Even this Mahakali cannot stop it...

Marag.:— Appa!... From now on the Congress can only go downhill... no one can stop that!...

Thiravi.:— No, Maragatham, no!... Just as prices rise, the fame of the Congress will keep rising higher and higher. However much the influence of black money rises, the influence of the Congress will rise just as much! Just as we piled up taxes, the people are going to pile up victories for the Congress!... Do you know why?... We have lakhs and lakhs of rupees for election expenses...

Marag.:— Appa!... For the suffering you have inflicted for eighteen years, the people are going to take all that money as a fine and drive your party from the seat of power... remove it... change it root and branch......

Thiravi.:— Maragatham!

Marag.:— Don't strain yourself. Rest and sleep, appa!

(She leaves.)

Thiravi.:— (Looking at Kali) Maakali... Mahakali... Mahakali... change your decision, mother... I'm going to sleep... at least come in my dream and change the boon, mother!... Ambike! Won't you change your decision?... We've amended even the Constitution nineteen times... can't you change your decision just once?...

(He goes to sleep.)

[The dream begins.]

(Curtain)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/14.md`; no OCR, MD first-pass, PDF or secondary English witness was used for drafting.
- The `மனக்கோட்டை` / `மண் கோட்டை` contrast is rendered as “castles in my mind” / “mud forts” to preserve the source's verbal turn.
- The source's list `பஸ் முதலாளிகளரி ஆலை அரசரரி? தொழில் மன்னரா? நிலப் பிரபுவரி?` contains irregular locked forms. The transparent occupational senses are rendered in English, but the Tamil archival layer is not corrected or normalized.
- The satire linking price rises, black money, taxes and Congress electoral victories is kept in the same escalating sequence.
- The source explicitly says the Constitution had been changed nineteen times; the translation preserves that source claim without external verification.
