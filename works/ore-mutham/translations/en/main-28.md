# Scene 28

Location: The gallows.

(Soldiers hold back the people, increasing their agitation. There is uproar and shouting. The three prisoners are on the gallows.)

Puthan: Be calm. If I must die, let me at least see your calmness as I die.

A Man in the Crowd: Young Maharaja! Must we watch this injustice?

Puthan: Do not. Go away. We who will die in a little while will die this very moment from this turmoil. Your anger cannot save our lives. Fulfil our ideal through your sympathy: a great life without discrimination; a beautiful life in which the condition of rich man and poor man is abolished; a life of equality; a life of peace. We laid the foundation for this. We are about to perish. Here is one called untouchable, one called caste-degraded; she and I will give up our lives together. At least after seeing our corpses, let caste feeling die. In the future, over our graves, let caste feeling die. Over our graves, let lovers of reconciliation who drove caste away sing songs of peace.

(A complete silence.)

Execution Official: Prince! Is there anything you wish to say at the end?

Puthan: What......? To whom shall I say it? I have already said what I had to say to the people of my country. O gallows! You have kissed the corpses of so many traitors condemned to death before justice. Today you will become a soft bed for the corpses of these innocents too! Conspirators—murderers—traitors to the crown—you have strangled them all. Today you will embrace us too. You have fed your terrible hunger on terrible criminals. Now you are going to taste us, who set out to end the turmoil in this country. Taste us! Taste and see whether there is a feeling of treachery in our blood! Taste and see whether conspiracy has pooled in our hearts! Taste and see whether thoughts of intrigue cling to our skeletons!

Execution Official: (To Yaaliththathar) Do you have anything to say?

Yaaliththathar: I have the pride of dying with the Prince; the satisfaction of dying according to the Maharaja's verdict; the consolation that my life takes leave before the Tamils of my country. Sir, one request before death. I must die before the Prince. I cannot watch the life of that noble man struggle on the gallows. I cannot watch the death-rope tighten around the neck that should wear the garland of victory. I cannot watch the golden body that should sit upon the throne fall as a corpse on this earth. I will die first.

Ponni: I will die first. It was I who brought the Prince to the gallows! Had there been no woman like me, this ruin would not have come upon this country. My love has become the Prince's sacrificial altar. I alone was born in this country—yes, born to destroy this very country. I will die first.

A Voice: “No one needs to die.”

(Everyone turns at the voice. Kumari enters. Mahaveerar and Vibhishanan enter from the opposite side.)

Kumari: Stop. Do not touch them.

Mahaveerar: Why should it stop? Kumari! Do not die uselessly too!

Vibhishanan: The leader of the revolt, Maharaja! This too is a form of treason to the crown.

Kumari: Treason! You tore away the Maharaja's unwavering love for his own son, crowned the man born to rule with the title of traitor, put him on the gallows and dared to murder the very justice of Inbapuri. That is pure royal loyalty—and what I do is treason. Is that it?

Vibhishanan: Kumari! No one called you here to shout at an execution ground. No one invited you to teach the Great King a justice unknown to him.

Kumari: No one needs to invite me. This is the duty I owe the royal family! The work I owe the country! The sacrifice I owe the Prince.

Vibhishanan: Say it is an exploit you owe your lust; that would fit better. (To the King) Maharaja! Every moment we delay is dangerous. Kumari has come with some prior arrangement. If we delay, revolt will break out. Leave immediately for the palace.

Mahaveerar: (To the executioners) Hmm. Proceed.

Kumari: Sire! Wait a little. I will prove that the Prince is innocent.

Mahaveerar: Ponni's love has proved he is base, and the letters found through Vibhishanan have proved he is a traitor.

Kumari: Even a father's affection for his son has been forgotten so quickly.

Mahaveerar: I do not depart from justice.

Kumari: But you act in haste.

Vibhishanan: Hmm. Proceed.

(Chithra enters with the cry, “Stop!”)

Vibhishanan: Chithra! You came here?

Chithra: To betray my husband—and do good to the country!

Vibhishanan: Chithra!

Chithra: Do not call me affectionately. I am your enemy. If these conspiracies had remained within the bounds of your selfish interest, Chithra would not have come against you. But if the Crown Prince becomes a sacrifice to your terrible design and I allow it, would that not be a sin against the country?

Mahaveerar: What? What?

Vibhishanan: Nothing, Maharaja! Some magic. They have bewitched even my wife.

Chithra: No one has bewitched me. I am speaking with a clear mind. The Prince is innocent!

Mahaveerar: How?

Chithra: The conspiracy letters my husband wrote to seize Inbapuri. (She hands them to him.) The Prince once seized these letters and later lost them. These are those very letters.

(Mahaveerar takes and reads them.)

Vibhishanan: Sire! Some illusion is taking place. Do not believe it.

Mahaveerar: (Finishing the letters) Believe? Vibhishanan, do not be afraid. I will not stray from justice. I am going to give you a reward worthy of the help you have rendered.

Vibhishanan: (Delighted) A reward is not important to me. The country is what matters.

Mahaveerar: Do not say that. This is not a reward I give. It is a reward given by the law of this country.

Vibhishanan: The law?

Mahaveerar: I am going to give you the Prince's place.

Vibhishanan: (Grinning) The Prince's place?

Mahaveerar: Yes. This place. (He points to the gallows. Vibhishanan is stunned.)

Mahaveerar: Do not be stunned! Read this letter from Madhavan, guard of Manikkak Kottai: “Madhavan will not sleep until the king is slain and the jewelled crown is placed on Vibhishanan's head.” And here is the map prepared for the invasion. Vibhishanan! Fine efforts.

Vibhishanan: These are forged letters, Maharaja!

Chithra: No. The false letters are the ones you prepared after luring the Prince, Yaaliththathar and Ponni into your trap, tying them to pillars and frightening each of them by threatening to kill another!

Vibhishanan: What magic is all this?

Mahaveerar: Traitor! You writhed like a snake and tried to ruin my dynasty. Tomorrow you and your associates Madhavan, Ellappan and the others will stand trial. Puthan! Forgive me.

(He embraces him.)

Puthan: The people will forgive you.

People: Long live the Prince! Long live the Maharaja!

Mahaveerar: Chithra! As long as Inbapuri exists, it will not forget the sacrifice you made for the country.

Vibhishanan: Sacrifice! No—desire for the Prince. The conspiracy of a chandala woman is called sacrifice! The scheme of a witch who betrayed her own beloved for the man she desired is called sacrifice!

(Tears fill Chithra's eyes.)

Mahaveerar: Hmm. Put this Vibhishanan in chains!

(Vibhishanan snatches a dagger from the guards who come to chain him. Before it can strike the Prince, Kumari leaps between them and takes it in her chest; the guards seize and restrain Vibhishanan.)

Mahaveerar: Sacrifice this avenger at once.

(Vibhishanan is placed on the gallows. Ponni and Chithra gather Kumari into their arms.)

Mahaveerar: Where is the palace physician?

(The physician emerges from the crowd.)

Physician: Here I am, sir.

Mahaveerar: Physician! Kumari must live. She needs urgent treatment. Take her to the palace.

(Kumari is carried away. The crowd disperses.)
