# Scene 12

Location: By the river.

(It is evening. Kumari is singing. Chithra arrives in a palanquin. The two meet.)

Chithra: Lightning!

Kumari: Madam! Where are you going at this hour?

Chithra: My mother's house is in the next village. She has called me, so I set out. It became dark on the way.

Kumari: There are many tigers, madam!

Chithra: Yes. What can I do?

Kumari: Madam! You can go in the morning.

Chithra: I was thinking the same. But where can I stay?

Kumari: Our [Source-held: one source word] is close by, madam. You can stay there. Perhaps you may not like a poor hut.

Chithra: If I worry about such things, will anything get done?

Kumari: Good thing I saw you. Otherwise you would have had to suffer along the forest path.

Chithra: Yes. It is my good fortune that I saw you.

Kumari: God himself shows the way in such things, madam. That day, when my heart was churning like the sea, you saved me. I will not forget the help you gave me.

Chithra: What you are doing now is the greater help. Do you know how much I would have had to wander if I had not seen you?

Kumari: All right, come, madam. That is our hut.

[Source-held: after the palanquin departs, the source has a transition into the hut interior; the exact wording of the stage direction remains unresolved.]

Kumari: This blessed woman is the one who saved me.

Puthan: So you must be Master Vibhishanan's wife. Greetings.

[Source-held: Puthan performs a source-visible action toward Chithra; the exact wording remains unresolved.]

Kumari: He is my *machan* by relation.

[Puthan's face changes.]

Chithra: One can understand just by looking!

(While giving fruits and other food.)

Kumari: Food from a poor household.

Chithra: I did not come for the food. Your affection is enough.

(After she has eaten, as Kumari spreads a bed for Chithra inside.)

Kumari: It is like a peacock going and marrying an ostrich—you went and married him, madam!

Chithra: What could I do? God wrote it that way on my head.

(Chithra lies down. Outside are Puthan and the warriors.)

Warrior 1: I somehow feel suspicious.

(Kumari comes.)

Kumari: There is no need for suspicion.

Puthan: It is wrong to give fire a place in a gunpowder store.

Kumari: You call her pure fire. (Chithra rises from the bed and listens.)

Puthan: Water and fire are both enemies of gunpowder.

Warrior 1: Whatever else she may be, isn't she Vibhishanan's wife?

Kumari: Vibhishanan is a pool of poison. Chithra is a spring of love.

(Inside.)

Chithra: (To herself) Can I throw a stone into her trust? Poor thing, a spotless jasmine flower.

Vibhishanan's Voice and Shadow:

“Yes. You saved the flower,
You betrayed the bridegroom.”

Chithra: No. I will not betray my beloved.

(Outside.)

Puthan: Hmm. A snake's wife is a snake.

Kumari: If she were, she would not have saved me. Believe me, Prince!

(Inside.)

Chithra: How can I deceive this unstained heart? I cannot. My love! Forgive me.

Vibhishanan's Voice and Shadow:

“Forgive you? Tomorrow there will be a case
for conspiring to seize the crown;
death is the sentence for that.”

Chithra: A terrifying sentence! My love! What life is there for Chithra without you?

(Outside.)

Kumari: Chithra has the heart of a child. She does not even know what deceit is. You must trust her.

(Inside.)

Chithra: A child's heart! Mine or hers? To begin plotting against a woman who knows no deceit?

(She searches and searches, opens the box, takes out the letters, then puts them back.)

Vibhishanan's Voice and Shadow:

“If you want to see me alive, you must recover the letters.”

[She takes the letters and hides them, places blank rolls of paper in the box, and closes it as before. She lies on the bed pretending to sleep. Source-held: the remainder of this long parenthetical stage direction remains unresolved at the exact-word level.]
