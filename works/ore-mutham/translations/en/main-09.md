# Scene 9

Location: Vibhishanan's room.

(Kumari stands there. Vibhishanan reclines on a bed.)

Vibhishanan: What did you say your name was?

Kumari: Lightning.

Vibhishanan: Fitting, fitting! Lightning in the gleam of your eyes, lightning along the curve of your cheek, lightning in every movement of your waist. Butterfly of many colours! There are no words to describe you.

(He gives her wine.)

Kumari: Drink this. Words will come tumbling out without your even knowing it.

Vibhishanan: Yes. Why have you still not married?

Kumari: I haven't married, sir.

Vibhishanan: Why?

Kumari: A vow stands in the way.

Vibhishanan: A vow! What trouble is that?

Kumari: If I marry, I will marry a king. Otherwise I will remain a maiden.

Vibhishanan: Then your vow has come true.

Kumari: Really?

Vibhishanan: Truly, truly!

Kumari: (Pouring him more wine) Hmm. Think carefully before you say that.

Vibhishanan: Today I am Prime Minister to Imaya, Royal Representative of Mullaikadu! Next month I will be Emperor of the Inbapuri Empire.

Kumari: That's a fine..... thunderclap! You becoming a king—you're playing with me.

Vibhishanan: Playing! Would I play with the future Queen of Inbapuri? Listen. Maharaja Mahaveerar's rule has faded. The crown of Inbapuri will henceforth belong to Vibhishanan's line.

Kumari: Even if it is a dream, it sounds beautiful!

Vibhishanan: You will not believe it until you are seated beside Emperor Vibhishanan on an equal throne! Until then you will not believe me.

Kumari: Can one believe a frog if it says it is going to Nagaloka? (She gives him more wine.)

Vibhishanan: Vibhishanan is no frog, girl; Garuda, Garuda! He will go to Nagaloka and kill the serpent king himself. (Coming closer) Lightning! Come!

Kumari: (Moving away) Is Mahaveerar some gullible fool? Can anyone answer his army? Can you even get inside the fort? Huh-uh!

Vibhishanan: You still do not believe me?

Kumari: Huh-uh! You're telling lies. You're trying to ruin my vow.

Vibhishanan: What I say is a lie! Fine—are these letters lying too? Open your eyes and look!

[He takes them out and shows her.]

This is a letter from Madhavan, commander of Manikkak Kottai. A great warrior who has joined me against Inbapuri. Look here—this is a letter from Singanagan, the petty ruler of Chithirakottai. Do you know what that lion has written? He is training ten thousand soldiers; the time and route for entering the Inbapuri fort are all ready!

Kumari: Inside the fort? Huh-uh.

Vibhishanan: Fool, fool! Here—this mouse called Ellappan has sent this from inside the fort itself. Still doubtful? If we go by this route, the fort will be ground to dust! Ha ha haa!

Kumari: Then I really am fortunate.

Vibhishanan: (Coming closer) Queen of Inbapuri—Lightning! Lightning!

Kumari: What if your Imaya ends up becoming king?

Vibhishanan: Impossible, impossible. Imaya is my puppet. I am using him as a tool to get the job done. After my victory he is a curry leaf to be thrown away. I have stirred up his hunger for a kingdom; the fool keeps licking his lips! Is he fit for kingship? A frog. Lightning! The very day I become king, that very moment Imaya's head will roll!

Kumari: You are a great genius. (She gives him wine.)

Vibhishanan: Otherwise, could I bring down a king? (He moves closer.)

Kumari: All right! Fulfil my vow. Fix the wedding day tomorrow itself.

Vibhishanan: Day and star can wait, Lightning! Must this night go to waste?

Kumari: Ayyo! Before marriage, according to the custom of our caste, it is a sin to touch a man.

Vibhishanan: You need not touch me; I will touch you.

(He advances in frenzy. Kumari circles around as though dancing, pouring him wine again and again. Reeling in lust, Vibhishanan forcibly embraces her. Kumari cries out. Chithra runs in. At that moment Vibhishanan scoops up and drinks another cup of wine. Chithra takes Kumari's upper garment and puts it on, then dims the lamp a little. Meanwhile Kumari flies away, taking the conspiracy letters with her. Mistaking Chithra for Kumari—)

Vibhishanan: Foolish girl! Still shy?

Chithra: Go on; I feel shy.

Vibhishanan: Ha ha haa! Lightning! You are lightning, I am thunder. Come! Let us pour down a rain of love. Won't you come? The Maharaja of Inbapuri calls you.

Chithra: Maharaja! You're only trying to deceive me, aren't you?

Vibhishanan: Deceive you?

Chithra: Yes. When you already have a wife......

Vibhishanan: (Laughing) A wife? Me? Foolishness. I am a bachelor!

Chithra: Hmm. A lie.

Vibhishanan: I swear by God! You are the first beautiful woman I ever saw, the first I ever loved. You alone are my wife. You alone are the Queen of Inbapuri.

(He embraces Chithra.)

Chithra: You won't abandon me, will you?

Vibhishanan: I would give my life for you.

Chithra: As proof of that, something...?

Vibhishanan: Here. Wear this ring as a token of love.

(Chithra puts out the lamp.)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/main-09.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- `மின்னல்` is translated as `Lightning` because the alias drives the repeated lightning/thunder wordplay in the scene.
- `நாகலோகம்`, `கருடன்` and the serpent-king image are retained as `Nagaloka`, `Garuda` and `serpent king` without adding mythological exposition.
- Imaya as a disposable `கருவேப்பிலை` is translated literally as a curry leaf to be thrown away after use, preserving the source metaphor.
- The locked Tamil line `இல்லாவிட்டால் மன்னனைக் முடியுமா?` is syntactically compressed; `Otherwise, could I bring down a king?` preserves its immediate dramatic sense without changing the Tamil artifact.
- The sexual coercion, disguise substitution and conspiracy-letter theft are translated directly without euphemizing or expanding the source stage direction.
