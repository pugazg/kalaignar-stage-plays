# Scene 20

Location: Malayan's house.

(Ponni looks at the ring and sings, melting in thoughts of Puthan. Malayan, who has been reading some manuscript, notices her.)

Malayan: Girl! Where did this ring come from?

Ponni: He gave it to me, Father.

Malayan: Who gave it? What are you babbling?

Ponni: The man who saved me that day—he did.

Malayan: You have gone too far, Ponni. Do not invite danger!

Ponni: What are you saying, Father?

Malayan: What am I saying! Even after telling you again and again like one teaches a pet parrot, you have decided to ruin yourself.

Ponni: He will not let my life be ruined, Father!

Malayan: Hmm. Who can change what is written on your head? Some man who came along the road somewhere—“he,” “him,” “he won't abandon me”!

Ponni: He isn't someone who merely came along the road. Do you know who he is?

Malayan: Whoever he is, will your caste and his caste match?

Ponni: Our hearts have matched.

Malayan: That is how he will boast at first! Then he will disappear and no one will know where he went.

Ponni: He will not break his word.

Malayan: Even Harishchandra—!

Ponni: He is a prince, Father!

Malayan: A prince? Ah! Ponni! You are ruined! Falsehood was born along with royal families.

Ponni: Do not say that. He has sworn upon the kingdom.

Malayan: Kingdom or oath—it is all acting, child, acting! Who is this man who swore upon the kingdom? Government itself is bound by shastra and custom.

In the shastra, one must not marry a woman who has lost caste [Source-held: final word/ending after `செய்யக்கூடா` on scan 77 remains unresolved]. Hmm...... what is going to happen!

(Two of Vibhishanan's soldiers enter.)

Soldier: You are Malayan, aren't you?

Malayan: Yes.

Soldier: The officer of this village?

Malayan: Yes. Why?

Soldier: You and your daughter Ponni must come immediately to Vibhishanan's palace.

Malayan: Why?

Soldier: Vibhishanan's order.

[The soldiers move away.]

Malayan: I don't understand anything... hmm. Come, Ponni!

[Source-held: a dense bold transition direction beginning `திரையில் ஒரு இருள் ...` on scan 77 leads into Kumari and Puthan appearing, with Puthan looking around; exact wording remains unresolved.]

Kumari: Whom are you looking for?

Puthan: No one. We can rest.

[Source-held: the final bold parenthetical on scan 77 describes Kumari and Puthan in different/simple dress; its exact full wording remains unresolved.]

Kumari: What a rough, rugged path! Prince! Don't poets give some comparison for a forest path?

Puthan: Yes. The path of life!

Kumari: I have heard a poet use that comparison—one by a foreign poet.

Puthan: A comparison for a forest path! Hmm... they call it the path of love.

Kumari: Is that also this difficult?

Puthan: More difficult than this.

Kumari: What if one rides the horse called determination?

Puthan: Flooded forest streams will break their banks and rush across.

Kumari: What if one climbs onto the raft called duty?

Puthan: The great wind called difference will blow.

Kumari: What if one is ready even to sacrifice life?

Puthan: Failure in love and ruin in life will arrive.

Kumari: Is not the fame of such lovers an undying flame?

Puthan: If they are lovers, yes. But for one life alone to burn away its existence for love is madness.

Kumari: And to leave that life to suffer alone?

Puthan: If they seek out that suffering themselves, who is responsible for it?

Kumari: Putting a bridle on the mind is not an ordinary thing.

Puthan: Trying to change another person's mind is the same.

Kumari: They call love a magnet. They call it a rope that joins hearts together. Is all that false?

Puthan: It is all true. But if the lover abandons the beloved, all of it becomes false for that person.

Kumari: Then you will not abandon your beloved? (Joyfully.)

Puthan: How could I? Even if the sea turns to rock and the sun to ice, I will not abandon my beloved.

Kumari: (Eagerly) Aththaan! (She moves to embrace him.)

Puthan: Keep away from me. Are you a woman—or what they call a Brahma-rakshasi? With lust risen to your head, destroying a man's life—you are unfit to remain in a world where men live!

[Source-held: scan 79 contains several compact historical-type letterform/spacing decisions within Kumari's dense lower-page speech. The secure dramatic sense below is translated, but the exact Tamil letter/spacing decisions remain unresolved.]

Kumari: Forgive me. Do not speak to me as though speaking to some contemptible prostitute. My love is not the frenzy of a Brahma-rakshasa spirit—it is the beating of my heart! Was it a crime to tell you of it? It is a flood of desire that no dam can stop. The waves of this helpless woman's mind, struggling in that flood, cry, “Love! Love!” For showing you my love, do I deserve words this harsh to the ear? Is this how the Prince of Inbapuri speaks? Is the man I loved with all my heart pushing me into the fire? No. Save this orphan.

Puthan: Do not speak!

[Darkness on the curtain. The next scene appears.]
