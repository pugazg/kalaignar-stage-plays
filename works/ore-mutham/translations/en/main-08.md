# Scene 8

Location: A part of Mullaikadu.

[Source-held: the dense opening parenthetical stage direction remains unresolved at the exact-word level; no wording is reconstructed from plot continuity.]

Guard 1: Hey! Take aim at this cuckoo!

Guard 2: Vibhishanan's favourite bird!

Guard 1: She is going to fly away.

[Source-held: the closing movement direction in this opening sequence remains unresolved at the exact-word level.]

Guard 1: Stop!

Ponni: Who are you?

Guard 1: We are hunters.

Guard 2: Very skilled at hunting.

Guard 3: Yes. No deer has ever escaped our aim.

Ponni: What brings you here?

Guard 1: It is all for your own good.

Ponni: For my good?

Guard 2: Yes. We want you to live with every good fortune, every wealth, every influence and every pleasure.

Ponni: Don't stretch it out. Come to the point.

Guard 1: Even when she speaks sharply, she is very beautiful.

Guard 3: Vibhishanan will enjoy exactly this.

A Friend: (Angrily) Stop your mockery! She is the village headwoman's daughter. Ponni! Come, let us go.

Guard 1: Ponni! Ha ha ha! We have no intention of letting you go.

(Ponni and the others begin to move away.)

Guard 1: We are government officers. In Mullaikadu, what we lay down is the law.

Guard 2: A peacock will not give you a feather merely because you keep calling, “Peacock, peacock.”

Guard 1: Hmm. Next comes force.

(At the cry of “Force,” a struggle breaks out between the men and women. The guards seize Ponni. The women run. Puthan suddenly appears and fights the guards. After a long struggle the guards flee. Ponni is freed. Puthan stands admiring Ponni.)

Ponni: You saved me from a pack of bears.

Puthan: I did my duty.

Ponni: A help I can never forget.

Puthan: A herd of cattle entered a flower garden. Humanity stopped it.

Ponni: Please accept my heartfelt thanks.

(Malayan, Ponni's father, comes searching for her.)

Malayan: Ponni! Ponni! Ponni!

(She runs to him.)

Ponni: Father!

Malayan: What happened here?

Ponni: Minister Vibhishanan.........

Malayan: Ah! Vibhishanan?

Puthan: Yes. The Vibhishanan beetle is thirsty with lust! A few insect-beetles flew out looking for a honey-flower.

Malayan: What? What? Beetle or crab? Girl, who is this man?

Ponni: Father! When they were tormenting me......

Malayan: Ah! He tormented you? He looks so mild! Scoundrel!

[Ponni tries to stop him; in anger he rushes at Puthan.]

“Ponni, let go of me.”

Ponni: Don't be hasty. Not him. Those cowards ran away.

Malayan: Just now you said he tormented you.

Ponni: I said that when they were tormenting me, he saved me, Father!

Malayan: Oh! Is that so? Greetings! You rescued my girl from the mouth of a mountain python. Ayyo, if you had not been here, my whole family would have been ruined by now. I said something without knowing.

Puthan: Never mind. One must be lucky to get such treatment!

Malayan: Hmm. God himself has cast an eye on us.

Ponni: Yes, Father! He too came from the world of the gods...

Puthan: That's right, Ponni. I have seen you there too—no, no, a woman who looked just like you.

[Malayan notices love showing in the glances between Ponni and Puthan.]

Malayan: Ponni! He's a young master from a great household. Poor thing—how long will friendship with him last? Come, let us go.

Puthan: What a joke.

Malayan: It is no joke. We are all untouchables!

Puthan: Untouchables! Not you, who possess pure hearts. The deceivers who divide and rule you by caste merely to fill their own bellies are the untouchables! Those poisonous ideologues who refuse the people of the country even the rights they grant to dogs are the untouchables! The conspirators who require a human being's labour for their own lives, yet keep that very human being enslaved in the name of caste, are the untouchables!

Malayan: I don't know. The elders have said so.

Puthan: The elders have said, “There are only two castes and no others”—a teaching of the righteous path; and the song, “One community, one God.”

Ponni: Yes, Father! That is what they have said.

Malayan: Hmm. Ponni! Has his scent rubbed off on you too? He is an upper-caste man; he may say anything. Can you speak like that?

Ponni: Why? He is just like me, isn't he?

Puthan: Ah! I am just like you. My heart is like your heart. Caste difference does not change these things.

Ponni: Caste cruelty has torn apart so many hearts that were united.

[She seems to be thinking of something elsewhere.]

Malayan: What? What?

Ponni: I have read in stories of so many people who sacrificed their very lives opposing caste. That is what I mean, Father.

Puthan: Yes. I too have read of them.

Ponni: Reading is easy. Living by it is difficult.

Puthan: It has been many days since I set foot on the path of those martyrs.

[Malayan, in haste.]

Malayan: Hmm. Ponni! Come along. (To Puthan) We will be going. For your help, once again......

Puthan: No need. It is enough if you go. Ponni! I will come.

(A parting in which neither can bear to part.)
