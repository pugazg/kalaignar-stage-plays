# Scene 5

Location: A city street in Mullaikadu.

(Vibhishanan's men drag in a young man who has been tied up. The curtain opens on an authoritative voice saying, “Do not talk back.”)

“Do not talk back.”

One Man: The order of Royal Representative Imaya. Pay the tax! (He strikes him again.)

Young Man: God! Is there no end to this beastly rule?

Thug: Ha ha haa! God, your devotee is suffering; come and save him!

(The young man's wife runs in and falls over her husband. She receives the blows meant for him. Then he rises in a burst of fury.)

Wife: Here! Your tax. At least leave him alive!

(She removes her *thali* and gives it to him.)

Young Man: The *thali*—the *thali* I tied! Ah! Rule of injustice! Is there no destruction for you?

Thug: No, man, no! Imaya's rule has received the boon of immortality.

Young Man: Such monstrous sins under Emperor Mahaveerar's empire? Is there no one to question this? Does this poor man's cry not reach your ears?

Thug: God is deaf, man—deaf. (Terrible laughter.)

(A Bhagavathar performs a *kalakshepam*.)

Bhagavathar: Banana and areca trees stand looking toward the sky; coloured flowers form row upon row of festoons; bunches and clusters of fruit hang as decoration in the pavilion. Forty-eight thousand rishis sit there tending the sacrificial fire, slaughtering goats, pouring pot after pot of ghee into the fire-pit, roasting and eating, drinking soma, while with great clamour the chant of mantras resounds. At that moment, a horde of merciless demons suddenly bursts in—

(Vibhishanan's men suddenly enter. Uproar, suffering, rioting and plunder. Houses are set on fire. Children run in panic. A baby lying in a cradle is lifted up, thrown down and killed.)

Thug: Why should dogs who refuse to pay tax have houses? Set them on fire and burn them!

(The dreadful *tandava* of arrogance.)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/main-05.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- `தாலி` is retained as *thali* because the marriage pendant itself is the dramatic object surrendered as tax.
- `காலக்ஷேபம்` is retained as *kalakshepam*, with `Bhagavathar` retained as the performer designation used by the source.
- The mythic-sacrificial narration is translated as narration rather than rationalized or corrected; its collision with the on-stage violence is a deliberate dramatic parallel.
- `தாண்டவம்` is retained as *tandava* in the final direction to preserve the source's image of a violent cosmic dance.
