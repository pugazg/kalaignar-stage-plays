# Scene 2

Location: The festival hall.

(King Mahaveerar, the Minister, Yaaliththathar and the Commander are present, along with citizens; in the women's section are Kumari, Ramela and many others. A dance is taking place. When it ends—)

Minister: Greetings. Dear friends! On this sacred celebration of Prince Puthan's birthday, I offer him long-life wishes on behalf of the people of Inbapuri.

(A roar of greetings rises from the assembly.)

“Long live the Prince!”

Yaaliththathar: (Rising.) In my capacity as the Royal Representative of Poonthottam, I present this gift to the Prince. This gift, signifying equality, is my dream for the Inbapuri of the future! I believe my dream will come true in the time of the Prince who will wear the crown after Emperor Mahaveerar. On this birthday, let us take a vow to labour for the greatness of our country. Long live the Prince!

(A set of scales symbolizing the worker and the wealthy as equals is presented as a gift. Representatives of Sela District, Mayilpannai and Vasanadu also present gifts.)

Puthan: Dear friends! My thanks for your enthusiasm. As my friend Yaaliththathar said, this is not merely my birthday; it is the annual festival at which we take a vow for the happy life of Inbapuri. The future of this country is a dream to Yaaliththathar; to me, it is the goal of my life. A lofty goal. A sublime goal! What is fused with my very life is the happy life of this country's people—a great life without divisions, a beautiful life in which the condition of aristocrat and poor no longer exists. This is my breath. The song of my heart. To triumph in this goal, I will sacrifice anything. If necessary, even the crown of Inbapuri! (Applause and cries of praise.) In the future Inbapuri Empire, there will be no poor man and rich man. There will be no master and slave. There will be only the human being!

(The cries of praise rise again. The gathering disperses. The festival ends and everyone leaves. Only Kumari and her friend Ramela remain. Ramela notices the change in Kumari's face as Kumari keeps looking at the gifts.)

Ramela: What's the matter, dear? You're upset because you didn't give any gift, aren't you? Don't worry. The Prince will accept you yourself as the gift.

Kumari: Ramela! “What is fused with my very life is the happy life of this country's people—a great life without divisions, a beautiful life in which the condition of aristocrat and poor no longer exists!”

Ramela: Oh! And he did not add Kumari to that, is that it?

Kumari: (Angrily.) Ramela!

Ramela: Kumari! Is that something to say before a crowd? A time will come. Then, on the couch, amid caresses, he will say it. At that time this country, the people, the aristocrat, the poor—none of them will come to his lips. (Imitating Puthan.) “What is fused with my very life is the cuckoo called Kumari! Her beauty, her couch......”

Kumari: Ramela! Will you not keep quiet?

(Kumari is lost in Puthan's portrait.)

Ramela: Oh! Are you talking to the Prince? That is a picture.

Kumari: If only this picture had the power to speak......?

Ramela: You would drive me away from here.

(Kumari moves away with a sigh.)

Ramela: You are pining away like this. Why not say just one word to him? He is your *aththaan*, after all.

Kumari: What if he refuses?

Ramela: Refuse a manoranjitha flower?

Kumari: Ramela! All the Prince ever thinks about is......

(She looks at Ramela longingly.)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/main-02.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- The source's equality symbolism in the `தராசு` gift is made explicit without adding a new political claim.
- The physical Tamil page split `சொல்` / `ளார்.` is not treated as a lexical uncertainty; the English renders the secure utterance naturally while the Tamil archival split remains untouched.
- `அத்தான்` is retained as *aththaan* because the source uses the kinship/affinal form inside romantic teasing and an English substitute would over-specify the relation.
- `மனோரஞ்சித மலர்` is retained as `manoranjitha flower`, preserving Ramela's flower comparison rather than replacing it with an inferred botanical equivalent.
