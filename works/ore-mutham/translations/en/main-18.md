# Scene 18

Location: Outside the inquiry hall.

Vibhishanan: Prince! Through your efforts I have obtained a fine post. I am obliged to thank you.

Puthan: Do not soar! Injustice has not received a boon of immortality. This is the first chapter of its destruction.

(Puthan leaves.)

Vibhishanan: Little insect. Its head must be pinched off first.

(In the same scene: Puthan, Kumari and the warriors.)

Puthan: It is astonishing that the worm that fell into the fire escaped! It can only have been that Chithra who stole the evidence-letters.

Kumari: A wrong idea. I was the one who forcibly brought Chithra whom I found on the road. If she had intended to steal, she could have set fire to the hut while we slept.

Warrior 1: Somehow a traitor has escaped. The Maharaja did not even give value to the Prince's word.

Puthan: The king acted according to imperial law. Without evidence, what could he do to him? Imaya is a fool. He admitted the conspiracy, and Vibhishanan betrayed him as well. Treachery succeeded.

Kumari: Even knowing all this, Vibhishanan should never have been raised to a higher post. That was a great mistake made by uncle.

Puthan: Father's way is strange! Everything he ought to hate becomes milk to him! He has fallen into Vibhishanan's poisonous speech and has come to believe that itself is justice.

Kumari: If Vibhishanan is left alone......

Puthan: If he is left alone, it means leaving all of Mullaikadu to burn! It means leaving a little fox to guard the chicken farm. It means allowing him to mount the [Source-held: dense place-name cluster on scan 69] throne.

Kumari: If we are to catch him again......?

Puthan: His life is in those letters. If we get hold of them, [Source-held: the consequence clause after `அவைகளை நாம் கைப்பற்றிவிட்டால்` on scan 69 remains unresolved at exact-word level].

Warrior 1: Then?

Puthan: We travel tomorrow. Make the preparations.

(The warriors leave.)

Kumari: Would it not be enough if the two of us went?

Puthan: You are not needed. I am going alone.

Kumari: Would my coming cause you any trouble?

Puthan: It would.

Kumari: The journey is about Vibhishanan, isn't it......?

Puthan: There may be something else too.

Kumari: A secret......?

Puthan: An unnecessary question.

Kumari: To stop a companion who comes along the road, saying she is not wanted......?

Puthan: To stop her is selfishness. And if she does not listen even after being stopped?

Kumari: My selfishness. Prince! If you go alone, I will have no peace.

Puthan: A fine story.

Kumari: Not a story—the dream I have of serving you throughout my life.

Puthan: Let the dream remain a dream.

Kumari: You are rejecting me.

Puthan: Not you. Your thought.

Kumari: My thought! Is it Alakala poison?

Puthan: It may be nectar. But there is no way for it to mix with my own thought.

Kumari: Unjustly, you are bringing a woman's castles of the mind to the ground.

Puthan: I will not unjustly bring a woman's castles of the mind to the ground.

Kumari: Then?

Puthan: Remove the thought.

Kumari: Is disappointment my reward?

Puthan: Puthan will not deceive a woman.

Kumari: Do I not even have the right to come with you like a servant?

Puthan: There is no need for you to come.

Kumari: Prince! When I stole those letters from Vibhishanan and brought them back, you said something.

Puthan: What did I say?

Kumari: That Kumari deserved a proper reward!

Puthan: Yes.

Kumari: I want that reward now.

Puthan: In that case...... what reward?

Kumari: Permission to come with you.

Puthan: What a predicament. Hmm..... all right, set out.

Kumari: (Smiling) Many thanks.

(They part.)
