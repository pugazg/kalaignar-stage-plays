# Scene 19

Location: The spring pavilion.

(Vibhishanan and Chithra are happy.)

Vibhishanan: He roared as though a sea-sluice had been opened and opened the box to take out the letters. Ah! What majesty! What delight! In a single instant it all vanished as though lightning had struck. Chithra! From now on, in Mullaikadu he is the uncrowned king. My rule begins.

Chithra: Wasn't what happened until now also your rule?

Vibhishanan: There was one Imaya in the way. With Puthan's help I buried him. From now on, as Vibhishanan wishes—the jewelled throne of Inbapuri. And you beside it!

Chithra: Your evil thought still has not left you.

Vibhishanan: Fortune comes by itself, Chithra!

(A female companion enters.)

Companion: A spy has come.

Vibhishanan: Tell him to come in.

[Guard 1 enters.]

Vibhishanan: What news? Is it done?

[Guard 1 whispers something secretly into Vibhishanan's ear. Chithra comes near and observes; noticing this, Vibhishanan speaks indirectly.]

Vibhishanan: What is her name? Ponni?

Guard 1: Yes. Malayan's daughter.

Vibhishanan: An untouchable woman, practising the courtesan's trade. She is ruining the names of great households. How did you find out?

Guard 1: First I [source wording here is carried only at secure dramatic sense], then I investigated. What I'm saying now is true. I've seen her directly. The whole town is laughing.

[For Chithra's benefit, Vibhishanan pretends to be angry while grasping the matter.]

Vibhishanan: The town will laugh—clap and laugh that such prostitution goes on under Vibhishanan's rule! Hmm.... Is that prostitute beautiful enough to practise prostitution?

Guard 1: She's a ripe mango in beauty, sir. That's what made that fellow lose his head.

Vibhishanan: The fool would lose his head! His being bewitched is one offence. Her bewitching him is the greater offence. The woman who enchants—what sort of woman is she?

Guard 1: A young woman of pure ten-and-a-half-grade gold, sir.

Vibhishanan: Gold! The demoness who sells beauty is named Gold! You call her gold—is that insolent creature golden in colour?

Guard 1: Pure gold, sir! Put briefly, if nine Rathis were joined into one, she would have that much grace.

Vibhishanan: A prostitute who brings men under her sway. A description of her beauty. Yes. Does she speak beautifully enough to draw that male fool in?

Guard 1: Her speech is like a parrot, sir! Ah! What speech!

Vibhishanan: That pretender must have learned to sing too....?

Guard 1: She sings like a cuckoo.

Vibhishanan: What did you say her name was?

Guard 1: Ponni, sir! Malayan's daughter Ponni!

Vibhishanan: Hmm. Bring her and her father here at once. Prostitution! Prostitution in Vibhishanan's kingdom! Let her come. I will deal with her.

(Guard 1 leaves.)

Vibhishanan: Did you see, Chithra? Prince Puthan is hunting pleasure. A prostitute called Ponni—he passes his time with her. Do you know who that Ponni is? Malayan's daughter from the Paraiyar village. A scorpion of an untouchable lineage! I have sent men to cut down the cruelty of her prostitution.

Chithra: This news is completely false!

Vibhishanan: I knew you would say that. How is it false?

Chithra: The Prince would not interfere in such matters the way you do.

Vibhishanan: He won't interfere. He will empty the field! Go, go, fool.

Chithra: Even if it were so, Ponni cannot be a prostitute. She may be Puthan's beloved.

Vibhishanan: Whatever sort of rat she may be, it makes no difference. Under the law of the Inbapuri Empire, it is a crime for upper-caste men to love a woman of a low caste. For the Prince to love one is an even greater crime. Chithra! Watch what is going to happen. Wait and see.

(He leaves. Chithra sighs deeply.)
