# Scene 26

Location: Mahaveerar's court.

(A Tamil poet recites. He is presented with a reward.)

Mahaveerar: Long live, Tamil poet! In your poem surging with feeling, Tamil courage is visible. In your poem overflowing with honeyed cadence, Tamil's sweetness is visible. This poem is a new garland for Mother Tamil! Let this emerald garland adorn those broad shoulders of yours that nurture Tamil. May your poems swell like great rivers to give Tamils a new life. Long live Tamil!

A Man: Tamil—

Another: Long live!

A Man: Tamil, born with the world—

Another: Long live!

A Man: Young Tamil that possessed the Himalaya—

Another: Long live!

[Source-held: the dense entrance stage direction is not exact-word secure. The source securely shows three dark-clothed men being brought in and Vibhishanan entering; no additional wording is reconstructed.]

Vibhishanan: Long live the Maharaja! Salutations to the Maharaja of the Inbapuri Empire. Long live! Long live!

Mahaveerar: Welcome, Vibhishanan! Welcome! Vibhishanan, who are these people?

Vibhishanan: They are traitors to the crown. Men who conspired to shatter the Empire. Men who drew up a plan to roll the Maharaja's head!

Mahaveerar: What? Did they conspire to kill me?

Vibhishanan: Yes. Beneath this veil is a poisonous torrent in which prostitution itself has taken form. This fish was caught in her net. (Pointing to one veiled figure) Bewitched by the weapon of desire, this man promised to make her Maharani of Inbapuri. If she is to become Maharani, must he not become Maharaja? If he is to become Maharaja, must the Maharaja not die? The man who prepared the plan for that is this royal loyalist. Sire! This three-way pact set out to overturn your crown.

Mahaveerar: Traitors! They must be hanged. Vibhishanan, do you have evidence?

Vibhishanan: Maharaja! One petition.

Mahaveerar: Speak.

Vibhishanan: The law of the government is common to everyone, is it not?

Mahaveerar: What doubt is there in that?

Vibhishanan: Even if I myself had committed these crimes, under law I too would have to die, would I not?

Mahaveerar: Without doubt. What of you? If I myself betrayed the country and its people, the verdict for me too, before the people's assembly, would be death.

Vibhishanan: Maharaja! Here is the evidence that they are guilty. (Handing the letters to Mahaveerar) These are the conspiracy letters that flew between two of them in the arrangement made to drink your life. My spies discovered them.

(Mahaveerar reads, trembles and stands stunned.)

Mahaveerar: Who?

Vibhishanan: No one else. Look here!

(The veils are removed. Ponni, Puthan and Yaaliththathar are revealed with their mouths bound. Vibhishanan slowly removes the bindings.)

Mahaveerar: Traitors! My own blood is a traitor to me! The flesh I raised is a traitor to me! This demon who lived eating my salt—(to Yaaliththathar)—is a traitor to me!

Yaaliththathar: None of us is a traitor, Maharaja! Take a little time and think.

Mahaveerar: Think? The testimony in these letters is enough.

Yaaliththathar: The letters are fabricated, Maharaja!

Vibhishanan: Maharaja, listen! Honourable members of the court, listen! Lord who is everywhere, listen! Goddess of Justice, listen! The Prince wanted to cast down the Maharaja, who never departs from righteousness, and crown himself. When he could not fulfil that desire because I stood in the way, he launched a campaign of hatred against me, arrested me too, accused me of treason and tried to have me killed. When justice stood by me and saved me, I learned through our spies that many secret schemes were again being made with Yaaliththathar against the Maharaja, and I turned my attention toward the Prince. That was when these conspiracy letters were seized. Next, news came that Yaaliththathar and the Prince were staying in this prostitute's house and engaging in some terrible conspiracy. I surrounded her house. They were caught red-handed. The law calls them traitors. Why? Their own consciences call them traitors. On my loyalty to the crown, I say this from my heart. Maharaja! If you wish, forgive the Prince this once. But Yaaliththathar, who aided the treason, and Ponni, the prostitute who incited it, must hang.

Mahaveerar: Justice admits no distinction. I was not born in a tradition that gives one justice to a son and another to the people. All three are traitors to the crown. All three must mount the gallows.

Puthan: Maharaja!

Mahaveerar: Do not speak, traitor!

Yaaliththathar: Great King! You are acting in haste.

Mahaveerar: Man of injustice! Not even half a moment should your cruel bodies remain in this world. They must be burned to ashes.

Vibhishanan: Even those ashes should be dissolved into the sea, Maharaja!

Yaaliththathar: Maharaja!

Mahaveerar: Minister! The gallows for all three. This is the verdict.

(The King leaves.)

Yaaliththathar: (Eagerly embracing the Prince) Prince!

Vibhishanan: What is it, Prince?..... Hmm. Drag them away.

(He strides away. The court disperses.)
