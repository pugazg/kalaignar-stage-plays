# Scene 6

Location: A house in Mullaikadu.

[Source-held: the opening parenthetical stage direction remains unresolved at the exact-word level; no wording is inferred.]

Puthan: Be careful! Reach the slopes of Pavalamalai. I too will come there tonight.

Kumari: Without falling into the enemies' hands......

Puthan: I know all that. You be careful!

Kumari: What if I have to enter the palace?

Puthan: You will need plenty of cunning. Try as far as you can. I will come.

(Puthan leaves. A man begins playing the mridangam.)

Another Man: Everybody come! She's going to dance, she's going to dance! (A crowd gathers. Kumari dances in the manner of a street performance. Another man dances too. The sound of horses' hooves is heard. The people realize that Vibhishanan's rowdy gang is arriving.)

A Man: Government rowdies.

Another: Vibhishanan's men.

A Man: Fine—bad luck has caught this girl!

Another: (Angrily) Hey! They're coming. Why are you all standing and watching?

Another Man: Why? What can we do?

Another: Come, let's run away!

(A guard comes and, standing at a distance, enjoys the dance.)

Guard 1: She dances like a peacock!

Guard 2: She flashes like a storm!

Guard 3: A fine face, a well-shaped body.

Guard 1: If we take this Rambha to our master?

Guard 2: He will give his life for her.

Guard 1: We'll get a proper reward!

Guard 2: Good. A deer has fallen into our lot. Come, come.

Guard 1: (Coming near) What do you call that dance you performed?

Kumari: Dance.

Guard 1: Hmm. Do you know how to joke too?

Kumari: A little.

Guard 1: You are ruining the dignity of your art of dance. This jewel of a dance—should it be performed like this in the street?

Kumari: We're poor! Where else, sir—can we dance in a palace?

Guard 1: If a woman sets her mind on it, she can dance not only in a palace but even on a king's head.

Guard 2: Like Goddess Ganga dancing on Lord Shiva's head.

Kumari: One needs luck for all that, sir.

Guard 1: Luck strikes women like you first. Look here—I cannot bear to see you wearing rags and tatters like this, with shell-beads around you! I want to see your whole body decked in necklaces of gems.

Kumari: I have the desire. But the man is only with me, sir!

Guard 1: (Calling Kumari closer and speaking softly) If our master Vibhishanan sees you, he will bite you and eat you alive.

Kumari: Ah! He sounds like a demon!

Guard 1: No. He will be utterly bewitched by your beauty and your dance and shower you with gifts.

Kumari: Really?

Guard 1: Is it his own property he spends? It is the money of the Inbapuri Empire. Royal Representative Imaya has the iron chest; Vibhishanan has the key. He spends as he pleases—pleasure expenses. I will even ask him to shower you in gold. Is that enough?

Kumari: Then who are all of you?

Guard 1: We are tax-collecting officers. If someone like you falls into our hands on the way, it is our custom to present her as a gift to the master.

Kumari: What if the women refuse to come...?

Guard 1: Then they're a flower-garland caught in a monkey's hand! But if they listen to us, we protect them as the eyelid protects the eye and deliver them to Vibhishanan.

Kumari: You are very good men.

Guard 1: (Pointing to Guard 2) Yes! He said the same thing too.

Guard 2: All right, move.

Kumari: (In a low voice) A good opportunity. We can meet the fox in its own den and crush it there.

A Warrior: Vibhishanan, the chandala! What if he plots something?

Kumari: I will escape through cunning. When the Prince arrives, tell him this: if I do not return tomorrow morning, it is a sign of danger.

[Kumari leaves with the guards.]
