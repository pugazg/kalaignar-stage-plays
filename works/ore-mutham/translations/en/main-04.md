# Scene 4

Location: Imaya's palace.

(A drinking feast. Two women dance for a few minutes.)

Vibhishanan: Wine and women—those two alone are medicines that cure worry.

Imaya: Yes. Medicines! If the whole sea became wine..........

Vibhishanan: We could turn all the sands into women and frolic in the sea of wine!

Imaya: Women all through the palace; jars of wine in the women's hands. Vibhishanan! That place......?

Vibhishanan: Nothing less than Indra's heaven! Wine cups; flower-like hands holding them—ah! Da, da, da! Pleasure! Pleasure! Pleasure!!

Imaya: Pleasure! Pleasure! Pleasure!! What if we poured the wine into those women's mouths and sucked it out to drink?

Vibhishanan: It would be enchanting, like a bee drinking honey flowing from a flower.

Imaya: Bravo; Vibhishanan! You speak like a poet.

Vibhishanan: (Pointing to the wine.) All of it is the grace of this Maheswari.

Imaya: Ah! Grace indeed!

(He stares at a woman's statue. It begins to look like Mahaveerar to him.)

Imaya: Maharaja! Mahaveerar is looking straight at us. Fool!

Vibhishanan: (Laughing.) A clod of a king!

(He kicks the statue and it falls.)

Imaya: Well said. Should not my statue stand where this statue stands?

Vibhishanan: Why only here! I will seat Imaya on Inbapuri's jewelled throne.

Imaya: You will seat me there. The jewelled throne! Beside it, fresh wine in a golden jar. Jewel-like women scooping it with a diamond cup and pouring it into my mouth—Vibhishanan, that position must come soon.

Vibhishanan: It will certainly come.

Imaya: It will certainly come.

Vibhishanan: I have cast the net. The work of conspiracy will soon be finished. All that remains is the invasion. The siege of Inbapuri Fort! The king fleeing! The beginning of our rule.

Imaya: You have forgotten Puthan.

Vibhishanan: That calf? He will tremble the moment he sees me.

Imaya: He will tremble—the coward! Vibhishanan! If there is to be an invasion, must I come too?

Vibhishanan: No, no!

Imaya: There is no need. Why should I trouble myself over such a petty matter?

Vibhishanan: Imaya! You may remain in safety.

Imaya: What safety! It will be enough if I stay inside a cave with ten thousand warriors. I am not afraid of anything.

Vibhishanan: Does not the whole country say that you are a heroic lion with a fearless heart!

Imaya: Ah! Is that what they say? (He strokes his chest.)

Vibhishanan: Yes.

Imaya: Hmm. The war plan will need a great deal of money, will it not!

Vibhishanan: The collection of money is already going on. In preparation for the war, I have filled half our treasury. (He pours wine into a cup.) Wherever you look, our men are collecting taxes.

Imaya: Taxes?

Vibhishanan: Yes! Tax if you are born, tax if you live! Tax even if you die! Nothing but taxes everywhere.

Imaya: Taxes! If the Maharaja comes to know, it will be a terrible danger!

Vibhishanan: Madman! Do not babble! I have devised a splendid plan to uproot the Maharaja himself.

Imaya: What if the citizens complain to the Emperor?

Vibhishanan: Citizens! I will tear out their guts. They will not even open their mouths!

Vibhishanan: Shh, do not speak! The ordinary Royal Representative of Mullaikadu............the crown of Inbapuri—which of these do you want??

(He points separately to the two wine cups.)

Imaya: The crown! The crown! The crown of Inbapuri!

Vibhishanan: The crown!

Imaya: The crown!

Vibhishanan: Wine!

Imaya: Wine!

Vibhishanan: Woman!

Imaya: Woman!

Vibhishanan: If you want it, do not argue against me.

Imaya: I am not arguing.

(They each take one of the two wine cups and drink.)

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: closed Tamil `../../scenes/main-04.md`; no OCR, PDF redrafting, later edition or secondary English witness was used.
- The recurring `மது, மங்கை` motif remains `wine and women` / `wine` and `woman` according to the source's singular/plural dramatic usage.
- `இந்திரலோகம்` is rendered `Indra's heaven`; the mythic comparison is preserved without explanatory expansion.
- `மகேஸ்வரி` is retained as `Maheswari` because the line personifies/deifies the wine and an explanatory replacement would flatten the joke.
- `மண்ணங்கட்டி ராஜா` is rendered conservatively as `a clod of a king`, keeping the insult without inventing a modern idiom.
- The final crown/wine/woman call-and-response is kept repetitive and abrupt, as in the Tamil scene.
