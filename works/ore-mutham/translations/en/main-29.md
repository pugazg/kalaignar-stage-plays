# Scene 29

Location: The palace garden.

[Source-held: the dense opening parenthetical remains unresolved at exact-word level. The source securely shows Chithra in Buddhist-nun dress moving through the garden, with the Prince, Ponni and Puthan involved in the entrance blocking. No missing wording is reconstructed.]

Puthan: Lady Chithra! What is this appearance?

Chithra: The dishevelled appearance of a woman who betrayed the lover she desired. The ascetic appearance of a woman who sacrificed devotion to her husband for her motherland.

Puthan: You need not go anywhere. You may live comfortably in the palace itself.

Chithra: Comfort! I destroyed the comfort of the man who cared for my comfort. Can there be comfort for me after that? From now on, comfort is only in this life.

Puthan: Do not grieve. At the moment you betrayed your husband, you saved this country. One Vibhishanan will curse you. Future generations of Inbapuri will bless you.

Chithra: Prince! Women through generation after generation will revile me as “the woman who betrayed her husband, the woman who betrayed him.” This alone is my atonement.

Puthan: In that case, I will arrange a place for you to stay [Source-held: one exact place/compound word in this clause remains unresolved].

Chithra: Everyone who deceives the town in the guise of a renunciant needs a place to sit! As long as the desires in my mind keep roaring, I will remain adorned like this. Prince! One request. Kumari, who laboured without rest to unite the two of you, lies on her deathbed. Do not forget her.

(She leaves singing.)

Ponni: My love! The king does not like our love at all. After all, am I not a Paraiyar woman?

Puthan: A pearl is born in a shell, and he places it in a crown. A red lotus is born in mud, and he admires its beauty. But he rejects you, born within the human race itself. Ponni! Even if the whole world opposes us, it cannot separate us.

Ponni: To unite us, Chithra lost her husband; Kumari... lost her love.

(King Mahaveerar enters.)

Puthan: (Sadly, to himself) It looks as though she may lose her life too...... Kumari.... guava fruit! It looks as though death will pluck her away.

Mahaveerar: (Entering) Puthan! As king of this country, I must tell you something.

(Ponni withdraws from sight.)

Puthan: Sire!

Mahaveerar: You were born into the royal family. You are one who should receive the honour of rulers of other lands. In this position, your love for Ponni...... does not appear proper.

Puthan: Your words are like a man who digs into the underworld, suffers every hardship, finds pure gold, then throws it away crying, “Father!”

Mahaveerar: As your father, I approve of it. As king, I say that under law your love is invalid.

Puthan: One problem, two standards. Father! Is rule not the connection between law and justice?

Mahaveerar: The world will call it selfishness if I alter the law for your sake.

Puthan: No. My love will become a signpost on the road to equality. It will lay down a higher path for a world ruled by rotten customs.

Mahaveerar: The inter-caste marriage you desire will stand in the way of your crown. You are the one who must rule the country! To lose a crown for the sake of a mere woman?

Puthan: Crown! Why do I need a crown if I cannot have freedom in love? Sire! You will call this my selfishness. No! To abandon the woman who has taken residence in my thought and crown myself over this great empire—that would be selfishness. If I cast her aside for a throne, this world will revile me as long as it exists.

Mahaveerar: Then...?

Puthan: For love... I sacrifice the crown.

Mahaveerar: Think about it. Your principle that you will labour for the people is being shattered for the sake of one woman.

Puthan: There is no law saying one must be a king in order to serve the people. One can do that work even as a labourer.

Mahaveerar: Then you have made up your mind to lose the crown?

Puthan: Yes.

Mahaveerar: Puthan! (Pleading) I lived trusting in you. You have disappointed me. To whom shall I hand this government?

Puthan: Hand it to the people, Father! What if I had perished that day under your hasty verdict?

Mahaveerar: You tell me a way—a way that will not make rulers of other lands despise us!

Puthan: Father! Even if you hand this government to me, I will not rule as a king. I will turn it into a democracy. I will work as a representative of the people. I will abolish monarchy.

Mahaveerar: In a democracy too, arrogant men swollen with pride will fling power about! What if democracy becomes a brutal rule that pours out such cruelty that the people begin to think monarchy itself was better?

Puthan: The power to change it will be in the hands of the people. Whoever commits injustice will be uprooted with root and all.

Mahaveerar: Whatever you say...

Puthan: Your obsession with the crown has not left you. Father! Remain king yourself until you die.

Mahaveerar: And after that, you...?

Puthan: I am going to live among the people. I am going to serve the people. I will have no rest until I remove the delusion from the people's minds.

Mahaveerar: After my lifetime, what of the country?

Puthan: It will not be destroyed. Do not worry. A good democracy will blossom. So that blossom does not wither...... I will strengthen the people's movement.

Mahaveerar: Son! Your spirit of sacrifice is an example to the whole world.

(A guard enters.)

Guard: King of kings! Lady Kumari is in great danger. They are calling you.

Puthan: Ah!...

(They leave in agitation.)
