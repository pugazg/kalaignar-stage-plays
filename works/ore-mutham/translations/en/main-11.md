# Scene 11

Location: Vibhishanan's private room.

(The curtain opens with Vibhishanan laughing loudly, “Ha! Ha! Ha!” Chithra stands angrily.)

Vibhishanan: Chithra...... come.

Chithra: Enough. Enough of ruining Chithra's life.

Vibhishanan: Anger is evil. It is one of the five great sins.

Chithra: The five great sins! You, born crowned with desire for injustice and atrocity, speak of the five great sins.

Vibhishanan: What! Injustice or atrocity—what do those mean?

Chithra: Do not pretend innocence! Do not pretend innocence after becoming the cause of the pitiful deaths of tender children! Do not pretend innocence after destroying the pure lives of young women!

Vibhishanan: Oh Lord!

Chithra: If the Lord truly listened, cruelty would never have sprouted in this world. The Lord must be blind—or deaf—or mute.

Vibhishanan: Hmm.....! Tell me each one! I will think them over.

Chithra: Think them over. How many maiden women have lost their lives to your lust? Think about it.

Vibhishanan: Did I violate their chastity? Am I a man vowed to one wife like Rama?

Chithra: Why Rama? When it comes to guarding chastity, you are Indra's elder brother! I know.

Vibhishanan: If women are Ahalyas, men must be Indras too, must they not.....

Chithra: Leave the stories aside. Think about how many cruelties your lustful acts create.

Vibhishanan: What is this, Chithra! You fling around languages no one can understand. Lustful acts, cruelty, me doing such things—when it comes to the one-wife principle I am Parandhaman himself.

Chithra: Shouldn't Parandhaman's principle be asked about from the Gopika women!

Vibhishanan: Chithra, what exactly are you saying? Your husband Vibhishanan is lust-blinded, murderous, base—is that your verdict?

Chithra: A verdict? Ask this ring; it will tell you.

Vibhishanan: (Startled) This ring. (He feels around his fingers, searching.)

Chithra: The very ring you put on the street dancer—that divine ring.

Vibhishanan: Deception! Where is she?

Chithra: She flew away long ago.

Vibhishanan: Both you tricksters have conspired against me.

[Chithra laughs lightly; Vibhishanan is furious. A terrifying silence prevails. Two guards enter.]

Guard 1: Master! The conspiracy letters we had have fallen into Puthan's hands.

Vibhishanan: Puthan? What nonsense are you babbling?

Guard 2: Yes! The Prince is investigating in disguise. A woman disguised as a street dancer stole the letters. (A storm rises in Vibhishanan's mind.)

Vibhishanan: Chithra! You have committed a fine act of treachery. Hmm. The conspiracy took place inside the lion's own cave. The woman who carried it out is my wife—an agent of the enemy. A hypocrite who dressed herself up like a chaste wife.

Chithra: A false accusation.

Vibhishanan: Pool of poison! Do not speak! Even a prostitute would not betray her husband like this. Deceiver who came claiming to be the companion of my life! You have darkened my whole life, traitor!

Chithra: Traitor! Am I the traitor?

Vibhishanan: Whom are you asking? Ask that *thali* shining on your neck! Ask the turmeric you have smeared on your face! Ask that mark you have placed on your forehead! Ask anyone and the answer is the same: the woman who betrayed her husband. Traitor!

Chithra: Swami! I did not betray you.

Vibhishanan: You merely handed over the letters, Chithra! Instead of doing this, you could have mixed poison into my food. You could have cut off my head while I slept. Or while I was fondly caressing you, you could have crushed my chest.

Chithra: Swami! I know nothing about it!

Vibhishanan: You know nothing. You acted with me all night like a street singer, and you do not know even this?

Chithra: It is true that I acted, but......

Vibhishanan: You did not seize the letters, crafty woman!

Chithra: She may have stolen them. I did not notice.

Vibhishanan: Last night you came between me and my desire and destroyed it in the bud. While I was playing a golden veena, you cut its strings. You let her escape.

Chithra: In your lust, I wanted to save an unstained flower from being scorched.

Vibhishanan: Yes. You saved the flower. You betrayed the bridegroom. All these calamities merely because I desired another woman?

Chithra: Swami!..... (She weeps.)

Vibhishanan: Read about the virtues of the chaste wives of old. Where is Nalayini, who carried her diseased husband to a courtesan's house to satisfy his desire? And where are you, more venomous than a cobra?

Chithra: Forgive me.

Vibhishanan: Forgive you? Tomorrow I will be tried for conspiring to seize the crown. The sentence for that is death.

Chithra: (Weeping.)

Vibhishanan: Why are you crying? If you want to see me alive, you must recover those letters.

Chithra: Then......

Vibhishanan: There is no time left even to think. Every letter now in the Prince's hands is evidence that can make me a criminal. Hmm...... I could not realize that your daring would make the sacrificial altar my swing.

Chithra: Do not stab my heart with a spear. Kill me with your own hands.

Vibhishanan: Why should you die? You who were eager to watch me stand on the gallows in ecstasy—why should you die? You who wanted to rejoice at seeing the pieces of my flesh thrown as a feast to dogs and jackals for the crime of conspiracy—why should you die? You who dream that once your husband is gone you can find new pleasure in Puthan's lap—why should you die?

(Chithra covers her ears and weeps.)

Vibhishanan: Pretence! Mere pretence! If you truly have goodwill toward my life, bring those letters back into my hands.

Chithra: Can I do it?

Vibhishanan: You can. Think of Savitri's sacrifice in rescuing her husband from the mouth of Death. You can. Why? Think of the daring you used last night to free the street dancer. You can. Then you will understand your own strength.

Chithra: I am going now. I will return with the letters.

(Chithra leaves.)

Vibhishanan: (To himself) Nalayini—Savitri! Fine stories that came to mind at the right time! Would she have gone otherwise? Stories perfectly suited to deceiving women! But..........

(He sinks into thought.)
