# Scene 1

## Entrance to Socrates' House

[People are moving here and there along the street. Socrates calls out loudly to them.]

Socrates : Handsome young men of lofty Athens! To spread fragrance through a society that has begun to stink—here is Socrates calling you; come running! Young bulls who tie up your waistcloth and go to the field while fresh blood races through you! It is you I am calling.... Come and gather around me in crowds! Courage will fetch no value if wisdom does not come to its aid! A sharpened sword and a spear raised upon shoulders hardened by daily exertion are not enough, heroes! Take up the weapon I give you! The weapon of knowledge! The weapon of knowledge!! A new instrument of war that will destroy in a single instant the demon of stupidity with its dark body and blind mind! A revolutionary weapon that reveals the inner world of warriors who never show their backs! Tiger-like young men in the prime of life! Set out with me! The enemy has entered our country—without any army!..... He has robbed the hearts of ordinary people..... He has drowned the city of Athens in the darkness of ignorance—that enemy called superstition! Strong comrades, form ranks and set out, so that beautiful Athens may become a grove of knowledge!

[Young men and warriors gather around and listen to Socrates' teaching. Socrates' wife Xanthippe, embittered by her husband's conduct, stands at the threshold looking on with disgust........ Socrates' friend, his elderly companion Crito, arrives. On seeing him........]

Soc. : Crito! You have come? Come, come!.... Here I am, in my old age, hurrying along in the duty of leading young men toward a good path!.... Look at the young wife I got, my second wife....Xanthippe; see how furiously she tries to stop me! Look! Look! Look at those eyes pouring fire! Are they eyes?.... Terrible lightning, man; lightning!....

Xanthippe : [Angrily] Yes; lightning indeed...... To this kinnara old man, his wife's eyes can only look like lightning....!

Soc. : What can I do..? If I were a poet of imagination, seeing your broad waist I would exclaim, ah, a lightning waist!.... Crito....Socrates will not lie, will he? I speak the truth... because I see the light of lightning in my wife's eyes! ........Isn't that so, friend....!

Xan. : Enough! Enough!.... Let your arguments, explanations and lamp-oil philosophies stay with the people coming and going in the street! Your first wife Myrto—the good fortune that held on to her died with her! I remain here to suffer like this........ A family—a wife in the family—three children—shouldn't a man have some concern for them?

Soc. : [Pointing to the young men around him.........] See, I labour for these children too.... My dear! The sun performs its duty for the earth, for the sea surrounding it, and for all the living creatures that live in them!....

Xan. : Talking like this about the sky and the earth, you leave the woman who trusted you stranded in midstream.... [Angrily to herself] Hey, Xanthippe, why did you need this trouble!....

Go to ruin somehow!.... Get lost!.... A cremation ground is far better than this poverty-stricken house!.... Rather than tie myself to this debater and weep, I could tie a noose around my neck and die! Die!.... die!.... die!....

[She goes inside making a tremendous noise. After she leaves, Socrates laughs and turns to the others....]

Soc. : Ah, at last, the thunder has thundered itself out... The moment the lightning appeared, I knew there would be thunder like this!.... Young men!.... Idealists face so many obstacles like these! At home, like this!.... In the country—one cannot even bear to hear of them! The politician Anytus thinks that no situation should ever sprout in which his injustices can be pointed out. Meletus, who delights in creating an imaginary world; Lycon, who earns distinction by shaping statues out of stone and metal—all of them are enraged that the very foundations are shaking because of my campaign for knowledge........ The fame of Greece is not unknown to the world! But trying to hide the cracks that have appeared here for that reason is like smearing civet perfume on a wound!....... That is why, comrades, I bow my head and call upon you: learn to think. Knowledge! I call you to seek it and obtain it from whatever corner of the world it may be found!.... “Know thyself!” My dear ones, dearer than my own life, I call all of you to understand the truth of this teaching....

[At that moment Xanthippe brings a vessel of water and pours it over Socrates' head.]

Crito : Ah! What is this? What is this?....

Soc. : Don't panic, Crito!.... It is nothing!.... First you saw the lightning; then the thunder thundered, and now the rain is falling..... That is all!.....

[The young men and Crito laugh. Xanthippe goes inside angrily.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/01.md`; no wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted.
- `அறிவாயுதம்` is rendered as `weapon of knowledge`, preserving the martial rhetoric that runs through the opening address.
- `மடமைப் பைசாசம்` is rendered as `demon of stupidity`; the supernatural image is retained rather than flattened to `ignorance`.
- `புறங் காணா வீரர்களின் அகங்காட்டும் புரட்சிக் கருவி` is rendered conservatively as a revolutionary weapon revealing the inner world of warriors who never show their backs.
- The verified source form `இந்தக் கிண்ணாரக் கிழவருக்கு` is not silently normalized; `kinnara old man` retains the unusual lexical form rather than inventing a smoother Tamil reading.
- `விளக்கெண்ணெய்த் தத்துவங்கள்` remains the comic `lamp-oil philosophies`.
- `நட்டாற்றில்`, `சுடுகாடு`, the lightning/thunder/rain joke, the attack on superstition, and `உன்னையே நீ அறிவாய்!` are all retained in the dramatic flow.
- The source-printed final `*` is retained.
