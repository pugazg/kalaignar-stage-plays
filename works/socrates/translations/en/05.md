# Scene 5

## Prison

[Socrates, his feet chained, is walking back and forth in a small space. The door opens—Crito enters.]

Soc. : Crito! My dear friend! Come, come........

[He comes forward and looks outside].... ....Who is that?

Crito : Your wife....the children!

[The two older children enter, and Xanthippe enters carrying the baby, all shedding tears........]

: !........

[She weeps; the children weep; even the infant cries out loudly.]

Soc. : My dear!........Did you see?.... ....You used to grow angry saying that I waste my time in useless philosophical inquiry and trouble myself with argument; now look—your husband has become a hero praised by the whole world, a martyr praised by the country!.... Xanthippe! You are indeed fortunate! You are the wife of a hero of action who stood against military strength, money strength, every kind of strength, bowed to no one, and is now about to close his eyes for the last time with that pride!.... Take good care of the children! When they grow up, if they stray from honesty, just as I tried to correct you all, you too must try to correct them. Go....it is getting late....the guards will grow angry!........ Crito, see them off!

[Crito leads them outside. Then Crito alone comes back in.]

Soc. : Crito! You know, don't you?.... ....My thirty days of prison life end today! According to the court's judgment....today is the auspicious day on which I must drink poison and die!

Crito : Socrates! Poison is going to destroy you, whom philosophical argument could not destroy!....

One thought, friend!........There is still time; and I have glittering gold! If we win over the guards somehow and escape....?

Soc. : I am ashamed, Crito!........Socrates is not one who fears death........ When I argued about the conditions imposed by the court, said that I was prepared to pay a fine, explained the order of banishment, and gave a great speech about the purpose of my campaign—it was not because I feared death! It was only to prove that, even when I was ready for all this, the court of Greece still engaged in the frenzy of destroying Socrates for nothing! Do not calculate from that that I fear death, Crito!........ The moment draws nearer when I must part from friends such as Plato, Crito and Apollodorus; from my wife and children; and from my dear city of Athens! Inside, poison is being prepared for me! In another quarter-nazhigai the guard will arrive, with deadly poison!

Crito : Dear friend! O hero whose mind does not tremble even if a mountain collapses! Those shining, revolving eyes of yours; those broad, throbbing eyebrows; those feeling-filled lips that pour out philosophical explanation without wilting even before enemies in ranks upon ranks—am I ever again going to see them in the form of a Socrates?

Soc. : Do not cry, friend! There—the nectar has arrived! The feast this government offers to the army of knowledge! The gift this wicked country gives to an old man who can barely stand! There, it has arrived!

[A guard enters with a cup of poison.]

Soc. : Prison guard! What must I do with this? Tell me the procedure!

Guard : [Removing Socrates' shackles and weeping] Great sir! You must drink all of the poison........ Then you must keep walking here and there! You must walk like that until your legs become numb.... Then you may sit.... Little by little the body will grow cold. Then you may lie down.

Soc. : A blissful sleep! A sleep that even the maiden of dreams cannot disturb!... Guard! Give it here!

[He takes the cup of poison.]

Crito : Friend!.... You may wait a little while before drinking it! The prison permits that!

Soc. : Crito! What a petty desire you have! Suppose I decide to drink the poison two more nazhigai later! If, before then, my heart suddenly bursts and I die... how will the court of Greece carry out its punishment?... Will a new Socrates be born and come here? No; no... I shall drink it now...

Crito : Friend! Tell me something, one last time!

Soc. : What new thing am I going to say—“Know thyself!” Ask of everything: why, how, and for what purpose! Only by asking in that way did this sculptor who carved statues become a sculptor of thought. “Do not lose your reason and stumble by believing merely because this man said it or that man said it—whatever words anyone speaks, examine them with your own natural rational faculty.” This is what I wish to say to you, and to this world.

Crito : O lion of Athens filled with awakening! Our treasure of gold!.... Great man of Greece! Is this poison going to separate us from you?... Alas, my heart trembles even to think of it! Socrates... how should we bury your body?...

Soc. : If you bury me, throw into the grave with me the bundles of lies that roam this country and bury them too!..... If you burn me, burn the manuscripts of deceivers together with me, reduce them to ash and dissolve them in water!.... Bid me farewell. The poison is calling!.... I am going to take leave of the great people of my country.... My dear great people of Athens! Here Sasrates is going to die happily!... The court of Greece has poured charges upon him by the basketful and filled a cup to the brim with poison, ordering: drink this life-drinking payasam!... The lips of Socrates, which shed teachings, are about to touch the rim of the poison cup! Only another second or two! After that the body and life of Socrates will cut their ties with one another and place a full stop at the end of life's continuing story!—I am coming, Greece!.......I am coming! My dear Athens, I am coming!..... Beautiful streets of Athens that helped me run about and speak with passion! Roadside tree-shades! Verandas beside the houses! This old man takes leave of all of you! Young men with proud gait and proud gaze! Good brothers who guard the country! Beloved people! Elders! Mothers who bore us! Good young lions! May Socrates' final salutation belong to all of you! If anyone truly, sincerely thinks that this old man corrupted the youth of Greece, let them forgive me! Let them forgive me! I am coming. Salutations, salutations. Do not fail to think! O world! Do not fail to think! Know thyself! Know!

[Socrates finishes drinking the poison. He walks until his legs begin to falter. Then he lies down. His body grows cold! The guard pulls the cloak over Socrates' face. A few seconds later Socrates moves the cloak away from his face.]

Soc. : Crito! I forgot to tell you one thing... We owe a chick to Angilipiyas in the neighbouring house. Do not forget to give it to him...

[With that, the breath of the great Greek stopped. Crito wept. After some days, Greece bathed his grave in tears.]

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/05.md`; no wording from the 2009 published-English `Socrates` witness was consulted.
- The bare source line `: !........` is retained as an unassigned structural line. It is not silently assigned to Xanthippe or another speaker.
- `தேசப் பிரஷ்ட உத்திரவு` is rendered as `order of banishment`; the Tamil source form remains untouched.
- The traditional time unit `நாழிகை` is retained as `nazhigai` rather than converted to a modern clock value not supplied by the source.
- The maxims `உன்னையே நீ அறிந்து கொள்!` and the instruction to test any person's words through one's natural `பகுத்த றிவு` are rendered directly as `Know thyself` and `natural rational faculty`.
- The verified assembled source form `சாச்ரடீஸ்` is represented visibly as `Sasrates` at its occurrence in the farewell speech; it is not silently regularized to `Socrates` there.
- The final creditor name `அங்கிளிப்பியசு` is retained as `Angilipiyas`; no external classical identification or famous alternative wording is imported.
- The source irony of poison as `amudham` / nectar and as a government feast for the `army of knowledge` is retained.
- The final source `*` is retained.
