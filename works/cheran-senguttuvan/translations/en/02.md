# Scene — 2

## Cheran's Hall

[Cheran Senguttuvan and Venmal are present.]

Ven: Tell me a delightful story, dear husband!

Cheran: Shall I? The Cheran married the Chola's daughter; a son was born to the Cheran! That son married this statue!

Ven: That is a story I already know.

Cheran: It is also a story that happened.

Ven: Tell me a story that did not happen, dear husband!

Cheran: It will have no savour, my dear.

Ven: Hmm ... then a story of death.

Cheran: Uh-huh. Here, in the *Purananuru*... ...

Ven: Enough; it is a tale of valour, isn't it?

Cheran: A love story wedded to valour! A *puram* scene of a woman who sacrificed both father and husband and then sent her own son to war! Venmal, listen a little!

I myself have written it in a new style.

On the fresh sands of Tamilakam given by the Kaveri,
when the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings
made the field of battle,
fighting to decide
whose flag would fly
upon the finial of the temple tower! In those days,
on one such field,

she heard the news that her father
had leapt forward and died
protecting the motherland;
a Tamil parrot became like wax struck by flame!
Her husband, who had gone to a neighbouring town,
returned with eyes like fire!
Through eyes that poured a flood,
she gave him the news of war.

“You grieve because your father fell in battle,” he said.
“No, my love!” If one warrior is lost
from the army of this mullai-girt land,
is it not like a tear appearing in a fine garment? I thought
that enemies would henceforth enter without hindrance;
the tears held back within me broke their dam, dear,” she said.

He,
like a tiger emerging from its cave,
like a volcano roaring smoke,
took up the sword that would cut down the enemy!
He swore an oath; he raised his radiant face;
“The enemy closing around us will be smashed to powder!” he said;
“I will not return home without recovering the land,” he said!

The maiden
laughed aloud and said, “Go forth, my dear!”
“Whether I return or not,
for whatever may happen, one kiss now—
here, turn around,” he said!
He gave it—and then sent
his arrows against the enemy!

War! War! War! Thus
roared the sound of the war-drum!
Look! Look! Look! The one who belongs to that green parrot—
look at his valour as he leaps upon
the enemy and kills!—
so all his comrades in the army cried in wonder! That
*kattani-muththaal* smiled through her eyes,
hearing of the victories
piling one upon another for her husband on the field!
Forts were freed! The enemy's
horses' legs were broken!
The elephant force was shattered! Spears were smashed!
The great drum beaten by the armies of our victorious king
sounds like thunder in summer! It sounds! It sounds!—
she leapt up and fashioned a new tune!

Warriors came—they said the victory belonged to your husband!
Messengers of the king came! They offered congratulations!
Women friends from nearby houses came—they read
the long victory message and rejoiced! That beauty
shed tears of joy—then,
why did that man come? To bring news of bereavement!

“My dear woman!
O jewel—do not be shaken!
The news of the field is the final news; listen,” he said—

Alas!
The beloved who, beneath mango-leaf festoons upon the wedding stage,
said, “We have become one in life”—
received a spear in his chest
and fell in a noble death—
that *aaranangu* who lives on even after the life has gone,
that imprinted Tamil lotus—
cried aloud—the cluster of flowers

that was this family!
She cursed cruel death,
which had torn apart that cluster—she reviled it!
“What remains now?” she cried,
and “the fruit has rotted,”
and wailed!

Showing eyes that outdid the dew,
speaking *panavella* words,
“Were all the days in which the bridal chamber sang poetry
only earth after all? Is this now an eye
that will never sleep?” she cried! She worshipped in the direction
of the field where her husband's body lay!

Into the ear of that woman fallen in grief,
the enemy's war-drum, beaten in the speed of victory,
struck—and that was enough!
“What is lost is lost—let our whole clan
be wiped away altogether,” she rose and said!
“I shall live not for the house,
but only for the land of boundless fame”—

That queen among Tamil women,
who knew the way of measuring a *nazhi* with a *vatti*,
remembered her pure son whom she had placed in the cradle
and raised, now gone without delay to learn
under the teacher beneath the banyan tree!
She went there—he leapt toward her crying, “Mother!”
A golden child of love, with beautiful baby speech!
“Have father and grandfather returned to the town?” he asked—
“They returned and have gone on to the town of death—
my sugarcane—come, you too!” she called.
“What did they bring back?” he asked!
“Honour! Honour! Honour that does not perish,” she said—
“My son! Come, you too, to taste it,” she said!
He came—the tender shoot of the clan!
The lamp of the family still burning, she tells him—
“Because of the enemy invasion—our family has become,
little brother, a tree whose leaves have fallen!
Do not grieve—
they went to the battlefield and died!
Yet, as long as this
earth exists, they have taken the name
‘Those who guarded honour’!

My son! As long as there is
strength in your shoulder, strike the enemy!
Sing a *parani*! This is your
sacred motherland!—run at once!”
She leapt and embraced the tender boy,
combed and bound his hair, adorned him,
placed in his hand a sword stained in the red ochre of blood,
and blessed him:
“Go, my son, toward the battle-front!”
Is there anyone who would not praise and sing
that sacred scene—
O goddess—in this flower-world?...

Poet: [Interrupting] There is! There is!

Cher: What?

Pul: There are indeed those bloated on plenty, O noble one! There are people in this world who revile our valour!

Cher: Ah!

Pul: Do not wonder that I have come to the silver terrace where the king and queen are alone. I am about to tell you something more astonishing than that....

Cher: Poet!....

Pul: Our valour has been slandered by wicked low fellows, O king!

Cher: Are there people who have slandered it?

Pul: There are! The kings of the northern land are stretching their tails.

Ven: What else? Would they stretch out their swords instead?

Pul: They roar that we marched upon the Himalaya only when Kanaka-Vijaya were not there—let us see them set foot there while they rule, they say.

Cher: They mocked our sword? I will make them bow at our feet. Venmal....work has arrived; a test has come for valour! I shall return!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/02.md`; no wording from the 2009 published-English witness was consulted.
- The source distinguishes `சேரன்:` from later `சேர:` and `புலவர்:` from `புல:`; the English preserves these as `Cheran:` / `Cher:` and `Poet:` / `Pul:` rather than collapsing them silently.
- The long embedded recitation is kept as verse-like lineation and is not reduced to prose summary.
- Cultural forms such as `Purananuru`, `puram`, `nazhi`, `vatti`, and `parani` are retained rather than replaced by externally supplied equivalents.
- Source-sensitive expressions including `கட்டாணி முத்தாள்`, `ஆரணங்கு`, and `பனவெல்ல` are not silently normalized; where their immediate sense is not secure, conservative transliteration remains visible in the English.
- The source's honour / war / family-sacrifice rhetoric, repeated `போர்! போர்! போர்!`, `பார்! பார்! பார்!`, and long-dash transitions are retained.
- The final `வாள்` / `தாள்` contrast is represented as `mocked our sword` / `bow at our feet` without importing wording from any later witness.
- No closing `*` is added because the verified Scene 2 assembly has none.
