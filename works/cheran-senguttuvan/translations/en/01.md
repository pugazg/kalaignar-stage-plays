# Cheran Senguttuvan

Voice before the play begins: A golden leaf from the *Silappathikaram* composed by Ilangovadigal, the younger brother of Senguttuvan!....

The story of bringing a stone from the northern land to install a statue of Kannagi!

Lines of valour that tell the ancient history of Tamilakam!

One day! The day when we sat upon the royal throne with ornamented chariots, horses and a vast army of men!

Then, in the northern region....in the court of Kanaka-Vijaya...

## Scene — 1

Unsurpassed heroes, protectors of the distressed, divine men, oceans of Vedanta, ornaments of the royal clan, emperors of the northern land, incarnations of god—Kanaka and Vijaya—parak! parak!

Kings: Namaskar!

Kanakar: Namaskar!

Vijayar: Namaskar!

Kan: It seems you are waiting for the beauty of the *swayamvara*. Hee! Hee!...

King: Yes, both of you have arrived! If one of you wins the *swayamvara* contest, how are the two of you going to divide that woman between yourselves?

Tamil-country Poet: Then it will be *Bharatham* indeed.

Vij. Who are you, man? What are you saying?

Poet: Tamil! Refined Tamil! Tamil born along with the world! Ancient Tamil that arose before stone arose and before earth arose!

Vij: What business do you have in the *swayamvara* hall?

Poet: Hmm; what a strange question, O king! So, in a *swayamvara* hall, only aged men like you have business there; young men like me have none.

Vij: No mischief. Tell me who you are!

Poet: I am a Tamil poet—I came on pilgrimage to the northern land.

Kan: A poet? Do you know how to compose poetry?

Poet: Tamilakam has never yet seen a poet who cannot compose poetry—or a warrior who cannot raise a sword.

Kan: We shall see that too. Come, recite a poem at once!

Poet: On the banks of the Ganges he fought—the Tamil; on the banks of the Kaveri he sang his fame. He ruled and bound the whole surging sea—the Tamil, whom the world praises as supreme upon the earth!

Kan: Stop! Stop!

Vij: Stop it, man!

Kan: Madman! The Tamil fought on the banks of the Ganges! He will fight! He will fight! If he comes and fights in the age of Kanaka-Vijaya, we shall break his legs—beware.

Kings: Ha! Ha!! Ha.!!!

Vij: Poet, what Kanakar said is worth a thousand lakhs! When your kings engraved their tiger—bow—fish emblems upon the forehead of the Himalaya, perhaps kings like us were not here!

Poet: Why? So that you might fall at their feet and offer tribute? Listen, you men of crazed minds! I return to the southern land today itself. I shall tell the Cheran the words spoken by Kanakan and Vijayan—men whose tongues know no restraint—together with the kings of their feast, ignorant of the power of noble Tamil. O drunken madmen! Tamil valour has not died! We shall meet again. The one who rules is Cheran Senguttuvan! Cheran Senguttuvan!!

## Translation notes

- Immediate authority: verified Tamil `../../scenes/01.md`; the complete 2009 published-English witness was not consulted.
- The pre-scene voice-over is retained as part of the dramatic work rather than omitted as editorial framing.
- `சுயம்வரம்`, `பராக்`, and `பாரதம்` are handled conservatively as `swayamvara`, `parak`, and `Bharatham` rather than replaced with an externally supplied cultural explanation.
- The source distinction between `விஜ.` and `விஜ:` is retained as `Vij.` and `Vij:` instead of silently regularizing the label.
- `புலி—வில்—கயல்` remains the source emblem sequence `tiger—bow—fish`.
- The closing phrase beginning `காவாநாவிற்...` is translated from its immediate Tamil context as an accusation of unrestrained speech and ignorance of Tamil power; no later English witness was used to resolve it.
- No closing `*` is added because the verified Scene 1 assembly has none.
