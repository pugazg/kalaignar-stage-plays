## Dravidian movement conceptual relation

Translation choices should preserve source-supported conceptual resonances with the Dravidian movement, especially Tamil linguistic-cultural self-respect, Tamilakam consciousness, rational agency, dignity, justice, anti-hierarchical reasoning, women's agency and Tamil historical/literary memory.

However, **interpretation must not be inserted into character dialogue**. Where useful, add a separate `Dravidian movement resonance — interpretive note` after the translation.

Scenes 33–35 add:

- Kannagi's immediate rejection of the state/social `thief` label as a dignity-bearing human refusal before full argument begins;
- grief becoming public agency without erasing intimate domestic memory;
- the `bent sceptre` and `tyrannous sceptre` as contrasting images of failed righteous rule;
- scene 35's explicit `good man / powerful man` challenge to power-defined justice;
- Tamil history, Muthamizh, Tamilakam and Tirukkural functioning inside Kannagi's own ethical-political rhetoric;
- a woman using argument and material evidence to displace the accused king from the role of judge;
- Nedunchezhiyan owning the hasty command as his own act and asking forgiveness of Tamil, homeland and mother rather than attributing the tragedy to fate.

Kannagi's vengeance and city-destruction curse remain dramatic character speech; do not present them as a programme of the Dravidian movement.
