## Publication text

### Scene – 2

(Anitas, Meletus, Lycon – all three deeply immersed in serious thought)

Anitas: Seething volcano! Turbulent ocean! More terrible than all these is Socrates! . . . Weapon of Wisdom! All the world would surrender to this missile, it is said!. . . . If that weapon were to be manufactured in Greece we would not be able to raise our heads, Meletus! What do you say, Lycon? . . . .

Lycon: I am a sculptor who carves God out of stone . . .! Once even this Socrates was a sculptor like me! But now he has metamorphosed into a sculptor of thoughts!. . . It’s all right! After the three of us – me who create God out of stones, Meletus the poet who with his Midas touch gives divine shapes to bundles of imagination, and political genius like you – once we have colluded together . . . . Socrates is nothing more than a worthless particle upon an ocean, isn’t he, Anitas. . . !

Meletus: Hurray, Lycon! Kudos. . . ! Only as long as the people are like donkeys would they patiently bear the burden of falsehoods we place on their backs! Only if they are blind will they follow the way we show! We must put an end to him before he succeeds in opening the intellectual eyes of the masses who dare not cross the line we draw . . . .

(All of them go away with a determination)

### Scene – 3

(Socrates is addressing a gathering at the market place)

Socrates: Oh dear people who come to the market to buy things . . . . ! I have something special for you which you cannot find anywhere in the market! An invaluable diamond! . . . A brilliant pearl that is got by diving into the deep ocean called research! A thing that is not sold in the shops! Wisdom! Wisdom! Come here dear men to buy this sweet product!

(A Greek soldier enters)

Soldier: Stop! Stop your harangue!. . . .

Socrates: If I stop, there are several disciples to continue!

Soldier: Stop your mumbo-jumbo! You are spoiling the youth! Raving in the streets like a madman!

Socrates: Go and tell those who sent you here. The teachings of reformers are not like the grass that is eaten by cattle to be exterminated! The brilliance of rationality is not like a wild horse to be noosed and controlled by authorities! . . . .
