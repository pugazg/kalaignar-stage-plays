# Scan 155

Printed page: 121

## Publication text

Chief Justice: Why do you laugh? What is the reason?

Socrates: It’s nothing, my lord! Anitas forgets for a moment that he himself is old, and calls me an old man. The thought came to my mind . . . and I laughed!. . . . My hair as well as Anitas’ hair has turned as grey as the froth on the sea, hasn’t it? Courtiers! . . . What say you, Anitas, ain’t I right?

Meletus: Socrates! The trial does not relate to your hair! I wish to draw your attention to this fact.

Socrates: Thank you very much Meletus! . . . but . . . one thing! . . . even as the head is most important to the eight-span tall body . . . , head is very significant in this case! My reasoning emanates from my head! . . . and to thwart nonsensical thoughts arising from your head. Political arrogance that comes out of Anitas’ head – the conflict is among the three forces – that is the cause of this case!

Chief Justice: Socrates! What is your reply to these charges levelled against you?

Socrates: Even the hardcore law would show mercy to me on hearing my explanations . . . but would this assembly show me mercy?

Anitas: Note this point my lord! It is contempt of court and the assembly! This is how he corrupts all young men!

Socrates: How can an old man spoil young men? . . . Am I a prostitute who casts her net to catch young men? . . . a damsel to feast their eyes?

Meletus: You have an enchanting language which sluts do not have . . . . Use of enthralling words which the whores do not have . . . Verbal tricks . . . endearing phrases . . . You have mastered such tricks! . . .

Socrates: You might even call me a wizard! . . . Dear loving young man! . . . Do I corrupt young men? Or do they come to me on their own and get spoilt?

Meletus: You voluntarily accost them and corrupt them! . . . At the market places, at junctions of streets, on pavements, in midstreets, in shades of trees where some four men would gather to rest . . . there is not a time when you do not harangue!

Socrates: So in the whole of Athens I am the only person who corrupts the young men . . . isn’t it so?

Meletus: Yes.

Socrates: You do not spoil?

Meletus: No!

Socrates: Anitas?

Meletus: No!

Socrates: Sculptor Lycon?

Meletus: No!
