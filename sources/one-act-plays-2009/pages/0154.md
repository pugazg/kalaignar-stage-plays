# Scan 154

Printed page: 120

## Publication text

Soldier: You big mouth! Let the court hear your declamations, tomorrow! Now come with me!

(drags Socrates away)

Crito: Atrocious! Where do you take away our Treasure-house of Wisdom in his old age? . . . Leave him . . . I say . . . let him go.

Socrates: No, my friend! Don’t stop him! Where do I go? Only to the court of law, don’t I? Not to the execution chambers! . . . Let me go! As long as there is clarity of thought and strength of conviction, the wise and the discrete need not fear anything! I’ll go. I am going to confront the unfair and unjust men who try to drive out the clear-headed thinkers!

(The soldier drags away Socrates)

### Scene – 4

#### Court of Law

(The Athenian court of law consisting of five hundred members has assembled. Judges are seated on a wooden bench. Some planks are covered with mat. Short wooden paling separates the rows of judges and the commoners. On the right side of the hall the chair for the Chief Justice is placed. By its side is another chair. That is where the plaintiff and respondent come to present their cases to the judge. On the left side there are seated separately the two groups with their witnesses. A box known as Hedge Log was brought and after opening the seal, the box was tipped and the contents were transferred to the table. An attendant reads out the charge sheets.)

“Meletus, son of Meletus of Pitanspet, presents the following charges against Socrates, son of Soberinnisco of Alopesil: That Socrates does not approve of the Gods worshipped by the country and the government. He tries to corrupt the young men with his preaching. It is fitting that he be sentenced to death.

(Like Meletus, Anitas and Lycon also level charges)

Anitas: Socrates! . . . A soul not fit to walk the street! . . . . The creature which finds fault with our democracy is venomous all over! . . . The aged man of mean character who rouses the youth against the government and the penal codes with his persuasive arguments which compel those who have not listened to him yet to crave for it . . . .

(Socrates laughs aloud)
