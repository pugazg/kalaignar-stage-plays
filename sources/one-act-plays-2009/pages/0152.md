# Scan 152

Printed page: 118

## Publication text

Socrates: What to do. . . ! Had I been an inventive poet, I would have eulogised your huge waist calling it lightning! Crito. . . . You know Socrates never tells lies; I speak the truth. . . because I see the brilliance of lightning in her eyes! . . . ain’t I right, my friend!

Xanthippe: Enough! Enough! . . . Limit your arguments, explications, discussions, shaky and slippery philosophy to the passers-by ! No wonder, your first wife Mirdo, it was her good time that she passed away! I am here to suffer with . . . . a family. . . . Should not this man ever have any concern about his wife and three children!

Socrates: (pointing at the youth surrounding him . . .) Look! I work for these children also . . . my darling! The sun keeps discharging his duty to all living creatures that populate the earth and the oceans surrounding it!

Xanthippe: You always speak of the earth and the sky, but desert your wife midstream . . . (angry with her own self) eh, Xanthippe, why do you get entangled in such rows!. . .

Get lost! . . . Live as you like! The graveyard is a better place than this confounded house!.. . Instead of struggling with this logician, it would be better to put a noose around my neck and hang myself.! Yes, die! Die!

(She goes into the house, making a big hue and cry. As soon as she goes in, Socrates looks around at all, and smilingly says . . .)

Socrates: What a relief! Thundering has ended. . . . When there was lightning I knew it would thunder. Young Athenians! . . . many a hindrance blocks the ways of aspirants! It has been so with the family! . . . What about the country . . . much more so! Politician Anitas does not want any circumstance to come up that might discover the misdeeds and atrocities he has done. Meletus is happy to be living in a world of myth he has created. Lycon, the carver in stone and smith, who makes statues of brass, fear that my intellectual arguments have started shaking their very foundations, and are furious with me. . . The greatness of Greece is not unknown to the world! But that does not permit the act of hiding the tears and scratches that have occurred. It is like trying to conceal the stinking wounds with the scent of civet! . . That’s why, my friends, I bow and beg of you to learn to think! Wisdom! Wherever it lies in the world I appeal to you to seek and acquire it! . . . . “Know yourself!” I call upon you all to realise this truth. Young men dearer to me than my life! I invite you to learn to inquire into every thing.

(Just then Xanthippe brings a pitcher full of water and pours it down on Socrates’ head)

Crito: Ah! What ‘s this? What’s this?. . . .

Socrates: Do not get shocked, Crito! . . . It’s nothing. . . . First you saw the flash of lightning. . . then it thundered a loud, now it is raining. . . that’s all!
