## Publication text

#### Scene – 2

**Cheran’s Assembly Hall**

(Senguttuvan and Venmal present)

Ven: Tell me an interesting story, my darling!

Cheran: Shall I tell you? Cheran married Cholan’s daughter; Cheran had a son by the marriage! The youth married this young lady!

Ven: This is an already known story.

Cheran: A story that has happened too.

Ven: Tell me a story that has not taken place, my dear!

Cheran: That may not be interesting, dear.

Ven: Hm. . . How about a love story?

Cheran: No. no. In Purananuru . . . .

Ven: Enough. It is about valour, isn’t it?

Cheran: It is a love story about marrying valour! It is about a woman who lost her father and her husband in war now sending her son to the battlefield! Listen to it for a while, Ven! I have written it myself in a new style.

There was a time when Chera, Chola, and Pandya kings

On the new sands of the Tamil Cauvery were waging

Wars as to who should fly the flag atop the tower!

Then on a battlefield her father plunged with power

Fought and died. Hearing the news the Tamil woman

Melted like a lighted candle! Now her husband who’d gone

Out returned, with eyes burning red! With tears in her eyes

She told him of the war and her father’s demise!

Did you cry for your father’s death, my dear!

‘No my dear!’

If one warrior dies in the army of jasmine land, one the less,

It’s like a tear in our clothes, and foes would enter fearless!

So ’s my fear; hence tears broke barriers!

He,

Like a tiger that comes out of its cave,

Like the smoke emitting volcano,

Grabbed the sword that’d chop the foes!

Vowed vengeance! Lifted up his fulgent face!
