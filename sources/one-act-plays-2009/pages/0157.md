When I was commissioned by the senate with three others to kill Lennon I refused, walked out and went home; that was the time when I could have been executed! Luckily, if that was the desire of the Court that it should witness the death of Socrates, rather than the Senate, then I would not protest now!

You may recommend imprisonment – It is better to die instantly than to remain forever the slave of the jail warders!

You would let me go if I am prepared to pay the fine! But, had I been rich enough to pay the fine I would rather have committed more of such crimes as my money could afford! Banishment can be accepted . . . . One can wander freely around in a different country . . . But there is a question: when my own country does not approve of my ways of thinking and arguments how can it be taken for granted that people of a different country would accept them? My dear Athenians! You might instead grant me the punishment that is given to the winner in the horse-drawn chariot race at Olympia – an honorarium for bravery – and let me live in joy and peace till the end! And continue to provide me with all necessities to continue with my propaganda! That would be the right respect that you could show me and my principles!

(All at the assembly are shocked)

Chief Justice: Irresponsible speech! . . ., an insult! Let the members of the jury vote and decide. . . .! Is it Death to Socrates? Or . . . . amnesty? Let us count the shells on both sides.

(Shells are arranged on a plank on both sides of the petitioner and the defendant. Poll is over. The shells are counted. 281 shells favoured Death sentence . . . and 220 shells for his pardon. The assembly looks at Socrates’ eyes. There is a new brightness in his eyes!)

Chief Justice: 220 voted for his pardon! 281 have voted in favour of his death! According to the votes, Socrates is condemned to death by poison!

Socrates: The court of law of the city state of Athens has a silent smile on reading out the proud decree! I am proud to be the first in history to receive this judgement from the Athenian court! They have rewarded me with death for having instructed them to hold up the lamp of intelligence in their hands! 281 . . .221! 31 votes . . . had they been polled on this side, the life of Socrates could have been saved! Even those votes were bought at a high price and this fact cannot be hidden! Nothing to worry! It is far better to speak in my style and die than to play the second fiddle to the verdict and expectation of the court! . . . .
