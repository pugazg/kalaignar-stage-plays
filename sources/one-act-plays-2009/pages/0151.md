## Publication text

With this background in mind, start reading the play.

(Short statured – sturdy build – eyes revolving like two bright lights – flat nose – expressive thick eyebrows – thick lips – a small beard – an ordinary single cloth made of silk and cotton covering the body – this is great Socrates, the greatest contribution Greece ever made to the world. This is the man of principles who stops and argues with the passers-by, in all nooks and crannies, and lanes and streets of Athens. The first scene of the play opens in front of his house.)

### Scene – 1

#### At the threshold of Socrates’ house

(People move up and down the street. Socrates calls their attention in a loud voice)

Socrates: Dear handsome youth of the glorious city of Athens! Come here! I call upon you to transform the stench of Athens and make it fragrant. Valiant youth who are prepared to rush to the war front impelled by impassioned blood! I call these young people . . . . gather around in large groups! Bravery is not saleable if not accompanied by tact and discretion! [Valour without discretion has no value.][Discretion is the better part of valour] Dear talented youth, a sword sharpened and the lance borne on urging shoulders are alone not enough! Take from me the weapon I give you and wield it well! It’s the weapon called reason! Rationality is the weapon! Weapon of knowledge!! It’s a new weapon, which can wipe out of existence the blind-minded, dark, devilish monster called ignorance! A revolutionary weapon that reveals the inside of the never – retreating warriors! Tiger-like youth! Come with me! The enemy has entered our country . . . . without an army! . . . . He has looted the hearts of the vulgar populace . . . . That enemy known as superstition has crept into Athens in the utter darkness of stupidity! Talented young men, now let us go about reforming the glorious Athens into an oasis of wisdom! March forward on our mission!

(The youth, and the warriors stand around and listen to the words of Socrates. Xanthippe, wife of Socrates, stands at the threshold frowning at her husband Socrates, unhappy with his conduct . . . Crito, an aged friend of Socrates, enters. On seeing him . . . .)

Socrates: Crito! Have you come? Come, welcome! . . . I am marching ahead in my old age, duty-bound, with the intention of taking the youth into the path of righteousness! . . . . I am blessed with a second wife . . . Xanthippe. . . . Look how wrathfully she tries to obstruct me! Look! Look! Are they eyes? . . . They are like a terrible flash of lightning; yes, lightning!

Xanthippe: (angrily) Yes, lightning, indeed. . . . To this old man, wife’s eyes appear like lightning . . .!
