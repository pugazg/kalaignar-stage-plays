Crito: My dear friend! Mountains might crumble; but you, brave man, you have a heart that will never crumble. When can I see again in your frame and form, those brilliant rolling eyes, those broad eyebrows, the thick lips that uttered explanations non-stop and unfailingly to the queries that were hurled by your opponents?

Socrates: Don’t cry, my friend! There comes the ambrosia! The feast hosted by the State to Socrates’ army of wisdom! A reward the sinister State gives to an aged person! There it has arrived!

(The guard comes with a cup of poison)

Socrates: Jailer! What should I do with it? Tell me the procedure of consuming it!

Guard: (Removes the shackles and says crying) Grand old man! You must drink the entire poison.... Then you must walk up and down! Continue to walk till the legs become numb.... then you can sit ... gradually your body becomes cold and chill ... then you can lie down.

Socrates: Happy blissful sleep! A sleep that cannot be disturbed even by dream-maiden! ... Guard! Give it to me.

(He takes the cup)

Crito: My friend! You can drink it even a little later! It is permitted in the prison!

Socrates: Crito! You have a trivial desire! Imagine I take the poison some forty minutes later! And if I happen to die of heart-attack in the mean time ..... How can the verdict of the Greek court be carried out? Will another Socrates be born again to fulfil this? No, no .... I shall drink it right now!

Crito: My friend! Tell me something in the end?

Socrates: What new thing am I going to say? ... Gnothi seauton ... Know yourself! Question everything as What, Why, and How? That is how the carver in stone became a carver of thought ... a thinker. “Never lose your sense by accepting anything as authority as this man said, or that man said - whatever any man says try to assess their words in your own way using your rationality.” That is the message I want to tell you and the world at large.

Crito: Oh, the colossal lion of Athens! Our treasure! Greek genius! Is the poison going to separate you from me? Alas! My heart trembles to think of it! Socrates! How do you wish your body to be disposed of?....
