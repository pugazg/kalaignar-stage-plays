### Scene – 5

#### Prison house

(Socrates’ legs are chained and locked; he keeps moving up and down in the small space. The door opens and Crito enters.)

Socrates: Crito! My dear friend! Come, come . . . .

(comes out to see) . . . Oh . . . . who is that?

Crito: Your wife . . . and children!

(She weeps, children weep, and the small baby also screams.)

Socrates: Oh my dear! . . . . did you see? You have always found fault with me as a worthless fellow wasting my time in idle philosophical debates; and been angry with me for getting involved in logical disputations. Now see, your husband has become a great hero praised by the world, and acclaimed by the country as a martyr! You are very fortunate, indeed! You are wife to a duty-bound hero who opposed the power of money and the army, and finally closing his eyes in death dauntless and in pride! . . . Take care of our children! When they had grown to be adults and deviate from the path of ethical goodness, try to correct their ways as I did yours . . . Go . . . it’s getting late. Guards will become angry. Crito! See them off.

(Crito leads them out. Then Crito alone returns)

Socrates: Crito! Don’t you know! Thirty days’ imprisonment is over today! According to the court verdict today is the final day when I must take poison and die!

Crito: Socrates! Now poison is going to kill you who could not be defeated by philosophical argumentations! . . . I have a suggestion! There’s still time; and I have a lot of glittering gold! What if we could make good our escape by winning them over to our side . . .

Socrates: I feel ashamed, Crito! Socrates is not afraid of death. The reason why I tried to make an issue of the conditions laid down by the court, expressed willingness to pay the fine, expatiated on the decree of exile, and elaborated on the purport of my propaganda . . . all these not out of fear of death! That was mainly to establish that the Greek court was quite unnecessarily intent upon killing me as if with a vengeance! So do not wrongly estimate that I was afraid of death, Crito!
