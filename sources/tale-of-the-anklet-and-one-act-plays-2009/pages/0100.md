Malaivanar bows to the royal will in apparent regret, but Arulalar is beside himself with grief.

Arulalar [pleading]: No, Your Majesty, let it not be recorded in history that a bonfire was made of a Tamil work here in the Court of the Pandyas who have, from time immemorial, nursed the Tamil language and literature. I relinquish my claim and I appeal to you, sir, to issue a decree in favour of Malaivanar, so that the work may not be lost to the people.

Nedunchezhian: Truth has spoken and I decree that Arulalar is the real author of the literary work. He shall also get a reward of 5000 kazhanjus of gold to equip him to pursue his research studies. As for Malaivanar, he has been shown to be a plagiarist; the ignominy of the exposure is sufficient chastisement for him and I let him off with the warning that he should desist from being a drag on society. The Court shall now disperse.

The courtiers stand, as the king and the queen leave the hall.
