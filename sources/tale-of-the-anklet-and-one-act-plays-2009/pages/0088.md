Kannagi: Madhavi is my sister, my Lord, and even in the depths of grief you should not think ill of her. How is Manimekalai, my Lord?

Kovalan: That child is the symbol of my sin, Kannagi, and I would rather blot it out of my memory.

Kannagi: Oh, do not say so, my Lord, for Manimekalai is my daughter too, although she knows only Madhavi as her mother.

Kovalan [overwhelmed by her magnanimity]: You are a deity, Kannagi, and not a woman of this earth. I can never hope to make amends for the wrong done to you, O my Goddess!

Kannagi: My Lord! My Lord!

Kovalan [in intense self-pity]: In the field of maritime trade I had helped to put Tamilnad in the forefront. But in my personal life I fell a prey to wiles and traded my honour for filth. Today I am a bankrupt in every sense of the term and now that I stand dishonoured, let me go down unwept by the world. Let the sea that nurtured my fortune draw a curtain over my head!

Kannagi: As long as there is life there is hope, my Lord. Let me fetch some gold from the coffers of my father, so that you may start to rebuild your business.

Kovalan: My dear, don’t you think I have had enough of disgrace, without it being said that I sponged on my in-laws?

Kannagi: Then let me seek the help of my father-in-law whose granary is stocked to the brim with pearls.

Kovalan: Having squandered the capital which my father provided, I cannot bear to touch another coin of his. I do not see a way out, Kannagi, except that which any man may embark upon, unfettered and free, when he yearns for a permanent end to his misery.

Kannagi [getting an idea]: My Lord, my anklets belong to me and no one else. They would fetch a very good price and the capital would suffice for the business.

Kovalan [mortified]: You want me to sell the anklets which are all that is left of your ornaments? What a sinner I am, to live to hear this?

Kannagi [rallying him]: I do not need an ornament, my Lord, if it is not to be of use to you. After all, cannot we secure a pair of new anklets, once your business is re-established?

Kovalan [coming round]: There is something in what you say; but I cannot bear to be seen here in Poompuhar, selling my wife’s anklets. Everyone knows me in this place and the ignominy of the transaction would prove too much for me.
