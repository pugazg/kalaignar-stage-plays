# Scan 130

Printed page: 96

## Publication text

Kanaka [shamefaced and sullen]: 18 years.

Senguttuvan: And how long did the battle last between Rama and Ravana?

Vijaya: 18 months

Senguttuvan What of the war of Bhaarata?

Kanaka: It raged for 18 days.

Senguttuvan : I find that the figure is etched on your memory. Let me brand it deeper by reminding you that Senguttuvan’s war in the North was crowned with success in 18 brief nazhigais*!

Kanaka and Vijaya [together]: We plead for mercy, sir!

Senguttuvan: We do not have the authority to pardon you. That prerogative rests with Taminad and it is on the soil of the South that you have to kneel and pray for forgiveness and freedom.

Villavan [to Senguttuvan]: 32 months have elapsed, Your Majesty, since our army set out from Vanchi.

Senguttuvan: Yes, Villavan, but it has been time fruitfully spent. Let us return, after hewing rock from the Himalayas, for the statue of Kannagi. Kanaka and Vijaya here shall have the honour of carrying the rock to Tamilnad. Let them realize the weight of their penance, ere they receive the benefit of the magnanimity of our land...

* * *

The Statue of Kannagi has been installed and is being consecrated at a solemn function attended by a vast concourse. Kings and princes are present, at the invitation of Senguttuvan; notable among them are the rulers of Malava and Kongu, and King Kayavahu of Lanka. The poets and men of letters are led by Ilango and Sathanar.

A wave of respectful silence spreads through the congregation, from the fringes, as a nun enters, leading a child. The nun is Madhavi and the child is her daughter Manimekalai. With a faraway look in her eyes, Madhavi walks up to the Statue and deposits her daughter at Kannagi’s feet.

The child gazes in wonderment at the deity and at the assembly plunged in momentary silence, too deep for words. As yet, Manimekalai, of course, has no means of knowing that she herself is to be the heroine of an epic, to be written by Sathanar, the sequel to Ilango’s Silappathikaram which is the Tale of the Anklet.

---

* nazhigai = a unit of time equivalent to 24 minutes.
