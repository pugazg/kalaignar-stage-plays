The Tamils have a long and venerable history and their writ had run even in far-off lands, across the high seas. Many foreign countries had established diplomatic relations with the land of the Tamils. *Silappathikaram* is not only a story of eddying emotions; it is also an elaborate history of Tamilagam.

Poompuhar, the birthplace of the characters of *Silappathikaram* – Kannagi and Madhavi and Kovalan – was a harbour-city handling export and import on a large scale, as has been recorded in detail in *Pattinappalai*: “Steeds from across the seas and loads of pepper delivered by barges from inland; gems and gold from the northern hills, sandalwood and *akhil* from the western ghats, pearls brought from the southern sea and coral from the eastern sea; goods from the plains of the Ganga and produce from the banks of the Cauveri itself – a resplendent sight, the scene of active prosperity here….” Karikaalan was a Chola monarch of great renown who ruled from this Capital and his period has been estimated to be from 60 B.C. to 10 B.C.

King Karikaalan has also been referred to as the “great Chola who attained rest in Kurapalli”. This description has evidently arisen on account of the place of his demise which was ‘Tiruvidaikazhi’, otherwise known as ‘Kurapalli’. In a poem praising Karikaalan, Kaarikkannanaar of Kavirippoompattinam has also mentioned the name of the Pandya king Peruvazhuthi who was described after his death, as ‘Peruvazhuthi who attained rest in Velliyambalam’. Kaarikkannanaar’s poem has been included under the serial number 58 in *Puranaanooru* and its gist is as follows: “You are the monarch of Woriyur which is the abode of virtue; and he wields his sceptre from Mathurai where Tamil flourishes. You are the Lord of the Cauveri; and he is of the Pandyan stock with a proud record in battle-valour, and among the natural assets of his kingdom are sandalwood of the Pothigai hills and pearls from the sea. Together, you two are as awe-inspiring as a pair of great gods. May your friendship flourish! May your spear dominate the field of battle! May you two march in splendour and engrave your respective emblems, the tiger and the fish, on the hills of other climes!”

From this poem of Kaarikkannanaar it may be inferred that Karikaal Chola and Pandyan Peruvazhuthi were contemporaries. As we have already seen, Karikaalan’s period was from 60 B.C. to 10 B.C. It was during this time that Augustus Caesar ruled in Rome, (i.e.) from 39 B.C. to A.D. 14. It is clear that the Tamil king who sent envoys to the court of Augustus Caesar was Pandyan Peruvazhuthi.
