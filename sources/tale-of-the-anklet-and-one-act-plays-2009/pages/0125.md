under his chariot-wheels now? What a fall, Nedunchezhian? You are an object of derision for the whole of Tamilnad and Thirukkural, the scripture of the Tamil people, points an accusing finger at your awry sceptre! (Her voice takes on a tremor that strikes terror into the hearts of the courtiers) Having lost your honour, do you still need a marbled hall and a terraced palace? What is your title worth now, that you cling to your throne? Having held justice to ridicule, why does your trembling hand still hold the sceptre? The cold wind of Truth is blowing belatedly into this Court and is that why you cower there in the shade of your gorgeous Umbrella? Archers and swordsmen, cavalry and elephant-brigade – will these shield you from the verdict of history? Your tears are too late, Pandyan, but weep all the same and as you do so, think how you thrust me back into the darkness of grief when I had worked my way to the distant dawn. When my defenceless husband fell a prey to your sword of tyranny, would he not have writhed in agony even as he bled to death? And now my words stab your heart like a hundred swords and there is not a soul to pity you as you squirm like a worm cast in the noonday sun....

Nedunchezhian (his voice broken): Kannagi, your husband was not a thief! I am the thief, for I have robbed you of your husband. In my haste, I uttered the order, “Kill and return” (konru varugha) instead of saying “Bring him here” (kondu varugha) and the fatal slip has brought a punishment worse then death on my head... O Tamil, O motherland, O my mother, forgive me, although I am undeserving of any grace...

He slides down from his throne and falls full length on the floor. Soon his body is still.

Kopperundevi: O, my Lord, my Lord! (she too falls.)
