### 29 Message of the Single Anklet

Kitchen in Madhari’s dwelling. Kannagi is busy cooking food. Kovalan is seated near the doorstep, a picture of dejection.

Kannagi [calling softly]: My Lord . . . why this spell of depression?

Kovalan: I was thinking, my dear, as to what best I can do to make a living and seek to rebuild our fortune.

Kannagi: The anklets I wear were your present to me, my Lord, on the day of our marriage. They are worth 5000 kazhanjus of gold.

Kovalan: The very thought of selling your anklets stabs my heart like a hundred daggers, Kannagi . . . [Drifting back into the past] The pageantry of the celebration is still etched on my memory ... the gorgeous procession through the streets of Poompuhar and the grand function witnessed by the elite of the city . . . With my own hands I fastened the anklets, while my demure bride blushed in fond pride and stood in tremulous joy on the threshold of a new life . . . And today in the very same hands, here in Mathurai, am I to carry the anklets for sale? . . . The chiming of your anklets is a constant reminder to me of the only ornament I have not robbed my wife of, and should I have to sink deeper in felony? . . .
