# 13 Kannagi in Loneliness — continued

Kannagi [smiling through her grief]: If I had been your daughter, auntie, how could I have got your son for husband?

Masathuvan [grimly]: I would have brought for you a bridegroom more handsome and noble than Kovalan.

Kannagi [with a touch of firmness]: Pardon me, uncle, but I cannot bear to hear my husband insulted. Do you think auntie here would keep quiet if I were to speak ill of you?

Masathuvan: That shows you don’t know your auntie. Had I strayed off the leash like Kovalan, your auntie would have started an epic war. At any rate she would not be dumb and passive like you.

Kannagi: Don’t you all worry overmuch, uncle. He will return soon, for the love he has for me is not a flash of lightning in the high skies; it is a column of light piercing the horizon even. He is mine, till eternity and what does a brief separation matter?

Masathuvan [disarmed by her devotion]: Until he returns, Kannagi, why don’t you come and stay with us?

Kannagi: Uncle, it was you who enabled us to set up a separate household, in keeping with the traditions of the Tamil people. Why should I wind it up unless I am proved to be defeated? He might have left me in the midst of flames but my duty is to stay at my post until my Lord and Master returns.

Masathuvan [won over]: Daughter of Manaykan, my tributes to your determination. You are an eloquent example of the greatness of the Tamil society, for you represent the essence of its fundamentals. May your grief vanish soon, Kannagi! [He gets up to leave.]

Masathuvan’s wife [also rising]: We came here to try and console you but you have given us strength to stand up to the ordeal, Kannagi. [To Devanti who has just come] Devanti, please look after our Kannagi; [pointedly] and if she needs any help, at least you should come and tell us.

The Elders leave. Kannagi goes up to the front-door with them.

* * *

Kovalan’s house. As Manaykan and his wife enter, Vasantamalai is leaving, with some jewel-caskets on a tray. At the sight of Kannagi’s parents she trembles in guilt and hurries away with face averted. Evidently, she has been imposing on Kannagi and abstracting the valuables in Kovalan’s name. With an added anxiety on their minds, the Elders enter the courtyard where Kannagi receives them.

Manaykan: Kannagi, your husband is lost to you, but is it necessary that you should part with wealth too?
