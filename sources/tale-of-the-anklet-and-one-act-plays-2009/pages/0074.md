Brahmin-lady: Sir, my husband has rejected me, all because he found me guilty of an unconscious fault. [Pouring out her misery] He had a mongoose for pet and one day when I returned from the river I found it blood-spattered and grinning. In my haste and panic, I thought it had bitten my child and so I threw my pitcher on the mongoose and it died on the spot. It was only afterwards did I discover that the mongoose had actually bitten a cobra to death. It had saved my child from the cobra and when I realised what I had done, my remorse knew no bounds. On my husband’s return in the evening, I told him of the day’s happenings and prayed forgiveness for having killed his pet. He was, however, too furious to pardon me and now he is going away there, in that boat. Won’t you do something to make him relent? If he does not deign to come back, I have no other go but to jump into the river with my child and put an end to my anguish that way!

Kovalan [shouting authoritatively, in the direction of the receding boat]: Hey, boatman, stop rowing and return to the bank. It is a matter of life and death.

*The boat stops and then returns. The brahmin-lady rushes to the water-edge and pleads with the passenger.*

Brahmin-lady: Once again I seek your forgiveness. Please come home, at least for the sake of this child of yours.

Her husband [callous]: A sinner like you is better dead than be alive. I will not have anything to do with you.

Kovalan [to the brahmin]: Sir, will your action in leaving your family and home help to bring the mongoose back to life?

Brahmin [sullen]: I am only going on a pilgrimage to wipe out the sin of killing, which my wife is guilty of.

Kovalan [patiently reasoning with him]: If, as a result of your departure now, your wife and child were to starve to death, would not that sin be on your head? And in that case, would you go on another pilgrimage to wash off that sin? Coming to the root cause of the trouble, you will admit that the mongoose could not have known whether the snake would bite the child; and yet it killed the reptile. Likewise, when your wife killed the mongoose she did not know whether the pet had bitten the child. Don’t the accounts tally, without your having to put yourself to the trouble and expense of a pilgrimage?

Brahmin [convinced by the reasoning]: I never analysed the problem that way! [Getting off the boat] I owe you many thanks, sir, for having come to my assistance. [He rejoins his wife whose gratitude to Kovalan is too deep for words.]
