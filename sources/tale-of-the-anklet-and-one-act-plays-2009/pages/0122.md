# Scan 122

Printed page: 88

## Publication text

Poompuhar, the capital city of the Chola Kingdom. I am the daughter-in-law of Masathuvan the merchant-prince...

Nedunchezhian: What is your petition in this Court?

Kannagi: Hasty Monarch who has ascended the throne to blunt the glory of the three royal houses of Tamilnad, cradled by three seas yielding pearls, and in the shade of three flags; with three drums; and three Sangams to nurture Tamil! Cultivate the patience to hear in full who I am, before you are ready for my petition...

Nedunchezhian: Who, then, are you?

Kannagi: I am Kannagi, who have come here with the single anklet, with the dust of the street on my person, with tresses unloosed and with brow in a frown. I am Kannagi who have come, making bold to revile you in your own Court. That is all you know so far.

O Pandya king, whose fame is spread far and wide! Your envoys are there in the Court of Rome and here are Romans in this Court of yours as envoys and warriors. Your emblem is known in distant climes and your flag is flying very high. In an act to break the flag-mast and make it come down in a crash, in an act to bring the contumely of foreigners on your anointed head, your tyrannous Sceptre inflicted the precipitate sentence of death on one by name Kovalan. Know now, Monarch, that I am Kannagi, the wife of Kovalan!

Nedunchezhian (exclaiming): Kovalan’s wife! The wife of the thief who stole the anklet!

Kannagi: Enough, Monarch, guard your tongue, although you guard nothing else. Was my husband a thief? No! On the other hand, the thieves are those evil men who foisted the blame on him.

Nedunchezhian: Let not grief and fury blind your vision, Kannagi. Your husband was caught red-handed and don’t you know that justice in Tamilnad prescribes death for the thief?

Kannagi: O Pandya king who cites the Scripture of justice! What a mockery have you made of justice here, where the law is not to protect the good but to serve the needs of the mighty! That is why you poured the treasure of my life into the abyss of death. O you who go in pomp on a caparisoned elephant and in the shade of an umbrella gorgeous, tell me why a case which ought to have been heard in this Court was disposed of in an abandoned hall somewhere in the city. Tell me, Monarch, why the sword of the executioner moved so fast, even without heeding the defence of the accused. Is that to be called justice and righteousness and rule of law and good governance rolled into one? Dare you call yourself a king practising virtue and upholding truth?
