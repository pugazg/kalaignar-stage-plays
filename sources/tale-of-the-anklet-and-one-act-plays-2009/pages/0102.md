gets up and hurries to greet the officer.

Chief Goldsmith: Pleased to see you, sir. You must have come with some message from the palace.

Officer [exhibiting the anklet]: The clasp of the queen’s anklet is broken and they want it to be mended quick.

Chief Goldsmith [examining the anklet]: Tell them, sir, that it will be ready by this time tomorrow.

The Chief Goldsmith places the anklet in the safe but before he can turn the key in the lock, he is distracted by the noise of hefty hammering from the anvil of a very junior assistant.

Chief Goldsmith [rushing to the assistant]: Is that the way to treat gold, you juvenile delinquent?

Taking the half-finished ornament from the assistant’s anvil, he hands it over to a more experienced artisan.

Chief Goldsmith [to the second assistant]: Here, you complete this work. [Turning to the junior assistant] Learn to respect the royal metal first, fellow, or switch over to some other profession.

He returns to his place and turns the key in the lock, blissfully ignorant of the fact that in his brief absence the officer has abstracted the queen’s anklet and concealed it in the folds of his dress.

Officer: So I shall tell them that the queen’s anklet will be ready tomorrow.

Chief Goldsmith: Yes, sir.

He escorts the officer up to the entrance of the workshop.
