# Scan 93

Printed page: 59

Running head: `Tale of the Anklet`

## Transcription

Mathurai? Then let me be your travel companion.

Kannagi [to Kovalan]: My Lord, how far is Mathurai from here?

Kovalan [evasively]: It would be five six kaadhams+, dear.

Kaunthi Adigal [to Kannagi]: He means five multiplied by six, that is thirty kaadhams, child. Why, are you tired? [To Kovalan] I can walk a little ahead of you, in case you would like to carry Kannagi in your arms. [Kovalan is embarrassed]. I suggested it because I thought it would be the appropriate penalty for having put her to this ordeal!

They resume the journey.

Kaunthi Adigal [talking, seemingly to reduce the tedium of the travel]: Kannagi, of course, would not be used to such journeys. We have to watch every step of ours, or we would find ourselves in pitfalls, set up by man and nature in conspiracy, when the hollows made for digging up tubers get filled with withered leaves and flowers. [Gently] Is not the path of life too similar?

Kovalan [with conviction]: Yes, Mother, and I say this from bitter experience . . .

Kaunthi Adigal [continuing]: We could cut across the paddy-fields but there in the canals, the fish seeking to escape from beavers would fall on us and that would scare Kannagi. There is a path through the sugar cane plantations but if we are ever so careless we would puncture beehives and the honey would go to waste as it flows into water ...

Kovalan: That was the way my wealth went, Mother!

Kaunthi Adigal [as if she had not heard Kovalan’s interjection]: We might walk through orchards but there we have to bow as we go, or else the jackfruit would bang our heads like enemies in war or like true friends who check us when we tend to go astray . . .

Kovalan [bursting out again]: Alas, no one—not even my conscience— checked me or blocked my way when I slipped off the path of good conduct.

Kaunthi Adigal: Don’t you know that the mind is a monkey which goes meandering only when the conscience is in slumber? Forget the past, Kovalan. The future presents a challenge; so rise to the occasion, boy, hoping for the best and setting store by nothing except sincere effort.

They keep walking.

* * *

Forest. Kovalan has come to fetch drinking water from a spring when Kausikan, a brahmin from Poompuhar meets him, with an epistle from Madhavi. Kovalan reads the message.

Madhavi’s voice: Cherished and Revered! My obeisance to you. No one but I myself

+ kaadham = a measure of linear distance of about 10 miles.
