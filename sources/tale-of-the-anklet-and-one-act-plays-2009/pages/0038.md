# 2 Senguttuvan’s Birthday

*Sanctum in the Chera palace. The youthful prince Senguttuvan is seated in front of a full mirror, dressing. His wife Venmaal assists. Gazing at her adoring face in the mirror, Senguttuvan turns to see the reality of the image and smiles in affection.*

**Senguttuvan:** My dear, you are all set to dress me up and send me to the Royal Court. I am just thinking that the day might not be far off, when I would have to change all this finery for battle-dress; and on that occasion too, I shall expect you to bid me leave in the same manner and with the same poise.

**Venmaal [pained a little, but brave nevertheless]:** Certainly, my Lord, for I have trained myself to school my emotions. Scars of battle are but trophies of valour. And the supreme confidence is mine that even if you were to sustain wounds in a war, miracle medication shall ever be there on my eager lips and finger-tips.

**Senguttuvan [moved by her answer, and changing the topic]:** My dear, doesn’t it strike you that every year we alone are celebrating our birthdays . . .?

**Venmaal [shy]:** You mean an heir is yet to be born; why don’t you ask the court astrologer today as to when we may rock a cradle here?

*There is a knock at the door and Senguttuvan’s younger brother Ilango enters. He looks more like a poet than a prince and around his head is the halo of learning.*

**Ilango:** What is all this talk about astrologers and predictions? Will people never learn to outgrow such superstitions? . . . Well, it is getting rather late, brother, please come at once or we would keep the court waiting.
