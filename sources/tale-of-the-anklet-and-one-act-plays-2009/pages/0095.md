# 23 Madhavi’s Renunciation

Masathuvan’s mansion in Poompuhar. Masathuvan is scanning the epistle.

Kovalan’s voice: Cherished and Revered! My obeisance to you. No one but I myself am responsible for the flight, accompanied by wife and unknown even to fond parents. Hapless am I, with no cure to offer for a lacerated heart and I can only beg to be forgiven, again and again.

Your Beloved.

Masathuvan sighs and gazes into the distance. When he comes out of his abstraction, he finds Madhavi standing silent before him, with Manimekalai in her arms. Behind her are five maids bearing trays laden with gold.

Masathuvan [surprised]: May I know . . .?

Madhavi: Pardon me, sir, for the intrusion. I am the sinner who is the cause of the tragic happenings in your family. I am Madhavi. I have come now, sir, to return the gold which had been taken from your son, without my being aware of it. You would relieve a part of my burden of sorrow if you deign to accept what is yours.

Masathuvan: You have a heart of unalloyed gold, Madhavi, and I realise it only now.

Madhavi: My heart is past mending, sir, for I am a withered flower which the plant has cast off.
