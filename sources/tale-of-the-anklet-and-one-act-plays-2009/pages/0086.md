## Transcription

Madhavi: I will call it off in time, so you need not worry about it. He is returning. Leave me now.

Madhavi goes near a warrior’s statue in the vicinity and imagining it to be Kovalan’s for a moment, she addresses it.

Madhavi: Oh, my beloved, you did not come to me with true love. Like a brigand you came to rob me of the pearls of my smile, the ruby of my lips and the roses of my cheeks. And yet I fell for you and now I cannot live without you. I am aching for your embrace, dear, so don’t leave Madhavi the Queen of fine arts to pine away like this.

Midway through her soliloquy, Kovalan has returned, after emerging from his wild dream. Listening to her, he is enraged.

Kovalan: Stop, you strumpet, stop!

Madhavi [running to him, terrified]: My Lord . . .

Kovalan [shaking her off]: Don’t dare to touch me. Beguiled by you I have lost my all and become an object of derision for my kith and kin and friends. Driving my devoted wife Kannagi to despair, I wallowed in your sinful lap and it has taken this long for me to wake up to the truth about you.

Madhavi [shocked, closing her ears with her hands]: Enough, my Lord, enough! Is it for this that the day dawned?

Kovalan: This day has redeemed me from your clutches!

Madhavi [in a daze]: Oh, what have I done to merit this treatment from you?

Kovalan: With my own ears I have listened to the prayer you addressed to your secret lover, and do you still think that your game can go on?

Madhavi [falling at his feet]: Do not torture me, my Lord. I have never denied I belong to a family of courtesans but I swear on all that is sacred that I have not even thought of any man other than you. You have got to believe me, my Lord.

Kovalan: I know you are well-versed in arts of all sorts, but I shall no longer be deceived by your seductive smile and succulent speech. Try your craft on the new lover you have hooked and thus progress in your trade. Get away from here, for your very presence is poison to me!

Madhavi swoons. In utter indifference Kovalan turns away.
