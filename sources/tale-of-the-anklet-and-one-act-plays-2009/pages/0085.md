## Transcription

O Cauveri constant.

The secret of your grace  
I’ve found out, coy maiden,—  
It’s because your Lord  
wields a sceptre ever straight!

Kovalan [sombre]: I see ... according to you, Cauveri’s constant grace is due to her husband remaining upright and just. The moral is that your affection for me depends on my affection for you and in case I neglect you, you would turn to ...

Madhavi: [raising her voice a little]: What a contorted construction you are putting on my simple lyric!

She lays the harp aside, leaves her seat and walks out on the sand. Kovalan follows her on the seashore. A fisherwoman walks between Madhavi and Kovalan. His fertile fancy sees Madhavi in the fisherwoman.

Kovalan [to himself]: Cruel indeed are the eyes of this damsel who is wielding flower-stalks to drive the birds away from her catch . . . [Rambling] Blossoms from the garden, flawless speech and a face rivalling the full moon . . .

Madhavi watches him from a distance.

Kovalan [still not himself]: A beauty of tender age, so delicate of form, with waist thin as gossamer ...

Harassing are the eyes  
of the gold-hued damsel here,  
her tresses-like curly clouds  
and her teeth brighter than pearl;  
the sand she walks upon  
and the grove she shelters in—  
Harassing are they  
but I dare not get away!

Madhavi: [to Vasantamalai who has joined her]: Did you observe his behaviour?

Vasantamalai: Yes, Madhavi. There is a limit even for the aberrations of a tiff.

Madhavi [simmering]: He has taken a fancy to some other woman and he is no longer able to conceal his perfidy ... I will make him suffer for this, by pretending to have fallen in love with some other man.

Vasantamalai: It is a dangerous game, Madhavi. Do not play with fire.
