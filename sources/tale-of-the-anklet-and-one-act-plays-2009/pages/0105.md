## Publication text

### 28 Theft on Record

Sanctum in the Pandya palace.

King Nedunchezhian is engrossed in writing. Palm-leaves are arranged neatly, on the carved desk in front of him. Kopperundevi enters.

Kopperundevi: I wonder why the anklet has not been brought yet... The goldsmith seems to be taking his own time, to mend it ... [Apparently oblivious of her presence, Nedunchezhian keeps writing.]

Kopperundevi [sulking]: Here I am, voicing a problem, and for all the attention you bestow on me, I might as well be talking to myself! What is there so very urgent to write about, my Lord, even while I am waiting here?

Nedunchezhian [looking up, as from a trance]: Oh, I am writing a poem, on the importance of learning . . .

Kopperundevi [still sulking]: When it is not an exotic poem, it is the thunder of battle. That is all the subject one hears in this place. [Angrily] Let me remind you, my Lord, that this is our bedchamber and not a classroom—nor a military camp!
