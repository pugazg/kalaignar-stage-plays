## Transcription

The reason, I know,  
for your constancy,  
is that the faith  
in chastity  
is immutable  
for womanhood.

And so, blessed are you,  
O Cauveri!

Madhavi: A lovely lyric, my Lord, and the melody was so rich; but ... [she hesitates and her face clouds.]

Kovalan [urging her]: Yes, Madhavi . . .

Madhavi: The content of the lyric? [she leaves the question incomplete.]

Kovalan: Why, my dear, did you not follow? [Explaining] The song was in praise of the Cauveri, whose Lord and Master Karikal Chola led an army to the North and extended his domain to include the River Ganga too. And yet his first wife Cauveri did not turn away from him. That shows the power of chastity in women and so Cauveri deserves to be blessed.

Madhavi: That is all right, so far as it goes. But your theme went further, my Lord, for, in the lyric, the Master of Cauveri did not halt his matrimonial adventures with Ganga . . .

Kovalan: Well, it is a historical fact that Karikalan conquered the Pandyas too and annexed Kumari. Even then Cauveri was the same loving wife.

Madhavi: Now I see the meaning of your lyric. The Master would make love to Ganga one day and to Kumari in the next. And still Cauveri will have to be passive and patient and constant. Likewise, the Master here would desert Kannagi for Madhavi and from Madhavi he would flit to ...

Kovalan [interrupting her]: Don’t misinterpret me, dear. All I had in mind was Karikalan’s glory and the boundless grace of Cauveri.

Madhavi [petulant, taking the harp]: Now, let me do a lyric.

[Song]

With honey-bees playing herald  
and decked in flowers bright,  
modest-eyed do you come,
