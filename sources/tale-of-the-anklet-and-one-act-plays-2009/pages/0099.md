# 25 Nedunchezhian the Upright

A solid fort enclosing the vast city of Mathurai. Flag with the emblem of the fish flies proud on the mast. The voice of Nedunchezhian the Pandya king is heard assuring the people that justice shall reign supreme in the land,

Voice: In the court of the Pandya king justice shall prevail, even if the skies fall . . .

King Nedunchezhian and Queen Kopperundevi are on the throne. Ministers and courtiers are at their appointed places. Two litigants stand in front of the king.

Nedunchezhian [addressing the litigants]: Both of you are scholars of repute and citizens of standing and I find it difficult to disbelieve either of you. [To one of them] Poet Arulalar, it is not disputed that the script of this literary work [pointing to a set of palm-leaves] is in your handwriting and on the face of this evidence the argument is valid that the author of the work could be only yourself. [To the second litigant] Poet Malaivanar, how do you propose to counter the evidence?

Malaivanar: I propose to explain it away, Your Majesty. [The courtiers titter.] Arulalar was my disciple and amanuensis and he wrote to my dictation.

Arulalar is taken aback by the audacity of the contention.

Arulalar: O Pandya king who nurtures the Tamil Sangam! on the greatness of Tamil, I swear that this literary work is wholly the product of my labour! At no time was Malaivanar my teacher!
