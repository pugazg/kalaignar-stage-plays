# Tale of the Anklet — Section 6 continued

**Kannagi [sulking]:** How long had you conspired, my Lord, to leave me alone and be happy elsewhere?

**Kovalan [caressing her as they walk on the damp sand]:** If agony could be called happiness, I was indeed happy, Kannagi. Remember that the sword of separation is sharp-edged on both sides.

*By now the two have reached dry sand. They stand in the steady sea-breeze as Kovalan takes out a small casket from the folds of his dress and gives it to Kannagi.*

**Kovalan:** This is from Greece and it is my special present to you.

*Kannagi opens the casket and sees an exquisite piece of carved ivory.*

**Kannagi [enraptured]:** Ivory exported from Tamilnad has come to life under the hands of the Greek craftsmen.

**Kovalan:** Materials from Tamilnad are much in demand there, Kannagi. Peacock feathers are made into the costumes of danseuses in Greece. In Rome and Egypt too, I found that possession of pearls from Tamilnad is considered an index of one’s wealth and standing in business circles.

**Kannagi:** What a rich tribute to our land!

**Kovalan:** But one thing they have not realised yet – that here in Tamilnad is a treasure which is far more precious than all the ivory and pearls and rubies...

**Kannagi [eager]:** What is there, my Lord, so very precious?

**Kovalan [softly]:** It is my Kannagi whose image is imprinted on my heart. Whenever I am in alien land, I think of Tamilnad as the birthplace of Kannagi and every time I chant her name, poetry wells up in me.

**Kannagi [proud and shy]:** My Lord, will you ever leave me again?

**Kovalan:** Is it ever possible to be away from Tamil?

**Kannagi [keeping up the metaphor]:** In case an alien language intervenes?

**Kovalan:** It will be swept away. Like light to the eyes and timbre to the harp and like the moon to the skies, shall I be with you for ever and aye.

**Kannagi:** Not like the moon, my Lord, for it plays truant. Don’t you know it is pitch-dark on the night of the new moon?

**Kovalan:** Kannagi, even if I forget my self I shall not forget your face of perfect beauty and grace which is the acme of the artist’s dream. Let us go home now and make up for all the days of separation.

*The elderly Greek gentleman walks up to them.*

**Greek gentleman:** Kovalan, I don’t blame you for forgetting an old man like me!
