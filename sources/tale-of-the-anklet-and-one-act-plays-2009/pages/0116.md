And when the news or this ignominy reaches you, how shall your heart bear the lightning-shock? When you became my wife, you must have dreamt of a future bright and placid, like an emerald-isle in the centre of the lake of contentment, where it is either sunshine or moonlight always, with not a streak of darkness in between and with not a murmur of sorrow in the air. Like a wanton boy I wrecked that dream and made your life a raging volcano; and not stopping with that, I have secured for you the title that you are the wife of a thief! . . . O Priceless Gem of my life, even as I walked here, I planned to build our home anew, brick by brick, and when the terrace-garden touched the skies, to rest my head on your lap, with the twinkling stars alone being witness to our love in fulfilment ... And now I am nothing more than a common thief, here in the streets of Mathurai.... You be my judge, Kannagi, and tell me, “Am I a thief? Am I a thief?”

Kovalan’s voice rises to a crescendo. Even as his cry of anguish reverberates in the air, the officer gives a signal to the chief of the guardsmen who thereupon mows down Kovalan with one sweep of his sword...
